# Exact coverage and physical verification

## 1. Coordinate convention and algebraic coverage

Write `z=x+i*sqrt(3)*y`, with real `x,y`. A coefficient `(a,b)` denotes
`a+b*w`. Each displayed norm equation is expanded over `Q[x,y]` and made
primitive. The code checks that the source rows give exactly the two signed
norm families, including every independent sign.

Use the invertible rational coordinate change `u=x+2*y`, `v=y`. The producer
computes a lexicographic Gröbner basis for each pair. Its proposed decomposition
is not accepted as a coverage certificate on its own. The checker independently
eliminates `v` with a resultant in `u`, factors that nonzero resultant over Q,
and considers every irreducible factor with a real root. Over the quotient
field for that factor it computes the Euclidean gcd of the two polynomials in
`v`. A constant gcd discards a spurious resultant factor. A linear gcd gives
all points above that factor. Rational exceptional fibres are factored and
checked separately. An unsupported nonlinear algebraic fibre, common vertical
component, or zero resultant raises an error; none is silently discarded.

Every real affine common zero must have its `u` coordinate at a resultant
root. The quotient gcd retains exactly the common affine zeros above it.
All retained charts are substituted into both original equations. Exact Sturm
root counts and rational isolating intervals account for all real embeddings;
complex-only factors do not define plane parameters.

For a nonrational chart, let `s` be any real root of its primitive irreducible
polynomial `q`. The monic fibre gcd is `v+h0(s)`, so the exact parameter is

```
y = -h0(s) in Q[s]/(q),
x = s - 2*y,
z = x + i*sqrt(3)*y.
```

This specifies coordinates without rounding. The two rational parameters are
`(-1/2,1/2)` and `(1/2,-1/2)` in the `(x,y)` convention. Rational charts use
an auxiliary `q=s` and store constant `x,y`. The full coordinate polynomials
are regenerated by the checker and may be exported; their long coefficient
lists are not an additional trusted input to the compact certificate.

The Gröbner and resultant decompositions agree entrywise on canonical charts
and their original equation-pair coverage. Across all 64 pairs there are 356
real solution incidences, which deduplicate to 66 charts and 340 parameters.
For nonrational charts `s=x+2*y`, so equal parameters require the same primitive
minimal polynomial of `s` and identical reduced `x,y` polynomials. If a reduced
polynomial vanished at even one real embedding, irreducibility would force it
to be zero. Rational points are canonical constants. `audit.distinctness`
checks these premises, so hashing identical canonical charts loses no parameter
and does not double-count one represented in several equation pairs.

## 2. Collision merging and the complete strict graph

For every chart the checker forms all 243 labelled sums from the definition of
A5. Arithmetic is exact in `Q[s]/(q)`. A point is represented by two reduced
polynomials `(X,Y)`, meaning `X+i*sqrt(3)*Y` at a chosen real root. Equal pairs
are merged, and every original label receives a physical point index.

For every unordered pair of distinct merged points it tests

```
(delta X)^2 + 3*(delta Y)^2 - 1 == 0 in Q[s]/(q).
```

An edge is included if and only if this exact identity holds. Because every
embedding of a field is injective, a nonzero residue cannot vanish at some
real conjugate. Thus both collision classes and the complete strict unit-edge
set are valid for **each** real root of that chart. This is stronger than a
prescribed-edge realization check; all incidental unit pairs are tested.

The producer uses the earlier exact event inventory to propose graph hashes
and colour words. The decisive checker uses the Cartesian all-pairs norm test,
without calling that event inventory for edges. Their coordinate, label-map,
and edge hashes must agree.

## 3. Ordinary chromatic decision

The producer obtains positive ordinary three-colour words with a SAT solver.
The checker needs no SAT solver: it verifies the word's domain, its length,
and unequal colours at every reconstructed edge. The physical labels for
`0,1,w` are distinct and mutually adjacent in every graph, giving the lower
bound three. Hence every physical graph in this cohort has chromatic number
exactly three. A non-four UNSAT certificate and a five-word are neither
present nor claimed.

## 4. Trust and scope

This is an exact computer-assisted finite result, with a human coverage
argument. It trusts CPython, SymPy's exact rational polynomial factorization,
resultants, irreducibility and real-root routines, python-flint's rational
arithmetic, and the small published helper/checker code. Producer and checker
share coordinate conventions and some arithmetic helpers; their different
elimination methods and different edge construction methods are **not**
independent peer review. SHA-256 hashes identify streams and are not standalone
mathematical proofs.

Controls cover rational and irrational solutions, no-real-root systems,
spurious resultant roots, collisions, complete triangle/lattice graphs, and
corrupted root, coordinate, edge and colouring data. They do not substitute
for the full cohort computation. The final verification also ran under
`python -O`; correctness checks use explicit exceptions rather than asserts.

The theorem concerns precisely the 64 stated pairs and their physical roots.
It does not close their entire pencil, all asymmetric pairs, all A5 supports,
all graphs in their fields, or the unrestricted sub-509 problem.
