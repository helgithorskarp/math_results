# Global cover, exact domains, and target equivalence

Let good43 mean a graph on 43 vertices with neither a red 5-clique nor a blue 5-clique, where blue denotes nonadjacency. No good43 is assumed to exist.

**Theorem.** Existence of a good43 is equivalent to existence of an index `0 <= k < M` whose physical graph from `index_family.unrank(k)` passes `verify_target.py`. Here M is `retained_rooted_family` in `COUNTS.json`, and M < 2^787. Equivalently, at least one of the 60 complete physical CNFs generated by `model.py` is satisfiable. No one of those existence questions is decided in this contribution.

## Forced vertex partition

We use the classical result [R(4,5)=25, McKay and Radziszowski, 1995](https://www.cs.rit.edu/~spr/PUBL/paper32.pdf). In a good43, every induced subgraph on at least 25 vertices contains a red 4-clique, since the alternative is a blue 5-clique. Repeatedly remove five red 4-cliques. Before these choices the remaining orders are 43, 39, 35, 31 and 27, so every choice is justified and 23 vertices remain.

Next remove two monochromatic 4-cliques. Their starting orders are 23 and 19, both at least R(4,4)=18. From the remaining 15 vertices remove four monochromatic triangles at orders 15, 12, 9 and 6, using R(3,3)=6. The final remainder has three vertices. All selected blocks are vertex-disjoint.

For completeness, the two small upper bounds have elementary proofs. In a two-color K6, a vertex has three incident edges of one color; either those three neighbors contain an edge of that color or they form an opposite triangle. Thus R(3,3)<=6. A graph on nine vertices with no red triangle and no blue 4-clique would have every red degree at most three (four red neighbors must form a blue K4) and at least three (six blue neighbors would contain a red triangle or a blue triangle extending to a blue K4). All nine degrees would then be three, contradicting the handshake lemma. Hence R(3,4)<=9. In K18 a vertex has nine neighbors in one color, and this R(3,4) bound produces a monochromatic K4. Only the upper bounds are needed.

Sort the seven 4-clique blocks with red first. Their red count r is 5, 6 or 7. Sort the four triangle blocks with red first; their red count s is 0, 1, 2, 3 or 4. By a permutation within the final triple its graph can be written with red edge mask 0, 1, 3 or 7 on the ordered pairs 01, 02, 12, corresponding to t=0,1,2,3 red edges. These are the four isomorphism types of three-vertex graphs.

Thus every good43 admits a branch `(r,s,t)` among exactly 60 possibilities. Assign the blocks consecutively to vertices 0–27 in groups of four and vertices 28–42 in groups of three. The first four triple blocks are monochromatic; the last has the prescribed three-vertex shape. This fixes 7·6+4·3+3=57 physical edges.

These operations are vertex relabelings. They impose no automorphism on the full graph and identify no cross edges. Blocks need not have equivalent neighborhoods. `normalize.py` implements the greedy choices, canonicalizes the indicated block order, last triple, and root signatures described below, and returns the entire vertex permutation. A supplied packing can also be checked and transported.

## Complete pair domains

The six atom types are R4, B4, R3, B3, E3 and P3: the two monochromatic cliques of each size, a triple with the single red edge 01, and a triple with the two red edges 01 and 02. For each ordered pair X,Y, enumerate all 2^(|X||Y|) cross matrices. Keep a matrix if and only if the physical graph on X union Y has no red or blue 5-clique. All 36 ordered domains are given as compact bitmaps in `DOMAINS.json`.

The producer forms the forbidden partial cross patterns arising from the literal five-subsets of X union Y. The independent checker instead constructs the physical adjacency graph in Gray-code order and recursively searches it and its complement for a 5-clique. It compares every bitmap entry, not only the cardinalities. It also verifies every transpose correspondence. In total, each calculation covers 335,872 cross matrices.

For a fixed branch, a cross matrix belongs to exactly one unordered pair of blocks. Its edges are disjoint from the coordinates of every other matrix. Internal atom edges are fixed. Therefore the constraints involving at most two blocks separate exactly, and the branch cardinality is

    N_(r,s,t) = product_{0 <= i < j < 12} |D(H_i,H_j)|.

There is no probabilistic independence assumption here: this is a Cartesian product of disjoint Boolean coordinate sets. These pair domains account for exactly

    21*C(8,5) + 35*C(7,5) + 10*C(6,5) = 1,971

physical five-sets. There are no five-sets inside one block. All other 960,627 five-sets meet at least three blocks and still impose global constraints.

## Normalizing all eleven root-to-child matrices

Keep the first red 4-clique as the root, with its four vertices in a fixed order. A vertex v outside the root has the four-bit red-neighbor signature

    sigma(v) = sum_{i=0}^3 2^i * edge(root_i,v).

Inside every monochromatic child block, reorder vertices so these signatures are nonincreasing. This is allowed because every permutation preserves that block's fixed internal graph. For an E3 child only vertices 0 and 1 are interchangeable; sort those two signatures. For a P3 child only leaves 1 and 2 are interchangeable; sort those two. Ties may be resolved arbitrarily. No permutation of the root is required.

These operations occur in disjoint vertex blocks and transport *all* their incident edges. They preserve the physical graph up to relabeling, the prescribed internal shapes, and every pair-domain condition. Thus every good43 still has a representative after all eleven orderings hold simultaneously. This does not assert that child permutations are automorphisms of the full graph: they are automorphisms only of the fixed internal atom and are used to relabel the whole graph.

For row-major cross-matrix integers, nonincreasing column signatures give the minimum matrix in the child-automorphism orbit. To see this, compare two columns whose signatures differ and use their highest differing root bit. Putting the larger signature in the earlier column lowers the highest changed matrix bit. Repeated adjacent exchanges sort every monochromatic child, while the mixed child permits exactly the one stated exchange.

The root domains therefore have sizes

    child:  R4    B4    R3   B3   E3    P3
    count:  1998  1931  596  680  1740  1740.

`ROOT_DOMAINS.json` gives every retained matrix. The producer uses signature inequalities; the independent checker enumerates every permutation preserving each child's actual internal adjacency, applies those permutations to every allowed matrix, and checks the exact orbit minima. It also verifies Burnside's fixed-point sum. The respective group orders are 24,24,6,6,2,2. This enumerates 1,817,036 allowed-matrix group images; it does not depend on any earlier graph-automorphism verifier.

## Counting and indexing the whole global family

The 60 branch families are disjoint as labeled physical graphs: their fixed 57-bit internal colorings are distinct. Consequently the unrestricted packing baseline has exactly 60·2^846 graphs. Write N for the total with just the 66 pair domains. For the final rooted family replace the eleven root domains by the canonical domains D*(H_j):

    M_(r,s,t) = product_{j=1}^{11} |D*(H_j)|
                * product_{1 <= i < j < 12} |D(H_i,H_j)|,
    M = sum_{r=5}^7 sum_{s=0}^4 sum_{t=0}^3 M_(r,s,t).

Every matrix still uses its own disjoint physical edge coordinates. In particular the eleven ordering constraints introduce no coupling between matrix coordinates. The product count is independently recomputed from atom-type frequencies after removing the root: same-type child pairs occur with exponent C(n_X,2), different-type child pairs with exponent n_X*n_Y, and root factors with exponent n_X. Every branch count matches. `EXACT_COUNTS.md` gives the closed formula and exact totals. Integer comparisons establish

    M < 2^787,
    2^65 * M < 60 * 2^846,
    2^36 * M < N < 2^823.

Thus 2^903/M exceeds 2^116. This is a cardinality reduction for an existential search using a chosen normal form, not the fraction of all labeled good43 graphs removed. Isomorphic graphs can have several represented labelings. The physical CNF still has 846 edge variables; the cardinality bound does not identify 116 individually fixed edges or imply a solver speedup.

Order branches lexicographically by r,s,t. Within each branch order the 66 block pairs lexicographically, and each allowed matrix by its unsigned row-major binary integer, using root domains for pairs (0,j). Mixed-radix ranking gives a bijection between `[0,M_(r,s,t))` and that branch's matrices, with the first matrix as the least significant digit. Adding preceding branch cardinalities gives the bijection with `[0,M)`. Each coordinate is decoded into its actual physical edges. Distinct indices yield distinct labeled physical graphs because every matrix coordinate and the branch's internal coloring can be recovered from the graph.

## Complete target constraints

`model.py` has one Boolean variable for each of the 846 cross-block edges and one forced-true constant. It first emits the root-signature comparisons. For each adjacent ordered pair of signatures, 120 clauses of eight literals forbid the assignments x<y with x,y in {0,...,15}. There are 3,360 such clauses when the last triple is monochromatic and 3,240 when it is mixed. No extra variables are required. It then emits both monochromatic-prevention clauses for every physical five-set, omitting a clause only if a fixed internal edge already makes it tautological. The coefficients of the product of the atoms' monochromatic-clique polynomials independently count the surviving clauses. A second calculation enumerates all 962,598 actual five-sets, aggregates their 3,272 distinct internal-edge stencils, and confirms every branch's clause counts.

A satisfying assignment supplies all 903 physical edges, has the stated block partition, and contains no monochromatic five-set. It is therefore good43. Conversely any good43 normalizes to one of the branches, its cross matrices belong to the pair domains, and the root normalization ensures the ordering clauses as well as all full-graph clauses. This proves both equivalences in the theorem.

The decoder accepts only a complete consistent SAT model satisfying the root comparisons and then checks every actual five-set before writing a target. A member of the pair-domain family, or a passing rank/unrank check, is not itself a Ramsey certificate. The independent final verifier requires exactly 43 vertices.

## Context and limits

Greedy applications of classical Ramsey bounds are standard; no novelty or priority is claimed for that argument. The contribution is the exact unconditional 60-branch cover, independent finite-domain and root-orbit census, complete integer indexing, and checked physical interface. The literature on [monochromatic clique decompositions](https://www.cantab.net/users/henry.liu/mono%20clique%20dec.pdf) includes partitions of edges; the elementary argument here partitions vertices, and its theorem is not imported.

No SAT solver is called, no complete branch is excluded, no good43 is constructed, and no lower bound is improved. The proof is not formalized in a proof assistant. Its external mathematical premise is the stated classical Ramsey bound; it does not use the invalidated automorphism claim, a saved parent or projection witness, any fixed H92/H93 subsystem, or a cut-rank assumption.
