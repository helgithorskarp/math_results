# Strict passages near the 30-axis orbit are excluded

Author: **six-rupert-1**, role **researcher**, 2026-09-30.

Let `K=conv(V)` be the standard deltoidal hexecontahedron, with the
62 exact vertices generated by [verify.py](verify.py). Let `P_n` denote
orthogonal projection onto `n`-perpendicular. Put `s=sqrt(5)`,
`phi=(1+s)/2`, and

```
E=((3s-5)/10,(5-s)/10,1),
r1=(-1,-(5+s)/2,(5+3s)/10),
r2=(-1,(-31+5s)/22,(7+s)/22),
Tstar=5/2-s,
astar=Tstar*r1+(1-Tstar)*r2=(-1,-phi,1/phi).
```

Write `G` for the body reflection group already verified in
[orientation_proof.md](orientation_proof.md). Its orbit of the
**unoriented** direction `E` contains 30 axes.

**Theorem.** For every unit representative `n0` of this orbit there are
`d(n0)>0` and `theta0(n0)>0` such that for every unit receiver normal
`n` with `||n-n0||<=d(n0)`, every proper rotation `Q` with full relative
angle `0<theta<=theta0(n0)`, and every planar translation `v`,

```
P_n(QK)+v is not contained in the interior of P_n(K).
```

Both receiver and inner directions may vary. The inner direction in
body coordinates is `Q^-1 n`. This theorem excludes **strict**
containment. The proper closed contact family in
[contact_family_proof.md](contact_family_proof.md) still exists and is
compatible with the theorem.

**Corollary.** For any sequence of strict passages whose nonzero full
relative angles tend to zero, every accumulation axis of the receiver
and inner normals lies in `G.[(0,0,1)]`, an orbit of **15 twofold axes**.
On each compact set of receiver normals avoiding these axes, a single
positive angle bound excludes strict passages.

This refines the earlier 45-axis reduction and closes its mirror-normal
branch at the 30-axis orbit. It does not exclude moving pairs near the
15 twofold axes or rotations bounded away from zero. The global Rupert
property of the named solid remains unresolved. No exact strict passage
is asserted.

## 1. Prior reduction and the missing rate

The necessary cone and incident-cell arguments in
[contact_family_proof.md, Sections 4–6](contact_family_proof.md#4-the-limiting-cone-uses-uniquely-projected-hull-vertices)
establish the following facts without assuming a differentiable path or
relative rates. If strict passages have receiver normal tending to
`E/|E|` and nonzero relative angle tending to zero, their oriented unit
rotation axes tend to `astar/|astar|`. Locally fold the receivers into
the body chamber. Two of its three cells incident to `E` exclude even
closed containments; the surviving cell is cell 0.

Let `S_h` be the body reflection with normal
`h=(-phi,-phi^2,1)=phi*astar`. It fixes `E`. The five support edges
used below are valid over the whole closed set

```
C=cell0 union S_h(cell0).
```

This is checked at all six polygon corners against all 62 vertices,
with 1,860 exact comparisons. The local folding uses either the
identity or `S_h`; the axial action `-S_h` fixes `astar`.

For large indices the Cayley vector of a hypothetical sequence has the
unique representation

```
w=epsilon*(astar+delta*(r1-r2)+z*E),
epsilon>0, epsilon->0, delta->0, z->0.
```

Here `delta=t-Tstar` in the earlier parameterization
`w=epsilon*(a(t)+z*E)`. The receiver can be scaled to the chart
`u=E+(X,Y,0)` with `X,Y->0`. The rotation is

```
Q_w v=((1-|w|^2)*v+2*(w cross v)+2*w*(w.v))/(1+|w|^2).
```

The preceding theorem gave convergence of `delta,z`, but no bound on
`z/delta` or on either quantity relative to `epsilon`. The two endpoint
contacts below supply the needed bound, with all receiver perturbations
included.

Translations can be removed throughout: `K=-K`, hence both projected
bodies are centered and centrally symmetric. If `A+v` lies in the
interior of convex `B=-B`, then `A-v` does as well, and midpoint
convexity gives `A` in the interior of `B`. The same argument applies
to closed containment. Thus it suffices to use centered support gaps.

## 2. A fixed axis vertex forces small axial drift

The exact vertex identity is

```
V45=-(s/2)*astar.
```

For a supporting contact `(a,b,j)` define the polynomial vector

```
R_(a,b,j)=((1+|w|^2)*Va-(1-|w|^2)*Vj
           -2*(w cross Vj)-2*w*(w.Vj)) cross (Vb-Va).
```

Its scalar gap numerator is `u.R`; the positive denominator is
`1+|w|^2`. Strict centered containment requires this gap to be strictly
positive at every nonzero supporting edge normal.

Use the two incident endpoint contacts

```
e_plus =(58,45,45),
e_minus=(45,34,45).
```

For each contact the **entire vector** `R` vanishes at `delta=z=0`
for every `epsilon`: the rotation fixes `V45`. Each component is
divisible by `epsilon` and lies in the polynomial ideal `(delta,z)`.
Thus the exact scalar identities are

```
G_plus /epsilon=delta*A_plus +z*B_plus,
G_minus/epsilon=delta*A_minus+z*B_minus,
```

where `G=u.R`, and the coefficients are polynomials in
`epsilon,delta,z,X,Y`. At the origin,

```
A_plus=A_minus=0,
B_plus=c, B_minus=-c, c=(40-16s)/3>1.
```

[mirror_branch_certificate.py](mirror_branch_certificate.py) reconstructs
the rows directly and checks each ideal decomposition coefficient by
coefficient. It proves on the box

```
0<epsilon<=r, |delta|,|z|,|X|,|Y|<=r, r=1/1000000
```

that

```
|A_plus|,|A_minus|<=eta=1/1000,
B_plus>1, B_minus<-1.
```

The respective absolute bounds for `A` are `9107/90750000` and
`3523/45375000`, both below `eta`. The errors in the axial coefficients
are at most `43/500000` and `39/500000`.

If both endpoint gaps are nonnegative, the second inequality for
`z>=0`, or the first for `z<=0`, gives the necessary rate bound

```
|z|<=eta*|delta|.                                      (1)
```

This handles arbitrary relative sizes of `epsilon,delta` and receiver
perturbations. In particular, `delta=0` forces `z=0`; then the fixed
vertex supplies an exact zero support gap, excluding strict containment.

The same identities also give `z=o(|delta|)` along any sequence of
closed containments approaching the origin with `delta!=0`: the `A`
coefficients tend to zero, whereas the axial coefficients tend to the
opposite nonzero constants. The explicit bound (1) suffices here.

## 4. Reflection covers negative delta in an explicit smaller box

Put

```
c_m=1+4s/5, d_m=(-5+4s)/11,
K=d_m+t*(c_m-d_m)=1+(c_m-d_m)*delta.
```

The verified axial reflection acts as

```
-S_h r1=c_m*r2, -S_h r2=d_m*r1, -S_h E=-E.
```

Since `d_m+Tstar*(c_m-d_m)=1` and
`d_m*(1-Tstar)=Tstar`, conjugating the rotation by `S_h` gives

```
epsilon'=epsilon*K, delta'=-delta/K, z'=-z/K.           (3)
```

The body and `C` are preserved by the reflection. The receiver is
renormalized to `(S_h u)/(S_h u)_z`, which has positive denominator
near `E`. Strict containment and support gaps are preserved geometrically.

Here is an explicit sufficient neighborhood, rather than an assumed
asymptotic rate. Restrict all five original variables to

```
0<epsilon<=r0, |delta|,|z|,|X|,|Y|<=r0,
r0=r/10=1/10000000.                                  (4)
```

The checker verifies `0<c_m-d_m<3`, hence `1/2<K<2` on this box.
The first three transformed variables in (3) are bounded by `2*r0`.
The reflection is orthogonal, so every matrix entry has absolute value
at most one; the checker verifies this exactly, as well as `S_h E=E`
and `|E_x|,|E_y|<=1`. With `D0=(X,Y,0)`, each coordinate of `S_h D0`
has absolute value at most `2*r0`. Therefore

```
|X'|,|Y'|<=4*r0/(1-2*r0)<=8*r0<=r.
```

For `delta<0`, the transformed delta is positive and the whole
transformed point lies in the box of Sections 2–3. That exclusion
applies. For `delta>=0` the original point already lies there.
At `delta=0` the fixed-vertex argument applies. Thus (4), with receiver
in `C`, excludes strict passages for **all signs** of `delta,z`.

## 5. From the parameter box to a receiver neighborhood

Suppose the stated theorem failed at `E/|E|`. Then for every integer
`k` choose a strict passage with receiver within `1/k` of that normal
and nonzero angle at most `1/k`. Remove translations and fold locally
into the body chamber. The prior incident-cell theorem rules out cells
2 and 4 for all sufficiently large indices. The prior strict-axis
theorem gives convergence of the oriented axes to `astar/|astar|`.
Thus the unique Cayley parameters and chart perturbations converge as
in Section 1. Eventually all lie in (4), with receiver in `C`. Section
4 contradicts the strict passage. This proves positive receiver and
angle neighborhood bounds. Their numerical cap radii are not computed
by this compactness argument; `r0` is a parameter-box bound.

Apply the finite body symmetry group and both signs of the normal to
obtain the theorem at all 30 projective directions. The
[previous stable theorem](stable_proof.md) excludes even closed
containments locally away from the 45-axis set. Adding the present
strict exclusions removes its 30-axis orbit. Any accumulating receiver
normal of a small-angle strict passage sequence must therefore lie
in the remaining 15-axis orbit. Inner normals have the same limits,
since `||Q^-1 n-n||<=theta`. Finally, a finite subcover of any compact
set avoiding that orbit gives a single positive strict angle bound.
