# A fixed-link symmetry reduction for `C(13,7,5)`

This directory records a reproducible first attack on the exact frontier

\[
77 \le C(13,7,5) \le 78.
\]

It establishes three things:

1. a definition-level reconstruction and verification of Bluskov's cyclic
   78-block upper-bound design;
2. an exact structural reduction for completing one published optimal
   41-block `C(12,6,4)` link to a 77-block cover; and
3. DRAT-certified nonexistence of completions of that fixed link invariant
   under automorphisms of five specified conjugacy types.

It **does not** decide whether the fixed link has an asymmetric completion and
does not decide whether `C(13,7,5)` is 77 or 78.

## Mathematical setting

A `(v,k,t)` covering is a family of distinct `k`-subsets of a `v`-set such
that every `t`-subset lies in at least one block. Charlie Krug proved
`C(12,6,4)=41`; the Schoenheim inequality therefore gives

\[
C(13,7,5)\ge \left\lceil\frac{13}{7}41\right\rceil=77.
\]

The lower-bound source is C. Krug, *The covering number C(12,6,4) is 41*,
[arXiv:2607.23766](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23766). The upper-bound source is
Theorem 2.3.24 of I. Bluskov, *New Designs and Coverings* (1997),
[Library and Archives Canada thesis PDF](https://central.bac-lac.gc.ca/.item?app=Library&id=nq24295&oclc_number=46548328&op=pdf).

## The verified 78-block construction

Bluskov starts with the cyclic projective plane on 13 points generated by the
line `[1,2,4,10]` modulo 13 and takes unions of pairs of lines. The 78 line
pairs give 78 distinct seven-blocks. `verify_upper_78.py` checks the projective
plane property, equality with Bluskov's six listed cyclic base-block orbits,
and every subset multiplicity through size five.

```sh
python3 verify_upper_78.py | diff -u EXPECTED_UPPER_78.txt -
```

The five-set histogram is `1:1170,4:117`, so all 1,287 five-sets are covered.

## Fixed-link reduction

The file `cover_41.txt` is the public 41-block `(12,6,4)` cover distributed by
Krug's proof package at
[dennisonbertram/covering-number-c12-6-4](https://github.com/dennisonbertram/covering-number-c12-6-4/blob/main/data/cover_41.txt).
The copied input has SHA-256
`0f701d9c281ef611bb4c2d9366839aee205d462bdc9cf5dbd774d9a6701e72d1`.

Fix point 0 and turn each six-block `L` into the seven-block `{0} union L`.
Any 77-block completion then has exactly 36 further seven-blocks on points
1 through 12. The fixed link covers 246 distinct five-sets on those 12 points,
leaving 546 residual five-sets.

For a candidate seven-block `B`, let `c(B)` be the number of residual
five-sets contained in `B`. Exact enumeration gives `c(B) <= 16` and

```text
c(B)             10  11  12  13  14  15  16
number of B       6  30 120  60  30 300 246
```

Consequently every completion satisfies

\[
\sum_{B\text{ selected}}(16-c(B))\le 36\cdot16-546=30.
\]

In particular it uses at least six capacity-16 blocks, since five such blocks
and 31 blocks of capacity at most 15 cover at most 545 residual incidences.

Exhaustive enumeration of all degree-preserving point permutations finds that
the fixed link has automorphism group of order 720. Restriction to its six
degree-20 points is faithful and onto `S_6`. The 792 candidate seven-blocks
split into 12 automorphism orbits, and the 246 capacity-16 candidates form just
three orbits. Thus, after applying a link automorphism, the first
capacity-16 block can be fixed to one of only three representatives:

```text
1 2 3 4 5 7 8
1 2 3 4 5 8 9
1 3 7 8 9 10 11
```

Reproduce the entire audit, including the 12-orbit table, using only the
Python standard library:

```sh
python3 analyze_fixed_link.py cover_41.txt \
  | diff -u EXPECTED_FIXED_LINK_ANALYSIS.txt -
python3 c1375_sat.py self-test
```

The automorphism enumeration is exhaustive: every automorphism preserves the
two six-point degree classes, and the program tests all `6! * 6!` such
permutations directly against all 41 blocks.

## Certified nontrivial-symmetry exclusions

Identify the link automorphism group with `S_6` through its action on the six
degree-20 points. If a completion is invariant under an automorphism `g`, its
36 variable blocks are unions of orbits of the cyclic group generated by `g`.
There is one conjugacy class per cycle type in `S_6`, so one representative
per type suffices.

`fixed_link_invariant.py` constructs the exact orbit-selection CNF for all ten
nonidentity cycle types. Coverage is encoded directly. Weighted constraints
encode at most 36 blocks, the capacity-deficit bound above, and the point
degree consequences of `C(12,6,4)=41`. The latter two are redundant but useful
cuts; their soundness also uses the known lower bound 77, which forces exactly
36 variable blocks.

The following five cycle types are UNSAT, with DRAT proofs independently
accepted by `drat-trim`:

| cycle type on degree-20 points | conclusion |
| --- | --- |
| `6` | no invariant completion |
| `5+1` | no invariant completion |
| `4+1+1` | no invariant completion |
| `4+2` | no invariant completion |
| `3+2+1` | no invariant completion |

Equivalently, if this fixed link does extend to a 77-block cover, the stabilizer
of point 0 contains no element of any of these five types. The other five
nonidentity types (`2+1+1+1+1`, `2+2+1+1`, `2+2+2`, `3+1+1+1`, and `3+3`)
remain open in this artifact.

`CERTIFICATE_MANIFEST.tsv`. Build the pinned solvers from their upstream

## Prime-order exact reductions (not yet exclusions)

By Cauchy's theorem, any nontrivial point stabilizer contains an element of
prime order. After the certified `5+1` exclusion, the five remaining relevant
cycle types are exactly the three involution types and the two order-three
types listed above.

For an automorphism of prime order `p`, every cyclic candidate-block orbit has
size 1 or `p`. If `f` fixed orbits and `m` moving orbits are selected, the known
lower bound 77 forces the exact equation

\[
f+pm=36.
\]

`augment_prime_exact.py` enumerates every feasible `f` and adds two exact
cardinality constraints in each branch. The branches are disjoint and cover
all invariant 36-block selections. In particular, type `3+3` has no fixed
candidate-block orbit and must select exactly 12 of its 264 size-three orbits;
type `2+2+2` must select exactly 18 of its 396 size-two orbits.

There is also a stronger residual-capacity inequality than the earlier
block-by-block one. For a cyclic block orbit `O`, let `U_O` be the union of the
residual five-sets covered by its blocks and put

\[
d_O=16|O|-|U_O|.
\]

For any selected orbit family `X` covering all 546 residual targets,

\[
\sum_{O\in X}d_O\le 576-546=30.
\]

Moreover, two selected orbits `O,P` necessarily satisfy

\[
d_O+d_P+|U_O\cap U_P|\le30.
\]

Indeed, the intersection already contributes that many repeated coverages,
while the total repeated-coverage budget is `30-sum(d_O)`. The script
`strengthen_union_deficit.py` adds the union-deficit PB constraint and every
violated-pair binary clause. It divides all weights and the bound by their
gcd before encoding. For `3+3`, 34 cyclic orbits have internal repeated
coverage; the normalized bin-merge constraint has 2,433 clauses and the pair
test adds 555 incompatibilities. For `2+2+2`, the corresponding counts are
4,897 and 24.

```sh
python3 augment_prime_exact.py --self-test
python3 augment_prime_exact.py \
  build/cases/cycle-3-3.cnf build/prime-exact/cycle-3-3
python3 strengthen_union_deficit.py cover_41.txt \
  build/prime-exact/cycle-3-3/cycle-3-3-exact-36-fixed-0.cnf \
  build/cycle-3-3-exact-union.cnf --encoder pb-binmerge
python3 split_capacity_orbits.py cover_41.txt \
  build/prime-exact/cycle-3-3/cycle-3-3-exact-36-fixed-0.cnf \
  build/cycle-3-3-capacity-branches
```

The last command independently lifts the centralizer from the faithful
six-point action. For `3+3` it finds centralizer order 18 and reduces 82
capacity-16 cyclic orbits to 15 representative first-orbit branches. Exact
instance hashes and sizes are in `PRIME_ORDER_MANIFEST.tsv`; compact expected
output is in `EXPECTED_PRIME_ORDER_REDUCTION.txt`.

These reductions do **not** yet exclude `3+3` or `2+2+2`. CaDiCaL 1.9.5 and
Kissat 4.0.4 each timed out at 300 seconds on both normalized `3+3` encodings
and on the bin-merge `2+2+2` encoding. All 15 single-capacity-orbit `3+3`
branches also timed out at 120 seconds. No solver conclusion or incomplete
proof from those runs is retained as mathematical evidence.

## Other exact encodings

`c1375_sat.py` produces a direct 1,716-primary-variable CNF for a cover with at
most 77 blocks. Its optional degree-41 symmetry break is sound because the
total point-degree excess over 41 is only six. `fixed_link_extension.py`
produces the unrestricted fixed-link completion CNF and its redundant degree
and capacity cuts. Five-minute CaDiCaL/Kissat runs on these unrestricted
instances ended `UNKNOWN`; no conclusion is claimed from those timeouts.

## Trust boundary

The upper design and structural orbit computations use only exact Python
integers and complete finite enumeration. The UNSAT results additionally trust
the interpretation implemented by this Python source and the PB encoding
produced by `pypblib`. The prime-order branch completeness and orbit-union
inequalities are checked independently of solver answers, but their CNF forms
also trust PySAT's cardinality/PB encoders. The certified exclusions do not
trust either SAT solver's answer: each retained answer has a DRAT proof checked
by a separately built `drat-trim`. The omitted proof files can be regenerated
by the commands above and compared against the manifest.
