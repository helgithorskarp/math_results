# Characteristic independence through five generators

Research note, 26 September 2026. Status: complete written proof attempt;
external mathematical review and priority assessment are pending.

## Statement

Let $S\subseteq\mathbb N^d$ be an affine semigroup minimally generated by
nonzero vectors $n_1,\ldots,n_e$, where $e\le5$. For every field $k$, set

$$
G_k=\operatorname{gr}_{\mathfrak m}k[S],\qquad
\mathfrak m=(t^{n_1},\ldots,t^{n_e}),\qquad P_k=k[x_1,\ldots,x_e].
$$

**Theorem.** All fine Betti numbers
$\dim_k\operatorname{Tor}^{P_k}_i(G_k,k)_{(s,j)}$, and therefore all ordinary
graded and total Betti numbers, are independent of $k$.
Here $\deg x_v=(n_v,1)$ and the initial monomial of $t^s$ has degree
$(s,\operatorname{ord}(s))$. In particular this holds for tangent cones of
numerical semigroup rings of embedding dimension at most five, including the
complete local presentation $k[[t^S]]$.

More precisely, every nonempty connected component of the absolute complexes
$C_{s,j}$ defined below has the homotopy type of a wedge of spheres. Every
integral relative homology group
$\widetilde H_q(D_{s,j},D_{s,j+1};\mathbb Z)$ is free abelian.
No claim of sharpness at six generators is made.

## 1. The chain complexes and the topological input

Write $n_F=\sum_{v\in F}n_v$ and let $\operatorname{ord}(a)$ be the maximum
factorization length of $a$ in the minimal generators. Positivity makes this
maximum finite. As proved at chain level in [THEOREM.md](THEOREM.md), put

$$
D_{s,j}=\{F\subseteq[e]:s-n_F\in S,
                \operatorname{ord}(s-n_F)+|F|\ge j\}.
$$

The fine Koszul strand is the augmented relative chain complex of
$(D_{s,j},D_{s,j+1})$, shifted by one. This identification also works over
$\mathbb Z$: all differential coefficients are $0,1,-1$. If $L=D_{s,j+1}$
is nonvoid, attach a cone with a new apex $v$ and set

$$
C_{s,j}=D_{s,j}\cup(v*L).
$$

If $L$ is void, set $C_{s,j}=D_{s,j}$. The acyclic augmented chain complex of
the cone gives, over $\mathbb Z$ and over every field,

$$
\widetilde H_q(C_{s,j})\cong
\widetilde H_q(D_{s,j},D_{s,j+1}).
\tag{1}
$$

The conventions distinguish the void complex from $\{\varnothing\}$; their
augmented homology causes no torsion issue.

We use the following precise external theorem: a connected simplicial complex
on at most six vertices is either the unique six-vertex triangulation of
$\mathbb{RP}^2$ or is homotopy equivalent to a wedge of spheres.
This is [Govc–Marzantowicz–Michalak–Pavešić, *Fundamental groups of small
simplicial complexes*, Theorem 3.6](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.02586v1).
The exception is a combinatorial isomorphism type, not merely a homotopy type.
Thus we need only exclude that particular complex as $C_{s,j}$.

## 2. Facets have factorizations with exactly their support

For a factorization $c=(c_1,\ldots,c_e)\in\mathbb N^e$ of $s$, write
$|c|=\sum c_v$ and $\operatorname{supp}(c)=\{v:c_v>0\}$.

**Support lemma.** A face $F$ belongs to $D_{s,j}$ if and only if there is a
factorization $c$ of $s$ with $F\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(c)$ and $|c|\ge j$.
If $F$ is a facet of $D_{s,j}$, such a factorization has support exactly $F$.
If also $F\notin D_{s,j+1}$, its length is exactly $j$.

**Proof.** A maximal factorization of $s-n_F$, together with one copy of each
generator in $F$, proves one direction. Conversely, remove one copy of each
generator in $F$ from $c$ to obtain
$\operatorname{ord}(s-n_F)+|F|\ge|c|$.
The same argument shows that $\operatorname{supp}(c)$ belongs to $D_{s,j}$,
so maximality of the face forces equality of supports. Length exceeding $j$
would put $F$ in $D_{s,j+1}$. $\square$

The facet hypothesis matters: a general face need not admit a factorization
whose support is exactly that face.

## 3. The projective plane is impossible

Suppose $C_{s,j}$ is the six-vertex projective plane. Then $e=5$ and $L$ is
nonvoid. Deleting the cone apex recovers $D=D_{s,j}$, and its link is $L$.
Label the remaining vertices $1,\ldots,5$ cyclically. The facets of $L$ are

$$
12,\ 23,\ 34,\ 45,\ 15,
$$

and the facets of $D$ are

$$
124,\ 134,\ 135,\ 235,\ 245.
\tag{2}
$$

Equivalently, the facets in (2) are the complements of the five nonedges of
the cycle. This description holds at every vertex of the unique triangulation.

Choose the positive linear functional $\ell(a)=a_1+\cdots+a_d$ on
$\mathbb N^d$ and write $a_v=\ell(n_v)>0$, $b=\ell(s)$.
Every facet in (2) lies in $D\setminus L$, so the support lemma gives a
factorization supported exactly on that triangle, of length $j$. In particular
$j\ge3$ and $b>0$. Put

$$
A=b/j,\qquad U=\{v:a_v<A\},\qquad H=[5]\setminus U.
$$

Every cycle edge is a facet of $L=D_{s,j+1}$. The support lemma supplies a
factorization supported on its two endpoints of length $r\ge j+1$.
Its weighted average of the $a_v$ is $b/r<b/j=A$. Thus at least one endpoint
belongs to $U$: the set $H$ is independent in the five-cycle.

Every independent set in a five-cycle is contained in a nonadjacent pair.
Choose such a pair $Q\supseteq H$. By (2), its complement $T=[5]\setminus Q$
is a facet of $D$, and $T\subseteq U$. The length-$j$ factorization supported
on $T$ now yields

$$
b=\sum_{v\in T}c_v a_v
  <\sum_{v\in T}c_v A=jA=b,
$$

a contradiction. Strict inequality uses $a_v<A$ for all $v\in T$ and
$c_v>0$. The possibility $a_v=A$ is already included in $H$ and causes no gap.

## 4. Conclusion and prior-work boundary

If a connected component of $C_{s,j}$ were the exceptional triangulation, it
would use all six available vertices. It would therefore be all of $C_{s,j}$,
which Section 3 excludes. The topological theorem gives the asserted homotopy
types and torsion-free integral homology. Equation (1) transfers this to the
relative groups. The universal coefficient theorem then makes their dimensions
over every field equal to their integral ranks, proving the Betti-number claim.

The general relative-divisor-complex method is classical:
[Bruns–Herzog, *Semigroup rings and simplicial complexes* (1997), Proposition
1.1](https://www.home.uni-osnabrueck.de/wbruns/brunsw/pdf-article/SimpSemi.published.pdf).
Their Corollary 1.4 proves characteristic independence for arbitrary monomial
quotients of positive affine semigroup rings with at most four generators.
With five generators, it covers unquotiented semigroup rings and monomial
quotients of polynomial rings. The tangent cone is a particular monomial
quotient of the length-homogenized semigroup ring, as explained in THEOREM.md;
it need not satisfy either of those five-generator hypotheses.

The additional step here excludes the projective plane using the maximum-length
filtration. We have not found this tangent-cone conclusion in the primary
sources checked; this is not a publication-priority claim. The universal result
depends on the written chain identification, support argument, and cited
topological theorem, not on a finite experiment or a proof assistant.

## 5. Reproducible checks

Run `python3 five_generator_check.py` for exact supplementary checks. The code
checks the projective-plane deletion/link description at all six vertices and
all 32 possible threshold partitions at each vertex. As a positive control,
the corresponding relative boundary has determinant of absolute value two:
without the factorization restriction it really produces different homology
over $\mathbb Q$ and $\mathbb F_2$.

The numerical regression corpus contains every minimally generated numerical
semigroup with five generators in `{5,...,15}` (109 semigroups), including two
whose tangent cones are not Cohen–Macaulay. All fine Betti entries are compared
in characteristics 0, 2, and 3. Use

```sh
python3 five_generator_check.py --singular /path/to/Singular
```

to additionally reconstruct all 327 fine tables by the independent elimination,
local-standard-basis, tangent-cone, and minimal-resolution route in
`singular_check.py`. These computations validate the implementations and catch
characteristic-sensitive topology; they do not replace the universal proof.
