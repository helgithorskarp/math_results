# The two-color generalized weak Schur number with eight summands

**Claim:** \(WS_8(2)=365\), in the least-forcing-integer convention.
This is a computer-assisted result with an arithmetic reduction and a separately
checked propositional proof. It is not a proof-assistant formalization.

Write \([N]=\{1,\ldots,N\}\). A coloring of \([N]\) is *avoiding* if neither
color contains eight distinct integers whose sum also has that color in \([N]\).
All colorings are allowed; no class size, interval structure, seed, or color
normalization is imposed in the upper-bound computation.

## 1. Published lower bound

Ahmed, Boza, Revuelta and Sanz [1, Lemma 2.1 and Table 4] give
\(WS_8(2)\ge365\). Their construction at this parameter is

\[
A=\{1\}\cup[44,329],\qquad B=[2,43]\cup[330,364].
\]

Here is a direct verification. The least sum of eight distinct members of
\(A\) is \(1+44+45+46+47+48+49+50=330\), greater than every member of \(A\).
For eight distinct members of \(B\), a summand at least 330 makes the sum at
least \(330+2+3+4+5+6+7+8=365\). Otherwise all summands lie in \([2,43]\),
and their sum lies between \(2+\cdots+9=44\) and \(36+\cdots+43=316\),
outside \(B\). Thus this is an avoiding coloring of \([364]\).

The lower bound and construction are prior work. The new claim here is the
matching global upper bound.

## 2. A complete finite encoding

The following reduction is stated for \(k\ge2\) distinct summands. Let
\(T_j=j(j+1)/2\), and choose an integer prefix length \(1\le R\le N\).
Use color variables \(X_t\) for \(t\in[N]\); \(X_t\) is true for color 1.
Let \(L_{c,t}\) mean \(X_t\) for \(c=1\), and \(\neg X_t\) for \(c=0\).

For each color \(c\), each \(1\le i\le R\), and each
\(1\le j\le\min(i,k)\), introduce \(D_{c,i,j,s}\) for

\[
T_j\le s\le\min\{N,j(2i-j+1)/2\}.
\]

Its intended meaning is that some \(j\)-element subset of the integers of
color \(c\) in \([i]\) sums to \(s\). Not every integer in the allocated
range must be attainable. Also regard \(D_{c,i,0,0}\) as the constant true;
all other out-of-range states are absent. The constant is never a CNF variable.

Include only the following implications, whenever their states exist:

1. **Carry:** \(D_{c,i-1,j,s}\Rightarrow D_{c,i,j,s}\).
2. **Add a new distinct element:**
   \(D_{c,i-1,j-1,s-i}\land L_{c,i}\Rightarrow D_{c,i,j,s}\).
   For \(j=1,s=i\), this is \(L_{c,i}\Rightarrow D_{c,i,1,i}\).
3. **All summands in the prefix:**
   \(D_{c,R,k,s}\Rightarrow\neg L_{c,s}\).
4. **One summand beyond the prefix:** for every \(R<x\le N-s\),
   \(D_{c,R,k-1,s}\Rightarrow
   (\neg L_{c,x}\lor\neg L_{c,x+s})\).

Each implication is converted directly to one clause. No reverse subset-sum
implications are needed.

**Soundness lemma.** Every avoiding coloring of \([N]\) extends to a
satisfying assignment of these clauses.

*Proof.* Assign every auxiliary variable its intended subset-sum truth value.
Carry is valid by inclusion of prefixes. In the extension implication, the
previous subset lies in \([i-1]\), so appending \(i\) preserves distinctness.
For a clause of type 3, a true antecedent supplies \(k\) distinct summands of
color \(c\) and sum \(s\); avoidance forces the opposite color at \(s\).
For type 4, the \(k-1\) prefix elements are distinct from \(x>R\), and their
sum with \(x\) is \(x+s\le N\). They cannot all have color \(c\).
Positivity and \(k\ge2\) ensure the sum is larger than every summand, so it
is also distinct from them. These are all the input clauses. \(\square\)

Soundness alone suffices to turn an UNSAT certificate into an unrestricted
upper bound, for any prefix length. The chosen length also gives completeness.

**Coverage lemma.** If

\[
R\ge\left\lfloor\frac{N-1-T_{k-2}}2\right\rfloor,
\]

then a color assignment extends to a model of the clauses if and only if it
is avoiding.

*Proof.* For a configuration \(x_1<\cdots<x_k\) with sum at most \(N\),
put \(a=x_{k-1}\). The first \(k-2\) summands sum to at least \(T_{k-2}\),
and \(x_k\ge a+1\). Therefore
\(N\ge T_{k-2}+2a+1\), so \(a\le R\). At most one summand is outside the
prefix. In any satisfying assignment, induction on the prefix using carry
and extension forces \(D\) true for each actually reachable subset sum.
Consequently a monochromatic configuration violates type 3 if all its
summands are in the prefix, and type 4 otherwise. The reverse direction is
the soundness lemma. \(\square\)

At \((N,k)=(365,8)\), the coverage threshold is
\(\lfloor(365-1-21)/2\rfloor=171\).

## 3. Certified contradiction at 365

The deterministic instance generated by `encode.py` at \((N,k,R)=(365,8,171)\)
has **629,905 variables and 1,059,130 clauses**. Its SHA-256 is

```
46811b4e83e608efd96d264ed7e6763b598bcd49c63c1ac955ed848e92bfe388
```

Glucose 3, through Python-SAT 1.9.dev15, returns UNSAT and an ASCII proof.
The external `drat-trim` checker is run with `-U`, which allows only
reverse-unit-propagation (RUP) additions. Each retained learned clause must
follow by unit propagation after negating it, using the input and earlier
clauses; accepted deletions only discard premises. Verification of the final
empty clause establishes propositional inconsistency. Exact proof and checker
metadata are in `expected.json`; the checker transcript is in
`evidence/check.txt`.

`audit.py` independently labels the auxiliary variables and classifies every
input clause by the five cases above (the singleton case is counted separately).
It imports no generator code. Every accepted clause is sound under the intended
subset-sum interpretation. Missing or duplicate valid clauses cannot invalidate
the UNSAT implication, so this soundness audit does not need to assert that
every possible clause was generated. The count by type is:

| Type | Clauses |
|---|---:|
| Singleton | 342 |
| Carry | 624,360 |
| Add a distinct element | 406,046 |
| Forbid all-prefix sum | 660 |
| Forbid one-tail sum | 27,722 |
| Total | 1,059,130 |

By the soundness lemma and the checked contradiction, no avoiding coloring of
\([365]\) exists. Together with Section 1, this proves \(WS_8(2)=365\).

## 4. Validation and scope of trust

`validate.py` compares the encoding under every complete coloring with direct
enumeration of distinct-summand equations in three small instances: 256
colorings for \((k,N,R)=(2,8,3)\), and 4,096 each for \((3,12,5)\) and
\((4,12,5)\). All 8,448 comparisons agree. It separately checks the published
\([364]\) coloring by exact integer-set subset sums, assigns the auxiliary
variables their canonical meanings, and verifies all 1,056,524 clauses of its
corresponding encoding. These are validation controls, not replacements for
the proof at 365.

The trust boundary consists of the written arithmetic reduction, the CNF
generator and semantic auditor, the proof checker, and the ordinary software
and hardware execution environment. The SAT solver's UNSAT answer is not
trusted without its checked proof. This package is not independently reviewed
by another researcher and is not formalized in a proof assistant.

The large generated CNF and proof remain outside Git. The package provides
their exact hashes and commands to regenerate and check them. Hash agreement
is provenance, not a substitute for proof verification; a different valid
proof is acceptable if checked against the same audited input.

## 5. Literature and novelty scope

The 2023 primary source [1] proves the preceding exact values
\(WS_6(2)=166\) and \(WS_7(2)=253\), but gives only the lower bound 365 at
eight summands. Its companion [2] does not settle the diagonal eight-summand
case. The August 2026 primary preprint [3] still identifies the 2023 weak
lower-bound family as best known; its eventual fixed-distinctness theorem
does not apply when the number of summands and distinct summands both equal
eight. Focused searches and the authorized repository/committed graph audit
on 2026-09-11 found no duplicate resolution. Thus this upper bound is new to
the sources searched; this statement does not assert priority over unpublished
or unindexed work. Eight denotes summands, not colors.
