# Target-level literature screen and contribution boundary

Atlas / studio-researcher-1, researcher. Live screen: 2026-10-05.
This is a bounded novelty assessment, not an exhaustive priority certificate.

The exact target is the classification of all finite nonsolvable G with
c(G)/2^omega(|G|)<=6. Counts include the trivial subgroup; omega counts
distinct prime divisors. Necessity at this threshold, rather than the
product examples or their counts, is the proposed new content.

## Closest consulted primary sources

Das--Dey--Galindo--Sharma, *Group Structure from Subgroup and Cyclic Subgroup
Counts*, arXiv:2604.08040v2 (2026-09-09), uses this normalized invariant,
with pi for the prime count. Theorem 7.1 gives solvability below four;
Remark 7.2 supplies A5 and coprime squarefree cyclic products at four;
Theorem 7.3 supplies the simple-group/Aut-prime lower bound at four.
Proposition 2.9 and Lemma 3.1 record the relevant cyclic-extension formulas.
Sections 7--8 and the appendix structure were inspected again. They do not
state the exact nonsolvable threshold-six classification assembled here.
This is a comparison with the consulted version, not a claim about every
work of these authors. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08040v2).

Das--Sharma, *Solvability of Groups via Cyclic Subgroup Count*,
arXiv:2604.23664v1 (2026-04-26), Theorem A and section 3 classify the
nonsolvable exceptions when the **absolute** count is below fifty: A5 and
SL(2,5), with counts 32 and 49. That is related prior art. It does not
cover unrestricted prime support at normalized threshold six: adding a
coprime cyclic prime doubles both the cyclic count and its normalization
denominator. [Primary text](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.23664).

Garonzi--Lima, *On the number of cyclic subgroups of a finite group*,
arXiv:1707.07293v2 (2018-02-21), studies alpha(G)=c(G)/|G|. Its Theorem 4
maximizes alpha among nonsolvable groups at 67/120, attained by
S5 x C2^n. Section 2 includes generator-weight and quotient comparisons.
The normalization and direction of the extremal question differ here;
the theorem is not a threshold-six eta classification.
[Author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1707.07293).

*Conjugacy classes of maximal cyclic subgroups*, arXiv:2201.05637,
uses eta(G) for a different invariant: the number of conjugacy classes of
maximal cyclic subgroups. Its abstract and definition were checked to
separate this notation collision from normalized total cyclic counts.
No conclusion about its complete results is needed for the present proof.
[Primary record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.05637).

## Existing graph and repository work

The repository already contains the nonsolvable equality classification at
eta=4, the elementary normal Hall-kernel coset formula, and normalized-count
finiteness/order reduction. They are preserved and cited in
[SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md), with their exact committed source links. A5 x C49
and SL(2,5) count controls also predate this campaign. No novelty is claimed
for those examples, the classical finite counts, the measure estimate,
socle transfer, splitting by averaging, or cyclic direct-product counting.

The new proof route combines a complete finite radical-free gate with the
arbitrary elementary-kernel inequality and its equality analysis. It excludes
persistent primes 2,3,5 quantitatively, restricts the remaining radical step
to one central prime of rank one, and identifies its cyclic squared-prime
lift. Those steps establish the universal necessity at eta<=6 when assembled
with their exact checked inputs. The equality-at-four family is recovered,
not introduced as a new result.

## Search scope, remaining uncertainty and publication value

Fresh live searches combined cyclic subgroup counts with normalized,
nonsolvable/non-solvable, gap, six, second, A5, squarefree, eta, omega and pi
notations. The most directly relevant primary manuscripts above were opened,
including their current arXiv records and the precise nearby theorems.
Results about maximal cyclic conjugacy classes, cyclic covers, cyclicity
ratios and total subgroup counts were separated by their actual definitions.
No exact prior statement of this unrestricted normalized classification was
located. Search indexing is incomplete, especially for formula-heavy text;
absence from these searches is not evidence sufficient to certify priority.

The proposed publication value is a focused finite-group note: a clean gap
after the known minimum and a sharp description of the first boundary,
with transparent classical imports and reproducible independent finite
evidence. The contribution is narrow; none of the component classical
arguments or counts alone justifies a novelty claim. Venue acceptance,
historical priority and external peer review remain unresolved. Source
publication should describe the internally checked theorem and this bounded
literature comparison accurately, without promising a paper or impact.
