## Complete independent two-anchor reduction

Let \(Q\) contain twenty distinct four-subsets of seventeen points, with
every pair occurring at most once. For the unit profile, let \(H\) be the
five replication-four points and \(W\) the twelve replication-five points.
Leave degree is \(16-3\rho\), hence four on \(H\) and one on \(W\).
If \(e,m\) count leave edges within \(H,W\), then
\[
20-2e=12-2m,\qquad e-m=4.
\]
The imported upper-six bound and this lower-four bound leave only five and
six to exclude. If \(e=5\), then \(m=1\); if \(e=6\), then \(m=2\).
Low-low leave edges form a matching because every low vertex has degree one.

Choose one such edge \(vw\). The five blocks through each endpoint partition
the same remaining fifteen points into five triples. The two ten-block stars
are disjoint since \(vw\) is uncovered. A triple from one partition meets a
triple from the other in at most one point, or their common pair would repeat.
The binary \(5\times5\) intersection matrix has degree three in every row
and column; its bipartite complement has degree two. Components are simple
alternating cycles, with half-length at least two and total half-length five.
Thus there are exactly two forms: \(C_{10}\), or \(C_6\sqcup C_4\).
This normalizes labels; it imposes no automorphism on the unknown packing.

As a separate completeness check, the audit enumerates all **2,040** binary
degree-three matrices and compares their complete disjoint row/column orbits
with these two forms, of sizes **1,440** and **600**.

Label the occupied cells lexicographically by \(0,\ldots,14\), and \(v,w\)
by 15 and 16. The anchor blocks are 15 plus each row triple, and 16 plus each
column triple. They cover sixty distinct pairs, of which thirty are among
cells. Exactly 75 cell pairs remain eligible. Every cell occurs twice in
the anchors; both anchors are low. Hence all five high points are cells,
and the ten remaining blocks have residual quotas two on high cells and
three on the other ten cells. Their total quota is forty.

Every residual quadruple is a four-matching of the occupied-cell graph:
repeating a row or column repeats an anchor pair. Conversely every such
quadruple is a legal single residual block. There are **95** and **96**
candidates. No other residual block is possible because both anchors already
have all five occurrences.

For \(e=5\), include every one of the \(\binom{15}{5}=3,003\) high subsets
and require every eligible low-low cell pair to be covered. For \(e=6\),
also include **every** eligible pair between low cells as the second low-low
leave edge. Forbid its occurrence and require every other eligible low-low
pair. There are **96,525** such raw high/second-edge assignments per model.
The selected second edge avoids both anchors automatically. No disjointness
or shape restriction beyond the required matching is omitted.

If the anchors already cover \(R\) high-high pairs, the residual high-pair
budget is \(10-e-R=5-R\) or \(4-R\). Negative budgets are direct
obstructions. Otherwise retain all candidates avoiding the selected forbidden
edge and costing at most the initial budget. During search the budget is
decreased exactly. These filters are necessary, not heuristic exclusions.

A positive quota solution supplies exactly ten distinct residual blocks,
repeats no pair, and covers precisely the mandatory low-low pairs. Restoring
the anchors gives the original unit profile. Leave degree one on low points
forces exactly the prescribed one or two low-low leave edges, so the degree
identity forces \(e=5\) or six. Conversely every forbidden packing gives
one of these quota solutions. This proves both directions of the finite
reduction; a negative verdict is meaningful only after complete coverage.

## Independent audit of the global bridge

In any twenty-quadruple pair packing, \(\rho_x\le5\), since blocks through
one point cover disjoint triples of sixteen neighbors. Set \(t_x=5-\rho_x\).
Then \(\sum t_x=85-80=5\). If \(H\) is the positive-deficit set of size
\(h\), and \(W\) its complement, then \(1\le h\le5\). Leave degree is
\(1+3t_x\). Degree sums on \(H,W\) are \(15+h,17-h\), respectively, so
\[
e-m=h-1,\qquad e+m=2e-(h-1).
\]
Here \(e+m\) counts homogeneous leave pairs, with both endpoints deficient
or neither. For \(h=1,2,3\), \(e\le\binom h2\) gives bounds 0, 1, 4.
For \(h=4\), the positive deficits are \((2,1,1,1)\), and the imported
generic mixed theorem gives \(e=3,m=0\), hence three. For \(h=5\), all
deficits are one; the independently proved unit theorem gives four. These
cases cover all seven positive partitions of five, including absent points
and deficits larger than one. The local homogeneous count is therefore at
most four with no ambient-code hypothesis.

Suppose an eighteen-point weight-five family has 72 words. Shortening through
one point produces a quadruple pair packing, so Brouwer's point cap gives
\(r_x\le20\). Since \(\sum r_x=5\cdot72=360\), all eighteen replications
are exactly twenty. For a pair \(xy\), disjoint three-point tails give pair
replication \(d_{xy}\le\lfloor16/3\rfloor=5\). Let the simple graph \(D\)
join pairs for which \(5-d_{xy}>0\). In the shortened star at \(x\), deficient
neighbors are exactly the neighbors of \(x\) in \(D\).

No triple is in two words, or two words would intersect in at least three
points. There are exactly
\[
\binom{18}{3}-72\binom53=816-720=96
\]
uncovered triples. The leave pairs in the star at \(x\) correspond bijectively
to uncovered triples through \(x\). A homogeneous incidence has \(x\)
adjacent in \(D\) to both other vertices or neither. Every graph on three
vertices has a vertex of degree zero or two: otherwise its degree sum would
be three. Thus the total homogeneous incidence count \(J\) satisfies
\[
96\le J\le18\cdot4=72,
\]
a contradiction. Every larger family contains a 72-word subfamily, so all
families have size at most 71. This last step avoids an unjustified assumption
that equality of replications holds above 72. The exact eight-graph check and
seven-partition enumeration in `bridge.py` support the ordinary written proof.

\[
106-4(18-k)=34+4k.
\]

nineteen-block stars contribute at least 54, so at least one has **eleven**
