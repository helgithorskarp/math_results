# Split one-involution degree-seven obstruction for exact 7-step domination

## Exact computer-assisted theorem

Let

\[
\Gamma=\operatorname{Cay}(A,X),\qquad
X=\{\pm g_1,\pm g_2,\pm g_3,u\},
\]

where `A` is a finite abelian group, `u` is a nonzero involution, the
displayed connection set is simple of cardinality seven, and

\[
u\notin B:=\langle g_1,g_2,g_3\rangle.
\]

Then `Gamma` has no exact 7-step dominating set of cardinality four or six.
Equivalently, this closes the split/direct-factor half of the sole remaining
one-involution orbit type in the abelian degree-seven problem.  It is not
restricted to rectangular tori: `B` and its marked triple are arbitrary.

Together with the existing complete degree-at-most-six, three-involution,
and five-involution obstructions, this leaves only the nonsplit
one-involution case `u in <g_1,g_2,g_3>` among finite abelian Cayley graphs
of degree at most seven.  Higher degree, nonabelian Cayley graphs,
non-Cayley graphs, and Hersh's value `m(7)` remain open.

## Split reduction and finite bound

First, no connectedness convention is needed.  In each connected component
`C`, the centers lying in `C` form an exact 7-step dominating set for `C`.
Hersh's general lower bound gives at least
`floor(log_2(7))+2=4` centers in every component.  Thus an exact set with only
four or six centers can meet only one component, while it must meet every
component; consequently the graph is connected.  Hence `A` is generated by
`B` and `u`.  Since `u` is not in `B`,

\[
A=B\oplus\langle u\rangle\cong B\oplus C_2.
\]

Let `Sigma_r(B)` be the radius-`r` sphere in
`Cay(B,{+-g_1,+-g_2,+-g_3})`.  A geodesic in the direct product uses the
`u`-edge zero or one times, so the radius-seven sphere is the disjoint union

\[
T=\Sigma_7(B)\times\{0\}\;\sqcup\;
  \Sigma_6(B)\times\{1\}.
\]

The three-dimensional Lee shells at radii seven and six have respectively

\[
4\cdot7^2+2=198,\qquad 4\cdot6^2+2=146
\]

coefficient vectors.  Consequently `|T|<=344`.  If an exact dominating set
has `k` centers, its translates of `T` partition `A`; hence

\[
|A|=k|T|\le6\cdot344=2064,
\qquad |B|\le1032,
\]

and `|B|` is divisible by two or three.

## Complete quotient enumeration

Every marked `B` is the quotient of `Z^3` by a unique column-Hermite lattice

\[
H=\begin{pmatrix}a&x&y\\0&b&z\\0&0&c\end{pmatrix},
\quad a,b,c>0,
\quad 0\le x,y<a,
\quad 0\le z<b.
\]

The number of index-`N` HNFs is

\[
h_3(N)=\sum_{abc=N}a^2b.
\]

Thus the exact eligible marked-HNF count is

```text
eligible_hnfs=590166412
cyclic_quotient_hnfs=506561900
noncyclic_quotient_hnfs=83604512
```

The cyclic count is independently obtained from
`J_3(N)/phi(N)`, the number of epimorphisms from `Z^3` to `Z/NZ` modulo
postcomposition by units.

### Cyclic quotients

For `B=Z/NZ`, a marked connection set is an unordered triple of distinct
non-involutory inverse pairs `{+-a,+-b,+-c}` with
`gcd(N,a,b,c)=1`.  Multiplication by a unit preserves the Cayley graph and
tiling question.  The cyclic enumerator losslessly chooses a generator whose
gcd with `N` is minimal, maps that inverse pair to the positive divisor, and
marks the entire normalized unit orbit before evaluating it.

### Noncyclic quotients

The HNF enumerator rejects cyclic quotients by the gcd of the two-by-two
minors and rejects nonsimple connection sets.  It then applies the same
lossless signed-permutation filter used in the verified complete degree-six
scan.  The signature records the element orders of the three generators,
their six signed pair sums, and four triple sums modulo global negation.  An
HNF is retained when its identity signature is lexicographically minimal
among all signed coordinate permutations; ties are deliberately retained.
Every orbit has a transform attaining the minimum, whose unique HNF appears
in the scan, so this cannot remove the only representative of a tiling.
The implementation evaluates these orders from the exact identity
`ord(v)=det(H)/gcd(det(H),adj(H)v)`.

The six disjoint order ranges returned in aggregate

```text

signature_representatives=11005856
four_candidates=683
six_candidates=29862
```

For every retained model the producer maps all Lee coefficient vectors
through radii six and seven, forms the mixed sphere `T`, and selects exactly
the models satisfying `k|T|=2|B|` for `k=4` or `6`.  It tests them by a
difference-compatibility clique search: after translating one center to
zero, all center differences must avoid `T-T`.  Pairwise disjointness plus
the counting equality is equivalent to coverage.

## Independent validation

The cyclic checker counts the complete universe of generating unit orbits
independently by Burnside's lemma.  For each emitted candidate it
canonicalizes the unit orbit, constructs the full `B x C2` graph, recomputes
all distances by BFS, and replaces the producer's clique test by direct
first-uncovered translate exact cover.

The noncyclic C++ checker independently derives all three HNF counts above.
For every emitted noncyclic candidate it checks the HNF and connection-set
conditions, reconstructs the full graph and radius-seven sphere by BFS,
checks the partition count, and performs first-uncovered exact cover.

A standard-library Python implementation supplies a cross-language check of
the same BFS and memoized first-uncovered exact-cover formulation using
arbitrary-precision bitsets.  In this run its first 6,000 yielded descriptors
agreed before the slower cross-language replay was stopped; it is included
for further replay, not used as the complete-theorem evidence.

All executables, candidate files, and run output belong under `/scratch`.
From this directory, compile with

```bash

  -Wall -Wextra -Wpedantic \
  enumerate_split_cyclic_one_involution.cpp \
  -o /scratch/enumerate_split_cyclic_one_involution

g++ -std=c++20 -O3 -DNDEBUG -march=native \
  -Wall -Wextra -Wpedantic \

g++ -std=c++20 -O3 -DNDEBUG -march=native \
  -Wall -Wextra -Wpedantic \
  check_split_cyclic_one_involution_candidates.cpp \
```

## Status, trust boundary, and literature scope

This is an exact computer-assisted theorem.  The mathematical trust boundary
contains the translate-partition reduction, the direct-product sphere
identity, the Lee-shell bound, HNF completeness, the cyclic unit-orbit
normalization, and losslessness of the noncyclic signature filter.  The
computational boundary contains complete candidate selection in the two
producers, the independent Burnside/HNF counts, all four exact
implementations, and their compiler/runtime.  The checkers independently
validate every emitted counting candidate and tiling verdict; they do not
independently rescan the mixed-sphere size of every rejected model.

Targeted exact-phrase, author, and concept searches through 2026-09-03 found
the foundational papers below, but no published obstruction for this marked
split abelian degree-seven class.  The result is therefore apparently new to
the searched sources, not a historical-priority claim.

- P. Hersh, *On exact n-step domination*, Discrete Mathematics 205 (1999),
  235--239, <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0012-365X(99)00024-2>.
- L. K. Williams, *On Exact n-Step Domination*, Ars Combinatoria 58 (2001),
  13--22,
  <https://combinatorialpress.com/ars-articles/volume-058-ars-articles/on-exact-n-step-domination/>.
