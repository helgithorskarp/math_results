# Gaussian majorisation and both ball-volume inequalities for orthocentric flaps

Complete author proof; independent mathematical review and formalization
are pending. This proves a finite-depth, all-weight, all-variance class in
dimension three. The unrestricted Gaussian-majorisation conjecture remains
open. The full sixteen-label map need not have a five-dimensional motion:
the proof constructs motions for its tournament selectors and then uses
their common target.

## 1. Statement and the prior reduction

Let v_0,...,v_3 in R3 be affinely independent and satisfy

    v_i . v_j = -c < 0  (i != j),       h_i = |v_i|^2+c.       (1)

The orthocenter is the origin and lies inside the tetrahedron. Our family
is exactly (1); it does not include all tetrahedra. Its four anchor labels
have x_i=y_i=v_i, and its twelve directed flap labels have

    x_ij=v_j-v_i,              y_ij=v_j+v_i  (i != j).        (2)

The depth is exactly one in these coordinates. Translations, orthogonal
changes of coordinates and common positive dilations preserve the conclusions.

**Theorem.** For every tetrahedron (1):

1. Every tournament selector of (2), consisting of the anchors and one
   directed flap from each of the six opposite pairs, admits a real-analytic
   contracting motion in R5 with endpoints in the original R3.
2. For arbitrary nonnegative weights of total one on all sixteen source
   labels, let mu be the source law and nu its image under (2). For every
   s>0 and every a>=0,

       H_(mu*gamma_s)(a) <= H_(nu*gamma_s)(a),
       H_f(a)=integral_R3 (f-a)_+,  gamma_s=N(0,s I_3).       (3)

   Thus this entire family satisfies full Gaussian majorisation, including
   zero weights, with no small-depth, variance or weight restriction.
3. Give each of the sixteen labels any radius r_b>=0. Then

       |union_b B(y_b,r_b)| <= |union_b B(x_b,r_b)|,
       |intersection_b B(y_b,r_b)| >= |intersection_b B(x_b,r_b)|.  (4)

   The radii need not agree, including on the opposite flaps with coincident
   target centers.

The classical regular-simplex flap construction is prior work of
Belk--Connelly and Cheng--Tan--Zheng. The fixed-target selector decomposition,
the safe sink selectors, and the two remaining ten-point templates are
[researcher 7's prior reduction](../gaussian_flap_tournament_reduction/PROOF.md).
We use it with attribution, and recall the necessary steps below.
The new step is the contracting motion for **every sinkless selector**.
It closes both residual template families for all the parameters above.
The general Gaussian and ball-volume transfer theorems are also prior work;
see [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md).

The Gram matrix of (1) is diag(h_i)-c 11^T, of rank three. Its kernel
is generated by the positive vector (1/h_i). In particular,

    sum_i c/h_i=1,           sum_i v_i/h_i=0,               (5)

and every three v_i are linearly independent. For example, any linear
dependence on three vertices would extend to a kernel vector with a zero
entry, which (5) excludes. The sixteen source sites are distinct: equality
between distinct differences would be a nontrivial affine relation;
anchor-difference equality would be a nontrivial linear relation on at
most three vertices. These observations make (2) a well-defined map.

The identity

    v_i . (v_k-v_j)=h_i(1_(k=i)-1_(j=i))                  (6)

gives squared-distance losses

    |x_ij-v_k|^2-|y_ij-v_k|^2 =4h_i 1_(k=i),
    |x_ij-x_kl|^2-|y_ij-y_kl|^2
                           =4(h_i 1_(l=i)+h_k 1_(j=k)).  (7)

Thus (2) is 1-Lipschitz on its source set; it has a global 1-Lipschitz
extension by Kirszbraun's theorem if desired. The conclusions concern
measures supported on the displayed finite set.

## 2. The free pair in a sinkless selector

A tournament on four vertices has total outdegree six. If it has no sink,
all four outdegrees are positive, so at least one vertex i has outdegree
exactly one. Write i->k for its sole outgoing edge. Let r,l be the other
two indices. The chosen flap with tail i is therefore precisely i->k.
Every chosen flap with tail k is k->b with b!=i, since the reverse edge
k->i is absent.

We will move the anchors by keeping them fixed and the selected flaps by

    z_ab(u)=(v_b,0)+R_a(u) in R3 direct-sum R2.             (8)

We preserve |R_a|=|v_a| and all inner products R_a.R_b=-c except the
one for tails i,k. This exceptional entry is allowed to increase temporarily.
Crucially, every selected distance involving that entry has the contraction
reserve from i->k. There is no selected tight distance that would force
the exceptional entry to remain constant.

The following sections give a quantitative motion, not just freedom of a
Gram matrix: the physical components are monotone multiples of their v_a,
the auxiliary coordinates lie in one fixed R2, and all distances are
checked throughout the motion.

## 3. Geometry supplying the two opposite time shifts

Let P=span(v_r,v_l), let p be the orthogonal projection of v_i onto P,
and set L=|p|^2. Because v_i and v_k both have inner product -c with each
of v_r,v_l, their projections onto P coincide. Their normal components
are nonzero, by independence of every triple. They have opposite signs,
by the positive dependence (5). Choose the unit normal n so that

    v_i=p+A n,       v_k=p-B n,       A,B>0.                (9)

Also L>0, since p.v_r=-c. Taking the inner product in (9) gives

    AB=L+c,      h_i=A(A+B),      h_k=B(A+B).              (10)

Fix an isometry J:P->R2. Define

    U=asinh(A/sqrt(L)),    V=asinh(B/sqrt(L)),    d=U+V>0,
    u_i=U,   u_k=-V,   u_r=u_l=0,
    E_i=cosh(U) Jp,        E_k=cosh(V) Jp,
    E_r=Jv_r,             E_l=Jv_l.                       (11)

Then |E_a|=|v_a| for each a. Apart from {i,k}, all pairs satisfy

    E_a.E_b=-c cosh(u_a-u_b).                             (12)

For example E_i.E_r=-c cosh U; the other cases are identical or immediate.
For the exceptional pair, write

    delta=E_i.E_k+c cosh(d).

In fact

    delta=AB(cosh(d)-1)>0.                               (13)

To verify this without an inequality estimate, put
Q=sqrt((L+A^2)(L+B^2)). Then E_i.E_k=Q and
cosh(d)=(Q+AB)/L. Substituting c=AB-L yields
Q+c(Q+AB)/L=AB((Q+AB)/L-1), which is (13).

## 4. Explicit motion and its distance signs

First use an unbounded real parameter w. Set

    lambda_a(w)=tanh(w+u_a),
    R_a(w)=(lambda_a(w) v_a, sech(w+u_a) E_a).             (14)

As w increases from -infinity to +infinity, R_a goes from (-v_a,0)
to (v_a,0), so (8) has exactly the required endpoints. Each norm is
constant and each lambda_a increases strictly. The hyperbolic identity

    cosh(x-y)+sinh(x)sinh(y)=cosh(x)cosh(y)

and (12)-(13) show that

    R_a.R_b=-c                                      if {a,b}!={i,k},
    R_i.R_k=-c+delta sech(w+U)sech(w-V).                    (15)

All expressions below called constant are independent of w. Formula (6)
gives

    |z_ab-(v_m,0)|^2=constant-2h_a lambda_a 1_(m=a).        (16)

For two flaps with distinct tails a,e,

    |z_ab-z_ef|^2=constant
                  -2[R_a.R_e+h_a lambda_a 1_(f=a)
                                  +h_e lambda_e 1_(b=e)]. (17)

If the tails agree, the R terms cancel and the distance is constant.
Thus every distance is already nonincreasing except possibly those
whose tail set is {i,k}. For such a pair the flaps are i->k and k->b,
b!=i. Its squared distance is

    constant-2[delta sech(w+U)sech(w-V)+h_k tanh(w-V)].    (18)

To sign (18), use t=tanh(w-V), q=tanh(d), so 0<q<1 and

    F(t)=sech(w+U)sech(w-V)
        =sqrt(1-q^2)(1-t^2)/(1+q t),       -1<t<1.

Direct differentiation gives

    F''(t)=-2(1-q^2)^(3/2)/(1+q t)^3<0,
    F'(1)=-2e^(-d).

Consequently the derivative of the bracket in (18), with respect to t,
is at least

    h_k-2delta e^(-d)
      =h_k-AB(1-e^(-d))^2
      =B^2+AB e^(-d)(2-e^(-d))
      >B^2>0.                                            (19)

Here (13) proves the first equality and 0<e^(-d)<1 proves the last
inequality. This strict margin holds for every geometry (1); no symmetry,
limiting variance, numerical enclosure or sufficiently small parameter
is used. It proves that every exceptional distance contracts as well.

For completeness, (14) gives analytic paths on a compact time interval,
not only asymptotic endpoints. Put tau=-cos(pi u), 0<=u<=1, and
theta_a=tanh(u_a). The equivalent formula is

    R_a(u)=((tau+theta_a)/(1+theta_a tau) v_a,
            sin(pi u) sech(u_a)/(1+theta_a tau) E_a).      (20)

The denominators are strictly positive since |theta_a|<1. This extends
real-analytically to neighborhoods of both endpoints. In the open interval
tau=tanh(w), so it is an increasing reparametrization of (14). Continuity
preserves all distance inequalities at the endpoints. This proves the
motion for every sinkless selector.

An equivalent implementation avoids hyperbolic functions: theta_i=A/|v_i|,
theta_k=-B/|v_k|, theta_r=theta_l=0, and the auxiliary factors
sech(u_i)E_i and sech(u_k)E_k both equal Jp. These formulas are useful for
exact algebraic or numerical reproduction of the paths.

## 5. Sink selectors and completion of the motion theorem

The sink case is prior work, recalled from
[the tournament reduction, Section 4](../gaussian_flap_tournament_reduction/PROOF.md)
and [the simplicial-cone motion](../gaussian_simplicial_cone_reflections/PROOF.md).
If k is the sink, the three possible tails form a basis b_1,b_2,b_3.
Project b_1 onto span(b_2,b_3) by P_0, choose an isometry J_0 of that
plane into R2, and let eta=|b_1-P_0 b_1|/|b_1| in (0,1). For -1<=t<=1,
define the linear embedding F_t by

    F_t b_1=((t+eta)/(1+eta t) b_1,
             sqrt(1-t^2)/(1+eta t) J_0 P_0 b_1),
    F_t b_j=(t b_j, sqrt(1-t^2) J_0 b_j),       j=2,3.     (21)

It preserves the entire Gram matrix, by

    (t+eta)^2+(1-eta^2)(1-t^2)=(1+eta t)^2,
    t(t+eta)+(1-t^2)=1+eta t.

Its physical coefficients increase from -1 to 1. Set R_a=F_t v_a in
(8). Equations (16)-(17) then prove contraction, with no exceptional
Gram entry. Taking t=-cos(pi u) makes (21) analytic on [0,1]. Together
with Sections 2-4 this covers every tournament and proves item 1 of the theorem.

## 6. All Gaussian hinges by common-target mixtures

For a fixed selector, apply Aishwarya--Li, Theorem 1.4(i)(a), to its
continuous contraction in R5. This orders the endpoint density values
sampled from their own densities. At either endpoint the density factors
as f(x)gamma_s^(2)(z). If Z has law gamma_s^(2), then

    gamma_s^(2)(Z)/C_2 is uniform on (0,1), C_2=(2 pi s)^(-1).

Therefore, for X sampled from f independently of Z,

    Pr[f(X)gamma_s^(2)(Z)>a C_2]
                   =integral f(x)(1-a/f(x))_+ dx=H_f(a).

The density-value order proves (3) for every selector and arbitrary
weights on its ten labels. This is the established two-coordinate
Gaussian cancellation, not a general cancellation rule for majorisation.

For arbitrary weights alpha_i,beta_ij on the full family, put
q_ij=beta_ij+beta_ji for an unordered pair {i,j}. Independently choose
i->j with probability beta_ij/q_ij when q_ij>0; choose either direction
arbitrarily when q_ij=0. Put the whole mass q_ij at the chosen source.
All selector laws mu_sigma have the **same target** nu because
y_ij=y_ji. Their product probabilities w_sigma give

    mu=sum_sigma w_sigma mu_sigma,  sum_sigma w_sigma=1.

Convexity of (z-a)_+ and linearity of convolution yield

    H_(mu*gamma_s)(a)
       <=sum_sigma w_sigma H_(mu_sigma*gamma_s)(a)
       <=sum_sigma w_sigma H_(nu*gamma_s)(a)
        =H_(nu*gamma_s)(a).                               (22)

This proves item 2. The case a=0 is equality of masses. Equivalently,
every convex internal energy with U(0)=0 and well-defined finite integrals
is ordered in the same direction, by the standard hinge characterization.

## 7. Arbitrary-radius unions and intersections: a direct geometric proof

Bezdek--Connelly, Theorem 1, implies both ball-volume inequalities for
any selector because its endpoints in R3 have a piecewise-smooth
contraction in R5. Our paths are analytic, so their regularity hypothesis
is satisfied. Apply that theorem to the reversed path if using its
expansion convention.

For the union in the full family, select the flap with the **larger radius**
in each opposite pair, breaking ties arbitrarily, and retain all anchors.
The target union is unchanged: two concentric balls are replaced by the
larger one. The source selector union is contained in the full source
union. Consequently

    |full target union|=|selector target union|
        <=|selector source union|<=|full source union|.    (23)

For the intersection, instead select the flap with the **smaller radius**
in each pair. The target intersection is unchanged, while the full source
intersection is contained in the selector source intersection. Thus

    |full target intersection|=|selector target intersection|
        >=|selector source intersection|>=|full source intersection|. (24)

This proves item 3, including radius zero (or by continuity from
positive radii). The intersection conclusion is obtained from (24) and
the motion theorem, not inferred from Gaussian majorisation. Independently,
the union conclusion also follows from (3) and Aishwarya--Li, Theorem 5.1(i),
since the Gaussian statement holds for every law and every variance.

## 8. Controls, scope and remaining frontier

The strict distance margin (19) is an explicit certificate for the only
entry not fixed by the selector motion. The finite checker verifies the
formal distance identities for every label pair in every sinkless selector,
all relevant elementary polynomial identities, and rational asymmetric
projection fixtures. See [VERIFY.md](VERIFY.md) for commands and the
computational trust boundary. The universal analytic argument is the
written proof, not an inference from sampled paths or computed Gaussian
integrals. No external solver or numerical quadrature is used.

The same motion is invalid on all sixteen labels at once. For distinct
i,k,r, the two flaps i->r and k->r have a tight source/target distance.
Formula (17) would vary it by -2delta sech(w+U)sech(w-V): a positive hump
in the Gram entry returns to zero, so this squared distance decreases and
then increases. This is a useful rejection control. The selector mixture
in (22), and the two different radius selections in (23)-(24), are essential.

The theorem concerns the interior-orthocenter condition (1) and depth one.
Target collisions used in (22)-(24) generally disappear at other depths.
It gives no reduction of arbitrary contractions to this family and no
solution of the unrestricted dimension-three conjecture. It does close
the two template sign obligations in researcher 7's packet. The earlier
arbitrary-tetrahedron shallow-depth theorem remains a distinct result.
Priority beyond the cited literature is not asserted; the new motion and
the resulting full-family conclusions await independent review.
