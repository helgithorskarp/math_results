# Problem source and dependencies

The sole problem source is Aishwarya--Li,
[Gaussian Convolution, Internal Energies, and the Kneser--Poulsen Conjecture](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07041v2),
arXiv:2609.07041v2. The unresolved endpoint is full Gaussian majorisation
in R3 for bounded laws and their contractions. This contribution does not
settle that endpoint or establish a new ball-volume comparison.

The Gaussian replica product formula is classical and is used in that paper
and throughout the team source. It is not a new method here. Nor are the
integer cross-pair count, arithmetic-geometric mean, or diagonal dominance.

The existing [Hankel criterion](../gaussian_majorisation_hankel_transport/PROOF.md),
original `bafkreids462diitdof5ijilzcq2xqwftk2bzjr5t2gihnxk327uezbndbm`,
provides the normalization, the complete hierarchy and the rational
curvature-square witness interpretation. Our moment bounds and finite-matrix
proof are written out independently; the claim that every failure has such
a witness uses that existing criterion. This work does not introduce another
certificate reduction or reimplement its general finite-atomic oracle.

The [paired-rank result](../gaussian_majorisation_rank_abel/PROOF.md) explains
the use of a seven-site rank-six fixture. That fixture supplies only exact
input and scope controls. It is not asserted to be outside every full
positive class. The closed orthocentric flap assignment stays closed.

The [peak-pruning theorem](../gaussian_beta_peak_pruning/PROOF.md) signs a
block of beta entries from a density peak. Its minimum-mass limitation
leaves low-index entries when degree grows. The present result instead
signs complete Hankel matrices of increasing order as variance tends to
zero, under a verified nearest-pair loss condition. Neither result implies
full majorisation, and no optimal comparison of their constants is claimed.

The [replica-curvature theorem](../gaussian_replica_curvature_sparse_energies/PROOF.md)
controls sparse energies in a high-noise domain. The new exclusion uses
separation and small noise, covers full finite matrix orders, and does not
claim to improve that theorem's range or constants. It also does not pursue
the analytic lane's open universal quartic inequality or adjacent beta rows.

The [signed radial tail exclusion](../gaussian_signed_radial_tail_exclusion/PROOF.md)
is the counterexample lane's previous result. It signs spherical log-MGF
tests under independence. The present theorem concerns actual Gaussian
polynomial energies, allows dependent and arbitrary finite geometries, and
has different hypotheses. Positive spherical gaps are not a premise.

For broader context, nearest-distance control of high-SNR Gaussian-channel
asymptotics is classical; see Alvarado--Brannstrom--Agrell--Koch,
[High-SNR Asymptotics of Mutual Information for Discrete Constellations](https://arxiv.org/abs/1212.6526).
That source concerns conditional entropy, MMSE and related channel quantities;
it is not invoked as a theorem about contraction Hankel gaps. The contribution
here is the explicit combination of replica bounds with diagonal dominance
that excludes bounded-degree counterexamples on the stated uniform class.
Targeted literature and graph searches found no matching degree-barrier
statement. This is not a historical priority guarantee.

All public executable evidence is standard-library exact arithmetic. Private
floating searches and interval checks helped select the question but are
not premises, expected outputs, or omitted public certificates. No isolated
positive numerical example is promoted to a new full Gaussian class.
