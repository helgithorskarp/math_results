# Positioning of the preserved disjoint-cap theorem

26 September 2026. The proof, certificate, primary checker, separate
winding checker and expected records from source commit
`58ef0e6a38d607cf14f56692d2e80d227760715a` are unchanged. This is a
documentation consolidation, with no new cap-count theorem or computation.
Independent correctness and historical priority review remain pending.

The exact positive class is one simultaneous reflection of pairwise
disjoint caps of a compact convex body in R3. The finite auxiliary-vector
condition is discharged for any finite number of normals in a closed
hemisphere and for any four normals. Full Gaussian majorisation holds
for all laws on the body and all variances; both arbitrary-individual-radius
ball union and intersection inequalities follow from its R5 motion.
Convexity and disjointness are hypotheses on the caps in the body, not
merely on a finite list of occupied sites.

The new [axial rigid-cloud comparison](../gaussian_axial_cone_rotations/CAP_COMPARISON.md),
source commit `d21c9924bf70504b12e02c10f3621591fec04c9b`, gives a precise
boundary with the existing axial benchmark. For one disjoint-cap map,
an isometrically moved spanning cloud follows either one global reflection
or the identity. Two such cloud isometries therefore have linear parts
whose difference has rank at most two. The axial benchmark has two
spanning clouds with relative linear parts I and -I, whose difference has
rank three. It cannot be represented by one simultaneous disjoint-cap map,
even with arbitrary cap count and independent rigid endpoint frames.

That is researcher 6's complete author argument, not an independent review
of this packet. It prevents claiming that this cap theorem directly
subsumes the axial benchmark by a one-map representation. It does not
exclude compositions of disjoint-cap maps, establish reverse containment,
or settle historical priority of either geometric consequence.

The twelve-normal certificate here obstructs only the sufficient
normal-only auxiliary condition. It does not obstruct the endpoint map
or the Gaussian comparison; shallow caps with the same normals have an
explicit positive motion. The hemispherical theorem, four-normal theorem,
finite motion example and sufficient-condition obstruction retain their
separate scopes. No additional cap-count variants are part of this update.

The finite-dependency lane now also has a
[uniform scalar-envelope bound](../gaussian_uniform_defect_bound/PROOF.md)
on the unrestricted Gaussian defect. That separate result does not use
this cap theorem or expand its exact positive class. Neither result settles
the full dimension-three majorisation conjecture. Author-side source and
replay evidence must remain distinct from independent mathematical acceptance.
