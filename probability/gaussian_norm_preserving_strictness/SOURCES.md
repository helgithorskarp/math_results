## Present increment and consumer boundary

The new functional step restricts the positive kernel to a uniformly sized
angular cap and interpolation interval near the target, then integrates a
smoothed hinge through a radial crossing. This gives a coefficient linear
in the ordered loss, without a level-set regularity assumption. A separate
posterior variational calculation supplies quantitative peak strictness
for every bounded contraction. Together these show single-hinge rigidity
and put the whole nonisometric norm-preserving class into the old ambient
interior criterion without damping. No covariance-free entropy-rigidity
theorem is assumed.

The [previous fixed-variance buffered theorem](../gaussian_fixed_variance_neighborhoods/PROOF.md),
graph6532 `bafkreihd4v747dxfgboufugaqbr2qk6mbnagyox6rh7vgtndxc5nxis7ke`,
retains its explicit uniform neighborhood under full covariance, a strict
homothety and MGF/reference-order hypotheses. Here the starting anchored
class needs none of those auxiliary hypotheses, but its ambient radius is
law-dependent. We do not assert that either entire class contains the other.

R2's exact certification and R3's support-net/compact-frontier machinery
can consume the explicit middle minimum in PROOF Section 5. This is the
only additional handoff: it does not substitute for signed endpoints or
claim that arbitrary finite moments determine the sign. The norm-preserving
source already covered arbitrary atom counts and bounded laws.

R3's affine-component localization and R6's nonlinear parallel-slice work
are distinct domain-wide motion criteria. R5's scalar replica countermodel
still closes the scalar-only linear beta-wedge route. R7's proper screw
remains an unverified fixed-variance input for this theorem. Those are
landscape boundaries, not assumptions used in this proof.

Every new analytic claim here is an author proof awaiting independent
review. Exact finite checks support the formulas and input guard; they
are neither an exhaustive search nor a substitute for the analytic limits.
