## Whole-cone criterion

For two directions put

```text
a=alpha(u), b=alpha(v), c=u.v,
d=sqrt((1-a^2)(1-b^2)).
```

The squared-distance loss at radii `r,q>=0` is

```text
(1-a^2)r^2+(1-b^2)q^2-2c(1-ab)rq.
```

Copositivity of this binary quadratic is equivalent to
`c(1-ab)<=d`. If the cross coefficient is nonpositive this is automatic. If
it is positive, minimization in `r/q` gives necessity and sufficiency,
including the cases where a diagonal coefficient vanishes. Thus the target
does not replace the whole-cone condition by the weaker contraction of one
chosen radius on each ray.

The independent checker exercises 252 rational factor/cosine combinations.
For all 62 inadmissible combinations it constructs an explicit unequal-radius
pair with negative loss; the other 190 combinations feed complete motion
controls. This is finite corroboration of the analytic copositivity argument,
not its substitute.

## Fractional-linear raise and lowering

Write `tau=cos(theta)`. The first stage uses

```text
A_tau(a)=(a+tau)/(1+a tau),
B_theta(a)=sqrt(1-a^2)sin(theta)/(1+a tau).
```

The denominator is at least one and `A^2+B^2=1`, so every individual radius
is preserved. For a pair of unit directions the lifted inner product is

```text
k(tau)=[c(a+tau)(b+tau)+d(1-tau^2)]/
       [(1+a tau)(1+b tau)].
```

Clearing denominators after differentiation gives

```text
k'(tau) numerator =
 [(a+b)(1+tau^2)+2tau(1+ab)] [c(1-ab)-d].
```

All factors except the last bracket are nonnegative on the parameter range.
Since `tau` decreases as `theta` increases, `k` increases and every pairwise
distance decreases. The second stage merely scales the added coordinates to
zero; its squared distance is the target squared distance plus
`(1-t)^2(r sqrt(1-a^2)-q sqrt(1-b^2))^2`.

The independent checker represents numerator and denominator as univariate
coefficient lists, unlike the target sparse multivariate implementation. A
complete `3 x 3 x 2 x 2` interpolation grid certifies every coefficient of
the cleared derivative identity using degree bounds `(2,2,1,1)` in
`(a,b,c,d)` and degree at most three in `tau`. It rejects the altered sign at
all 36 grid tuples. Separate checks cover 36 norm identities and 570 complete
two-stage rational paths. The tight pair `a=0,b=4/5,c=3/5,r=3/5,q=1` keeps
all six sampled raised distances exactly `16/25`, as the equality case
requires.

The regularity argument is also sound. Nonexpansiveness makes
`alpha(u)=|T(u)|` continuous on `K`; the formulas are jointly continuous on
the cone and at its origin. Each fixed trajectory is analytic in the first
piece and linear in the second, with uniformly bounded derivatives on bounded
support. No directional differentiability of `alpha` is assumed.

## Gaussian transfer

Padding the motion by one zero coordinate gives a continuous contraction in
`R^(n+2)`. Aishwarya--Li Theorem 1.4(i)(a) supplies a coupling under which the
source endpoint density sampled from itself is no greater than the target
endpoint density sampled from itself. The theorem only requires continuity
of the contracting trajectories for this conclusion.

At both endpoints the enlarged densities factor as `f(x)gamma_(2,s)(z)`.
With `C=(2 pi s)^(-1)` and `Z` having the two-dimensional Gaussian law,
`|Z|^2/(2s)` is exponential with mean one, hence

```text
Pr[f(X)gamma_(2,s)(Z)>hC]=integral(f-h)_+.
```

Applying sampled-density order at level `hC` gives the claimed hinge
direction. The independent checker verifies the exact conditional algebra on
255 density/threshold pairs. This algebra is elementary; the universal
sampled-density order remains the external theorem dependency.

The primary statement and hypotheses were checked against
[Aishwarya--Li, arXiv:2609.07041v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07041v2).

## Arbitrary-radius ball transfer

For a finite center list, reverse the two-piece motion. It becomes a
piecewise-smooth expansion in `n+2` dimensions between configurations lying
in `R^n`. Bezdek--Connelly Theorem 1 therefore gives both the union and
intersection inequalities with arbitrary individual radii and the claimed
directions.

The collision regularization is valid. For `0<epsilon<1`,

```text
T_epsilon=(1-epsilon)T+epsilon I
```

is nonexpansive by the triangle inequality and has strictly positive factor
on every ray. Distinct source points remain distinct throughout both motion
stages: unequal radii have unequal preserved norms in the raise, while equal
radii on different directions have distinct positive first-coordinate
components; the lowering retains the injective target coordinates. Passing
to `epsilon=0` uses ordinary continuity of finite ball-union and
ball-intersection volumes in their centers. Repeated centers and zero radii
are reduced or obtained by limits exactly as stated.

The theorem, arbitrary-radius hypotheses, and inequality directions were
checked against [Bezdek--Connelly,
arXiv:math/0108098](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0108098).

## Global three-dimensional example

For

```text
alpha(u)=|u1 u2 u3|,
T(x)=|x1 x2 x3|x/|x|^3,
```

the differential calculation is correct on every open orthant. In the
orthonormal plane spanned by `u` and `grad_S alpha`, the derivative is the
shear `[[alpha,g],[0,alpha]]`. Its norm is at most `q>=alpha` precisely when
`g<=q-alpha^2/q`. Writing `z_i=u_i^2` gives

```text
alpha^2=z1 z2 z3<=1/27,
|grad_S alpha|^2=z1z2+z1z3+z2z3-9z1z2z3<=1/3.
```

At `q=2/3`, the shear allowance is at least `11/18`, whose squared reserve
over `1/3` is `13/324`. A line segment crosses only finitely many coordinate
planes unless it lies in one; on such a plane `T=0`. Piecewise integration
and continuity therefore prove the global `2/3`-Lipschitz bound, including
segments through the nonsmooth set and the origin.
