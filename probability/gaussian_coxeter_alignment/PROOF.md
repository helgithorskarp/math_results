# Alternating reflection-group orbits and Gaussian majorisation

27 September 2026. Complete author proof; independent review pending.
The unrestricted dimension-three conjecture remains open.

The algebraic object is the index-two character decomposition of a finite
real reflection group. Positivity of its alternating Gaussian projector,
a classical chamber heat-kernel fact, supplies the sign. This extends the
team's coordinate-parity theorem to nonabelian reflection groups. Neither
a contact assumption nor a finite list of moment inequalities is used.

## 1. Full comparison for arbitrary mixtures of oriented orbits

Let W be a nontrivial finite group generated by orthogonal hyperplane
reflections of R^d. Let C be an open fundamental chamber and
H=ker(det:W->{+1,-1}). Fix any reflection r in W. For a in the closed
chamber define labelled probability measures

    nu_(a,+) = |H|^-1 sum_(h in H) delta_(h a),
    nu_(a,-) = |H|^-1 sum_(h in H) delta_(r h a).

Repeated points retain multiplicity. For any probability Lambda on
closure(C) x {+1,-1} with bounded a, set

    mu = integral nu_(a,sigma) dLambda(a,sigma),
    mu_aligned = integral nu_(a,+) dLambda(a,sigma).          (1)

**Theorem A.** For every s>0 and threshold q>=0,

    integral (mu*gamma_s-q)_+
        <= integral (mu_aligned*gamma_s-q)_+.               (2)

Here gamma_s has covariance s I_d. All orbit masses, orbit representatives
and the number of orbits are arbitrary; nonatomic mixtures are included.
This is full Gaussian majorisation. Every bounded H-invariant law admits
the representation (1). Uniformity within H-orbits is essential to the
statement. No Lipschitz condition is needed for the alignment inequality
itself; a contraction application must separately check its endpoint map.

### The classical positive alternating projector

Write

    C_(s,a)(x) = |W|^-1 sum_(w in W) gamma_s(x-w a),
    D_(s,a)(x) = |W|^-1 sum_(w in W) det(w) gamma_s(x-w a).

Then C_(s,a) is W-invariant, D_(s,a)(w x)=det(w)D_(s,a)(x),
and |D_(s,a)|<=C_(s,a). The substantive classical input is

    D_(s,a)(x)>0 for x,a in C; D_(s,a)(x)>=0 for x,a in closure(C).
                                                               (3)

Indeed |W|D_(s,a)(x) is the Dirichlet heat kernel of C at time s/2
for generator Delta. This is Corollary 1.2 of
[Stempak](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.03045), specialized to Omega=R^d.
The Dirichlet kernel is positive on this connected chamber. Continuity
gives the closed-chamber assertion. This classical kernel identity and
positivity are credited inputs, not results established by the checker.

Equivalently, the finite alternating exponential sum

    sum_(w in W) det(w) exp(<x,w a>/s)

is positive for x,a in C. The reflection-group hypothesis supplies a
common sign for all a; positivity of an arbitrary signed group sum is not
being assumed. On a reflecting wall the alternating sum is zero, by
pairing with its fixing reflection. In particular nu_(a,+)=nu_(a,-)
when a is on the chamber boundary.

For the octahedral group B3 there is also a direct algebraic proof of (3).
Take C={x1>x2>x3>0}. Summing coordinate signs and then permutations gives

    sum_(w in B3) det(w) exp(<x,w a>/s)
                   =det[2 sinh(x_i a_j/s)]_(i,j=1)^3.       (3a)

Expand 2sinh(z)=2 sum_(k>=0) z^(2k+1)/(2k+1)!. Cauchy--Binet expresses
the determinant as a sum over 0<=k1<k2<k3 of positive coefficients times

    det[x_i^(2k_j+1)] det[a_i^(2k_j+1)].

Each product is strictly positive for x,a in C: a generalized Vandermonde
determinant at positive strictly ordered arguments has the sign of the
ordinary Vandermonde. This follows by factoring the Vandermonde and a
Schur polynomial, whose monomial coefficients are nonnegative. Truncated
series have these positive terms; entrywise convergence and continuity of
the determinant give the infinite identity. The k=(0,1,2) term already
ensures strict positivity. This is a classical alternant/total-positivity
argument, supplied explicitly here. Thus the octahedral sign needs no
external heat-kernel positivity premise; the general W statement uses (3).

### Two-character calculation

The orbit densities are exactly C_(s,a)+sigma D_(s,a). Set

    C0(x)=integral C_(s,a)(x) dLambda,
    B(x)=integral sigma D_(s,a)(x) dLambda,
    D0(x)=integral D_(s,a)(x) dLambda.

Then f=mu*gamma_s=C0+B and g=mu_aligned*gamma_s=C0+D0.
On the chamber C, (3) gives |B|<=D0; covariance under W gives
|B(x)|<=|D0(x)| everywhere. Define theta(x)=(1+B(x)/D0(x))/2
where D0 is nonzero, and theta=1/2 otherwise. Thus 0<=theta<=1,
theta(r x)=theta(x), and

    f(x)=theta(x)g(x)+(1-theta(x))g(r x).                  (4)

At the paired point r x the coefficients are interchanged. The resulting
two by two matrix is doubly stochastic. Jensen's inequality and the
measure-preserving involution x->r x prove (2). This also proves every
integrable convex-energy comparison. The kernel depends on the entire
law and on s; it is not a common componentwise channel for all priors.

For the representation assertion, each regular W-orbit splits into its
two H-orbits, and invariance makes each conditional finite-orbit law
uniform. On boundary orbits an odd fixing reflection identifies the two
halves. Finite-group averaging, or disintegration over the chamber
representative, proves (1), including laws charging walls.

When W is the group of independent coordinate sign changes, (3)
factorizes as a product of hyperbolic sines. This recovers the accepted
[parity-alignment theorem](../gaussian_parity_alignment/PROOF.md).
For rank three the present theorem also covers A3, B3, H3 and reducible
reflection groups, with generic H-orbits of sizes 12, 24, 60 and the
corresponding dihedral-product sizes. These are actual full-threshold
signs, not tests on finitely many energies.

## 2. Both ball-volume inequalities

Take finitely many complete H-orbits, with centres in H a_j or r H a_j,
and give every label in orbit j one radius rho_j>=0. Align every orbit
to H a_j, keeping rho_j. Then

    volume(union of aligned balls) <= volume(union of original balls),
    volume(intersection of aligned balls)
                        >= volume(intersection of original balls). (5)

Radii may vary arbitrarily between orbits. There is no assertion for
arbitrary label-dependent radii within an orbit. Alignment need not
contract distances, so (5) is first a rearrangement inequality. Whenever
the final centre map is a contraction, it is a Kneser--Poulsen case.

Here are explicit limiting arguments. The character calculation permits
any positive finite orbit coefficients. For epsilon>0 use

    F_epsilon(x)=|H|^-1 sum_(j,h)
       exp((rho_j^2-|x-z_(j,h)|^2)/(2epsilon)).

Its aligned counterpart G_epsilon majorises F_epsilon by (4). Concavity
of min(z,1) yields integral min(F_epsilon,1)>=integral min(G_epsilon,1).
As epsilon decreases to zero these converge almost everywhere to the
respective union indicators. If all centres have norm <=L and all radii
are <=R, outside |x|>=L each exponential is bounded by
exp((R^2-(|x|-L)^2)/(2epsilon)). For epsilon<=1 this supplies an integrable
Gaussian bound outside a fixed larger ball; inside use min(F,1)<=1.
Dominated convergence proves the union assertion.

For intersection use instead

    P_epsilon(x)=|H|^-1 sum_(j,h)
       exp((|x-z_(j,h)|^2-rho_j^2)/(2epsilon)).

The common radial factor is exp(|x|^2/(2epsilon)); the alternating
exponential sum is evaluated at -x, and its sign is again common to every
a_j. Thus the same two-character proof, now using that common sign at
-x, gives P_epsilon=theta Q_epsilon+(1-theta)Q_epsilon(r x), with theta
invariant under r. Convexity of exp(-z) implies
integral exp(-P_epsilon)<=integral exp(-Q_epsilon). The integrands converge
to intersection indicators away from sphere boundaries. Retaining one
summand gives a uniform integrable superexponential bound outside a fixed
ball. This proves the second assertion of (5). Zero radii and repeated
labelled centres cause no exception; the empty list is excluded.

## 3. A nonabelian indecomposable contraction family in R3

This supplies contraction applications of the algebraic sign, rather
than merely listing reflection groups. It also identifies what geometric
obstruction is actually established. It generalizes the two-root argument
in the cited parity packet; it does not revisit orthocentric depth-one
flaps or assert that every weight-preserving matching is obstructed.

Let W be an essential finite reflection group in R3 containing -I.
Let n1,n2,n3 be the inward unit normals to the simple chamber walls,
and choose the unique a in C with <a,n_i>=1. Put

    S=|a|^2, m=2-S, H=W intersect SO(3), V=H a,
    R>3-4/S, c=R-2+4/S.                                  (6)

Thus c>1 and c<R. The simple normals have nonpositive mutual inner
products. Cauchy--Schwarz applied to <a,n1+n2+n3>=3 gives S>=3,
so m<0. Every other inward unit root is a positive combination of the
simple unit roots; its coefficients sum to at least one, by the triangle
inequality. Consequently a is at distance at least one from every
reflecting hyperplane.

**Theorem B.** The labelled endpoint map

    u -> u             (u in V),
    -R v -> c v        (v in V)                            (7)

is a contraction. For every pair of nonnegative total orbit masses
summing to one, it satisfies full Gaussian majorisation at every s>0.
For arbitrary two radii, one per orbit, it satisfies both Kneser--Poulsen
volume inequalities. Its full labelled squared-distance interval in R3
has exactly the two endpoint congruence classes. The paired affine rank
is six. No assertion rules out a contracting motion in R5 or alternative
weight-preserving rematchings.

### Contraction and sign

Let s_i be reflection in n_i perpendicular, and g_i=-s_i in H. Then

    g_i a=-a+2n_i,  <a,g_i a>=m.

For all u,v in V, <u,v>>=m. To see this, -h is odd for h in H,
so a and (-h)a lie in different chambers. The line segment between
them crosses a reflecting hyperplane. Both endpoints are at distance
at least one from that plane, hence |a-(-h)a|>=2. Expanding gives
<a,h a>>=2-S=m. Equality is attained by every g_i.

Core-core distances are unchanged and outer-outer distances shrink by
c/R. For a cross pair the squared-distance loss is exactly

    |u+R v|^2-|u-c v|^2 = 2(R+c)(<u,v>-m)>=0.             (8)

Because -I is odd, Theorem A aligns the negative outer orbit to R V
while retaining the core V. Now map radii sqrt(S) to sqrt(S) and
R sqrt(S) to c sqrt(S), using a nondecreasing, nonnegative, 1-Lipschitz
profile rho with rho(0)=0: take the identity below sqrt(S), interpolate
linearly between the two radii, and continue with slope one above.
The straight interpolation of the radii to rho is a continuous
contraction. In fact for two radii r>=t their nonnegative difference
decreases, both radii decrease, and the squared distance is

    (r-t)^2+2rt(1-cos(angle)).

The credited continuous-contraction Gaussian theorem of Aishwarya--Li
therefore completes the sign. Apply (5), then the classical
continuous-contraction union and intersection theorems, for the ball
volumes. The two radii remain independent throughout.

### Exactly two intermediate configurations

The orbit V spans R3 and has centre zero. Indeed its span contains the
sums a+g_i a=2n_i, so it is full. A common fixed vector of H would
be fixed by each half-turn g_i, and hence lie on all three distinct axes
n_i; it must be zero. Averaging V therefore gives zero.

In any intermediate labelled configuration, all core distances remain
fixed, so apply one isometry to fix the core pointwise. The outer label
v=h a has its three distances to the fixed core points h g_i a tight
by (8). Those three anchors are noncollinear and lie in the plane

    {x:<x,v>=m}.

The three prescribed spheres have exactly the two intersections -R v
and c v: they are reflections across that plane, are distinct, and the
intersection of three spheres with noncollinear centres has at most two
points. Hence each outer label has a binary choice.

Join v=h a to w=h g_i a. Then <v,w>=m. If their binary choices differ,
the mixed squared distance minus the target squared distance equals

    |-R v-c w|^2-|c v-c w|^2
                  =2(R+c)(c-1)m<0.                        (9)

Such a mixed choice lies below the required target distance. These edges
connect V: the g_i generate H, since g_i are in H and every even word
in the s_i is the same even word in the g_i. Thus all outer choices are
identical. Both uniform choices are the stated endpoints. This proves
the exact interval assertion without enumerating all binary choices.

For affine rank, a relation alpha.u+beta.y=k on all paired points has
k=0 by core averaging, then alpha+beta=0 by core spanning, and
-R alpha+c beta=0 by outer spanning. Thus alpha=beta=0 and the paired
affine rank is six. This rank is only the scope of the usual linear
paired-span lifting criterion, not an obstruction to every R5 motion.

## 4. Concrete sizes and exact control

For B3, take all signed coordinate permutation matrices, and

    a=(1+2sqrt(2),1+sqrt(2),1), S=13+6sqrt(2), R=3,
    c=1+4/(13+6sqrt(2)).                                   (10)

H consists of the 24 determinant-positive matrices; it is not the
four-element coordinate-parity group. The source and target have 48
distinct labels. The checker works in Q(sqrt(2)), verifies all 1128 pairs,
the 72 tight cross pairs, the three independent anchors per outer point,
the connected incompatibility graph and paired rank six. It also checks
the two-character Gaussian identity and all hinge breakpoints on one
exact observation orbit. That last control does not replace (3).

An explicit alternative matching also contracts in this example: keep
the core fixed and replace the outer targets c v by -c r v, where r
changes the third coordinate sign. Since -r is in H, this preserves
the target orbit measure and both orbit radii. The checker verifies all
pairs of this rematching too. It is not evidence against the labelled
interval claim, and forbids interpreting that claim as a complete
obstruction to alternative matching or lifting methods.

Every finite support contraction here has a global 1-Lipschitz extension
by the classical Kirszbraun theorem; only its support values enter (2).

The same uniform proof gives 120 labels for H3, and 4k labels for
I2(k) x A1 when k is even. These are not extra enumerations. H3 and the
dihedral-product cases use the standard real reflection representations;
their group orders and the general proof are written inputs, not
computations performed by verify.py.

## 5. Scope and trust boundary

The new sign is the nonabelian extension of the existing parity result,
with arbitrary bounded oriented-orbit mixtures and orbit-dependent radii.
The explicit indecomposable family shows a change in the signed finite
frontier, not a small noise window or a further contact reformulation.
Arbitrary within-orbit priors, arbitrary contractions of H-invariant laws,
and the unrestricted dimension-three conjecture remain unresolved.

The proof uses classical finite-reflection chamber facts, Stempak's
Dirichlet-kernel identity and positivity, and the published
continuous-contraction Gaussian/ball-volume theorems. The finite checker
uses exact rational and quadratic-field arithmetic. It is an author
consistency check, not independent review, a formal proof, or an exhaustive
classification of Gaussian contractions. Historical novelty of the
resulting geometric family is not certified by the bounded literature
search; the sources and preceding team work are explicitly credited.
