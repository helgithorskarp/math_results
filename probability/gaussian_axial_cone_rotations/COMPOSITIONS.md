# A uniform obstruction to finite compositions of strong contractions

Complete author proof, 26 September 2026; independent mathematical review
is pending. This strengthens the comparison with existing methods for the
axial-cone theorem in [PROOF.md](PROOF.md). It does not introduce another
positive subclass or assert a counterexample to Gaussian majorisation.

The circular range $1/2<pq\le2/\pi$, and the existing rational 25-point
witness, cannot be obtained by **any finite sequence of strong coordinatewise
contractions in three dimensions, allowing independent rigid alignments at
each step**. This includes any finite sequence of one-sided hyperplane folds
with arbitrarily changing hyperplanes. The proof gives a reusable matrix
obstruction for anchored rigid clusters in every dimension.

## 1. The class of compositions being excluded

For two finite labeled configurations $x_i,y_i\in\mathbb R^d$, call the step
strong after alignment if there are orthogonal matrices $U,V$ such that

$$
|(V(y_i-y_j))_k|\le |(U(x_i-x_j))_k|
\quad\text{for every }i,j,k.
\tag{C1}
$$

Translations do not affect this definition. Each step is a Euclidean
contraction, by summing the squared coordinate inequalities. Independent
endpoint frames are allowed, and a different pair of frames may be chosen
at every step. Thus this definition includes rigid motions and their
compositions with strong contractions in a fixed frame.
The same definition applies to infinite labeled sets, requiring every pair
inequality in the same frames.

Strong contraction is the definition in
[Bezdek--Naszodi, Section 1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.05074). Their
Theorem 1.3 gives the ball-volume inequalities for each such step;
orthogonal invariance of Euclidean balls permits the alignments in (C1).
Transitivity then gives the same inequalities for any finite composition.
The present obstruction concerns this whole finite-composition method.

In particular, folding one side of an affine hyperplane onto the other is
strong in a frame with one coordinate normal to the hyperplane. Allowing
arbitrary hyperplanes at successive stages is included in (C1), not just
folding in one fixed coordinate system.

## 2. The rank-one matrix obstruction

Let $A,B\subset\mathbb R^d$ each span $\mathbb R^d$. Include a distinguished
origin label, and consider the prescribed endpoint map

$$
(0,A,-B)\longmapsto(0,A,B).
\tag{C2}
$$

The sets can be finite; for infinite sets the argument below uses the same
finite spanning subsets and all their additional pair inequalities. Define

$$
A^*=\{u:u\cdot a\ge0\ (a\in A)\},\qquad
B^*=\{v:v\cdot b\ge0\ (b\in B)\},
$$
$$
\mathcal R_{A,B}
=\left\{\sum_{\ell=1}^N u_\ell v_\ell^{\mathsf T}:
N<\infty,\ u_\ell\in A^*,\ v_\ell\in B^*\right\}.
\tag{C3}
$$

This is a cone generated by rank-one matrices; nonnegative coefficients
can be absorbed into either factor. No closedness or representation theorem
for tensor cones is needed in the following argument.

**Theorem C1.** If (C2) is a finite composition of steps satisfying (C1),
with all intermediate configurations in $\mathbb R^d$, then

$$
I_d\in\mathcal R_{A,B}.
\tag{C4}
$$

Consequently, if a real matrix $H$ satisfies

$$
u^{\mathsf T}Hv\ge0\quad(u\in A^*,v\in B^*),
\qquad \operatorname{tr}H<0,
\tag{C5}
$$

no such finite composition exists. This is a necessary condition only;
membership in (C3) is not asserted sufficient for a factorization.

**Proof.** Every distance within either anchored cloud $\{0\}\cup A$ and
$\{0\}\cup(-B)$ is the same at the two endpoints. Since every step
contracts distances, all these distances must be constant at every stage.
Subtract the position of the origin label at each stage. The preserved Gram
matrices and spanning imply that the clouds have the form $G_j A$ and
$F_j B$, for orthogonal matrices $G_j,F_j$. Apply the rigid normalization
$G_j^{\mathsf T}$. The stage configuration is now

$$
(0,A,L_j B),\qquad L_j=G_j^{\mathsf T}F_j\in O(d),
\qquad L_0=-I_d,\quad L_m=I_d.
\tag{C6}
$$

Independent rigid normalization preserves the existence of the frames in
(C1). It suffices to understand a single normalized step with matrices
$L,M\in O(d)$ and source and target coordinate axes $n_k,m_k$.

For pairs within $\{0\}\cup A$, the sum of the squared inequalities (C1)
is equality. Therefore each coordinate inequality is equality. A
distance-preserving map of a subset of the real line fixing zero is a
single sign. Hence, for each coordinate, some $\epsilon_k\in\{-1,1\}$
satisfies $m_k\cdot a=\epsilon_k n_k\cdot a$ for all $a\in A$.
Spanning gives $m_k=\epsilon_k n_k$.

To spell out the scalar fact, choose a nonzero scalar value $t_0$. If its
image is $\epsilon t_0$, equality of squared distances to zero and to
$t_0$ gives $s^2=t^2$ and $s\epsilon t_0=tt_0$, hence $s=\epsilon t$.
Such a nonzero value exists here because the clouds span.

Applying the same fact to $\{0\}\cup B$ gives signs $\sigma_k$ with

$$
n_k^{\mathsf T}M=\sigma_k n_k^{\mathsf T}L,
\qquad
M=\left(\sum_k\sigma_k n_k n_k^{\mathsf T}\right)L.
\tag{C7}
$$

For a coordinate with $\sigma_k=-1$, the cross-pair inequality becomes

$$
|n_k\cdot a-n_k\cdot Lb|
\ge |n_k\cdot a+n_k\cdot Lb|,
$$

so $(n_k\cdot a)(n_k\cdot Lb)\le0$ for every $a,b$. Neither collection
of scalar values is identically zero, by spanning and orthogonality.
Their signs must therefore be opposite and uniform within each collection.
Choose the sign of $n_k$ so that

$$
u_k=n_k\in A^*,\qquad v_k=-L^{\mathsf T}n_k\in B^*.
$$

The other coordinates do not contribute to $M-L$. Equation (C7) now gives

$$
M-L=2\sum_{k:\sigma_k=-1}u_k v_k^{\mathsf T}.
\tag{C8}
$$

Sum (C8) over the finite sequence in (C6). The left side telescopes to
$2I_d$, proving (C4). Finally apply the linear functional
$Z\mapsto\operatorname{tr}(H^{\mathsf T}Z)$: it is nonnegative on every
generator in (C3), but negative on $I_d$ if (C5) holds. QED.

The argument tracks original labels throughout. The origin label and the
spanning assumptions are essential to this proof. No assumption that an
intermediate step fixes either cloud was made: their rigidity follows from
the endpoint distances, and the anchor is fixed only by normalization.

## 3. The entire circular range beyond the simplicial threshold

For $A=C_p$, $B=C_q$ in three dimensions, their positive dual cones are
$A^*=C_{1/p}$ and $B^*=C_{1/q}$. Take

$$
H=\operatorname{diag}(-pq,-pq,1).
$$

For $u\in C_{1/p}$ and $v\in C_{1/q}$,

$$
u^{\mathsf T}Hv=u_zv_z-pq\,u_\perp\cdot v_\perp
\ge u_zv_z-pq|u_\perp||v_\perp|\ge0.
$$

But $\operatorname{tr}H=1-2pq<0$ whenever $pq>1/2$.
Theorem C1 rules out every finite composition (C1) on those full cone
domains. In particular, throughout

$$
\frac12<pq\le\frac2\pi
$$

the axial theorem supplies full Gaussian majorisation and arbitrary-radius
Kneser--Poulsen inequalities via an R4 motion, whereas no finite sequence
of three-dimensional strong steps, with arbitrary rigid alignments, can
realize the same prescribed map. The obstruction is independent of the
number of steps. At $pq=1/2$ this matrix has trace zero and gives no
obstruction; no converse or exact factorization threshold is claimed.

The same exclusion holds after intersecting both cones with any ball of
positive radius centered at the origin: these bounded truncations have
the same positive duals. Unbounded support is not needed for this obstruction.

## 4. A strict rational certificate for the existing 25-point witness

Retain exactly $A,B$ and the origin from PROOF equation (21), with
$p=3/4$, $q=4/5$ and the twelve rational directions $\mathcal D$.
Every nonzero vector in $A^*$ or $B^*$ has positive axial coordinate:
the inequalities from opposite cardinal directions prove this immediately.
Their height-one sections are the bounded polygons

$$
P_A=\{u:p\,d\cdot u\le1\ (d\in\mathcal D)\},\qquad
P_B=\{v:q\,d\cdot v\le1\ (d\in\mathcal D)\}.
$$

PROOF Section 5 bounds their circumradii by
$\sqrt{10}/(3p)$ and $\sqrt{10}/(3q)$, respectively. Thus for their
height-one generators $\widehat u=(u,1)$ and $\widehat v=(v,1)$,

$$
u\cdot v\le\frac{10}{9pq}=\frac{50}{27}.
$$

The integer matrix

$$
\boxed{H=\operatorname{diag}(-8,-8,15)}
\tag{C9}
$$

therefore satisfies

$$
\widehat u^{\mathsf T}H\widehat v
\ge15-8\frac{50}{27}=\frac5{27}>0,
\qquad \operatorname{tr}H=-1.
\tag{C10}
$$

Positive homogeneity extends nonnegativity to the full dual cones. This is
a strict certificate of (C5) for the existing finite fixture, excluding all
finite compositions in (C1). The separate exact checker enumerates every
pair of defining boundary lines, retains every feasible intersection, and
obtains all twelve vertices of each dual polygon. It verifies (C10) on
all 144 vertex pairs, with minimum exactly $5/27$. Bilinearity then covers
their convex hulls and positive hulls; no direction grid is used.

The strict inequality also shows that the exclusion is stable under
sufficiently small perturbations of these two finite clouds, with the
origin retained and the endpoint rule still $(0,A,-B)\mapsto(0,A,B)$.
Indeed the dual height-one polygons remain bounded uniformly and vary
continuously under small perturbations of the defining inequalities, so
the positive minimum in (C10) persists. Spanning is open as well. The axial
motion has a strict margin $pq=3/5<2/\pi$; slightly larger circular
envelopes preserve it. Thus this comparison is not dependent on exact
rational symmetry. No numerical perturbation radius is asserted.

## 5. What this settles about the prior-method comparison

This strengthens the earlier exclusion of a **single** independently
aligned strong contraction to its whole finite-composition closure in R3.
It also excludes arbitrary successive one-sided hyperplane folds. The
positive axial theorem and its original checker are unchanged. The new
information is a reusable factorization obstruction and a sharper account
of the geometric input needed for its Kneser--Poulsen consequences.

For the 25-point law with distinct positive weights in PROOF Section 5.1,
the common-target rigidity theorem forces each deterministic component to
have the original source and matching. The same argument therefore also
excludes a common-target mixture whose components factor into the strong
steps considered here. With repeated weights, rematching is a separate
question. Pairwise distinct ball radii similarly force any permutation
that preserves every radius label to be the identity; this does not rule
out other representations of a union or other support-level arguments.

The obstruction does not cover chains with intermediate configurations in
higher dimensions, arbitrary continuous R4 or R5 motions, compositions
using other sufficient theorems, stochastic or post-convolution couplings,
or limits of approximate factorizations. In particular, the exhibited R4
motion is compatible with this obstruction. It supplies no negative hinge
or volume comparison, and makes no exhaustive historical-priority claim.
Here a fold always means a one-sided hyperplane fold as defined in Section 1;
general piecewise distance-preserving maps are not being excluded.
