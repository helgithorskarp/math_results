## Geometry and the reference-test class

Write `Q=[-1/2,1/2]^3`, with source centers `-8e_1,8e_1` and target centers
`-4e_1,4e_1`.  For cross-cube offset difference `h in [-1,1]^3`, the exact
squared-distance loss is

```text
|16e_1+h|^2-|8e_1+h|^2=192+16h_1 >= 176.
```

The lattice-set frontier condition is also satisfied: the center loss is
`192>2|8e_1|_1=16`, and the source and target center separations are at least
two in `l_infinity`.  Within each component the map is a translation.  Thus
this is a strict whole-cube contraction in the cited reduction class.

If a reference law had support points in both cubes, agreement of the map
with one ambient isometry would preserve their distance, contradicting the
strict loss above.  Every isometric reference is therefore supported in a
single translate of `Q`, including singular references.

For `p=sigma*gamma_1`, differentiation of the compactly supported mixture
gives

```text
Hess log p(z) = -I + Cov(X | z).
```

For a unit vector `b`, the range of `b.X` in a unit cube has length at most
`sum |b_j| <= sqrt(3)`.  Popoviciu's inequality bounds the posterior variance
by `3/4`, hence `Hess log p <= -I/4`.  Every positive superlevel set is convex.
The mixture is positive analytic and tends to zero at infinity.  Its positive
level sets are null, while strict log-concavity and continuity make the
superlevel-volume parametrization continuous and strictly decreasing over the
relevant range.  Thus the prescribed-volume reference test exists and is
unique modulo null sets.

These arguments establish directly that every isometric-reference test lies
in the much larger family of convex sets used by the obstruction; no
classification of the reference laws is being assumed.

## Exact threshold separation

Let `P=[-1/4,1/4]^3`, `P_+=8e_1+P`, `P_-=-8e_1+P`, and `V=|P|=1/8`.
For

```text
mu=1_E/2,  f=mu*gamma_1,  a=1/100,  A={f>a},
```

the submitted rational estimates are sound.  If

```text
k(t)=integral_[-1/2,1/2] (2pi)^(-1/2) exp(-(t-u)^2/2) du,
```

then `pi<4` and `exp(-r)>1-r` give `k(0)>7/24`.  Symmetry gives
`k(t)/k(0)>=exp(-t^2/2)`.  Retaining only the nearer component yields on each
end box

```text
f > (1/2)(29/32)(7/24)^3
  = 9947/884736 > 1/100.
```

On the middle box the first-coordinate source distance is at least `29/4`.
Bounding the other factors by the Gaussian maximum gives

```text
f <= c^3 exp(-841/32)
  < exp(-5) < (3/8)^5
  = 243/32768 < 1/100.
```

Therefore `P_+ union P_-` is contained in `A`, while `P` is disjoint from
`A`.  In particular `0<|A|<infinity`, and analyticity makes `{f=a}` null.
The threshold set is the unique volume-`|A|` maximizer of the integral of
`f`, modulo null sets.

## Separation, with a stronger independent constant

Let `C` be convex with `|C|=|A|` and put

```text
delta=|A\C|=|C\A|.
```

The submitted proof bounds the missing mass in each end box separately by
`delta`, obtaining `2delta>=1/12`.  Tracking the two disjoint deficits jointly
is stronger.  Set

```text
m_+=|P_+\C|,  m_-=|P_-\C|.
```

Because both end boxes are disjoint subsets of `A`, `m_++m_-<=delta`.
Translate `C intersect P_+` and `C intersect P_-` into the common box `P`.
Inclusion-exclusion now gives intersection volume at least

```text
V-m_+-m_- >= V-delta.
```

Every point in this intersection has both translated end points in `C`, so
convexity puts their midpoint in `C intersect P`.  Since `P` is disjoint from
`A`, this intersection has volume at most `delta`.  Hence

```text
delta >= V-delta,
|A symmetric-difference C|=2delta >= V=1/8.
```

This independently implies, and improves, the submitted `1/12` bound.

For a finite positive average `phi=sum lambda_j 1_(C_j)`, the fact that
`1_A` is an indicator gives the exact pointwise-affine identity

```text
||1_A-phi||_1
  = sum lambda_j ||1_A-1_(C_j)||_1.
```

It follows that the `1/8` lower bound survives finite mixtures and `L1`
closure.  Tonelli gives the same conclusion for any well-defined measurable
probability mixture.  `L1` closure also preserves `0<=phi<=1` and
`integral phi=|A|`.

Finally choose `eta<a/2` so that the bounded shell
`{|f-a|<eta}` has volume below `1/24`; continuity from above and the null
level set guarantee such an `eta`.  Equal volumes and the superlevel signs
give

```text
integral_A f - integral phi f
  = integral |1_A-phi| |f-a|.
```

The submitted constants yield the stated lower bound `eta/24`.  The stronger
distance above actually yields `eta(1/8-1/24)=eta/12`.  No explicit value of
`eta` is required for the claimed uniform positivity.

## Positive control and external theorem scope

Moving the cube centers along `+/- (8-4t)e_1`, `0<=t<=1`, preserves all
same-cube distances.  Every cross-cube squared distance has derivative

```text
-16(16-8t+h_1) <= -112.
```

This is a continuous contraction from the source map to the target map.
The cited primary source is Aishwarya--Li,
[*Gaussian Convolution, Internal Energies, and the Kneser--Poulsen
Conjecture*, arXiv:2609.07041v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07041v2).
Its Theorem 1.4 is stated for a continuous contraction on an arbitrary subset
in every dimension and every probability measure supported there; part (ii)
gives the Gaussian-convolved internal-energy inequality for every
nonnegative-pressure energy and every variance.  Thus it covers this motion
and, in particular, all convex energy densities defining majorisation.

This external theorem is used only to certify that the example is a known
positive case.  The convex-hull separation and its constants do not depend on
that theorem.

## Trust boundary and nonclaims

The accepted fact is a method boundary: fixed-volume positive averages of
isometric-reference source indicators cannot approximate this mixed law's
maximizing source indicator, even though the underlying contraction is a
known positive Gaussian-majorisation example.  This does not refute the
isometric-reference theorem or any quantitative method retaining reference
slack.

In particular, neither the target nor this review:

- constructs a Gaussian-majorisation counterexample;
- settles the full dimension-three Gaussian-convolution frontier;
- rules out signed combinations, spatially varying coefficients, different
  test volumes, common-target decompositions, or quantitative error/slack
  arguments;
- accepts the headline frontier merely from this supporting obstruction; or
- establishes novelty beyond the cited paper and the repository context.

The primary theorem was checked at version 2.  Its proof was not independently
reproved here, and later revisions could change bibliographic or theorem
numbering details without affecting the self-contained separation proof.
