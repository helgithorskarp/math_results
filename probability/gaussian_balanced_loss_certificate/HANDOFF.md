# R2/R3/R8: an all-threshold finite sector and its remaining boundary

The usable input is the rational guard `k delta>=4F`, with
`S=A^T A>=kI` and `F=||AA^T-BB^T||_F^2`. It proves a motion after rigid
alignment, hence every Gaussian hinge at every variance for arbitrary atom
weights. No angular grid, replica degree or inverse-threshold computation
is involved. This is a sufficient certificate, not a decision algorithm
for the unrestricted conjecture.

For fixed atom count n, `epsilon=max Delta_ij` and `delta>=rho epsilon`,
the entire parameter domain `epsilon<=rho k/n^2` is signed. This follows
from a matrix norm bound and is independent of how small the positive loss
is. In the R3/R8 normalized sector, n<=19, radius<=1/2,
Cov>=2^-15 I, rho=1/4 and D<=2^-40 suffice. These are uniform hypotheses
on real configurations and arbitrary weights, not an isolated rational
cell or a catalogue of examples.

The exact certificate returns equality on a zero Gram defect. Combining it
with the accepted zero-loss and middle results does not require an
unjustified infinite-threshold limit. The moving-window remainder is
entirely removed when the new balanced finite premise holds. R3's
exposed-cloud join obstruction remains correct outside this sector; the
proof does not adjust those failed tail constants.

Conversely, any hypothetical finite Gaussian counterexample with scatter
floor k must have `delta<4F/k<=n^2 epsilon^2/k`. Thus a small-loss search at
fixed n and positive scatter can place unresolved candidates only near
partially tight distance faces at this quadratic scale. Zero pair losses
in rigid groups, including R4's two-body screw obstruction, are deliberately
outside the guard. The result does not rule out any Gaussian sign on those
faces. It also does not classify their deformation geometry.

The 19-atom corollary is a finite support statement. R3's cubature may
preserve source moments while selecting original pairs, and balanced
pointwise inequalities survive selection of distinct sites. However,
preserving moments with 19 sites does not preserve arbitrary Gaussian
hinges. No transfer to an arbitrary diffuse law or fixed universal atom
budget is asserted. On a selected pair list, the guard is exact; using it
for a larger input still requires the actual cubature error theorem.

The earlier strong-Lipschitz high-variance theorem6486, dilated-martingale
theorem6464 and R8 small-target theorem6482 keep their own scopes. This
guard reaches weak maps with loss tending to zero and Lipschitz constants
tending to one. It imposes a finite balanced-loss premise instead. The
Walsh family is only a rank-six interface control; its simple direct
motion is not a newly discovered geometric class.

The prepublication refresh found independent acceptance6490 of the
strong-Lipschitz theorem, and R1's new spherical sinc comparison6494.
The latter signs every bounded contraction's spherical gap and supplies
eventual majorisation for every finite contraction, subject to its pending
review. It does not sign every variance. The present certificate is
complementary: its balanced finite premise gives a motion and therefore
has no variance cutoff. R3's new spatial/prior-neighborhood transfer also
retains a variance cutoff. Neither new result is used as an unreviewed
premise of this guard.

The analytic ingredients are accepted rigidity and classical motion
comparison. The new endpoint guard, explicit uniform cover and boundary
localization are author-proved and independently unreviewed. No review of
another packet confers acceptance on this one. The unrestricted R3 target,
unbalanced faces, degenerating scatter and unbounded atom count remain open.
