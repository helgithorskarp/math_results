1. [Equality and sharp-exponent stability for Gaussian Renyi entropy under
   contractions](../gaussian_contraction_rigidity/PROOF.md), and its
   [independent review](../gaussian_contraction_rigidity_review1/REVIEW.md).
   The source proves the compact dissipation identity, arbitrary-law
   equality classification, bounded-support pair-distortion estimates,
   and exponent `1/2` under a fixed positive covariance lower bound.
   Our starting point is exactly that identity and its double-centering
   argument. The source's review does not review this extension. Its
   requests for explicit Gaussian domination and singular-safe alignment
   are incorporated in Sections 3 and 4 of the present proof.

2. Gautam Aishwarya and Dongbin Li,
   [The Kneser–Poulsen phenomena for entropy](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.03664v3),
   IMRN 2025(12), rnaf140;
   [journal version](https://academic.oup.com/imrn/article/2025/12/rnaf140/8160080).
   The qualitative Gaussian Renyi comparison, posterior-divergence method,
   and doubled-dimensional contraction path are prior work. The path has
   classical geometric antecedents. We use those mechanisms; no new
   qualitative monotonicity theorem is claimed.

3. Aishwarya and Li,
   [Gaussian Convolution, Internal Energies, and the Kneser–Poulsen
   Conjecture](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.07041v2),
   [version record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07041).
   Theorem 1.3 and Examples 2.8 already remove moment assumptions for the
   qualitative Renyi comparison. Theorem 1.2 concerns Gaussian
   majorization in the plane. Neither that problem in higher dimension
   nor removal of a moment condition is our claimed advance. The latest
   version found during this audit was v2, 13 September 2026.
   Equation (61) also records the elementary Gaussian replica identity
   used for our finite corroboration; that identity is not claimed new.

4. Anchal Aggarwal and Mandeep Singh,
   [A generalization of Powers–Stormer inequality](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.03913),
   [manuscript](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.03913), introduction and (1.1).
   This primary research paper records the classical square-root trace
   inequality, attributing the exponent-`1/2` case to Powers–Stormer
   (1970). Only that classical case is used here; a self-contained
   finite-dimensional proof is supplied. No new matrix square-root
   inequality or generalization from that manuscript is claimed.

5. Ery Arias-Castro, Adel Javanmard, and Bruno Pelletier,
   [Perturbation Bounds for Procrustes, Classical Scaling, and Trilateration,
   with Applications to Manifold Learning](https://jmlr.org/papers/v21/18-720.html),
   JMLR 21(15), 1–37 (2020);
   [paper](https://jmlr.org/papers/volume21/18-720/18-720.pdf).
   Procrustes control by Gram perturbations is established methodology.
   Their estimates organize robustness using singular values. We do not
   claim invention of Procrustes perturbation theory. Our application
   combines a classical singular-safe trace bound with the nonnegative
   distance loss of a contraction, obtaining an explicit optimal constant
   for the radius-normalized probability-law problem.
