# Two exact Moser reflection rounds strictly reduce whole-support colour relations

This package gives a positive but non-record Hadwiger--Nelson construction
checkpoint.  Starting from the exact 25-point complete common-neighbour
support of a Moser spindle, two simultaneous reflection-closure rounds give
strict plane unit-distance graphs on respectively **115 points / 447 edges**
and **398 points / 2,084 edges**.  Each round strictly reduces the complete
named four-colour relation on the entire preceding support.  Both graphs have
chromatic number exactly four.  The next simultaneous round has 1,020 points,
so this exact architecture stops at the 508-point cap.

There is no five-chromatic graph here and no improvement on Parts's
509-point record.  The strict relation gains are genuine physical signals,
not abstract relations: all coordinates, collisions and unit contacts are
reconstructed exactly on the same supports.  They also concern 25- and
115-terminal whole-support relations, not pair projections; no smaller set
of essential roles is claimed.

## Exact construction

Work in

```text
K = Q(sqrt(3),sqrt(11)),  basis (1,sqrt(3),sqrt(11),sqrt(33)).
```

All Cartesian coordinates have denominator 12.  The source `S0` is the
25-point set `C` in the sibling exact certificate
`hadwiger_nelson_moser_all_terminal_contacts/certificate.json`.  It contains
the standard seven-point Moser spindle and is the complete set of common unit
neighbours generated by pairs of spindle vertices.

For any finite support `S`, define one simultaneous round

```text
R(S) = S union {p+q-r : |p-r|=|q-r|=1 and p,q,r are in S, p!=q}.
```

If `p` and `q` are two unit neighbours of `r`, the point `p+q-r` is the other
intersection of their two unit circles (or the existing tangent point).
Thus the operation is intrinsically planar and preserves `K^2`; it performs
no abstract quotient or spatial flattening.  Equal coordinate vectors are
merged, and every unit pair is then reconstructed from scratch.

The complete exact census is:

| support | points | strict unit edges | unit two-path routes | next round |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `S0` | 25 | 53 | 258 | 115 |
| `S1=R(S0)` | 115 | 447 | 3,713 | 398 |
| `S2=R(S1)` | 398 | 2,084 | 22,980 | 1,020 |

The verifier makes 85,858 all-pairs distance decisions across `S0,S1,S2`
and separately checks both defining unit contacts for every generated route.
Point, edge and route streams are SHA-256 pinned in `certificate.json`.

## Two strict unrestricted relation gains

Let `Col4(S)` be the set of all named proper four-colourings of the complete
strict graph on `S`.  Coordinate order embeds each support as the initial
segment of the next one.

The certificate supplies a proper colouring of `S0` that cannot extend to
`S1`.  Ordinary colour-list propagation removes 20 colours, forces three new
vertices, and empties the list of vertex 33.  It also supplies a proper
colouring of `S1` that cannot extend to `S2`; 40 removals force eleven vertices
and empty vertex 128.  Every removal is justified by an adjacent singleton of
that colour and is replayed against the exact edge graph.  Hence

```text
restriction(Col4(S1), S0) is a proper subset of Col4(S0),
restriction(Col4(S2), S1) is a proper subset of Col4(S1).
```

The relations are nonempty: the checked proper four-colouring of `S2`
restricts to proper colourings of both earlier supports.  These are complete
unrestricted named relations; the proof excludes specific admissible input
words without imposing a symmetry, palette pattern or restricted colouring
family.

The same `S2` word proves the upper bound four.  For the lower bound, the
checker extracts the seven original Moser vertices from `S0`, reconstructs
their eleven edges, and tests all `3^7` named three-colour words; none is
proper.  Thus all three supports have chromatic number exactly four.

## Reproduce

CPython 3.11 or later and the standard library suffice.  From this directory:

```sh
python3 -B verify.py --certificate certificate.json --check-expected --controls
python3 -O -B verify.py --certificate certificate.json --check-expected --controls
python3 -B produce.py --out /tmp/hn-moser-reflection-certificate.json
cmp certificate.json /tmp/hn-moser-reflection-certificate.json
sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS
```

The output path supplied to the producer must not already exist.  The
verifier imports no producer module, solver or floating-point package.  It
uses a separately written multiplication table for `K`, reconstructs every
round and every unit edge, checks the positive word and the two propagation
contradictions, and rejects three damaged certificates.

## Scope and campaign consequence

This is the maximal natural capped member of this one frozen reflection
architecture.  It does not classify selected subsets of the 1,020-point next
round, other starting supports, asynchronous reflections, deformations, or
arbitrary common-neighbour constructions.  Since `S2` is four-colourable and
the next complete event loses the point budget, the architecture is banked at
this boundary rather than widened.

The unrestricted published order record remains Parts's
[509-point construction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.12665).  Haugland's
[2026 v4 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04542v4) likewise identifies 509
as current; its 2,131-point result concerns the distinct Moser-spindle-free
restriction.  This package is therefore source-quality progress, not record
progress.

trust boundary.
