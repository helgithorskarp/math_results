Verdict: **accepted with high confidence at source commit
`b0c50ccb772029a11d0ae9498aeef230cd414909`**.

The accepted result is a negative source classification, not a target graph.
It establishes the general inertial-conjugation colouring lemma, proves that
the field generated by Radchenko's specified quartic direction and a cube root
of unity has unit-distance chromatic number exactly three, and proves that
`UD(F(i))` is bipartite whenever the real number field `F` has a place above 2
of ramification index one. The latter condition applies to the explicit tower
fields used by Alon et al. This supplies no five-chromatic graph through 508
vertices and no vertex lower bound.

## Re-derived proof

Let `c` be physical complex conjugation and let `v` be a finite place of a
conjugation-stable field `K`, fixed by `c`, on whose residue field `c` is the
identity. If `d` is a unit-distance difference, then

```text
d c(d) = 1,       so       2 v(d) = 0.
```

Thus `d` is an integral unit at `v` and its residue satisfies `d_bar^2=1`.
Inside each additive coset of the valuation ring, reduction maps every edge to
a step `+1` or `-1`. Those steps form copies of a `p`-cycle in residue
characteristic `p`; they require two colours when `p=2` and at most three when
`p` is odd. Pulling back a colouring proves the general lemma. If a finite
place of the real fixed field ramifies in the quadratic extension `K/(K∩R)`,
its inertia group is `{1,c}`, so the lemma also proves the stated necessary
unramifiedness condition for four-chromatic fields.

For the Radchenko field, put

```text
t=(1-sqrt(13))/2,  b=i sqrt(3),  g^2=(1-t)/3,
alpha=(t+b g)/2,   omega=(-1+b)/2.
```

The quartic of `alpha` is irreducible modulo two. The identities
`t=alpha+alpha^-1`, `b=1+2 omega`, and `g=(2 alpha-t)/b` show that
`Q(alpha,omega)=Q(t,g,b)`. The former field has degree eight: `Q(alpha)` has
degree four and a real embedding, so it cannot already contain the imaginary
quadratic field `Q(omega)`. Hence the eight displayed target basis elements are
independent.

At the 3-adic branch `T=1 mod 3`, the element `G` with `G^2=-1/T` gives the
unramified quadratic extension with residue field `F_9`. Write a unit
difference as `X+bY`, with `X,Y` in that extension. Its norm equation is

```text
X^2 + 3Y^2 = 1.
```

The two summands have valuations of opposite parity, so no cancellation is
possible. The equality forces `v(X)=0` and `v(Y)>=0`. In the residue field,
`X_bar^2=1`, whose only solutions are `+1,-1`; writing
`X=A(T)+B(T)G` therefore gives `A(T)_bar=+1 or -1` and `B(T)_bar=0`.
The submitted zeroth-ternary-digit formula for `A(T)` consequently changes on
every unit edge, including points with arbitrary rational denominators.
The exact triangle `0,1,(1+b)/2` uses all three colours, proving equality.

For the dyadic theorem write a unit difference in `F(i)` as
`d=X+Y(1+i)`. With `v(2)=1`, its norm is

```text
X^2 + 2XY + 2Y^2 = 1.
```

If `m=v(X)<=v(Y)=n`, the unique smallest term has valuation `2m`. If
`m>n`, it has valuation `2n+1`. The second case cannot equal zero, while the
first forces `m=0` and `n>=0`. Hence every unit difference has `X_bar=1` in
characteristic two. Summing around an odd closed walk would give both zero and
one after reduction, so no odd cycle exists. Since `0--1` is an edge, the
chromatic number is exactly two.

The similarity claim is also sound: if `uK` has a unit difference `uz`, then
`uK=(uz)K` because `zK=K`, and `uz` has modulus one. Otherwise its
unit-distance graph is edgeless. Translations and reflection do not change the
bound.

## Independent executable audit

[audit.py](audit.py) imports no target code. It uses SymPy Gröbner reduction
for the independent quotient

```text
Q[T,G,B]/(T^2-T-3, 3G^2+T-1, B^2+3)
```

and a second quotient for `Q(H,i)`, `H^2=H+1`. It verifies the defining
identities, modular irreducibility, the nonzero `9/256` change-of-basis
determinant, all nine elements of `F_9`, and 64 independently computed 3-adic
trace-root precisions using `sqrt_mod` rather than either lifting algorithm in
the target package.

The audit then checks 252 translated edges from 126 independently generated
quartic/equilateral unit directions, including denominator 3-valuation 40. A
separate dyadic model generates 96 exact Hilbert-90 directions and checks 192
translated edges, including denominator 2-powers through 45. It also verifies
the three-coloured unit triangle and the ramified-base counterexample. These
finite controls test the implementations; the written valuation arguments,
not sampling, establish the whole-field theorems.

The target SHA-256 manifest passed. Its normal and optimized verifiers both
matched `expected.json` byte for byte, recovering 64 basis products, 160
rational operation controls, 516 translated Salem edges, 320 translated
dyadic edges, and all reported hashes.

## Primary-source scope checks

The pinned primary sources were inspected directly:

* [Radchenko, arXiv:1807.03726v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.03726) supplies
  the reciprocal quartic and its nonreal unit-circle root; no chromatic result
  is imported.
* [Alon et al., arXiv:2605.20695v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20695v1)
  explicitly choose totally real `L_j` unramified outside an odd-prime set and
  put `K_j=L_j(i)`. Their projected supports lie in translates of the relevant
  embedded fields, so the similarity extension makes the bipartite theorem
  apply to all additional unit contacts.
* [Sawin, arXiv:2605.20579v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20579v1) starts over
  a real quadratic field ramified at two and constructs the CM quadratic
  extension unramified at every finite place. It therefore genuinely evades
  the inertial hypothesis; no colouring conclusion about Sawin's fields is
  accepted or asserted.

This checks only the field and ramification facts used here, not the full
density proofs or their quantitative conclusions.
