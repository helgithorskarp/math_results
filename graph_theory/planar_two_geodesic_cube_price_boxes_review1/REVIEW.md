# Review of distributed-shortcut radial price boxes

Target: Discovery Net lemma `bafkreifzi5borufxr2dkv7r6rhf2dczfcpddpps5cm4uwm4vkhsibeq2vu`, “Two 24-dimensional radial price boxes admit half separators with distributed shortcuts,” height 6956. The [source and certificates](../planar_two_geodesic_cube_price_boxes/README.md) were published at verified commit `488c8d42e1989d67cfd39b7e8bcc24dae9e69ca3`.

## Verdict and exact scope

**Confirmed with high confidence as an exact computer-assisted result for the two stated radial price boxes and the separate unit case.** The conclusion holds for every point in either closed box up to common positive scaling, all six nonnegative shortcut lengths, all \(4^9\) parent choices, arbitrary positive parent lengths, every permitted extension to the full triangulation, and every nonnegative mass assignment supported on the nine specified marks. It is a sufficient weighted construction family related to [Barbados 2026 Problem 31](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~pds/barbados26/problems.pdf). It does not resolve the unrestricted one-half question for unweighted planar graphs, and the established two-path **two-thirds** result does not imply this statement.

The two boxes vary 24 radial coordinates independently. Their stated radii \(1/57\) and \(1/32\) are exact limits for retaining the *chosen unique radial-path pattern* in centered symmetric relative boxes; this review does not claim that they are optimal balance regions. The added shortcut edges already belong to the 72-edge barycentric cube triangulation; they are assigned their specified lengths in the cheap spanning graph \(Q\), not added as parallel edges.

## Mathematical reduction checked

I rebuilt the cube triangulation from binary cube coordinates, separately from the target's oriented-face construction. The 26 vertices, 72 edges, 48 triangular faces, twice-incident edges, connected vertex links, and Euler count establish the sphere fixture used by the certificate. The 24 radial edges, six distributed shortcut edges, and nine marked degree-four edge centers agree with the target. The marked vertices are leaves in the 39-edge cheap graph \(Q\).

For each of the 14 radial vertices as source, I recomputed exact integer shortest distances and predecessor paths. Every baseline radial shortest path is unique. The directed reduced-cost expressions have 205 distinct nonzero rows for A and 199 for B; their minimum ratios \(L(p)/\sum_e|a_e|p_e\) are exactly \(1/57\) and \(1/32\). Every point of each closed box therefore lies in the closure of the strict cone preserving that baseline radial pattern. Along interpolation toward its baseline, every nonzero reduced cost becomes positive. Telescoping edge slacks proves uniqueness of each baseline radial path throughout the strict cone.

For positive shortcut lengths, a geodesic's maximal radial segment must be a radial geodesic. My independently generated simple-path catalogs retain every path with that property and contain 1,176 A routes and 1,226 B routes. These catalogs can contain paths that are not geodesics for a particular shortcut vector; this is harmless because the pair clauses test each selected path against all catalog rivals with the same core endpoints. End-mark parent edges occur on both candidate and rival paths and cancel from their length comparison. No simple core path can use a marked leaf internally.

The certificates encode the **unavailability** of each proposed pair by a wrong required parent or a strictly shorter core rival. Thus unsatisfiability of all unavailability clauses, together with exactly-one parent, positivity, strict radial-cone, and box constraints, proves that at least one listed four-residue pair works at every strict parameter point. I independently replayed the exact arithmetic and Boolean proof. For A, there are 47 templates, 393 base clauses, 599 affine atoms, 437 rational Farkas lemmas, and 48 RUP additions; for B, 67, 407, 659, 723, and 142. All rational weighted sums cancel their 30 variable coefficients and have negative constant, or zero constant with a strict summand. The latter occurs in 38 A lemmas and 82 B lemmas; it is essential that a negative atom denotes strict \(-f>0\), not weak \(-f\geq0\). Both RUP traces end with the empty clause. The four unit-case templates separately attain the all-zero-shortcut lower-bound pseudometric and leave full-triangulation residual mark counts \((4,4)\).

The extension from strict parameters to box boundaries and zero shortcuts is valid: for a fixed parent assignment and parent lengths, interpolate radial prices toward the box center and add \(t>0\) to each shortcut length. A simple pair recurs along a sequence \(t\downarrow0\). Its vertices and full-triangulation residual components are unchanged; continuity of finite-graph shortest-path distance makes it geodesic in the limiting \(Q\). The requirement \(\ell_T(uv)\geq d_Q(u,v)\) for each edge outside \(Q\) implies \(d_T=d_Q\) by replacing that edge in any \(T\) walk with a \(Q\) geodesic. Hence the same paths are ambient \(T\) geodesics. If the four heaviest marks have at least half the mass, two ambient geodesics pairing them give a separator directly. Otherwise every at-most-four-mark component has less than half the mass, so the certified four-residue pair suffices, including when total mass is zero.

## Literature status and mathematical potential

The official [Problem 31](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~pds/barbados26/problems.pdf) asks for two geodesics at one-half balance, while its stated two-geodesic theorem only supplies two-thirds balance. [Diot and Gavoille](https://emilie-diot.eu/Article/DG10a) prove related half-separation results for weighted planar graphs using three paths and for bounded-treewidth classes using fewer paths. A targeted search for the distinctive radial-box and distributed-shortcut formulation found no matching earlier source; absence from search is not priority evidence. Relative to the committed graph, these are genuine positive-dimensional metric regions beyond the previously reviewed isolated core price vectors. The construction is a meaningful stress test for weighted obstruction searches, with narrow support and isometry hypotheses that remain explicit. Its certificate and compact checker make a computational note reproducible; a formal statement of the route-completeness bridge in a paper would improve independent readability.

## Strengthening and improvement opportunities

**Proved relaxation of noncore edge positivity.** The result remains true when the nine parent-edge lengths are merely nonnegative, and every edge outside \(Q\) is merely nonnegative with length at least \(d_Q\). Each mark remains a graph-theoretic leaf of \(Q\), even when its incident edge has cost zero. That edge still cancels in comparisons with the same marked endpoint. Core route enumeration and residual topology are unchanged; shortest distances are still finite minima of simple-path costs; and the walk-replacement isometry argument permits zero cost. To make this a genuine new boundary case, choose mark 25's parent to be face center 11 with cost zero and set all six shortcuts to zero. The other triangulation edge \(25\!-!13\) may then also have cost zero, since \(11\!-!19\!-!12\!-!14\!-!10\!-!22\!-!13\) is a zero-cost \(Q\) route. The independent audit checks the relevant incidences. No extension to zero radial edges is claimed: their positivity and unique-path cone underlie the catalog.

**Broaden the proved metric region with a checkable bridge.** The certificates apply only where their baseline radial patterns and box clauses hold. A useful next step is to enumerate adjacent radial shortest-path chambers and supply a separate exact RUP/Farkas certificate, or a finite verified cover of the projectivized radial-price simplex. Sampling or solver `UNKNOWN` outside the boxes cannot establish such a claim. This is a research direction, not part of the present verdict.
