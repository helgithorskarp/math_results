# Proof of the centered-cover obstruction

All graphs are finite, simple and undirected. A triangle cover is a set
of edges meeting every triangle; its minimum size is `tau`. Triangle
packing always means edge-disjoint packing. In a specified split
partition `V(G)=C disjoint-union I`, a centered triangle has its unique
independent vertex in `I`. Write `nu_c` and `H=nu_c*` for the integer and
fractional centered packing optima. A fractional packing gives each
triangle nonnegative weight, with total weight at most one on each edge.

## 1. A nine-vertex gadget

Take `C={0,1,2,3}` as a clique and five independent vertices `a,b,c,d,e`
with respective neighborhoods

```text
a: 012,     b: 013,     c: 03,     d: 123,     e: 23.
```

There are 19 edges and 11 centered triangles. Giving each centered
triangle weight `1/2` is feasible: every graph edge belongs to at most
two such triangles. Thus `H>=11/2`.

Here is an upper certificate that needs no optimization theorem. Give
base edges `03` and `23` weight one, give spokes `a0,a1,a2` weight `1/2`,
and give spokes `b1,d1` weight one. All other edges have weight zero.
Every centered triangle has edge-weight sum at least one:

- An `a`-triangle contains two of the three half-weight spokes.
- A `b`-triangle contains `b1` or base edge `03`.
- The `c`-triangle contains `03`.
- A `d`-triangle contains `d1` or base edge `23`.
- The `e`-triangle contains `23`.

The total dual weight is `2+3/2+2=11/2`. Summing the edge-capacity
inequalities against these weights gives `H<=11/2`, hence equality.

Every integral triangle cover has size at least `ceil(H)=6`. To see
this directly, the total fractional weight of triangles hit by one
cover edge is at most one; sum over the cover edges. Deleting all six
clique edges is a cover, so `tau=6`.

The following five centered triangles are edge-disjoint:

```text
a02, b01, c03, d12, e23.
```

Consequently `nu_c=5`, since `nu_c<=floor(H)=5`. The full packing optimum
is also five. Indeed, if an arbitrary fractional packing has centered
mass `h` and clique-only mass `z`, then its clique-edge load gives
`h+3z<=6`. As `h<=11/2`,

```text
h+z <= (6+2h)/3 <= 17/3 < 6.
```

Thus an integral packing has at most five triangles. This last full
packing equality is only a gadget statement; we make no corresponding
claim about the full packing optimum of the larger family.

## 2. Amplification by a decomposition into four-vertex cliques

Suppose the edges of `K_k` are partitioned into `B` copies of `K_4`.
Necessarily `6B=q`, where `q=k(k-1)/2`. Give each block an arbitrary
ordering of its four vertices. At each block add five private independent
vertices with the five neighborhood patterns in Section 1. All these
added vertices are mutually nonadjacent, so the resulting graph is split.

The 19-edge gadget subgraphs are pairwise edge-disjoint. Distinct blocks
share at most one clique vertex, because otherwise they would share an
edge. Their center vertices and spokes are private.

Every centered triangle belongs to exactly one gadget. The centered
packing LP therefore separates into `B` independent copies of the
gadget LP. Both the primal and dual certificates concatenate, proving

```text
H = 11B/2 = 11q/12.
```

The integer centered packing optimum also adds, so `nu_c=5B=5q/6`.
In particular, `H-nu_c=B/2=q/12`.

Every triangle cover of the whole graph restricts to a triangle cover
of each gadget. Each restriction needs at least six edges. Because the
gadget edge sets are disjoint, these lower bounds add to `6B=q`.
Deleting the entire clique is a cover of size `q`; therefore

```text
tau = q.
```

There can be additional clique triangles that cross different blocks.
They cause no difficulty: the lower bound only uses the gadget triangles,
and deleting all clique edges covers these additional triangles as well.
We do not assume all graph triangles lie in gadgets.

Every center neighborhood has size two or three. Two different blocks
cannot produce the same neighborhood, since equality of such sets would
give a shared clique pair. The five neighborhoods within a block are
different. There are exactly `r=5B` types, all of multiplicity one. The
graph has `k+5B` vertices, `19B` edges, and `13B` spokes.

## 3. An explicit infinite supply of decompositions

Use the field `F_4=F_2[a]/(a^2+a+1)`; the polynomial has no root in `F_2`,
so this quotient is a field. For any integer `d>=1`, the vector space
`F_4^d` has `k=4^d` points. Its affine lines are sets

```text
{x+t v : t in F_4},       v != 0.
```

Each line has four points. For distinct points `x,y`, the unique line
containing both is `x+F_4(y-x)`: any line containing both has direction
space generated by their nonzero difference. Thus every unordered pair
lies on exactly one line. Taking the six pairs within each line gives
the required edge partition of `K_k`, with `B=k(k-1)/12`.

The implementation represents each field element by its two polynomial
bits, and each vector by consecutive pairs of bits. Addition is bitwise
XOR. It generates lines by normalized directions and translation cosets;
a second implementation generates lines from all point pairs using
polynomial multiplication. Checking pair incidence alone is also a
complete finite certificate for the amplification step.

## 4. The failed inequality and its quantitative failure

Consider the proposed bound

```text
Phi_k(H) = k^2/4-k/2+H-H^2/k^2+1/4.
```

For any `H`, put `delta=q-H`. A direct identity gives

```text
q-Phi_k(H) = delta/k + delta^2/k^2.
```

For the constructed family `delta=q/12=k(k-1)/24`, hence

```text
tau-Phi_k(H) = (k-1)/24 + (k-1)^2/576.
```

It is positive for every `k=4^d`. At `k=16` it is `65/64>1`, and it
increases with `k`. Thus even `tau<=ceil(Phi_k(H))` is false for every
`d>=2`. Moreover,

```text
lim_(d -> infinity) [tau-Phi_k(H)]/k^2 = 1/576.
```

Therefore no error term that is uniformly `o(k^2)` over all split graphs
can repair this proposed inequality.

The existing spoke-saturated theorem substitutes `H=E/2`, where `E` is
the number of spokes. Here `E/2=13B/2>11B/2=H`, so its hypothesis fails.
All multiplicities are already capped. This establishes a limitation of
removing saturation through this particular scalar cover bound, while
preserving the conditional theorem.

The construction has `r=5k(k-1)/12`, which is unbounded. It does not refute
a bound with an `O(rk)` error, an estimate with a constant depending on
fixed `r`, or the existence of an effective Tuza threshold for fixed
neighborhood type count. It also gives a centered packing of size
`5q/6`, so these graphs themselves satisfy `tau<=2nu` immediately.

## 5. Status of the evidence

Sections 1--4 are the complete unformalized proof. The exact checker
verifies the gadget certificates and directly enumerates its cover and
packing optima. For `d=1,2,3,4`, it checks complete pair partitions,
actual expanded graph edges, centered primal and dual certificates,
integer centered packings and the additive cover certificate. This
checks the implementation on four finite members; it is not a substitute
for the universal argument. No solver, numerical tolerance, unprovided
dataset or probabilistic assertion is a proof premise. Independent
review of this new obstruction is pending.
