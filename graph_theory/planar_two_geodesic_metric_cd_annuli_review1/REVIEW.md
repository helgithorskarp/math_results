## Verdict and exact scope

**Confirmed with high confidence under the stated induced, isometric core and attachment hypotheses.** For every positive metric satisfying the arc inequality \(\ell_j\geq d_0(\phi(c_j),\phi(c_{j+1}))+|s_j-s_{j+1}|\), the quantitative bound \(\max\{M/2,B\}\) follows from the two-pair sweep. The all-mass one-half corollary additionally requires each attachment torso to have treewidth at most three. The theorem allows arbitrary positive wheel and cross lengths, and arbitrary positive subdivisions of inner arcs. It does not cover A-steps, attachments at inner vertices, mass introduced by arbitrary outer or cross subdivisions, or nonisometric cores. Its unit-edge planar instances are a sufficient class for [Barbados 2026 Problem 31](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~pds/barbados26/problems.pdf), not a solution of the unrestricted one-half question. The known two-path two-thirds theorem does not imply this result.

## Proof audit

The metric factorization is exact. Replace a branch-to-branch inner arc by a wheel path between its parents. Inequality (1) makes that replacement no longer than the arc. To bound a branch \(c\) to a wheel vertex \(x\), consider the first cross exit at branch \(t\). The inner route pays at least the parent-distance changes plus the absolute changes in cross lengths; adding \(s_t\) pays at least \(s_c+d_0(\phi(c),\phi(t))\). The remaining wheel route costs at least \(d_0(\phi(t),x)\). The triangle inequality gives \(d_H(c,x)\geq s_c+d_0(\phi(c),x)\), and the direct cross edge followed by a wheel geodesic attains equality. Core isometry transfers this identity to the original graph. Following one inner arc toward the opposite parent in both directions proves that the displayed arc inequality is necessary for **this distance identity**; it is not asserted necessary for half balance.

The component bookkeeping includes every exterior component, even one of zero mass. After deleting \(S_0=\{r,a_0,c_0\}\), components attached only through \(r\) or \(a_0\) detach and cost at most \(B\). The remaining vertices partition into outer single-site groups \(\alpha_i\), inner branch groups \(\gamma_j\), outer sector groups \(\kappa_i\), and arc interiors \(\tau_j\). The sweep starts with formal masses \((L,R)=(0,M)\) and ends at \((M,0)\). At a state, two root-to-branch geodesics cut all core components into its formal left or right side; one attachment cannot bridge the cut because its active outer sites are one vertex or two consecutive vertices. Additional vertices of a chosen wheel geodesic only delete more. If no state already has both sides at most \(h=\max\{M/2,B\}\), monotonicity supplies a transition with old \(R>h\) and new \(L>h\); the opposite old and new sides are light.

At a C-step the two endpoint root distances differ by the cross-length difference. The weighted midpoint threshold \(\ell+t-s\) lies in \([0,2\ell]\), and its prefix and suffix cuts have indices \(q\leq p+1\). The corresponding root-to-arc paths are geodesic by the two-entry-point distance test. Their potentially heavy supports are disjoint subsets of the represented mass, so one cut works.

At a D-step, the anchor carrier \(E_a\) is a genuine original-graph geodesic from \(c_0\) to \(a\), even if a direct outer-wheel route from \(a_0\) to \(a\) is not shortest. When \(E_a\) includes \(r\), the second path starts at \(b\) and its suffix threshold is \(\ell+t-s-\delta\). Otherwise it starts at \(r\) through \(b\), with threshold \(z=\ell+t-s+R_b-R_a\). The reflected pair uses threshold \(\ell+t-s+\delta\) if \(E_b\) includes \(r\), or \(z\) otherwise. The exact branch-to-wheel distance identity proves shortestness in all four cases. Triangle inequality gives \(\ell+t-s-\delta\leq z\leq\ell+t-s+\delta\); inequality (1) puts these in \([0,2\ell]\). Thus the forward suffix index \(q\) is at most the backward prefix index \(p+1\). The first pair leaves only the new light right side or \(L\cup\gamma_c\cup\) the early arc interior. The second leaves only old light \(L\) or \(R\cup\gamma_d\cup\) the late arc interior. The two enlarged supports are disjoint and together have mass at most \(M\), so they cannot both exceed \(M/2\). Sector attachments at \(ab\) detach individually after both outer endpoints and \(r\) are deleted. This verifies the part of the argument that the earlier four-cut method did not handle.

The heavy-attachment corollary uses a separate, valid reduction. A boundary contained in \(\{r,a_i,a_{i+1}\}\) is a clique of size at most three. A width-three torso decomposition has a bag containing it. Attaching one outside bag and taking a weighted centroid gives a local bag of at most four vertices whenever \(w(K)>W/2\); the outside leaf cannot be a centroid in that case. Two ambient geodesics pairing the bag vertices contain it, and deleting more vertices preserves balance. If all attachments are light, the quantitative bound is at most \(W/2\). This is the [previously reviewed heavy-attachment argument](../planar_two_geodesic_prescribed_attachment_review1/REVIEW.md), used here without a global treewidth-four claim.

## Literature and mathematical potential

[Diot and Gavoille](https://emilie-diot.eu/Article/DG10a) give half separators with three paths for weighted planar graphs and two-path results under stronger structural hypotheses, including treewidth at most three and face separability. The included D⁵ weighting has treewidth at least four and no facial half separator in any embedding, so those two mechanisms do not explain this example. This does not separate the theorem from every known sufficient class. A targeted search for the distinctive metric C/D annulus exchange found no matching primary source; absence from search cannot establish historical priority. Relative to the committed graph, the theorem materially extends the unit annulus work along the metric axis while restricting the incidence pattern to C/D. Its two-pair repair is a useful reusable method when one fixed-anchor quadrilateral repair fails. A paper should isolate the component-containment statement for attachments as a formal lemma, since that is the least local step of the proof.

## Strengthening and improvement opportunities

**Proved nonnegative-length extension.** Positivity of edge lengths is not needed for the quantitative sweep or heavy-attachment corollary if shortest paths are defined as simple minimum-cost paths. Allow every wheel, cross, arc, and outside edge to have **nonnegative** length, retain the same arc inequality, core isometry, induced-core topology, and attachment boundaries. The projection and branch-to-wheel arguments use only triangle inequalities and nonnegative costs. Finite graphs still have simple shortest paths. Zero-length ties do not affect the two-entry-point comparison; even if several arc vertices share a coordinate, taking the first index above and last index below each threshold still yields \(q\leq p+1\). Component containment and centroid reasoning depend on the graph topology, not positive costs. This genuinely adds cases: in the D⁵ control, making the edge from \(r\) to the first sector attachment cost zero while leaving its two outer incidences at four preserves all core distances; the independent audit checks that every displayed candidate remains geodesic. Negative lengths are excluded, and arbitrary nonisometric attachments remain outside the conclusion.

**Next mathematical bridge.** The arc inequality exactly characterizes the displayed branch-to-wheel distance identity, so weakening it requires new candidate-distance formulas, not just a different mass sweep. A focused extension would admit one inner attachment or one A-step and prove a replacement component-containment lemma for the extra incidence. The D⁵ control should be retained as a regression: it rules out restoring the old four fixed-anchor candidates merely by adjusting a weighted midpoint.
