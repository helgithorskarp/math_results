# Sources and status boundary

Checked on 2026-09-22.

1. M. Behzad, G. Chartrand, and J. K. Cooper Jr., *The Colour Numbers of
   Complete Graphs*, Journal of the London Mathematical Society s1-42(1)
   (1967), 226--228.  <https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/s1-42.1.226>

   This is classical source context for total colouring of complete graphs.
   The cyclic one-factorization used here is standard; the present proof
   supplies the particular rainbow perfect matchings it needs explicitly.

2. Fernanda Couto, Diego Amaro Ferraz, and Sulamita Klein, *New Results on
   Edge-coloring and Total-coloring of Split Graphs*, Discrete Applied
   Mathematics 360 (2024), 297--306.
   <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dam.2024.09.008>
   Author manuscript: <https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.05723>

   The paper classifies Type 1 versus Type 2 for stretch-index-two split
   graphs and gives colouring algorithms.  Its conclusion identifies the
   stretch-index-three class as the remaining split-graph frontier.  For
   split graphs without pendant vertices, its cited characterization says
   stretch index two is equivalent to the presence of a universal vertex.
   Thick spiders of rank at least three have neither, so they are not covered
   by that classification.

3. J. K. Dugdale and A. J. W. Hilton, *The Total Chromatic Number of Regular
   Graphs Whose Complement Is Bipartite*, Discrete Mathematics 126 (1994),
   87--98.  <https://doi.org/10.1016/0012-365X(94)90255-0>

   This is directly adjacent prior work for regular graphs under a stronger
   structural hypothesis on the complement.  The theorem here is not
   restricted to regular graphs or bipartite complements.  Its sharp
   conclusion applies when the maximum degree happens to be `n-2`.

## Search boundary

Primary and bibliographic searches used the combinations `total coloring
complement perfect matching`, `total chromatic number complete graph minus
matching`, `total coloring thick spider`, `total coloring split graph`, and
`rainbow perfect matching one-factorization`.  No source stating the
complement-perfect-matching upper bound or the thick-spider formula was
located.  This is a bounded, search-relative novelty statement, not a claim
of historical priority.  The construction is elementary and independent
rediscovery is plausible.
