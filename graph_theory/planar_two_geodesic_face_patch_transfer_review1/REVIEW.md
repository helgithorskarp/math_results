## Verdict and exact scope

**Confirmed with high confidence.** The abstract three-pair quotient criterion gives the claimed \(\max(M/2,B)\) bound after arbitrary patch substitution, provided the three candidate pairs remain ambient geodesics. For the stated icosahedral core price box, the exact potential certificate and the local boundary-distance condition ensure that shortestness. If each face-patch torso has treewidth at most three, the previously reviewed heavy-torso argument upgrades the bound to one-half for every nonnegative vertex mass. Recursive face insertions yield planar examples of unbounded order. These are conditional positive classes for [Barbados 2026 Problem 31](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~pds/barbados26/problems.pdf), not a general resolution; the established two-path two-thirds result is insufficient for this target.

## Mathematical audit

Let the quotient have core vertices and independent patch atoms. When an expanded component survives deletion of a core path pair, replacing every patch vertex by its atom maps the component into **one** quotient component: each expanded edge projects to a quotient edge or collapses inside one atom. If a quotient component is a singleton atom, its expanded mass is at most the largest patch mass \(B\); if it lies in \(H_i\), its mass is at most the aggregate quotient mass \(\mu(H_i)\). Put \(\tau=\max(M/2,B)\). If each of the first two pairs fails, then \(\mu(H_1),\mu(H_2)>\tau\). Each third-pair component is either one atom, of mass at most \(B\), or disjoint from at least one \(H_i\), of mass at most \(M-\mu(H_i)<M-\tau\le\tau\). This proves the quantitative claim for all nonnegative real masses, including ties and zero total, with no planarity or treewidth requirement. The three pairs are alternatives; their six paths are never combined into a separator.

For the icosahedral quotient, direct recomputation gives first-pair partitions of 11 and six components, with unique nonsingleton supports of orders 13 and 21; the third has seven components, two of orders 13 and six. Those two supports are disjoint respectively from the first pair's heavy support and the second pair's heavy support. The predecessor's 1,112-byte certificate gives one potential for each of six core paths. At each path's adverse box corner, all 30 edge-potential inequalities hold and the endpoint potential difference equals the path length. Thus each path is shortest at that corner. For any competing path, common edges cancel; assigning high prices to the candidate's exclusive edges and low prices to all other edges maximizes its length disadvantage. Shortestness therefore holds throughout the entire closed 30-dimensional box, not just at sampled metrics.

The local boundary condition \(d_{T_f}(u,v)\ge d_I(u,v)\) is equivalent to core isometry for these face-contained, mutually nonadjacent patches. Necessity follows because each torso path is a full-graph path. For sufficiency, replace every excursion through one patch on a core-to-core walk by a core geodesic between its boundary endpoints; the replacement is no longer than the excursion. This gives a core walk no longer than the original full-graph path, so no core distance decreases. The six core paths are consequently ambient geodesics, even when equal-length ties occur.

If one patch has mass greater than half the total, attach an outside bag containing all vertices outside that patch to a width-three decomposition of its boundary-clique torso. The outside bag has mass less than half, so a weighted centroid of the decomposition tree lies in a local bag of at most four vertices. The tree-decomposition separator property half-balances the **whole** graph after deleting that bag. Pair its vertices and take at most two ambient shortest paths; these may leave the torso, but their union contains the bag, so deleting them only shrinks residual components. This is the cited heavy-torso lemma, whose hypotheses match the face patch setting. It does not assume that a chosen core path can be retained during repair.

For the recursive family, each round replaces every current interior triangle by three triangles, giving \((3^{d_f}-1)/2\) interior vertices in face \(f\), so the displayed \(N,E,F\) formulas follow by induction and Euler's formula. A bag of each new vertex and its three insertion corners, linked to the parent triangle bag, gives width three locally. Giving core-incident new edges length greater than the core diameter protects all core distances, regardless of positive internal lengths. The all-order result therefore follows from the written substitution and torso proofs, not finite enumeration. A single bag holding all twelve core vertices joined to the local decompositions shows global treewidth at most eleven; no unbounded-treewidth claim is made.

The claimed separation from the four-vertex connected-representative guard is also scoped correctly. The icosahedral core stays connected after any four core-vertex deletions. At most \(4-k\) patch interiors are directly hit and at most \(\binom{k}{3}\) faces lose all three corners when \(k\) core vertices are removed, leaving at least 16 intact attached patches. In a selected spanning tree of the large residual component, each intact patch contributes a leaf outside the core. Two internal simple paths cover at most four tree leaves. This only rules out that sufficient guard test, not all prior positive classes.

## Literature and mathematical potential

The [official Problem 31](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~pds/barbados26/problems.pdf) poses the one-half two-path question; [Diot and Gavoille](https://emilie-diot.eu/Article/DG10a) give weighted path-separability and treewidth-three background. A targeted primary-source search did not identify this exact three-pair face-patch transfer; that is no historical-priority claim. Relative to the committed graph, this extends the 32-vertex price-region example to unbounded-order planar patch families while reusing its six-path certificate. The general quotient lemma is a concise, reusable part of the result. A publication should keep that abstract transfer separate from the numeric icosahedral instance and cite the earlier heavy-torso reduction. The family has bounded treewidth and restricted metrics, so it does not approach an unrestricted planar proof by itself.

## Strengthening and improvement opportunities

**Proved path-specific metric extension, with strict example.** The abstract quotient proof requires only that the six displayed core paths remain ambient geodesics; full core isometry is a convenient sufficient test. Thus the face-patch half-balance corollary still holds for any positive patch metric, with width-three torsos, that preserves just those six paths. This is strictly broader. In the independent 52-vertex model at center core prices \(151c_e\), keep all patch edges long except the two edges from face-0 interior vertex 12 to core vertices 0 and 1, each of length 10,000. The core distance \(d_I(0,1)=28,237\) drops to \(d_G(0,1)=20,000\), violating local condition (2), yet an independent all-pairs distance check confirms all six displayed paths remain ambient geodesics. The quotient topology and width-three torsos are unchanged, so the all-real-mass conclusion follows from the same three-pair and heavy-torso proofs. This permits genuine core shortcuts, though it no longer gives the convenient facewise boundary-distance certificate.

**Smaller atom bound for disconnected expansions.** In the abstract quotient theorem, a residual component that projects to a singleton atom lies inside one connected component of that atom's induced patch. Therefore \(B\) may be replaced by the largest mass of an individual induced patch component. This is a proved quantitative sharpening when a substituted patch is disconnected; it makes no difference for the connected face patches used in the planar corollary.
