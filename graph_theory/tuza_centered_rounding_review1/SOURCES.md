# Primary literature checked for this review

Checked live on 24 September 2026.

- J. Misra and D. Gries,
  [*A constructive proof of Vizing's theorem*](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~misra/psp.dir/vizing.pdf),
  Information Processing Letters 41 (1992), 131--133.  This supplies the
  constructive `Delta+1` edge coloring invoked by the target.  The universal
  proof only needs Vizing's existence theorem.
- G. J. Puleo,
  [*Maximal k-Edge-Colorable Subgraphs, Vizing's Theorem, and Tuza's
  Conjecture*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.07017), Discrete Mathematics 340
  (2017), 1573--1580.  It explicitly observes that centered triangle
  packings in a one-neighborhood join `I_k join H` correspond to partial
  `k`-edge colorings of `H`, and proves Tuza for a related special class.  It
  does not state the target's multi-type fractional LP rounding bound.  This
  is relevant prior context missing from the target's source list.
- M. Bonamy et al.,
  [*Tuza's Conjecture for Threshold Graphs*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.09871),
  DMTCS 24(1), 2022.  This proves Tuza for threshold graphs and uses complete
  split matching/edge-coloring constructions, not the present arbitrary
  multi-type centered relaxation.
- L. Chahua and J. Gutierrez,
  [*On Tuza's Conjecture in Dense Graphs*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.11409),
  proves Tuza for split graphs under the minimum-degree condition
  `delta(G)>=3|V(G)|/5`.  That density hypothesis is different from spoke
  saturation.
- Z. Zeng,
  [*Tuza's Conjecture for Split Graphs with an Eight-Vertex Clique Part and
  Two Neighborhood Types*](https://www.preprints.org/manuscript/202608.1304),
  is a 2026 non-peer-reviewed preprint giving an exact finite theorem at
  clique order eight.  It does not give a multiplicity-independent additive
  fractional rounding estimate.

Targeted searches combined “centered triangle packing,” “fractional
rounding,” “split graph,” “neighborhood types,” “edge coloring,” and the
constant form of the bound.  They did not locate the same multi-type theorem
or conditional numerical cutoff.  This supports only an apparently-new,
search-relative assessment; it cannot certify historical priority.
