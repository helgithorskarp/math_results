## Verdict and exact scope

**Confirmed with high confidence.** For a connected induced cactus, testing the intact graph after independently deleting either no edge or one edge of each cycle is necessary and sufficient for an internal \(k\)-geodesic cover of every surviving fragment component in every spanning edge subgraph. The number of tests is exactly \(\prod_i(1+m_i)\) for the cactus cycles of lengths \(m_i\). This is a terminal criterion, not a global half-separator theorem. The four-vertex guard gives a sufficient one-half separator class under its additional hypotheses; it does not resolve the unrestricted planar question in [Barbados 2026 Problem 31](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~pds/barbados26/problems.pdf). The established two-path two-thirds theorem does not imply the desired one-half conclusion.

## Mathematical audit

The bridge restriction lemma is sound for an arbitrary connected induced fragment \(F=G[C]\). If one deletes only bridges of \(F\) internally, a component \(D\) of the surviving \(H[C]\) is a union of whole bridge blocks. An internal simple path \(P\) cannot meet \(D\), leave through a deleted bridge, and later re-enter \(D\): that bridge separates \(F\), and a return would have to repeat it. Thus \(P\cap D\) is empty or a contiguous retained subpath. When \(P\) was an ambient \(G\)-geodesic, its subpath is also geodesic; deleting further edges outside \(F\) or bridges inside \(F\) cannot decrease endpoint distance. Intersections of the original \(k\) covering paths therefore cover each \(D\) with at most \(k\) ambient \(H\)-geodesics. The argument uses both contiguity and metric monotonicity; either alone would be insufficient.

In a cactus, deleting at most one edge per cycle and no original bridge preserves connectivity. If an arbitrary \(H\) deletes edges of a cycle, select one such edge for the representative \(A_\sigma\). Every further missing edge on that now-broken cycle is a bridge in \(F-A_\sigma\); original cactus bridges remain bridges. Cycles may share articulation vertices, but they share no edges and their block structure supplies no alternate route around an edge of a broken cycle. Apply the bridge lemma in \(G-A_\sigma\), then delete the remaining internal bridges and any exterior edges to reach \(H\). This proves sufficiency. Necessity tests \(H=G-A_\sigma\), whose fragment is connected, so the all-spanning hypothesis supplies the required full-vertex cover. The cycle choices are independent, giving the stated product count. For a tree the product is one, exactly recovering the previously reviewed [induced-tree persistence theorem](../planar_two_geodesic_initial_tree_cover_review1/REVIEW.md).

For one induced cycle and \(k=2\), the empty representative checks an intact internal two-arc cover. Each one-edge deletion produces an induced path, so the reviewed [exact edge-window criterion](../planar_two_geodesic_edge_window_characterization_review1/REVIEW.md) applies using **full ambient distances in \(G-e\)**. This gives the advertised finite distance version. Testing only the intact cycle would be insufficient: the ten-cycle with a hub at ports \(0,3,8\) has an intact internal two-cover but fails after deleting edge \(9\!-!0\). That failure concerns internal full coverage; the earlier star certificate separately proves a stronger failure allowing exterior endpoints. Neither statement is an unrestricted planar half-separator counterexample.

The guard corollary uses the established four-vertex heavy-component descent. If a pair covering the guard leaves a heavy residual, that residual lies in a surviving component of one specified cactus. The representative theorem covers the whole component by two internal ambient geodesics, so replacement removes the heavy mass. Components of order at most four are covered by pairing vertices in arbitrary positive-edge hosts; the order-five allowance uses the separate unit-edge planar rule. This conclusion is weighted, all-spanning, and conditional on the guard decomposition.

A targeted search found no matching primary source for this exact cactus representative reduction; that does not establish priority. ## Strengthening and improvement opportunities

**Proved connected-representative theorem for any induced fragment.** Let \(F=G[C]\) be any connected induced graph with \(m\) edges and cycle rank \(\beta=m-|C|+1\). The all-spanning internal \(k\)-cover property is equivalent to testing an internal \(k\)-geodesic cover of all \(C\) in every \(G-A\) for which \(A\subseteq E(F)\) and \(F-A\) remains connected. Necessity takes \(H=G-A\). For sufficiency, given arbitrary deleted internal edges, repeatedly select a currently nonbridge missing edge into \(A\). Each selection preserves connectivity and lowers the cycle rank by one, so at most \(\beta\) selections occur. At termination every remaining missing internal edge is a bridge of \(F-A\); the bridge restriction lemma transfers the tested cover to every surviving component. Thus at most \(\sum_{j=0}^{\beta}\binom{m}{j}\) connected hosts need be considered, a polynomial count in \(m\) for fixed \(\beta\). For cacti, the connected choices are exactly no edge or one edge on each cycle, recovering the target's product. The independent audit checks the greedy bridge reduction for every one of 32 deletion sets of a noncactus diamond: 14 connected representatives suffice. This broadening may be costly for high cycle rank and is not claimed to be a minimal test family.

**Nonnegative edge lengths.** The bridge and connected-representative proofs remain valid with nonnegative edge lengths when geodesics are simple minimum-cost paths. Subpath optimality and monotonicity under edge deletion do not require strict positivity. This refinement does not change the combinatorial representative count or relax the induced-fragment requirement in the guard application.
