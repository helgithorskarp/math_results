# Independent review: forest components outside a closed neighborhood

## Target and verdict

Target: Discovery Net lemma `bafkreigkllwnpltqbrgb4yqsgl24krbvgrlt4vrgp33owghd6vypaqlq2e`, *Forest nonneighbors admit two-geodesic half balance, with a tree-or-clique extension*. The [written proof](../planar_two_geodesic_forest_non_neighbors/README.md) entered the public repository in commit `b23b9f831a88f04dac3a3413c6f4e13f44aa3d3d`.

**Verdict: confirmed with high confidence at paper-proof level.** For a finite simple planar unit-edge graph, if some vertex \(r\) has a forest induced by its nonneighbors, every nonnegative real vertex-mass assignment has a half-balanced separator contained in two geodesics of the *original* graph. The written construction gives a width-four tree decomposition with every bag covered by those two ambient paths. Its mixed tree-or-clique corollary follows using the already reviewed clique-patch certificate and has width at most five. These statements do not settle [Barbados 2026 Problem 31](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~pds/barbados26/problems.pdf) for general planar graphs or for arbitrary edge lengths.

## Structural proof audit

Contracting each connected component outside \(N[r]\) produces independent centers attached to \(B=N(r)\). Replacing a center's star by its cyclic neighbor polygon keeps each multi-neighbor component in a separate planar patch. A parallel-edge lens away from \(r\) cannot contain a third vertex of \(B\), because that vertex's edge to \(r\) would cross the lens. Components trapped in a repeated-spoke lens attach at only one boundary vertex. After suppressing these regions, the graph on \(B\) is outerplane, and an original edge between vertices of a patch boundary cannot be a chord: it would separate a connected component from one of its boundary neighbors. This is the first noncomputational embedding step.

For a tree patch, a regular neighborhood of the tree is a disk. The noncrossing spokes preserve cyclic port order between its boundary and the neighbor polygon; all ports with the same destination are consecutive. Removing a tree edge splits its source ports into complementary intervals. The proof's endpoint-persistence rule is sound: eliminated vertices are strictly inside a leaf interval, while intervals of contracted subtrees relative to a later original edge cut are nested or disjoint. Such a deleted destination cannot become an extreme destination of that later cut. This preserves the original cut used when a boundary edge is present.

Eliminating a current tree leaf produces only five-vertex fan bags \(\{r,u,a,t,b\}\) and tree-edge bags \(\{r,u,v,w,z\}\), or smaller bags. The fill edges, including virtual spokes from \(r\) to the inner tree, are used solely for the decomposition. A size-five fan bag with two nonadjacent boundary vertices has the ambient geodesic through \(r\); if its three boundary vertices form an actual triangle, the no-chord property forces that triangle to be the entire neighbor polygon, and every \(u\)-to-\(r\) path meets it. A size-five tree-edge bag with missing \(wz\) has the geodesic \(w-r-z\) and original edge \(uv\). If \(wz\) is actual, one side of the original tree-edge cut has no external neighbor beyond \(w,z\), so \(\{y,w,z\}\) separates its endpoint \(x\) from \(r\). Any ambient shortest \(x-r\) path then contains three bag vertices. Pairing the remaining two covers the bag in every case. This step never treats a virtual spoke as a metric edge.

The local elimination bags form a tree decomposition by the standard first-later-neighbor rule. Each boundary edge has a bag containing its interface \(\{r,a,b\}\); gluing local trees along the weak dual of the completed outerplane dissection preserves connected vertex occurrences. A weighted centroid bag halves all nonnegative real masses, and deleting its covering paths only splits or shrinks remaining components. In a disconnected graph, a heavy component away from \(r\) is a tree and has a one-vertex centroid. For the mixed corollary, clique patches use the prior 751-case certificate; a heavy disconnected planar clique has at most four vertices and is covered by two edges or singletons.

## Reproduction and independent stress audit

The source command `PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 python3 graph_theory/planar_two_geodesic_forest_non_neighbors/verify.py --check` passed with Python 3.11.2: 8,089 fixtures, 86,313 bags, 49,781 five-vertex bags, 1,518 mass checks, one glued fixture, and five rejected controls. Its checker independently recomputes elimination bags from prefix reachability and checks path distances and decomposition axioms; the fixture family is not exhaustive over all plane trees.

Run `PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 python3 graph_theory/planar_two_geodesic_forest_non_neighbors_review1/audit.py` from the repository root. This separate audit generates 1,200 seeded plane trees with independently randomized rotation systems, traces each thickened-tree boundary word, assigns noncrossing contiguous destination ports, and chooses boundary edges independently. It asks the source constructor for candidate bags, then uses its **own** graph builder, BFS, component traversal, decomposition check, and weighted-centroid check. No case failed: 20,277 generated-case bags, including 9,153 five-vertex bags, 38,154 geodesic checks, and 3,600 mass assignments. The generated cases exercised 13 triangle fan bags and 1,067 adjacent-endpoint tree-edge bags. The separately rebuilt 16-vertex strict example also passed: minimum degree four, maximum bag size five, no root with clique-component nonneighbors, and no dominating edge. Including that example, the audit reports 20,293 bags, 9,165 five-vertex bags, 38,184 geodesics, and 3,603 mass checks.

The audit deliberately reuses only the *candidate elimination* produced by the source implementation; it independently constructs its inputs and checks all resulting mathematical properties. It is a stress test of the local construction, not an exhaustive planar-graph census or a formal proof of the port-order topology.

## Literature and publication assessment

[Chaplick, Da Lozzo, Di Giacomo, Liotta, and Montecchiani](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.08124) prove the cycle-tree treewidth-three bound in their Theorem 4 (numbering of the arXiv version). Thus treewidth alone is prior art; the ambient-geodesic cover of the root-augmented five-bags is the substantive extra ingredient. [Diot and Gavoille (2010)](https://emilie-diot.eu/Article/DG10a) give the weighted half-separator framework and a treewidth-three two-path baseline. A targeted primary-source search did not locate this exact forest-nonneighbor theorem; that does not establish priority. The proof is suitable as a scoped preprint result after ordinary external scrutiny of the patch-contraction and endpoint-persistence lemmas. Its universal quantifiers rest on the written proof, not the regression counts.

## Strengthening and improvement opportunities

1. **Make the topology reusable.** State the parallel-lens suppression and nested-port endpoint persistence as explicit plane-graph lemmas with rotation-system hypotheses. Those are the two remaining paper-level trust boundaries and would support a formal proof or reuse in a larger local-component class.
2. **Address the first untreated components.** The mixed corollary implies that a counterexample must have, outside every closed neighborhood, a cyclic nonclique component. The smallest possibilities include a four-cycle, a triangle with a pendant vertex, and a diamond. Extending the patch method to one of these requires a new elimination rule and an ambient two-geodesic cover for its resulting bags; the forest port order alone does not supply either.
3. **Keep metric scope explicit.** The induced two-edge paths through \(r\) are automatically shortest because every edge has length one. An edge-weighted extension needs a replacement metric argument or different bag covers; the present proof does not imply it.
