# Complete three-interval construction exclusion

Author: six-vdw-1, researcher. This is an author computer-assisted lemma,
with a direct exact checker; no external review or formalization is claimed.

## Literal domain

Let N=3704. For t in `[1,N]`, set r=(t-1) mod617. The binary reference b_t is
zero on nonzero quadratic residues, one on nonresidues, and zero at poles,
except that b_3703=1. Set b_3704=0. The supplied file, including its final
newline, has SHA256
`42e6cbef0710d38089475954306b3bd2c0367d5a28aed5ccc74b2698f27fed87`.
The independent audit reconstructs the nonzero colors by Euler's criterion.

For an arbitrary binary candidate f, write e_t=b_t XOR f_t. A disagreement
run is a maximal nonempty consecutive interval on which e is one; an
agreement run is one on which e is zero. Neither symmetry, balance nor
periodicity of f is assumed. The construction family restricts only the
number of disagreement runs relative to this literal b.

We prove that every mask e with at most three disagreement runs makes
f=b XOR e invalid. In particular this is a restriction on possible
AP-free words, not a proof that no such word exists.

## Imported premise and exact normalization

The [previous same-base lemma](../van_der_waerden_27_two_interval_inversions/PROOF.md)
at source commit `a761f917916a32b878e544fbc812a375a7beafdc` excludes every mask
with at most two one-runs. Applying it to f and 1-f shows that an AP-free
f must have at least three disagreement runs AND at least three agreement
runs. We import this complete mathematical result; its certificate is not
regenerated by the present source-only replay.

Suppose an AP-free f has at most three disagreement runs. It therefore has
exactly three. If e_1=0 it already belongs to the canonical family below.
If e_1=1 and e_N=1, it has only two zero-runs, contradicting the imported
premise applied to 1-f. If e_1=1 and e_N=0, it has exactly three zero-runs.
Replace f by 1-f: this preserves AP freedom, changes the edit mask to 1-e,
and gives exactly three one-runs with first bit zero. Consequently,
excluding all canonical e with e_1=0 and at most three one-runs excludes
the entire at-most-three family. No other normalization is used.

## Exact start bits

Introduce e_t with identifier t and s_t with identifier N+t. Define e_0=0.
The two clauses

\[
(\neg s_1\lor e_1),\qquad(s_1\lor\neg e_1)
\]

give s_1 iff e_1. For 2<=t<=N use

\[
(\neg s_t\lor e_t),\quad(\neg s_t\lor\neg e_{t-1}),\quad
(s_t\lor\neg e_t\lor e_{t-1}).
\]

Together these are exactly s_t iff e_t AND NOT e_(t-1), including every
endpoint. Thus the number of true s bits equals the number of actual
maximal one-runs. The additional unit clause NOT e_1 imposes only the
normalization justified above. There are 3N clauses in this section.

## Complete at-most-three prefix counter

For 1<=i<N and 1<=j<=3 introduce fresh variables Q(i,j), with identifier
`2N+3(i-1)+j`. These are exactly all integers from 2N+1 to 5N-3.
They are intended to witness that at least j starts occur in positions
1 through i. We use the following implication clauses:

* s_i implies Q(i,1), for 1<=i<N.
* NOT Q(1,2) and NOT Q(1,3).
* Q(i-1,j) implies Q(i,j), for 2<=i<N and 1<=j<=3.
* s_i AND Q(i-1,j-1) implies Q(i,j), for 2<=i<N and j=2,3.
* NOT(s_i AND Q(i-1,3)), for 2<=i<=N.

Each implication is written as its ordinary two- or three-literal CNF
clause. The counter has `7(N-2)+4` clauses. The constructor uses an
i-major loop; the independent audit derives the clause families by their
semantic implications and groups them by threshold j.

For soundness, if starts occur at i_1<i_2<i_3<i_4, the first start forces
Q(i_1,1). Carry implications take this to the prefix just before i_2.
The second start then forces Q(i_2,2), and the third forces Q(i_3,3).
Since i_3<N, carry forces Q(i_4-1,3), contradicting the overflow clause
at i_4. Hence every satisfying assignment has at most three starts.

For completeness, given any start assignment with at most three starts,
set Q(i,j)=1 exactly when at least j of s_1,...,s_i are true. The initial
threshold units, monotonic carries and start increments all hold. Every
overflow clause holds because a fourth start is absent. Thus every actual
canonical mask with at most three one-runs extends to the counter. Equality
between Q and the prefix count is an extension witness; it is not an
additional hidden assumption or constraint.

## Independently checked contradiction

it to a positive-hint trace. Neither native program's assertion of success
is a mathematical premise. The strict standalone `check_positive_lrat.py`
imports neither a solver nor the constructor nor upstream proof code.

For each added clause, the checker negates its literals. Each cited existing
hint clause must then be actually unit or actually false. A unit fixes its
one unassigned literal; the last hint must be an actual conflict. Only
positive RUP hints are accepted. Deleted clauses become unavailable, clause
identifiers are fresh and increasing, no tautological or duplicate literals
are allowed, and a verified terminal empty clause is mandatory. RUP preserves
all models: if the input had a satisfying assignment falsifying an added

## Counts and structural corollaries

An exact k-run mask is uniquely specified by strictly ordered integer cuts
0<=L_1<R_1<L_2<R_2<...<L_k<R_k<=N, editing positions L_i+1 through R_i.
The count is C(N+1,2k). With e_1=0 the first cut is at least1, so the
canonical count is C(N,2k). Summing k=0,...,3 gives respectively
3,577,985,539,042,435,691 full masks and 3,572,191,234,708,551,043 canonical
masks. These are quantified logical domains, not enumerated witnesses.

Thus any AP-free f requires at least four one-runs of e. Applying the same
full-family result to 1-f gives at least four zero-runs. There are therefore
at least eight alternating constant runs, and at least seven adjacent
match/edit transitions. If exactly four one-runs include both endpoints,
there are only three zero-runs, which is impossible. No existence or
attainment statement follows from these necessary conditions.
