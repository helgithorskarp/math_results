## Primary literature

- Nicole Berline and Michèle Vergne, *Local Euler--Maclaurin formula for
  polytopes*, Moscow Mathematical Journal 7 (2007), 355--386;
  [arXiv:math/0507256](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0507256).  The imported
  local theorem uses their cone identity, translation invariance, local
  assembly, and affine-period bound, specifically Theorems 19(d), 20(a,e),
  and Corollary 30(a,b) in the cited version.

- Christian Haase and Tyrrell B. McAllister, *Quasi-period collapse and
  `GL_n(Z)`-scissors congruence in rational polytopes*;
  [arXiv:0709.4070](https://arxiv.org/abs/0709.4070).  Their Theorem 2.2
  records denominator-`D`, period-one polygons for every `D>=2`, and their
  geometric explanation uses piecewise unimodular rearrangements.

- Tyrrell B. McAllister and Matthew Moriarity, *Ehrhart quasi-period
  collapse in rational polygons*;
  [arXiv:1509.03680](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.03680).  This develops
  polygonal pseudo-integral polytopes and broader constructions and
  constraints.

- Tyrrell B. McAllister and Kevin M. Woods, *The minimum period of the
  Ehrhart quasi-polynomial of a rational polytope*;
  [arXiv:math/0310255](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0310255).  This is the
  original source for the arbitrary-denominator period-collapse family
  discussed by Haase--McAllister.

## Literature boundary

Bounded searches on 2026-09-21 covered rational polygonal PIPs, arbitrary
denominator period collapse, local Euler--Maclaurin formulas, and
Fourier--Dedekind methods.  Period-one denominator-five polygons are not
new, and no such priority is claimed.

The scoped advance is instead the compatibility theorem exposed by the
Discovery Net review: all four equal-weight diagonal profiles in the
prime-index local formula occur simultaneously in one polygon, so the
formula's codimension-two cancellation is geometrically realizable.  No
matching normal-fan realization or parametric octagon was found in the
bounded primary-source search.  Equivalent constructions may exist under
different coordinates or methods.
