# Copy permutations of ordinal powers: effective h*, ineffective gamma

Let P be a nonempty finite poset on N elements. Write C(P) for its chain
polytope, and define its ordinary Ehrhart numerator h by

    F_P(t) = sum_(m>=0) |m C(P) intersect Z^N| t^m
           = h(t)/(1-t)^(N+1).

Let Q=P^(oplus k) be the ordinal sum of k identical copies: all elements in
an earlier copy precede all elements in a later copy. The symmetric group
S_k permutes the copies of the coordinate space. This preserves C(Q), since
it preserves the comparability graph, although nonidentity copy permutations
are not automorphisms of the ordinal-sum poset.

For a coordinate action rho, our equivariant Ehrhart normalization is

    h*_g(t) = det(I-t (1 direct-sum rho(g)))
              sum_(m>=0) |(m C(Q) intersect Z^(kN))^g| t^m.       (1)

The first summand in the determinant is the fixed homogenizing direction.
An effective character is the character of an actual complex representation;
a permutation character is a stronger property. Negative character values
alone do not disprove effectiveness: negative irreducible multiplicities do.

## 1. The cycle-product formula

**Theorem 1.** If g in S_k has cycle lengths l_1,...,l_c, then

    h*_g(t) = product_(i=1)^c h(t^(l_i)).                       (2)

In particular, the equivariant h* is a polynomial with permutation-character
coefficients, for every finite P, without a gradedness assumption.

**Proof.** For a nonnegative integer vector x on P put

    M(x) = max {sum_(p in C) x_p : C is a chain of P}.

This is a nonnegative integer. Write E(a)=|a C(P) intersect Z^N|,
E(-1)=0, and B(a)=E(a)-E(a-1). Exactly B(a) vectors have M(x)=a, and

    sum_(a>=0) B(a) u^a = (1-u) F_P(u).                       (3)

A maximal chain in Q consists of one maximal chain in each copy. Choices
of chains in different copies are independent. Consequently a tuple
(x_1,...,x_k) is in m C(Q) if and only if

    sum_(i=1)^k M(x_i) <= m.                                  (4)

A g-fixed tuple is constant along each cycle of g. A vector of height a
on a cycle of length l uses l*a units in (4). Counting the remaining
nonnegative slack gives

    sum_(m>=0) |(m C(Q) intersect Z^(kN))^g| t^m
      = 1/(1-t) product_i [(1-t^(l_i)) F_P(t^(l_i))]
      = product_i h(t^(l_i)) /
        [(1-t) product_i (1-t^(l_i))^N].                      (5)

On the coordinates, each cycle of length l contributes N permutation
cycles, so the denominator on the last line is exactly the determinant in
(1). This proves (2), including the homogenizing factor.

For effectiveness, choose a natural labeling of P. Stanley's transfer and
linear-extension formulas identify

    h(t) = sum_(w in L(P)) t^des(w),                           (6)

where L(P) is the finite set of linear extensions, read as label words.
Give L(P)^k the degree sum_i des(w_i), and let S_k permute its factors by
ordinary place permutation, with no signs. A tuple fixed by g is constant
on each cycle, so the graded fixed-tuple enumerator is the right side of
(2). Thus each coefficient is the permutation character of one degree
part of L(P)^k. This proves effectiveness and polynomiality. This is a
character model, not an assertion that Stanley transfer commutes with the
copy permutations. QED.

For k=2 and the exchange involution s, formula (2) reads

    h*_1(t)=h(t)^2,                 h*_s(t)=h(t^2).             (7)

The formulas are about chain polytopes. They do not extend the group of
poset automorphisms in the equivariant order-polytope theorem.

## 2. A reusable obstruction from a root at -1

Assume now that P is graded of rank r, where each maximal chain has r+1
elements. Its h is palindromic of degree d=N-r-1. These are the standard
graded-poset facts recalled in D'Ali--Higashitani, Lemma 4.1 and Theorem 4.5,
combined with Stanley transfer. In particular h(1)=e(P)>0, the number of
linear extensions. Q is graded, with rank k(r+1)-1 and h*-degree k*d.

Formula (2) shows that every character evaluation is palindromic of this
same degree. Hence there are unique virtual S_k-characters Gamma_j with

    h*_(S_k)(t)=sum_(j=0)^floor(k*d/2)
                 Gamma_j t^j (1+t)^(k*d-2*j).                 (8)

Their integrality follows directly by the unit-triangular change of basis
over the character ring. Gamma-effectiveness means all Gamma_j are actual
characters.

**Theorem 2.** If h(-1)=0, then C(P^(oplus k)) is not gamma-effective under
copy permutations for every k>=2. Failure is already visible on a subgroup
generated by one transposition of copies. Nevertheless all its h*
coefficients are permutation characters, by Theorem 1.

More precisely, let s>=1 be the exact multiplicity of -1 as a root of h,
write h(t)=(1+t)^s b(t), and set q=(d-s)/2. For a transposition tau, put

    j=k*q+s,         c=(-1)^j e(P) b(-1)^(k-2).

Then q and j are nonnegative integers, j<=floor(k*d/2), c is nonzero, and

    Gamma_j(1)=0,                 Gamma_j(tau)=c.             (9)

On <tau>, this coefficient is exactly

    (c/2)*1 - (c/2)*epsilon,                                  (10)

where epsilon is the sign character of C2. One multiplicity is negative.

**Proof.** A palindromic polynomial f of degree D has a unique expansion
sum a_i t^i(1+t)^(D-2*i). If i_max is its largest nonzero gamma index,
the exact order of vanishing at -1 is D-2*i_max. Indeed, the summands have
distinct orders there, so the unique term of smallest order cannot cancel.
In particular d-s is even and q is the largest gamma index of h.

At the identity, h* is h(t)^k and has root multiplicity k*s. Thus its
largest nonzero gamma index is k*q. At a transposition, (2) gives
h(t^2) h(t)^(k-2). Since h(1)=e(P)>0, its exact root multiplicity at -1
is (k-2)*s. Its largest gamma index is therefore k*q+s=j. Dividing that
evaluation by (1+t)^((k-2)*s) and evaluating at -1 yields

    (-1)^j Gamma_j(tau) = h(1) b(-1)^(k-2).

This proves (9). A character of dimension zero can be effective only if
it vanishes everywhere, so (9) already proves failure. Restriction to C2
gives the explicit multiplicities (10). They are integers: h(1) is even
because h(-1)=0 and h has integer coefficients. QED.

**Odd-degree square corollary.** For k=2 and d odd, palindromicity forces
h(-1)=0, and the top gamma coefficient has the particularly simple form

    Gamma_d = (e(P)/2) * (epsilon-1).                          (11)

Here j=d and c=-e(P), independently of the multiplicity of the root.

An even d is not asserted to imply success. For example, the three-element
antichain has h=1+4t+t^2 and e=6. Its ordinal square has top gamma values
(4,6), so Gamma_2=5*1-epsilon. Theorem 2 is a sufficient obstruction, not
a classification of all actions without a root at -1.

## 3. The bipartition-exchange frontier

Let P be a disjoint union of n chains, each with a elements, and take its
ordinal square. The comparability graph within either copy is the disjoint
union of n cliques K_a; every vertex in one copy is adjacent to every vertex
in the other. Thus the comparability graph of Q is precisely K_(n,n)[K_a].
Stable sets in a comparability graph are antichains, so

    C(Q) = STAB(K_(n,n)[K_a]).

The copy exchange sends each of the n left blocks to its matching right
block, preserving the position inside each block. This is a base-graph
bipartition exchange, not an internal block permutation.

Here N=n*a, r=a-1, d=a(n-1), and

    e(P) = (n*a)!/(a!)^n.

**Corollary 3.** For every even n>=2 and every odd a>=1, this C2 action on
STAB(K_(n,n)[K_a]) has an effective polynomial h*, but it is not
gamma-effective. Its top gamma coefficient is

    ((n*a)! / (2*(a!)^n)) * (epsilon-1).                       (12)

This disproves the unrestricted extension to bipartition-swapping
automorphisms suggested in item 3 of the height-1961 graph review. It does
not contradict the height-1949 theorem: the latter assumes that the base
automorphisms preserve the bipartition. Nor does it refute Stapledon's
effectiveness conjecture: all h* coefficients here are effective.

The smallest member of this displayed family is C4=K_(2,2), with a=1:

    h*_1(t)=1+2t+t^2,        h*_s(t)=1+t^2,
    h*_(C2)(t)=1+(1+epsilon)t+t^2,
    Gamma_0=1,              Gamma_1=epsilon-1.

The negative trivial multiplicity, rather than the negative value of a
character, is the obstruction. No global minimality claim is made.

## Sources and scope

- R. P. Stanley, [Two poset polytopes](https://math.mit.edu/~rstan/pubs/pubfiles/66.pdf)
  (1986), Theorems 2.2, 3.2, 4.1 and Corollary 4.2: chain-polytope vertices,
  integral transfer, Ehrhart equality, and the linear-extension volume.
  The ordinary descent numerator also follows from the standard
  linear-extension decomposition of order-preserving integer maps.
- A. D'Ali and A. Higashitani,
  [Order polytopes of graded posets are gamma-effective](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.07623),
  Lemma 4.1, Theorem 4.5 and Section 4.1. Their action hypothesis is a
  subgroup of the poset automorphism group. Our copy permutations lie
  outside that hypothesis.
- A. Stapledon, [Equivariant Ehrhart theory](https://arxiv.org/abs/1003.5875):
  determinant-normalized equivariant Ehrhart series and effectiveness.

The universal claims follow from the shell-counting proof, the ordinary
linear-extension numerator, and the gamma-basis vanishing-order argument.
The finite checker is corroboration. It is not a formal proof or an
independent peer review. No unreviewed computational result from Discovery
Net is needed as a premise. Targeted primary-source searches found no
matching copy-permutation obstruction; novelty is search-relative. The
cycle formula is elementary, and no claim of a broader breakthrough is made.
