# Cyclic-cokernel local formula and the index-four phase theorem

Work in the fixed lattice `Z^d`.  Let `P` be bounded, full dimensional, and
half-integral (`2P` is a lattice polytope), with a fixed integral
actual-facet description.  Define

    N(P)={F nonempty face:aff(F) cap Z^d is empty},
    g=min_{F in N(P)} codim(F),
    M(P)={F in N(P):codim(F)=g},  k=d-g.                  (1)

Assume `N(P)` is nonempty.  For every `F` in `M(P)`, assume that `F` lies in
exactly `g` facets and that its active row map `C_F:Z^d->Z^g` has cyclic
cokernel

    Z^g/C_F Z^d isomorphic to Z/N_F.                      (2)

Write `L=C_F Z^d`, `G=Z^g/L`, and let `b_F` be the active right side.  Since
`aff(F)` has no lattice point but contains a half-integral vertex, the class
`a=[b_F]` is the unique order-two element of `G`.  Thus `N=N_F` is even.
Choose an isomorphism `G->Z/N` carrying `a` to `N/2`, put

    q_j=[e_j] in Z/N,                                    (3)

and let `zeta=exp(2 pi i/N)`.

**Cyclic-cokernel theorem.**  The even-minus-odd local cone jump at `F` is

    Delta_F=(2/N) sum_(0<=m<N, m odd)
                 product_j(1-zeta^(m q_j))^(-1).          (4)

The value is rational and independent of the chosen cyclic identification.
In the parity decomposition `L_P(n)=A(n)+(-1)^nB(n)`,

    deg B<=k,
    [n^k]B=(1/2) sum_{F in M(P)} vol_k(F) Delta_F.        (5)

For `N=4`, every `q_j` belongs to `{1,2,3}`.  If

    r_F=#{j:q_j=1},  s_F=#{j:q_j=3},  h_F=#{j:q_j=2},

then

    Delta_F = Re product_j (1-i^q_j)^(-1)                (6)
            = 2^(-g) Re((1+i)^r_F(1-i)^s_F)              (7)
            = 2^(-h_F-(r_F+s_F)/2)
                cos((r_F-s_F)pi/4).                      (8)

Consequently, if the signed sum in (5) is nonzero, `P` has exact
quasiperiod two, `deg B=k`, and reduced Ehrhart-series denominator

    (1-t)^(d+1)(1+t)^(k+1).                              (9)

For `H_P=(1-t^2)^(d+1)E_P`, the residual is

    [H_P/(1+t)^g]_(t=-1)
      =2^d k! sum_{F in M(P)} vol_k(F) Delta_F.          (10)

At order four a single local jump vanishes exactly when
`r_F-s_F=2 mod 4`.  The sign otherwise is that of
`cos((r_F-s_F)pi/4)`.

## 1. Proper faces force nonzero coordinates

We reuse an elementary face fact, spelling it out because it is the geometric
hinge.  If a codimension-`g` face lies in exactly `g` facets, their normals
are independent.  At a relative-interior point, a direction can preserve any
chosen active equalities and strictly decrease all the other active rows;
small displacement retains every inactive slack.  Thus every active subset
`I` cuts a genuine face of codimension `|I|`, whose affine span is
`C_I x=b_I`.

For `F` in `M(P)` and proper `I`, minimality of `g` says this larger face has
an integral point in its affine span.  Therefore

    C_I z_I=b_I for some z_I in Z^d.                     (11)

Deleting row `j` and passing to `G`, (11) says

    N/2 belongs to the cyclic subgroup generated by q_j. (12)

For every odd `m`, a character with `zeta^(m q_j)=1` would be trivial on the
subgroup generated by `q_j`, hence trivial on `N/2`; but its value there is
`(-1)^m=-1`.  Thus every denominator in (4) is nonzero.  At `N=4`, (12)
says exactly `q_j in {1,2,3}`.  This explains why the profile is stronger
than merely knowing the active determinant.

## 2. The local character calculation

Pass to the transverse quotient

    V=R^d/ker(C_F),
    Lambda=Z^d/(ker(C_F) cap Z^d).

The induced real map `C:V->R^g` identifies `C Lambda` with `L`.  Put

    K_n={x in V:Cx<=n b_F},  v=C^(-1)b_F.                (13)

The slack map `u=n b_F-Cx` identifies the lattice points of `K_n` with
nonnegative integer vectors satisfying

    sum_j q_j u_j = nN/2 mod N.                          (14)

With `ell_j(xi)=<xi,C^(-1)e_j>`, the cyclic character filter gives

    exp(-n<v,xi>)S(K_n)(xi)
      =(1/N) sum_(m=0)^(N-1) (-1)^(mn)
        product_j(1-zeta^(m q_j) exp(-ell_j(xi)))^(-1).  (15)

This holds in a convergence chamber and then meromorphically.

For every positive-dimensional face of `K_n`, the equality set `I` is
proper.  Equation (11) makes `v-z_I` lie in its direction space.  Hence the
even and odd transverse apex shifts differ by an actual element of the
induced quotient lattice.  In the Berline--Vergne cone identity, after
removing the vertex exponential, both the normalized face integral and the
transverse `mu` term agree.  Every positive-dimensional face term cancels
between `n=0` and `n=1`, including the full cone.

Only the vertex remains.  Subtracting (15) at zero and one selects the odd
characters and proves (4).  Galois automorphisms permute the odd indices,
proving rationality; changing the cyclic identification does the same.

For `N=2`, (12) forces every `q_j=1`, and (4) is `2^(-g)`, recovering the
accepted positive index-two theorem.  For `N=4`, the odd indices are `1,3`:

    Delta_F
      =(1/2)[product_j(1-i^q_j)^(-1)
             +product_j(1-i^(3q_j))^(-1)].               (16)

The terms are conjugates, giving (6).  Since

    (1-i)^(-1)=(1+i)/2,
    (1-i^2)^(-1)=1/2,
    (1-i^3)^(-1)=(1-i)/2,

equations (7) and (8) follow.  Formula (7) makes rationality explicit:
the real part of the Gaussian-integer numerator is divided by `2^g`.

This is where index four differs qualitatively from index two.  The latter
has one contributing character and a positive jump.  Here the two conjugate
characters may add positively, cancel, or add negatively.

## 3. Global assembly

The Berline--Vergne local Euler--Maclaurin formula is

    L_P(n)=sum_{F face of P}
      mu(t(nP,nF))(0) vol(F)n^dim(F).                    (17)

Half-integrality makes every coefficient period divide two.  By (1), all
faces of dimension greater than `k` have lattice affine spans, so their
coefficients do not vary.  In degree `k`, exactly `M(P)` may vary, and (4)
gives their signed jumps.  The degree-`k` coefficient of `B` is half the
even-minus-odd difference, proving (5).

If the sum is nonzero, the remaining assertions follow from the standard
generating series of a degree-`k` polynomial.  At `t=-1` its pole limit is
the leading coefficient times `k!`; multiplication by
`(1-t^2)^(d+1)` yields (9) and (10).  When the sum is zero, (5) asserts only
`deg B<k`: lower-dimensional contributions can still vary.

## 4. A realization for every profile

Fix any profile `q=(q_1,...,q_g)` in `{1,2,3}^g` containing an odd entry, and
let

    L_q={u in Z^g:sum_j q_j u_j=0 mod 4}.

This is an index-four lattice with cyclic quotient.  Let `b` represent class
`2`, and put `lambda_j=2` for odd `q_j` and `lambda_j=1` for `q_j=2`.  In
`R^g` with lattice `L_q`, define

    P_q=b-conv(0,lambda_1 e_1,...,lambda_g e_g).          (18)

The apex `b` is strict half-lattice, because `2b` lies in `L_q`, while each
other vertex `b-lambda_j e_j` lies in `L_q`.  Thus the apex is the unique
face whose affine span misses the lattice.  Its `g` coordinate facets have
active cokernel `Z/4` and profile `q`; the final simplex facet is inactive
there.  Choosing a basis of `L_q` turns (18) into an ordinary rational
simplex in `Z^g` with an integral actual-facet description.

For a direct count, lattice points of `nP_q` correspond to

    u in Z^g_>=0,
    sum_j u_j/lambda_j<=n,
    sum_j q_j u_j=2n mod 4.                              (19)

Every positive-dimensional face contains one of the integral nonapex
vertices.  Hence only the constant Ehrhart coefficient can vary, and (6)
gives the exact identity

    B_Pq(n)=Delta_q/2.                                   (20)

Products with `[0,1]^f` have one minimum bad face of dimension `f`, volume
one, and

    B_(P_q x [0,1]^f)(n)=(Delta_q/2)(n+1)^f.             (21)

Three profiles show all phases:

    q=(1,3):    Delta= 1/2,
    q=(1,1):    Delta= 0,
    q=(1,1,1):  Delta=-1/4.                              (22)

The middle simplex is the denominator-two collapsing triangle from the
McAllister--Woods family, up to a lattice-affine identification.  The point
is not a new collapse example; it is its location in the complete local
phase formula.  The negative family shows that a universal positivity
extension from index two is impossible even with one locally simple bad
face.
