# Review: exponent-four local Ehrhart parity and the radical--Arf criterion

## Verdict

**Accept, high confidence.** I independently reviewed the theorem, proof,
source notes, producer, and verifier at target commit
`2dcd8dfb65885cef9d6cfd9a96d6be4f7160b447`. I found no mathematical,
computational, completeness, dependency, or source-integrity defect.

The verdict covers the stated domain only: a bounded full-dimensional
half-integral polytope, a fixed integral actual-facet presentation, locally
simple bad faces of minimum codimension, and active cokernels killed by four.
It does not extend the result to nonsimple faces, exponent eight, arbitrary
period collapse, or lower Ehrhart coefficients after leading cancellation.
The claimed novelty is expressly relative to a bounded source search.

## Claim alignment

The proof supports every material target claim:

1. A locally simple minimum bad face with exponent-four active cokernel has
   cokernel `C2` or `C4 x F2^s`; multiple `C4` factors and noncyclic
   elementary-two groups cannot occur there.
2. In the second case the coordinate classes have the stated order-two/odd
   form, and the binary columns `(1,x_j)` have full rank.
3. The even-minus-odd local coefficient is
   `2^(-g) sum_(z in D) (-1)^Q(z)` with the stated even code `D` and quadratic
   form `Q`.
4. The jump vanishes exactly when `Q` is nonzero on the radical. Otherwise
   its magnitude and sign are given by the radical dimension and the Arf
   invariant of the nonsingular quotient.
5. The `C2` jump is `2^(-g)`.
6. Globally `deg B<=d-g`, and the leading possible coefficient of `B` is one
   half the volume-weighted sum of the local jumps. A nonzero sum forces
   minimum period two and the stated reduced series denominator.
7. Every allowed generating profile is realized by a half-integral simplex
   with a unique bad vertex; for this class the radical criterion is exactly
   the period-collapse criterion.

## Human premises and completeness reductions

The following obligations were checked as a proof chain. Agreement among
programs is not being used in place of any of them.

1. **The domain is nondegenerate.** The full-dimensional face `P` is never
   bad because its affine span is the ambient space, so the minimum bad-face
   codimension satisfies `g>=1`.
2. **Active rows have full row rank.** A codimension-`g` face contained in
   exactly `g` actual facets has independent active normals, so its active
   row image has finite index in `Z^g`.
3. **Every proper active subsystem is geometric.** At a relative-interior
   point of the face, surjectivity of the real active map produces a direction
   preserving any selected rows and strictly decreasing the rest. A small
   step preserves inactive slacks. Hence every row subset defines an actual
   face of the expected codimension and has precisely the indicated affine
   span.
4. **Minimum badness supplies integer solutions.** Every proper row subset
   corresponds to a lower-codimension face, so its affine span contains an
   ambient lattice point.
5. **The right-side class is a nonzero involution.** It is nonzero because the
   minimum face is bad. Twice a half-integral vertex lies in the ambient
   lattice and maps to twice the right side, so the class has order exactly
   two.
6. **The coordinate classes generate.** Images of the standard basis generate
   `Z^g/C_F Z^d` by definition. Deleting row `j` from premise 4 shows that the
   right-side involution belongs to the cyclic subgroup generated by every
   coordinate class `q_j`.
7. **The group obstruction is exhaustive.** In a group killed by four, such
   a `q_j` is either the involution itself or has order four and doubles to
   it. Thus `2G` is zero or generated by that single involution. Finite
   abelian classification gives exactly `C2` or `C4 x C2^s`.
8. **The coordinate normal form is exact.** After sending the involution to
   `(2,0)`, the only allowed order-two column is `(2,0)` and every order-four
   column is `(epsilon,x)` with odd `epsilon`. These columns generate exactly
   when `(1,x)` spans `F2^(s+1)`.
9. **The transverse lattice is retained.** Quotienting the ambient vector
   space by the kernel of the active map carries the projected ambient
   lattice isomorphically to the active image lattice. No coordinate
   `Z^g` lattice is silently substituted.
10. **Slack vectors are a bijection.** A lattice point in the translated
    transverse cone corresponds exactly to a nonnegative integral slack
    vector whose class is `n b` in the cokernel.
11. **Character orthogonality has the right sign.** Applying the finite-group
    indicator to `[u]=n b` gives `chi(-n b)` and the displayed geometric
    products after the vertex exponential is removed.
12. **Selected characters are analytic.** If `chi(b)=-1`, premise 6 prevents
    `chi(q_j)=1` for every coordinate. The even-minus-odd difference can
    therefore be evaluated at zero without a singular denominator.
13. **Positive-dimensional cone faces cancel.** Every such face has a proper
    equality set. The integer solution from premise 4 makes the even and odd
    apex shifts differ by a lattice translation in its direction space.
    With one fixed rational scalar product and induced quotient lattices,
    Berline--Vergne translation invariance identifies both the transverse
    `mu` term and the normalized face integral. Only the vertex term remains.
14. **The finite-group prefactor is correct.** Even minus odd selects exactly
    `chi(b)=-1` and contributes the factor `2/|G|`, yielding equation (8).
    In `C2` the sole character gives `2^(-g)`.
15. **All exponent-four odd characters are indexed.** For `C4 x F2^s`, they
    are exactly `(a,y)` with `a in {1,3}` and `y in F2^s`. Order-two columns
    contribute `1/2`, and the two values of `a` are complex conjugates.
16. **The code reduction loses no terms.** Expansion followed by binary
    character orthogonality imposes zero `x`-sum; taking real parts imposes
    even cardinality. The surviving subsets are precisely `D`, with the
    claimed phase `Q` and total factor `2^(-g)`.
17. **`Q` is genuinely quadratic.** Its polarization is `z.w`, since the
    weight-halving cross term is intersection parity and the sign-column term
    is linear. Every codeword has even weight, so the polar form is
    alternating on `D`.
18. **Radical cancellation is if and only if.** On the radical, `Q` is
    linear. A radical word of value one pairs every Gauss summand with its
    negative. If no such word exists, `Q` descends to the quotient by the
    radical.
19. **The Arf magnitude and sign are normalized correctly.** The quotient
    polar form is nonsingular and has even dimension. Each symplectic plane
    contributes `2(-1)^(Q(u)Q(v))`; the radical supplies its number of lifts.
    This gives `2^((ell+rho)/2)` and the stated Arf sign.
20. **The alternative magnitude agrees algebraically.** Substituting
    `g=h+m`, `ell=m-s-1`, and `rank(beta)=ell-rho` yields exactly
    `2^(-h-s-1-rank(beta)/2)`.
21. **The global degree reduction is complete.** Half-integrality bounds all
    coefficient periods by two. Above dimension `k=d-g` no bad face exists;
    in dimension `k`, the bad faces are exactly `M`. Berline--Vergne's
    dimension-specific coefficient formula therefore leaves only the stated
    local jumps.
22. **The factor one half is necessary.** In
    `L(n)=A(n)+(-1)^nB(n)`, the even-minus-odd coefficient difference is
    twice the corresponding coefficient of `B`.
23. **The period and pole conclusions follow.** A nonzero leading parity
    coefficient makes `B` nonzero, hence the minimum period is two. Polynomial
    and alternating-polynomial generating functions have independent poles
    at `1` and `-1`, with orders `d+1` and `k+1` respectively.
24. **The realization lattice has the prescribed quotient.** The kernel
    `L` of the homomorphism `Z^g -> G` defined by the profile has quotient
    exactly `G` because the profile generates.
25. **The simplex has exactly one bad face.** The apex represents the
    nonzero involution and is strict half-lattice. Subtracting one coordinate
    for an order-two column or twice a coordinate for an order-four column
    gives an integral nonapex vertex. Every positive-dimensional simplex face
    contains such a vertex.
26. **The active presentation is realized.** Coordinate functionals are
    integral on `L`; their active image is `L` inside `Z^g`, so the cokernel
    and standard-coordinate classes are the prescribed ones. A lattice basis
    identifies the construction with an ordinary half-integral simplex in
    `Z^g`; the remaining rational facet can be cleared to an integral actual
    facet equation.
27. **The collapse criterion is exact for the realization.** Since the only
    bad face is a vertex, `B` is the constant `Delta/2`. It vanishes exactly
    in the radical-cancellation case and is otherwise a nonzero period-two
    term.
28. **Presentation and scope are explicit.** Rescaling nonprimitive facet
    rows can change the cokernel. The theorem correctly makes the chosen
    integral actual-facet presentation part of the hypotheses and makes no
    representation-free classification claim.

## Limitations

This is an unformalized proof audit. I verified the scope and use of the
Berline--Vergne results but did not reprove their analytic construction.
Finite enumeration does not prove the arbitrary-dimensional face reduction.
The independent code assumes a supplied abstract profile and does not
recognize minimum bad faces from a general inequality description. No claim
is made about nonsimple faces, exponent eight, or global cancellation below
the leading possible parity coefficient.

   `mu(t(2P,2F))(0)-mu(t(P,F))(0)` (or the equivalent even/odd normalized
   transverse-cone difference) before displaying the character formula. The
   current phrase “local cone coefficient” is correct but makes the imported
   normalization harder to audit.
2. Add the cube-product corollary
   `B_(P_q x [0,1]^f)=(Delta_q/2)(n+1)^f`. It follows immediately and gives a
   transparent realization of the global leading-coefficient statement for
   every `k=f`, not only the vertex case.
3. Repeat in the theorem box that the integral actual-facet presentation is
   fixed and may use nonprimitive rows. The introduction states this, but it
   is the easiest hypothesis for a reader to overlook because the active
   cokernel is presentation-sensitive.
4. A proof-assistant formalization could cover the face-subset lemma, common-
   involution group obstruction, character-to-code reduction, and radical/Arf
   evaluation while taking the precise Berline--Vergne coefficient formula as
   an explicit imported axiom.

These are noncorrective improvements and do not lower the verdict.
