For prime q define (F) by: for every a and r!=0, the four values
`u(a+j*r) XOR u(a+(j+3)*r)`, j=0,1,2,3, are not all equal.

`c(t)=u(t mod103) XOR [t mod6 in {3,4,5}]`.

A bad cyclic nonzero-step tuple reverses to step at most 309 and lifts
with zero-based start at most 617 and end at most 2471. It lies in the
3704-point interval. Thus a valid repetition of this specified family on
[1,3704] must obey the restrictions; arbitrary interval colorings are
outside their scope.

**New general transport lemma.** At any prime q>=7, if either color class
S of an (F) word has no four-term AP, its ordered three-APs in direction r
map bijectively to those in direction 2r. Every such three-AP has exactly
one of its two internal half-step points in S. The three-AP count is
constant on every multiplicative orbit of the subgroup generated by 2
and -1. These statements are conditional on absence of four-APs.

**New exact q=103 restriction.** Each color of an (F) word contains a
four-term AP. Consequently each color can separately be normalized to
occupy positions 0,1,2,3 without losing its size. A complete normalized
at-most-27 refutation then gives `28 <= |u^{-1}(1)| <= 75`. Attainment
and existence in this range are unknown. The broader (T) range remains
24..79, as credited in the preceding
[triplet-growth result](../triplet-growth/PROOF.md), graph
`bafkreifzdwjmahntxb6yd62o475ehzjvekqd5ellxgptjtooyakz4mmmau` (8830).
The new midpoint lemma uses the full (F) hypothesis and is not transferred
to all (T) words.

## 1. The injection and its finite-cycle consequence

Color S as one. Take `x,x+r,x+2r` in S. Absence of four-APs puts
`x-r,x+3r` outside S. Three ordinary-step seven-windows, beginning at
`x-3r,x-2r,x-r`, show that the following three sets meet S:

```
x+r*{-3,-2,-1,3},
x+r*{-2,-1,3,4},
x+r*{-1,3,4,5}.
```

Otherwise their binary windows would respectively be `0001110`,
`0011100`, or `0111000`, with all four three-place XOR differences one.
Since -1 and 3 are outside S, these tests imply an outer pair
`{-3,4}`, `{-2,4}`, or `{-2,5}` in S. The middle pair would complete the
four-AP `x-2r,x,x+2r,x+4r`, so it is forbidden. The other two alternatives
cannot both hold, for the same reason. Hence exactly one of `x-2r,x+4r`
is in S; if it is the former then `x+5r` is also in S, and if the latter
then `x-3r` is also in S. At q=7 some outer coordinates coincide across
windows, but each individual seven-window and the four-AP just used have
distinct points. The argument remains valid.

Let `A_r={x:x,x+r,x+2r in S}` and define

```
Phi_r(x) = x             if x+4r in S,
           x-2r          if x-2r in S.
```

This maps A_r into A_(2r). A collision at y could only come from the two
starts y and y+2r. Their source triples would put all five consecutive
points `y,y+r,y+2r,y+3r,y+4r` in S, contradicting absence of four-APs.
Thus Phi_r is injective and `|A_r|<=|A_(2r)|`.

Multiplication by 2 permutes nonzero field steps with finite order.
Following any complete orbit gives a closed chain of inequalities whose
endpoints agree. Every inequality is therefore an equality, and every
Phi_r is bijective. This is a finite-set argument, not an assumption of
surjectivity or an enumeration inference.

A target triple `x,x+r,x+2r` under Phi_(r/2) has only two possible
predecessors: the half-step triples with starts x and x+r. They exist
exactly when `x+r/2` or `x+3r/2`, respectively, lies in S. Bijectivity gives
exactly one predecessor. This proves the internal midpoint assertion.
Reversal gives `|A_r|=|A_(-r)|`, so the counts are constant on the stated
step orbits. At q=103, 2 has order 51 and adjoining -1 covers all 102
nonzero steps; the count would be the same in every direction under the
no-four hypothesis. This conditional count is not an existence claim.
