# Sharp62 with fixed-point replications20 and18

Actual author **six-code-2, researcher**, 2026-10-01. This is an
author-checked exact computer-assisted result with ordinary, unformalized
completeness bridges. Independent review and historical priority are
unassessed. Agreement between the author's algorithms is validation, not
independent peer review.

**Theorem.** Let F consist of distinct five-subsets of an eighteen-point
set, any two meeting in at most two points. Suppose F is invariant under
an involution with eight transpositions and two fixed points X,Y, and

    r_X=20,  r_Y=18,  lambda_XY=4.

Then |F|<=62, and62 is attained. If exactly four of the words through Y
are fixed by the involution, the sharp bound is60.

The replication conditions, pair multiplicity, and symmetry are all
hypotheses. This does not exclude all70-word codes, all codes with this
symmetry, or the other fixed-point degrees and pair multiplicities.
The unrestricted campaign interval69--71 is unchanged.

## Imported twenty-star cover and the actual involutions

Deleting X from its twenty words gives a pair packing of twenty
quadruples on the other seventeen points: two quadruples meet in at most
one point. The author's published
[complete twenty-star cover](https://github.com/helgithorskarp/math_results/blob/main/round-two/six-code-2/free_involution_upper68/PROOF.md)
positively maps all such pair packings into23 literal fixtures. Its
classification covers3060 deficit assignments,108 rooted cases, and352
rooted packings. This is a mathematical input, still awaiting independent
review, not an assumption that every twenty-star has a freely acting
involution. The free-involution upper68 is not applied here.

The four XY words have disjoint three-point tails on the sixteen moved
points. No such tail can be fixed: a fixed subset of a point set on which
the involution acts freely has even size. Thus the four tails form two
exchanged pairs. Every possible actual involution is generated by
pairing the four tails in3 ways, choosing a bijection between the two
tails in each pair in6^2 ways, and matching the four remaining points in3
ways. This gives exactly324 maps for each eligible fixture mate.

The already published
[two-fixed-point carrier](https://github.com/helgithorskarp/math_results/blob/main/round-two/six-code-2/two_fixed_saturated_upper60/PROOF.md)
tests these maps on every twenty-star fixture. There are7 valid maps:
1 in fixture17,3 in fixture20,3 in fixture22. Actual fixture
automorphisms quotient them to one root in each of these three fixtures.
No other fixture survives. Normalization sends each exchanged pair to
(0,1),...,(14,15), X to16, and Y to17. The carrier depends only on
r_X=20 and lambda_XY=4; the older hypothesis r_Y=20 is not used.
The fresh default replay checks this carrier against the supplied
actual point maps as well as replaying the full preceding proof.

## Every possible Y18 star is covered

The XY words are already in the X star. The fourteen other Y words avoid
X and correspond to quadruples on the sixteen moved points. A fixed Y
word consists of Y and two whole exchanged point pairs. Two fixed Y
words cannot use the same pair, since their intersection would contain
Y and that pair. There are therefore at most four fixed Y words. All
other Y words occur in orbits of size two. The possible fixed-word
counts f_Y and numbers p_Y of additional paired orbits are exactly

| f_Y | p_Y | actual34-word XY anchors |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 4 | 5 | 392 |
| 2 | 6 | 3106 |
| 0 | 7 | 0 |

The last count is an exact enumeration result, not a parity deduction.
Four fixed Y words form a perfect matching of the eight point-pair
labels; two form a matching of size two. Fixed-first enumeration uses
all compatible such matchings, followed by the required paired-orbit
cliques in the canonical triple-resource model. There are58,261,3
fixed-first configurations for f_Y=4,2,0 respectively across the three
roots. Their paired completions give3498 distinct anchors.

A second enumeration begins with the paired Y quadruple orbits and uses
actual unordered pairs of points as resources. It chooses all5,6,7
compatible paired rows respectively, then chooses the fixed rows last.
It calls neither the primary fixed-word domains nor their clique search.
Every actual anchor, including its fixture and fixed-word count, agrees
entry by entry. Per-fixture totals are870,1317,1311. Every generated
anchor is also checked directly against the five-subset definition.

This covers all Y18 stars because each has exactly one of the three
fixed-word counts, its private words avoid X, and both enumeration orders
take every compatible orbit selection in the respective finite domains.
Canonical triple resources are used only after actual word orbits and
their internal validity have been established.

## Exact finite completions

After the whole X20/Y18 star is selected, an extension preserving these
replications can add only words avoiding both fixed points. On the
sixteen remaining points the involution is free, so every five-subset
orbit has size two. A valid orbit must not repeat a triple
internally, and it must share no covered triple with the anchor.

Construct a graph whose vertices are these valid orbits and whose edges
join two orbits exactly when their actual words share no triple. A
clique of size a gives an extension of size34+2a; conversely, every
extension is such a clique. The canonical-resource graph is compared
with direct word intersections on every vertex and edge, for every
anchor. Filtering out further Y words is essential here: Y is not
saturated at20.

Python uses a proper-coloring branch bound to enumerate every maximum
clique. C++ uses P/X pivot branching and a different first-fit coloring
bound, with a static degree order and independent color classes.
The searches agree on every one of670750 maximum families. The complete
distribution of maximum code sizes is

| maximum words | number of anchors |
|---:|---:|
| 50 | 3 |
| 52 | 8 |
| 54 | 639 |
| 56 | 1806 |
| 58 | 952 |
| 60 | 68 |
| 62 | 22 |

The largest residual clique has14 vertices, so34+2*14=62 proves the
bound. In the392 anchors with f_Y=4, the largest clique has13 vertices,
giving60, attained in14 anchors. These are full maxima; an incomplete
targeted70-word pivot pilot was not used as proof.
