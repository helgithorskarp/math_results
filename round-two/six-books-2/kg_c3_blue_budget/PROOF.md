# A blue-deletion budget around the cyclic KG(7,2) seed

Actual agent **six-books-2**, role **researcher**, ordinary Book Ramsey
R(B4,B7), 2026-10-01. Exact computer-assisted construction obstruction.
Two algorithms are implemented by the same author; independent peer review is
not claimed. The finite enumeration and the written completeness bridges are
not proof-assistant formalizations. The located Ramsey interval remains **22–23**.

## 1. The construction and the standalone theorem

Let V be the 21 two-element subsets of {0,1,2,3,4,5,6}. Initially color uv red
exactly when u and v are disjoint: this is the classical Kneser graph KG(7,2).
Its vertices have red degree ten, every red edge has three red common neighbors,
and every blue edge has five blue common neighbors. These classical seed
properties and the consequent known 21-vertex lower-bound witness are controls,
not new results.

Act on the ground set by sigma=(012)(345), fixing 6. It induces seven free
vertex orbits of size three and, on each original edge color, 35 free edge
orbits of size three. Add a fixed vertex x, red-adjacent to an arbitrary union J
of three vertex orbits, so d_R(x)=9. Promote a set P of p distinct original blue
edge orbits to red. Delete a set D of q distinct original red edge orbits.
All other colors are determined. In particular, promotions and deletions are
disjoint, and the x-joins are retained.

**Blue-budget theorem.** If the resulting blue graph has at most six common
blue neighbors at every blue edge, then:

- for p=1, q<=3;
- for p=2, q<=5.

Both bounds are attained in this blue-only statement. It assumes no red-book
bound, degree bound away from x, root neighborhood type, or prescribed total
edge count. The sharp controls below contain red B4s and are not Ramsey witnesses.
The theorem quantifies over every J, P and D in the specified family, including
arbitrarily large deletion sets; it is not just a search at 99 or102 red edges.

## 2. Monotonicity gives a small exact certificate

Fix J and P and denote the promoted graph before deletions by F. Deleting red
edges only adds blue edges. A blue edge and its blue common neighbors therefore
persist under additional red deletions. Conversely, every smaller deletion set
of a blue-valid construction is itself blue-valid.

If F already has a forbidden blue book, discard the entire case. Otherwise
form the pool S of original red edge orbits whose individual deletion from F
is blue-valid. Every permissible D lies in S. Form the compatibility graph
Gamma on S: two different members are adjacent exactly when their joint deletion
is blue-valid. Every permissible D is a clique of Gamma. If every k-clique
actually creates a forbidden blue book on deleting its members, no permissible
deletion set can have size at least k: take any k-subset of such a larger set.

Use k=4 for one promotion and k=6 for two promotions. The producer builds every
necessary single and pair domain from actual graphs and enumerates every k-clique.
Every surviving clique has a supplied literal blue spine with at least seven
actual page vertices. The checker rebuilds the entire k-clique family by
Bron--Kerbosch maximal cliques followed by all k-subsets; it checks equality of
the complete families, no duplicates, and every spine/pages witness using
ground-pair disjointness and explicit neighbor sets.

For one promotion all 35*35=1225 labeled (J,P) cases are used. Only three
four-cliques survive the pair conditions; all three have eight blue pages:

## 4. Consequence for ordinary Ramsey witnesses and edit distance

The following corollary explicitly imports earlier results. The ordinary
[degree reductions7526](https://github.com/helgithorskarp/math_results/blob/main/book_ramsey_4_7_degree_reductions/capacity.md)
give minimum red degree at least seven; the ordinary
[upper-ten theorem8012](https://github.com/helgithorskarp/math_results/blob/main/book_ramsey_b4_b7_degree11_gram_exclusion/PROOF.md)
gives maximum red degree at most ten. Thus any valid22 graph with the above
action must have d_R(x)=9, since that degree is a multiple of three. The
credited [free-seven C3 theorem8971](https://github.com/helgithorskarp/math_results/blob/main/round-two/six-books-2/c3_free_seven_105/PROOF.md)
gives at most102 red edges. Its own stated corollary, using the ordinary
[edge floor97 from7526](https://github.com/helgithorskarp/math_results/blob/main/book_ramsey_4_7_degree_reductions/proof.md),
leaves only99 or102 red edges. No historical minimum-eight, full-Petersen root,
dirty-root completion, or newer teammate sector classification is imported.

Write such a graph relative to any KG(7,2) seed with this ground-action labeling:
promote p original blue orbits and delete q original red orbits. Its red edge
count is

    e = 105 + 9 + 3p - 3q = 114 + 3p - 3q.

For p=0, add an arbitrary original blue orbit to red. Blue avoidance persists,
so the one-promotion theorem still forces q<=3. Consequently p=0 or2 forces
e>=105, while p=1 forces e>=108. Each contradicts the credited e<=102 theorem.

**Ordinary corollary.** Every valid22 graph with C3 cycle type3^7 1 must promote
at least three original KG blue orbits (at least nine blue seed edges). At102
red edges, q=p+4, so the original210 edge colors on V change in3(p+q)>=30
places. At99 edges, q=p+5, so at least33 original edge colors change. These
bounds hold relative to every equivariant KG seed: relabeling transports the
entire universally quantified construction theorem.

This is a construction/edit-distance obstruction. It does not exclude the
three-or-more-promotion family, all C3-invariant22 graphs, other action cycle
types, or the unrestricted Ramsey endpoint. The minimum distances30/33 are
lower bounds; no Ramsey witness attains them here.
