perturbations**, avoiding angular sorting. At an incident direction \(d\),

\[
u=d+\eta\bigl(\tau(d\times b_i)+\epsilon\sigma b_i\bigr),
\qquad \tau,\sigma\in\{-1,1\},

## Strengthening and improvement opportunities

**Proved strict supremum refinement.** Define

\[
B=\left(\frac{641+67s}{722}\right)^{1/4}.
\]

Let \(\mu(K)\) be the supremum of scales admitting strict projected
containment with arbitrary proper relative motion and planar translation.
Then

\[
1\le\mu(K)<B.
\]

This strengthens the target's \(\mu(K)\le B\). It is a qualitative
positive gap, not an explicit stronger numerical bound.

Proof: consider all closed fits of scales \(1\le\lambda\le B\).
The receiver normal ranges over compact \(S^2\), and the relative motion
over compact \(SO(3)\). Since the origin is inside the source body,
the translation point \(t\) belongs to the receiver shadow. Thus
\(\|t\|\le R\), where \(R^2=(11+4s)/4\), and \(t\cdot n=0\).
Closed containment is preserved under parameter limits: the projected
finite convex hulls vary continuously in Hausdorff distance. Consequently
this nonempty feasible set is compact and a largest closed-fit scale
\(\Lambda\) is attained. The identity unit-scale fit ensures nonemptiness.
Area comparison bounds every closed scale by \(B\), so this is also
the unrestricted largest closed scale.

If \(\Lambda=B\), the chain

\[
B^2a_0\le B^2A(Q^{\mathsf T}n)\le A(n)\le a_{\max}=B^2a_0
\]

has equality throughout. The source is a minimum shadow, the receiver
a maximum shadow, and their equal areas plus closed containment force
equality of polygons after scale, isometry and translation. An invertible
planar similarity preserves the number of strict corners. This would
identify a 12-corner polygon with an 18-corner polygon, which is impossible.
Thus \(\Lambda<B\).

Finally \(\mu(K)=\Lambda\). A strict fit is a closed fit. Conversely,
for any closed fit of scale \(\lambda>0\), its source shadow contains
the origin in its interior. Shrinking that shadow about the origin to
any \(0<\lambda'<\lambda\), with the same translation, places it
strictly inside the old full-dimensional source polygon, hence inside
the receiver interior. Thus closed scales are approached by strict
scales. In particular identity fits give all strict scales below one,
establishing \(\mu(K)\ge1\). This proves the asserted refinement.

The argument is a general compactness/nonattainment principle for convex
bodies with incompatible extremal shadow shapes. No historical priority
claim is made for that principle or its present application.

**Concrete next improvement, unproved here.** To replace the qualitative
gap by \(\mu(K)\le B-\delta\) for an explicit rational \(\delta>0\),
one needs a quantified mismatch bound between the finitely many scaled
minimum polygons and the two maximum polygons over all planar rolls and
translations, together with uniform projection/brightness continuity
bounds away from the extremal axes. Merely counting corners supplies no
numerical modulus. A rigorous support-inequality or interval certificate
for those planar containment problems could supply it. No value of
\(\delta\), open receiving neighborhood, or global non-Rupert conclusion
has been proved in this review.

The closed common-shadow cone may support further transfers only after
the relevant source and receiver hypotheses are checked. Replacing the
original J74 by its centrally symmetric difference body without proving
the direction of the needed containment implication would change the
problem. The current target correctly retains the original body.
