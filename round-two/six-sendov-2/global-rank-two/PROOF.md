## 6. Unique real and jointly simple scalar roots; complete positive test

If R(t0)=0 then (t0²,t0,1) is a nonzero vector in ker M. The proved
rank lower bound and three columns force rank M=2 and nullity one.
Different scalar roots give distinct such vectors; their last coordinate
1 prevents proportionality. There is therefore at most one complex
common root. Since M is real, a nonreal root would give a distinct
conjugate root, a contradiction. Also R(0)=C!=0 by wC=1.

If EVERY derivative R_i'(t0) were zero, the common root and derivative
would give

    L=-2t0*A,  C=t0²*A,                                    (14)

making M have rank at most one. This is again impossible. Thus at
least one scalar equation has a simple root at t0. This is a joint
statement; individual zero rows or double-root rows are not excluded.

For real parameters write S=sum a_i², T=sum a_i*b_i, U=sum b_i².
At a nonzero common root (1) gives Q(t0)=0 and a*t0+b=0. If S=0,
reality gives a=0, then b=0 and rank M=1, a contradiction. Hence
S>0 at every common root. The9602 Gram theorem now has the global
positive-root criterion

    T<0,
    SU-T²=0,
    alpha*T²-beta*T*S+S²=0,
    uniquely t0=-T/S.                                     (15)

Necessity follows from that theorem and the just-proved S>0. Conversely
T<0 already forces S>0 for real a,b (S=0 would force T=0). The Gram
equality makes b=(T/S)a, and the last equality gives Q(-T/S)=0.
Then (1) gives ALL FIVE original equations and a positive scalar.
There is no rank-one exception or generic row choice. For an actual
stationary profile the separate strict real feasibility conditions
from Section1 still must be checked.

There is a pointwise transversality bound at such a root. Let
kappa=1+||C||2*||w||2. Differentiating (1), and using Q'(t0)=wR'(t0)
and b=-t0*a, gives

    (I-Cw)R'(t0)=t0*a,
    ||R'(t0)||2 >= |t0|*sqrt(S)/kappa >0.                   (16)
