\[

\]

\[

\]
\[

 \qquad b=(u_1,\ldots,u_7)^T.
\]
Balance ensures \(Tb=u\). Thus \(A\) represents \(H_u\), and \(A^TG=GA\). It is similar to a real symmetric matrix. The root field is \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)\) for the author's witnesses and \(\mathbb Q\) for both new witnesses. The real embedding uses the exact squared bracket \(17/10<\sqrt3<7/4\).

The rank-one operator associated with \(u\) is \(U=bb^TG\). In the trace inner product on self-adjoint operators, let \(Z\) be its orthogonal projection onto the algebra generated by \(A\). If this algebra has dimension \(d\), write \(Z=\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}z_iA^i\). Direct matrix powers, through order \(2d\), give
\[
 M_{ij}=\operatorname{tr}(A^{i+j}),\qquad

\]
The checker proves every leading principal minor of \(M\) positive, verifies the complete matrix closure \(A^d\in\operatorname{span}(I,A,\ldots,A^{d-1})\), solves the rational system and verifies
\[

\]
The full matrix equality in the closure check, rather than a sample or numerical rank, proves the claimed minimal-polynomial degree. Self-adjointness then makes that degree the number of distinct eigenvalues.

For a simple spectrum, the generated algebra is the full diagonal algebra in the eigenbasis. Its projection of \(U\) is
\(Z=\sum\|\Pi_\lambda u\|^2\Pi_\lambda\), hence
\[

For an eigenvalue of multiplicity \(m>1\), a center projection would instead contribute \(\|\Pi_\lambda u\|^4/m\). **That issue is not ignored.** In the compact profiles the two constant triple blocks have two-dimensional sum-zero subspaces. Thus the center projection equals the required squared-weight sum here too. No arbitrary splitting of a repeated eigenspace is used. The all-distinct profiles have matrix-algebra dimension seven, independently certifying simple compression spectrum.

This is a direct seven-dimensional matrix calculation. It does not generate spectral weights by polynomial inversion, use numerical roots, or use the target's active-cubic three-moment reduction as an input.
