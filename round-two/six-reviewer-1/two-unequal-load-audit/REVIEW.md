## Family, universal rank obstruction and empty lift

Every intersecting family containing a fresh singleton has at most two sets.
Spokes at different marks are disjoint, so a family with spokes uses just one
mark. Its old members must contain that mark. Such a family has at most
\(q+D\) sets at the heavy mark and \(q+t\) at the light mark, with equality
in the first bound only for the whole heavy star. A family with only old cube
members meets at most one set of each complementary pair, so has size at most
\(q\). Since \(D\ge2\), the heavy star is the unique maximum family.

For any real H matrix, write its centered indicator as
\(z=\mathbf1_{\mathcal S_x}-(s/N)\mathbf1\). Support gives the uncentered
quadratic form \(s^2\) under \(L\), while \(L\mathbf1=N\mathbf1\);
centering gives \(z^TLz=0\). PSD implies \(Lz=0\), so
\(\operatorname{rank}L\le N-1\). This uses neither symmetry under cube
permutations nor a cap. Real symmetry of \(M\) remains part of H's definition.

For a PSD core \(C\) on nonempty members, diagonal \(w\) and mandatory
intersecting off-diagonals \(-1\), define

\[
T_0=\begin{pmatrix}-\mathbf1^T\\I\end{pmatrix},\quad
Q=T_0CT_0^T,\quad M=(Q+J-sI)/(N-s).
\]

Then \(Q\mathbf1=0\), support and row sums hold,
\(L=Q+J\), \(\operatorname{rank}L=1+\operatorname{rank}C\), and

\[
(N-s)(I-M)=NP_N-Q. \tag{1}
\]

The actual empty vector is minus the **entire** nonempty Gram sum. Nonzero
eigenvalues of \(Q\) are those of the resulting full Gram frame. A bound on
the old core or an empty-omitted frame would not prove (1).

## Rational completion and the full frame

The old core on nonempty cube sets is

\[
C_D=(q-D)P+(q+D)I-J,
\]

where \(P\) exchanges proper complementary pairs and has zero full-set row.
Its complete spectrum has \(2D\) on \(q-1\) antisymmetric directions,
\(2q\) on \(q-2\) symmetric zero-sum directions, and a two-plane matrix
\(\begin{pmatrix}2&-\sqrt{2q-2}\\-\sqrt{2q-2}&w\end{pmatrix}\).
The latter's trace is \(q+D+1>0\), determinant \(2D>0\).
Thus the old core is PD even for \(D>q\); a negative complementary coefficient
is an allowed weight, not an obstruction.

Let \(G=\sum g_A\), \(f=g_X\), and \(H_i=-\sum_{A\ni x_i}g_A\).
Complementary-pair counts give

\[
G^2=f^2=w,\ G\cdot f=D+1-q,\ H_i^2=qD,\ H_1\cdot H_2=0,
\ G\cdot H_i=f\cdot H_i=-D.
\]

For loads \(d_1=D,d_2=t\), take orthogonal spoke residuals with within-group
Gram \((q+D)(I-J/D)\), and put \(S_{ij}=H_i/D+T_{ij}\).
The heavy residual has rank \(D-1\), sum zero; the light one has rank \(t\),
because \(t<D\). Spokes have squared norm \(w\), required same-mark product
\(-1\), different-mark product zero, and all old-spoke intersection products
\(-1\). Put

\[
L_2=\tfrac1t\sum_jS_{2j},\quad Z=L_2-H_2/D,\quad
Z^2=(q+D)(1/t-1/D)>0,
\]
\[
K=G+H_1+tL_2,\quad B_0=K^2=w+m(q-2)+t(D-t)>0.
\]

Solve the own-spoke singleton product for each group:

\[
c_i=\frac{m(w-d_i-m-1)}{(m+1)((m-1)w-1+d_i)},\quad
Y_j=c_jS_j-\tfrac1m\sum_kc_kS_k,\quad U_j=-K/(m+1)+Y_j+W_j.
\]

Indeed \(K\cdot S_j=w-d_i\) and
\(Y_j\cdot S_j=c_j((m-1)w+d_i-1)/m\), so \(U_j\cdot S_j=-1\).
The denominator is positive, even when a numerator is zero or negative.
These are the only mandatory fresh singleton intersections.

The heavy mean of \(Y\) is \(y=t(c_HH_1/D-c_LL_2)/m\); the light mean is
\(-Dy/t\). Determine the required residual diagonals directly from the Gram:

\[
\eta_H=w-\|-K/(m+1)+y\|^2-c_H^2(q+D)(1-1/D),
\]
\[
\eta_L=w-\|-K/(m+1)-Dy/t\|^2-c_L^2(q+D)(1-1/t).
\]

For \(E=D\eta_H+t\eta_L\), let
\(\zeta_i=m[\eta_i-E/(m(m-1))]/(m-2)\). The universal exact signs below
prove both positive. The Gram
\(\mathcal W=P_m\operatorname{diag}(\zeta_H^D,\zeta_L^t)P_m\) is PSD,

All \(W\)'s are orthogonal to old/spoke vectors. Consequently every core
entry is rational and correct. The nonempty sum is \(K/(m+1)\), hence the
empty vector is \(-K/(m+1)\), with squared norm \(B_0/(m+1)^2\).

Set \(h_0=-(G+f)/2\), \(G_p=G+h_0\), \(A_i=H_i-h_0\), and let \(W_s\)
be the heavy \(W\)-mean. Their Gram is

\[

\[

## Strengthening and improvement opportunities

**Proved quantitative repair refinement.** Put
\(\ell=T_{\rm raw}-N+1\). The raw empty energy is a squared norm and
\(w\ge3,N\ge10\), so

\[
\ell\ge(w-1)(N-1)\ge18.
\]

Using the raw row-zero trace bound without discarding \(NP_N\) gives, for
any \(0<\epsilon<1\),

\[
NP_N-[(1-\epsilon)Q_{\rm seed}+\epsilon Q_{\rm raw}]
 \succeq [1-\epsilon\ell]P_N. \tag{7}
\]

Consequently the **published matrix itself** has the larger guaranteed gap

\[
\beta=1-\frac{T_{\rm raw}-N+1}{2(1+T_{\rm raw})}
     =\frac12+\frac{N}{2(1+T_{\rm raw})}>\frac12. \tag{8}
\]

For any rational \(0<\gamma<1\), choosing

\[
\epsilon_\gamma=\frac{1-\gamma}{T_{\rm raw}-N+1} \tag{9}
\]

gives a rational greatest-rank H matrix with full scaled gap at least
\(\gamma\). Its coefficient is positive and below1, so the same kernel
argument applies. In particular, for \(\gamma=1/2\),
\(\epsilon'=[2(T_{\rm raw}-N+1)]^{-1}>\epsilon\). This increases the raw
rank-restoring component while retaining the half-unit cap. It is not an
optimal mixing coefficient claim. The trace bound is coarse, and the strict
seed cap alone gives no published quantitative margin for \(\gamma=1\).
For real \(\gamma\), (9) gives a real matrix; rationality requires rational
\(\gamma\). The idea is a direct refinement of the credited trace repair.

At the smallest boundary \((2,2,1)\), \(T_{\rm raw}=334/9\), published
\(\epsilon=9/686\), \(\beta=433/686\), and improved
\(\epsilon'=9/506\). These and the complete original-set matrices are
exactly checked. Equations(7)–(9) prove the unbounded refinement, rather than
extrapolating these values.

**Proved scoped finite-product corollary.** For any finite nonempty list of
9005 factors on disjoint coordinate supports, use either their published
matrices or the refined matrices with \(0<\gamma_j<1\). Write
\(N_* =\prod_jN_j\), \(\rho=\max_j s_j/N_j\), \(S=\rho N_*\), and
\(E=\{j:s_j/N_j=\rho\}\). Their tensor matrix is rational capped H when the
factor choices are rational, with

\[
\operatorname{rank}[(N_*-S)M_*+SI]=N_*-|E|,\qquad
\operatorname{rank}(I-M_*)=N_*-1.
\]

Every maximum intersecting family in the product is exactly a heavy-star
cylinder of one eligible factor. Its lower rank is universally greatest.
An explicit upper gap is

\[
\alpha_* =1-\max_j\max\{s_j/(N_j-s_j),\ 1-g_j/(N_j-s_j)\}>0,
\quad (N_*-S)(I-M_*)\succeq(N_*-S)\alpha_*P_{N_*}, \tag{10}
\]

where \(g_j=\beta_j\) for the published choice or \(\gamma_j\) for (9).

Here is a proof, retaining the credited framework7578 boundary. Tensor support
and row sums are immediate. Each factor has a simple unit eigenvalue and every
other eigenvalue has absolute value below1. A negative tensor product has
magnitude at most \(\max_j s_j/(N_j-s_j)=S/(N_*-S)\); equality requires one
eligible factor at its simple negative endpoint and units everywhere else.
Thus the lower kernel consists of exactly \(|E|\) independent centered
heavy-cylinder indicators. All other tensor eigenvalues have absolute value
at most the maximum in (10), proving the upper cap and simple unit.
Eligible cylinders force those lower kernels in every H matrix, proving the
universal rank obstruction.

For equality families, a centered maximum-family indicator lies in that lower
kernel, hence is a sum of functions of separate eligible coordinates. If two
summands are nonconstant, independently varying their arguments gives at least
three values (use minima, a strict change in one coordinate, and maxima),
contradicting a binary indicator. A single surviving summand is proportional
to a heavy-star indicator; its complement has density \(1-\rho>\rho\), so
only the star cylinder has the required size \(S\). This proves the equality
classification. Two finite tensor controls, orders100 and120, check the actual
product matrices in tied/unequal-density cases. They validate the implementation;
the spectral proof gives arbitrary finite products. This is an application of
the established conditional tensor mechanism, not a new generic closure rule,
and it does not assert inertia Conjecture I.
