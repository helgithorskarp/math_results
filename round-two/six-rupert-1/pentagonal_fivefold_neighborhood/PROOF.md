# Closed minimum fits and positive receiving neighborhoods

Author: **six-rupert-1, researcher**, 2026-10-01.

This is an author-checked intermediate result. It is unformalized and has
not undergone independent review. The full Rupert problem for the standard
pentagonal hexecontahedron remains open. The neighborhood radius proved
here is **positive but not numerically specified**.

## 1. Statement, model and dependency

Put
\[
\phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2,\quad h=\phi+2,\quad N=(1,0,\phi),
\quad n_0=N/\sqrt h.
\]
Let \(G\) be the proper rotation by \(72^\circ\) about \(N\):
\[
G=\frac{\phi-1}{2}I+\frac{2-\phi}{2}NN^T+\frac12[N]_\times.
\]
The proper icosahedral group \(\mathcal I\) of order sixty is generated by
\(G\) and \(\operatorname{diag}(-1,-1,1)\). It contains
\(T_y=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,-1)\), which sends \(N\) to \(-N\).

Use the model
\[
K_p=\operatorname{conv}\big(
\mathcal I(-u,-r,-1)\ \cup\ a(\pm W)\ \cup\ t\mathcal D\big),
\qquad p=(u,r,a,t),
\tag{1}
\]
where
\[
W=\{(1,0,\phi),(-1,0,\phi),(\phi,1,0),(\phi,-1,0),
(0,\phi,1),(0,\phi,-1)\}
\]
and \(\mathcal D\) consists of \((\pm1,\pm1,\pm1)\) and the cyclic
permutations of \((0,\pm1/\phi,\pm\phi)\). The closed parameter box is

| Parameter | Lower | Upper |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(u\) | 0.0919831 | 0.0919833 |
| \(r\) | 0.1041858 | 0.1041860 |
| \(a\) | 0.5565538 | 0.5565540 |
| \(t\) | 0.5828994 | 0.5828997 |

Write \(\mathcal A=\mathcal I n_0\), a set of twelve unit directions
representing six unoriented axes, and let \(\pi_n\) be orthogonal
projection onto \(n^\perp\).

The preceding
[minimum-diameter lemma](../pentagonal_minimum_diameter/PROOF.md), source
commit `86ab225fb8becbe66601a5da0b5b017e872e1833`, is a proof dependency.
It establishes for **every** \(p\) in this box that
\[
\min_{n\in S^2}\operatorname{diam}(\pi_n K_p)
=\delta(r)=2\sqrt{1+r^2(h-1)/h},
\tag{2}
\]
and that equality occurs exactly for \(n\in\mathcal A\). It also checks
the proper group and the original 92-point model. Its exact named-solid
audit identifies the standard pentagonal hexecontahedron, normalized by
its coordinate constant \(C_{19}\), at a parameter point strictly inside
the box. In particular, this is not a theorem about a decimal surrogate.

**Theorem.** For every \(p\) in the box:

1. At a fivefold receiving direction, the only closed fits
   \[
   \lambda\pi_n R K_p+b\ \subseteq\ \pi_n K_p,
   \quad R\in SO(3),\quad b\in n^\perp,\quad\lambda\ge1,
   \tag{3}
   \]
   have \(\lambda=1\), \(b=0\), and \(R\in\mathcal I\).
2. There is a **single** \(\rho>0\), uniform on the entire closed
   parameter box, such that the same classification holds whenever
   \(\operatorname{dist}(n,\mathcal A)\le\rho\).

Thus all these receiving directions exclude strict interior inclusion,
with every proper moving orientation, every roll, every physical planar
translation and every scale at least one covered. Both handed standard
solids satisfy the theorem by reflecting the whole setup. Only
same-handed copies are compared; the moving solid is never independently
reflected. No central symmetry is assumed.

## 2. Exact fivefold shadow and its symmetries

Let \(P_p=\pi_{n_0}K_p\). The checker uses the deterministic original
92-point order in the preceding verifier. The thirty indices in
`polygon.json` give a counterclockwise boundary of \(P_p\), for every
parameter point in the box. For consecutive original points \(v,w\), it
forms
\[
m=(w-v)\times N.
\]
It verifies that \(m\perp N\), that \(m\cdot v>0\), and that
\[
m\cdot(v-z)>0
\tag{4}
\]
for each of the other ninety original points \(z\). The two endpoints
satisfy equality as polynomial identities. It also checks each projected
turn is strictly positive. These connected supporting segments form a
strictly convex closed boundary, and all original projected points lie
in its halfplanes. Consequently this is the complete shadow polygon,
not a selected subset of its vertices.

All signs in (4) are proved with rational interval enclosures of
degree-two polynomials over \(\mathbb Q(\phi)\). The box is never sampled.
There are 2,760 support tests: sixty exact endpoint identities and 2,700
strict inequalities. The smallest certified, unnormalized strict support
gap is greater than \(0.0087072\).

The shadow has exactly five shortest edges. They are the \(G\)-orbit of
the genuine original edge with endpoints
\[
v=(-r,-1,-u),\qquad w=(r,-1,u).
\tag{5}
\]
Put
\[
\alpha=\phi r-u>0,\qquad m_0=(0,-1,0).
\]
The projected squared length of (5) is
\[
\frac{4\alpha^2}{h}.
\tag{6}
\]
The checker verifies exactly five instances of (6), and proves that the
other twenty-five projected edge lengths have larger squares throughout
the box. Their certified squared-length difference is greater than
\(0.0254627\). The outward unit normals of the five shortest edges are
\(m_i=G^i m_0\), \(0\le i<5\). They form a regular five-element set of
directions in \(N^\perp\).

The proper planar symmetry group of \(P_p\) is exactly \(C_5\). To see
this, its area centroid is zero: it is fixed by the nontrivial rotation
\(G\) in the plane. Any symmetry therefore fixes zero and permutes the
five shortest-edge outward normals. A proper planar rotation preserving
that regular five-element set has angle a multiple of \(72^\circ\).
All five such rotations are present, since \(G\) preserves the body.
The full orthogonal planar symmetry group is \(D_5\): \(T_y\) induces
the required reflection, and the five normals permit at most five
reflections. These ten planar symmetries all lift to proper body
symmetries. In particular, the shadow has no extra half-turn symmetry.

## 3. Every closed minimum-shadow fit is an actual body symmetry

It suffices to take the receiving direction \(n_0\), since every element
of \(\mathcal A\) is a proper body image of it. Suppose (3) holds there.
Diameter and (2) give
\[
\lambda\delta(r)
\le\lambda\operatorname{diam}(\pi_{R^Tn_0}K_p)
\le\operatorname{diam}(P_p)=\delta(r).
\]
Hence \(\lambda=1\) and \(R^Tn_0\in\mathcal A\). Choose
\(M\in\mathcal I\) such that \(M n_0=R^Tn_0\), and put \(S=RM\).
Then \(S^Tn_0=n_0\), so \(S\) is a proper rotation about \(n_0\).
Because \(M K_p=K_p\), inclusion (3) becomes
\[
S P_p+b\subseteq P_p.
\]
The two planar convex bodies have equal area and nonempty interior, so
this is equality. Their area centroids force \(b=0\). Section2 gives
\(S|_{n_0^\perp}=G^j|_{n_0^\perp}\) for some \(j\). Both fix \(n_0\),
so \(S=G^j\), and \(R=G^jM^{-1}\in\mathcal I\).

Conversely each of the sixty proper matrices in \(\mathcal I\), with
scale one and zero translation, gives the identical shadow. This proves
the full closed-fit classification, including all oriented source-axis
branches. It does not merely classify fits whose source direction was
prescribed in advance.

## 4. Ten original contacts eliminate every infinitesimal motion

Use the original points (5), and define
\[
v_i=G^iv,\quad w_i=G^iw,\quad m_i=G^im_0,
\quad a_i=G^ia_0,\qquad a_0=(-u,0,r).
\]
The following are exact identities:
\[
m_i\cdot v_i=m_i\cdot w_i=1,\qquad
v_i\times m_i=a_i,\quad w_i\times m_i=-a_i,
\qquad\sum_{i=0}^4m_i=0.
\tag{7}
\]

Let \(q\in\mathbb R^3\) be an infinitesimal proper rotation, let
\(b\in N^\perp\) be an infinitesimal translation, and let \(s\ge0\)
be an infinitesimal increase of scale. The ten first-order contact
inequalities at the identity fit are
\[
m_i\cdot b+a_i\cdot q+s\le0,\qquad
m_i\cdot b-a_i\cdot q+s\le0,
\quad0\le i<5.
\tag{8}
\]
Adding all ten gives \(10s\le0\); hence \(s=0\). Their sum is now zero,
while each left side is nonpositive, so every one is zero. Taking sums
and differences in each pair gives
\[
m_i\cdot b=0,\qquad a_i\cdot q=0\quad\text{for all }i.
\tag{9}
\]

The vectors \(m_i\) span \(N^\perp\): the Gram determinant of the first
two is \(1/2+\phi/4>0\). The vectors \(a_i\) span \(\mathbb R^3\).
Their axial average is
\[
\sum_i a_i=\frac{5\alpha}{h}N\ne0,
\]
and their transverse component is nonzero because
\[
(\phi,0,-1)\cdot a_0=-(\phi u+r)<0.
\]
The rotation by \(72^\circ\) gives two independent transverse vectors,
so that average and the transverse span give rank three. Throughout the
closed box the checker proves \(\alpha>0.0765928\) and
\(\phi u+r>0.2530175\). Thus (9) forces \(b=q=0\).

We have proved that the closed cone (8), with \(s\ge0\), consists of
zero only. This argument includes translation and scale; neither was
removed by an assumed centering or symmetry of the whole body.

## 5. The contacts persist when the receiving direction changes

For unit \(n\) near \(n_0\), put
\[
\widetilde m_i(p,n)=(w_i-v_i)\times n.
\tag{10}
\]
At \(n_0\), this is \((2\alpha/\sqrt h)m_i\), a positive multiple of
the original outward normal. It is always perpendicular to both \(n\)
and \(w_i-v_i\). By (4), every other original point has a strict support
gap at \(n_0\). These finitely many gaps vary continuously in \(p,n\).
Consequently (10) continues to support the **same original edge** for
\(p,n\) near any \(p_0,n_0\) in the box. Both endpoints remain exact
contact points. This supplies genuine receiving-shadow inequalities in
the perturbed plane; simply projecting an old supporting normal would
not by itself supply that fact.

In particular any closed fit there must satisfy
\[
\widetilde m_i(p,n)\cdot(\lambda Rv_i+b-v_i)\le0,
\qquad
\widetilde m_i(p,n)\cdot(\lambda Rw_i+b-w_i)\le0.
\tag{11}
\]
The projection disappears from these scalar inequalities because the
normals are perpendicular to \(n\).

## 6. Compactness gives a uniform positive receiving neighborhood

Suppose no common positive neighborhood has the classification stated
in Section1. There would be parameters \(p_k\) in the closed box,
receiving directions \(n_k\) with
\(\operatorname{dist}(n_k,\mathcal A)\to0\), and nontrivial closed fits
\((R_k,\lambda_k,b_k)\). A fit is called trivial exactly when
\(R_k\in\mathcal I\), \(\lambda_k=1\) and \(b_k=0\).

All parameters and rotations belong to compact sets. The bodies have a
uniformly bounded radius and, by (2), a uniformly positive minimum
shadow diameter. Inclusion therefore bounds \(\lambda_k\) uniformly.
Also \(0\in K_{p_k}\), since the hull contains the centrally paired
icosahedral points, so \(b_k\) lies in the receiving shadow and is
uniformly bounded. Pass to a subsequence. The twelve-element axis set is
finite, so a fixed proper body symmetry lets us arrange
\[
p_k\to p_0,\quad n_k\to n_0,\quad
R_k\to R_0,\quad\lambda_k\to\lambda_0,\quad b_k\to b_0.
\]

Projection and the finite point hulls are continuous. Closed inclusion
passes to the limit, giving a closed fit at \(p_0,n_0\). Section3 implies
\(R_0\in\mathcal I\), \(\lambda_0=1\) and \(b_0=0\). Right-compose
\(R_k\) by \(R_0^{-1}\); this leaves each moving body unchanged and
gives \(R_k\to I\). Every parameter point shares the same body group,
so this adjustment is valid for all \(k\).

Near the identity, write \(R_k=\exp([q_k]_\times)\), with \(q_k\to0\),
and put \(s_k=\lambda_k-1\ge0\). Because these fits are nontrivial,
\[
\eta_k=\sqrt{\|q_k\|^2+\|b_k\|^2+s_k^2}>0.
\]
After a further subsequence, their normalized vectors converge to
\((q,b,s)\) with norm one, \(s\ge0\), and \(b\perp N\). The latter
follows from \(b_k\perp n_k\). Uniformly for the bounded original
vertices,
\[
\lambda_kR_kv-v
=q_k\times v+s_kv+O((\|q_k\|+s_k)^2).
\tag{12}
\]
Apply (11) to the ten original endpoints, divide by \(\eta_k\), and
take limits. The supporting normals tend to
\((2\alpha(p_0)/\sqrt h)m_i\), whose common multiplier is positive.
Equation (12) gives exactly the ten inequalities (8), evaluated at
\(p_0\), for \((q,b,s)\). Section4 forces this unit vector to be zero,
a contradiction.

This proves a positive common neighborhood. Choosing a smaller radius
if necessary gives the stated closed caps
\(\operatorname{dist}(n,\mathcal A)\le\rho\). Every fit in them is the
identical shadow, which cannot be contained in its own interior. The
theorem follows.

**Corollary (qualitative diameter gap).** There is \(c>0\), also uniform
on the box, such that every receiving direction of a strict passage
satisfies
\[
\operatorname{diam}(\pi_nK_p)^2\ge\delta(r)^2+c.
\]
Indeed strict passages lie outside the closed caps. On the compact set
of parameters and directions with \(\operatorname{dist}(n,\mathcal A)
\ge\rho\), the continuous nonnegative function
\(\operatorname{diam}(\pi_nK_p)^2-\delta(r)^2\) has no zero by (2),
and hence has a positive minimum. No numeric \(c\) is asserted.

## 7. Evidence and limits

The exact checker verifies the finite polygon, support, edge-length,
group-action and contact-span premises. Sections3--6 are the written
geometric proof, including closed-fit completeness, actual supporting
normals in changing planes, and the compactness/linearization argument.
The existence of a neighborhood is not inferred from a sampled or
unsuccessful passage search. Floating-point reconnaissance only suggested
the polygon indices. It supplies no proof premise or radius.

The positive neighborhood is useful even without an explicit radius:
all limiting closed minimum fits are classified, and the contacts needed
for an effective radius are now identified. The next task is to replace
compactness with quantitative bounds on source localization, roll and
translation. Receiving directions away from these caps remain unresolved.

The general local-exclusion principle and the translated support-gradient
method have existing literature and published campaign precedents, listed
with exact references in [README.md](README.md). No priority is asserted
for those principles. This contribution's scoped information is the
thirty-gon and closed-fit classification, the paired original contacts,
and the resulting uniform all-source neighborhood for (1).
