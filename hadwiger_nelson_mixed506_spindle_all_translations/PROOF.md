# Every translation at the fixed mixed506 rotation (7+i sqrt(15))/8

Let `A159,V214` be the exact archived source sets in
[the source package](../hadwiger_nelson_nonmono159_214_lowden2/SOURCE.md), and set

\[
\nu=(5+i\sqrt{11})/6,\qquad B=A159\cup\nu A159,
\qquad u=(7+i\sqrt{15})/8.
\]

**Theorem (exact computer-assisted fixed-family closure).** For every
complex translation `h`, the entire strict Euclidean unit-distance graph
on `B union (u V214+h)` is four-colourable. The sources have 292 and 214
vertices, so disjoint unions have 506 vertices. The same assertion holds
for `u bar(V214)+h`, because the archived second source is invariant under
complex conjugation.

There is no denominator, magnitude, or algebraicity restriction on `h`.
The relative rotation and the two source sets are fixed. No other angle,
inner composition, or sealed Parts family is closed by this theorem.
No five-chromatic graph or improvement of the 509-vertex benchmark is found.

The [preceding overlap certificate](../hadwiger_nelson_mixed505_spindle_rotation/PROOF.md)
closes every translation for which the sources intersect. This package
closes all remaining disjoint translations using exact intersections of
unit circles. It does not apply the earlier trace-1/2 colouring rule to
trace 7/4. The [previous all-translation result](../hadwiger_nelson_mixed506_fixed_rotation/PROOF.md)
concerns the different rotation `(1+i sqrt(15))/4`.

## 1. Sources, coordinate fields and unrestricted translations

Put `alpha=i sqrt(3)`, `beta=i sqrt(11)`,

\[
E=\mathbb Q(\alpha,\beta),\quad
F=\mathbb Q(\sqrt{33},\sqrt5),\quad K=E(\sqrt5)=F+\alpha F.
\]

Here `alpha^2=-3`, `beta=alpha sqrt(33)/3`, and complex conjugation fixes
`F` and negates `alpha`. The real subfield of `K` is exactly `F`.
The biquadratic field `E` has quadratic subfields generated by
`sqrt(-3), sqrt(-11), sqrt(33)`, so `sqrt(5) notin E` and `u notin E`.
Also `u=(7+alpha sqrt(5))/8` has norm one. The field `K` is closed under
complex conjugation.

The exact source reconstruction gives `|B|=292`, with 1,251 strict unit
edges, and `|V214|=214`, with 977 strict unit edges. The producer and audit
assemble the sources independently, and all stored source colourings are
checked directly. A source integer tuple `(a,b,c,d)` means

\[
(a+b\sqrt{33}+c\alpha+d\beta)/72.
\]

The second source's archived denominator 12 is converted to 72.

For each potential cross contact `e=(p,q)` define an offset centre
`z_e=p-uq in K`. There are 62,488 distinct centres: equality of two would
give `p-p'=u(q-q')`; since `u notin E`, both differences must vanish.
The cross contact is a unit edge at translation `h` exactly when

\[
|h-z_e|=1.\tag{1}
\]

If a disjoint placement has at most two cross edges, take any proper
four-colouring of each source and permute one. Each edge forbids six of
the 24 permutations, leaving at least twelve choices. Such a placement
is four-colourable.

If it has at least three contacts, three distinct centres lie on the
unit circle about `h`; they are noncollinear. Subtracting their squared
unit-distance equations gives, with `w_i=z_i-z_0` and `k=h-z_0`,

\[
k\bar w_i+\bar k w_i=N(w_i),\qquad i=1,2.
\]

The determinant `bar(w_1)w_2-bar(w_2)w_1` is nonzero. Solving over `K`
shows `h in K`. Thus **every disjoint placement that might fail
four-colourability is represented by a translation in K with at least
two contacts**. Translations outside `K` are covered by the elementary
permutation argument, rather than silently excluded by the computation.

## 2. Complete two-circle criterion

Let two distinct centres have difference `w in K`, and put `d=N(w)`.
Then `d in F`, and its value in the specified Euclidean embedding is
positive. The two unit circles have their intersection centres in `K`
if and only if

\[
f=\frac{d(4-d)}3\quad\text{is a square in }F.\tag{2}
\]

If `t in F` satisfies `t^2=f`, all their intersections relative to the
first centre are

\[
c_\pm=\frac w2\ \pm\ \frac{\alpha w t}{2d}.\tag{3}
\]

For completeness, a point equidistant from `0,w` has the form
`w/2+alpha w a` for real `a`: the quotient of its displacement from the
midpoint by `w` is purely imaginary. If the point is in `K`, then
`a in F`. Its unit norm is equivalent to
`d(1/4+3a^2)=1`. Taking `t=2da` proves necessity of (2) and (3).
Conversely, substitution into the norm verifies both unit distances.
Because `t` is real, a solution automatically has `d<=4`. For `d=4`
there is one tangent intersection; otherwise a square gives two distinct
intersections. If (2) has no square root in `F`, neither intersection is
in `K`. The case `w=0` is excluded: it cannot describe two distinct
contact labels.

For an actual pair of contact labels `e_0=(p_0,q_0)`, `e_1=(p_1,q_1)`,
put `x=p_1-p_0`, `y=q_1-q_0`. Then `w=x-u y`.
The source difference sets, including zero, have 17,337 and 4,419 elements.
They give 76,612,203 ordered pairs, of which exactly one has `x=y=0`.
All **76,612,202 other pairs** are considered. In particular `x=0` or
`y=0` is allowed; pairs sharing a source endpoint are not omitted.

## 3. Exact finite square screening and certificates

All coefficients of `f` in the basis `1,sqrt(33),sqrt(5),sqrt(165)` have
denominators divisible only by 2 and 3. We use necessary square tests at
splitting primes. For an odd prime `p` not dividing `3*5*11`, and roots
`r^2=33`, `s^2=5` modulo `p`, a square in `F` must evaluate to a square
or zero under `sqrt(33)->r`, `sqrt(5)->s`.

This remains valid even if a proposed square root has unknown denominators.
Hensel lifting embeds `F` into `Q_p` with the specified nonzero root
residues. The evaluated `f` is integral. If `f=t^2`, the image of `t` has
nonnegative valuation and therefore a residue whose square is the
residue of `f`. We do not assume a homomorphism from all of `F` into a
finite field, or a denominator bound on a square root.

The producer uses modular Cartesian coordinates at the following primes.
For each prime it tests all four signs of `sqrt(33)=sqrt(3)sqrt(11)` and
`sqrt(5)`. The first square root is held fixed and the last two signs vary.
After obtaining `d`, it tests `d(4-d)/3` for being a quadratic residue,
including zero.

| Prime | sqrt(3) | sqrt(5) | sqrt(11) | Pairs remaining after all four tests |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 131 | 38 | 23 | 50 | 5,121,159 |
| 181 | 33 | 27 | 83 | 352,681 |
| 229 | 71 | 66 | 34 | 31,567 |
| 239 | 106 | 31 | 49 | 9,427 |
| 359 | 163 | 148 | 27 | 7,567 |
| 421 | 74 | 200 | 46 | 7,347 |

Primality, root identities and all residue tables are checked. Zero
residues are retained, so reduction of a nonzero distance to zero causes
no exception. The complete 24-stage stream and ordered survivor hash are
recorded in `expected.json`.

Exact square-root generation in the tower
`Q subset Q(sqrt(33)) subset F` proposes centres for 7,028 surviving
nonzero difference pairs. They produce 13,936 relative centres: 120
tangent pairs with one centre, and 6,908 pairs with two. A verifier then
checks every proposed centre using generic real-radical arithmetic:
its distances from `0` and `w` are exactly one, the two-centre cases are
distinct, and one-centre cases have exactly `d=4`. Thus all intersections
of every positive pair are certified by their geometry. Completeness of
the square-root generator is not a verification assumption.

Exactly 318 other nonzero pairs remain after the first screen. Every one
has a nonsquare witness already at **p=29**, using one of
`r=+/-2`, `s=+/-11`, where `r^2=33`, `s^2=5` modulo 29. The audit evaluates
`f` in the real-field representation and checks `f^14=-1 mod29` for each
rejected pair. This certifies all negative answers independently of the
square-root algorithm. The final survivor is the zero/zero pair, which
is explicitly discarded. These three disjoint lists partition every
survivor of the complete first screen.

The first six primes deliberately support modular Cartesian roots.
Such primes split the larger real Cartesian field as well as `F`; their
tests alone do not decide squareness in `F`. The additional prime-29
certificates resolve that field boundary. Passing the first screen is
never treated as proving (2).

## 4. From difference pairs to all relevant translations

For each certified relative centre `c` of a difference pair `(x,y)`,
project every incidence

\[
p_1-p_0=x,\quad q_1-q_0=y
\]

to the actual translation

\[
h=p_0-uq_0+c.\tag{4}
\]

Retain only contact-label pairs with `e_0<e_1` in the fixed encoding
`e=214*i+j`. This removes the duplication from reversing the pair.
Both circle intersections, when present, are retained.

Every projected `h` has both listed contacts. Conversely, every `h in K`
with at least two contacts arises from (4). Moreover the accumulated
contact set of a retained translation is its **entire** strict cross-edge
set: any actual contact can be paired with another, and the corresponding
difference pair and intersection have been enumerated. This justifies
checking the accumulated graph without testing 62,488 distances anew at
each translation.

All equal translations are identified by exact rational coordinate keys.
A translation with an overlap is exactly one of the 62,488 offsets `z_e`;
such a key is discarded using the preceding overlap theorem. No colouring
of that already closed family is re-enumerated here.

There are 14,001,884 projected unordered-contact-pair intersection events.
Of these, 10,125,443 belong to the 62,488 overlap translations. The others
combine to **1,438,349 distinct disjoint translations in K with at least

## 5. Complete positive colouring cover

Every component row is checked against every strict source edge.
For each disjoint translation, choose source rows `b,v` and a permutation
`pi` of four colours satisfying

\[
b[i]\ne\pi(v[j])\quad\text{for every recorded cross edge }(i,j).\tag{5}
\]

This colours the entire strict union, since the source vertices are
distinct and their internal edges are already proper. The final library
has eleven rows for `B` and ten for `V`; two additional rows, one per
source, are supplied here as a 508-byte positive certificate. The
preceding rows are imported and checked from the earlier packages.
The verifier finds and explicitly checks a witness for all 1,438,349
translations, with zero residuals.

The inherited ten/nine-row library misses exactly one translation:

\[
h=-\frac78-\frac{3\sqrt5}{16}
   -\frac{\alpha(9+2\sqrt5)}{16}.
\]

It has six cross contacts, encoded
`17073,18143,19132,44679,53453,61585`. A bounded SAT query returned a
positive four-colouring. It is not a counterexample to four-colourability;
it is only a failure of the inherited library. The independent direct
control reconstructs all 506 physical points, tests all 127,765 point
pairs, and obtains exactly 2,234 strict edges. It verifies a new positive
colouring on every edge and rejects a one-colour mutation.

The generator used CaDiCaL195 through `python-sat==1.8.dev24`, with a
one-million-conflict budget for this single query. Four variables per
vertex encode exactly one of four colours, each edge forbids equal
endpoint colours, and source vertex zero is assigned colour zero by
colour symmetry. The CNF has 2,024 variables and 12,479 clauses. Only the
explicit SAT colouring is retained as evidence. Proof replay requires
no solver, no UNSAT claim, and no solver-completeness assumption.

Combining the complete finite cover, the at-most-two-contact argument,
and the imported overlap certificate proves the theorem for every
complex translation.

## 6. Independent validation and exact trust boundary

The optional C++ filter accelerates the initial finite screen. Its output
was compared entry by entry with a Python reference on 128 complete rows,
and the checking build with address/undefined-behaviour sanitizers covered
that same 565,632-pair domain. A complete independent Python scan then
checked all 76,612,203 pairs, using `F+alpha F` and the norm `a^2+3b^2`
instead of modular Cartesian coordinates. All stages and survivor entries
matched, not merely their totals. Fixed-width arithmetic is confined to
the accelerator: input moduli are at most 10,000, and its largest squared
coordinate sum is below `2*10000^2`, evaluated in signed 64-bit integers.
The standard-library Python screen is also available directly.

The main audit reconstructs both sources using independently pinned
radical multiplication. It checks all positive circle intersections with
generic integer arithmetic in
`Q(sqrt(3),sqrt(5),sqrt(11))`, and every negative square witness at 29.
It independently reconstructs source incidences by endpoint lookup and
projects all events in a **Cartesian radical basis**, including its own
denominator normalization and overlap test. Only after grouping does it
convert back to the producer's `F+alpha F` keys.

It then compares every translation, every accumulated cross-edge list,
and every selected colour witness with the producer's complete stream,
entry by entry. The full 1,438,349-record stream matches. Separate controls
cover zero and tangent circle cases, a pair with two intersections, a
nonintersecting pair, 81 explicitly squared small field elements, and the
fact that `sqrt(3)` is outside `F`.

These are independent author implementations, not external review or
proof-assistant formalization. The trust boundary is the archived source
coordinates, the imported overlap certificate, the unformalized elementary
field/circle and finite-reduction arguments, complete finite enumeration,
checked positive colourings, and ordinary exact integer execution. The
audit does not trust completeness of a symbolic square-root routine or
any SAT verdict. No floating-point equality is used.

The [README](README.md) gives complete reproduction commands, dependency
pins, runtime observations and local storage requirements. The large
regenerated translation stream stays local; source and compact expected
results suffice to reproduce all checks.

Primary record context remains Parts,
[Graph minimization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.12665), and the 509-vertex
benchmark in [Haugland's introduction](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04542v4).
The latest Haugland abstract was checked on 2026-09-05 and still identifies
v4 of 17 August 2026 as its latest version. No novelty priority is claimed
for the circle-intersection or square-screening methods. This bounded
fixed-family milestone is complete; another construction phase is not
part of this result.
