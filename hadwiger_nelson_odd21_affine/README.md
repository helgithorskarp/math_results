# Every affine image of the 21-point odd-distance spindle is four-colourable

**Exact computer-assisted theorem.** Let P be the 21-point configuration below,
obtained from Figure 1 and Corollary 1 of Ardal, Manuch, Rosenfeld, Shelah and
Stacho, *The Odd-Distance Plane Graph* (2009). For every real affine map
`f: R² -> R²`, the strict unit-distance graph on the distinct points of `f(P)`
has maximum degree at most four and is **3-degenerate**, hence four-colourable.
All its vertex- and edge-deleted subgraphs inherit these bounds. Singular maps,
including their vertex coincidences, are covered.

The motivating graph on P, with edges at distances **1, 3, 5 or 7**, has
chromatic number five: it has 21 vertices and 55 edges. The theorem closes the
proposal to homogenize enough of those distances, or introduce different unit
contacts, by an arbitrary affine deformation. It is a complete decision for
this fixed geometric source, not a record improvement or an exclusion of
nonlinear deformations, other drawings, or auxiliary-vertex gadgets.

The transferable method is a finite **incident-direction metric test**. It
reduces all affine images of any given finite planar set to at most
`n * binomial(n-1,3)` linear systems over its coordinate field, plus a
2-degenerate remainder. This package applies that test to P. It also performs
a separate, larger global enumeration using different exact arithmetic.

## Source configuration

In ordinary Euclidean coordinates write `h(n,m)=(n+m/2, sqrt(3)*m/2)` and take
the following eleven lattice points, in the stated label order:

| Label | Name | (n,m) |
|---:|---|---|
| 0 | A | (0,0) |
| 1 | B | (8,0) |
| 2 | C | (3,0) |
| 3 | D | (0,3) |
| 4 | E | (5,3) |
| 5 | F | (5,0) |
| 6 | G | (0,5) |
| 7 | H | (3,5) |
| 8 | I1 | (2,3) |
| 9 | I2 | (3,3) |
| 10 | I3 | (3,2) |

Call this set B0. Define `rho=(127+i*sqrt(255))/128` in complex notation and
`P=B0 union rho*B0`. The rotation fixes A; the other ten rotated points receive
labels 11 through 20 in the original order. Since `|rho|=1` and
`|8-8*rho|=1`, this is the spindle in the cited corollary. All 21 points are
distinct, as checked in two exact coordinate implementations.

The primary source is the authors' [published manuscript](https://shelah.logic.at/files/95939/923.pdf),
Figure 1 on journal page 135 (PDF page 4). The table above transcribes the
marked lattice positions; no image, external coordinate file, or numerical
embedding is needed to run the proof. No novelty is claimed for this spindle
or its original five-colour argument.

For completeness, suppose a four-colouring of the 11-point distance graph
gives A and B different colours, named 0 and 1. The odd edges force
`C,E,G` to colour 2 and `D,F,H` to colour 3, successively in the order
`C,D,E,F,G,H`. Each of I1, I2 and I3 is distance 3 from a vertex in each of
these two colour classes: use the pairs `(E,F)`, `(C,D)` and `(G,H)`.
Their mutual distances are one, so they cannot all use just colours 0 and 1.
Thus A and B must agree. The two copies then contradict their unit bridge
`B--rho*B`. The checked five-colouring in [source_expected.json](source_expected.json)
proves equality, rather than only a lower bound of five.

[source_check.py](source_check.py) independently exhausts the half's
endpoint-separating four-colour search (13 nodes), the whole four-colour
search (1,881 nodes), and checks the five-colouring. Exact reconstruction gives
7 edges of length 1, 26 of length 3, 14 of length 5, and 8 of length 7.
Only the seven unit edges occur in the undeformed strict unit-distance graph.

## Complete affine reduction

For computation replace an ordinary point `(x,y)` by coefficient coordinates
`(X,Y)=(256*x,256*y/sqrt(3))`. This one fixed invertible linear change does not
alter the family of all affine images. In these coordinates the points lie
in `K²`, where `K=Q(sqrt(85))` and the positive square root is intended.
The first half is

```
X = 256*n + 128*m,   Y = 128*m.
```

The second half is generated by

```
(X,Y) -> ((127*X-3*sqrt(85)*Y)/128,
          (sqrt(85)*X+127*Y)/128).
```

All resulting field coefficients are integers. The transformation acts on
these coefficient coordinates, not on unscaled Euclidean coordinates.

For an invertible affine map, translations disappear from pair differences.
Its squared distance is a positive-definite quadratic form

```
q(X,Y) = a*X² + 2*b*X*Y + c*Y²,   a>0, ac-b²>0.
```

Conversely every such form comes from a real invertible linear map. A pair
with difference `(u,v)` is an edge precisely when
`(u²,2uv,v²) dot (a,b,c) = 1`. All pairs are tested; source long edges never
become constraints merely because of their original chromatic role.

Three nonzero vectors in three distinct unoriented directions give three
linearly independent rows of this form. For example, choose the first two
vectors as a linear basis; the third then has both coordinates nonzero,
making the determinant nonzero. Hence three incident unit-edge directions
determine at most one metric. The solution lies in K, even though the affine
map itself need not have algebraic coefficients.

Enumerate every vertex and every unordered triple of the other vertices.
Solve the three equations and retain every positive-definite solution.
Singular triples are safely skipped: every triple of distinct directions
is nonsingular. Nonpositive solutions cannot be metrics of invertible maps.
Duplicates are identified by exact rational coefficients.

**Remainder lemma.** If a metric is absent from this list, each vertex has
unit neighbours in at most two unoriented directions. Each such line has at
most two neighbours, one on either side, so maximum degree is at most four.
In any nonempty induced subgraph choose a vertex uniquely maximizing a
generic linear functional. Along each incident line it has at most one
neighbour, since only the inward ray is available. Its degree is at most two.
Thus every unlisted metric gives a 2-degenerate graph.

For every listed metric the checker reconstructs all edges, verifies maximum
degree at most four, and repeatedly removes a vertex of degree below four.
It reconstructs and directly checks a proper four-colouring in reverse
removal order. This proves 3-degeneracy and four-colourability on the entire
invertible family. For a rank-one affine map, the distinct image points lie
on a line, and their unit-distance graph is a disjoint union of finite paths.
A rank-zero image is a single point. This covers all affine maps.

## Exact census and two verification routes

| Quantity | Incident triples | Global triples of difference rows |
|---|---:|---:|
| Triples examined | 23,940 | 695,520 |
| Singular coefficient matrices | 1,880 | 13,198 |
| Nonsingular nonpositive solutions | 13,350 | 440,934 |
| Positive-definite solutions before deduplication | 8,710 | 241,388 |
| Distinct positive-definite metrics | 8,345 | 236,214 |

The local metrics comprise 8,296 graphs of maximum degree three and 49 of
maximum degree four. Every one is 3-degenerate. Counts refer to metrics,
not isomorphism classes of graphs. The remaining continuous family is covered
by the remainder lemma, not by rounding or sampling.

[verify.py](verify.py) uses `Fraction` arithmetic and Gaussian elimination in
K on incident triples. [full_audit.py](full_audit.py) independently constructs
the coordinates from doubled Cartesian coefficient rows, enumerates all
triples of the 162 distinct unoriented difference rows, and obtains metrics
by integer cross products and conjugate rationalization. This larger route
also proves 3-degeneracy for all 236,214 metrics; metrics with fewer than three
global edge directions are 2-degenerate by the same extremal-point argument.

As a cross-check, the global audit selects exactly those metrics whose
reconstructed graphs have a vertex with three incident directions. The
resulting 8,345 canonical metrics have the same SHA-256 as the local
enumeration:

```
5ee06794bd29839ea11ad2d3c38936b39302bdab13af7ade7fbc1f2cf588225d
```

This compares different enumeration reductions and linear algebra methods;
both still depend on the written geometric argument and ordinary exact
arithmetic execution. It is internal validation, not independent peer review
or proof-assistant formalization.

## Reproduce

Python 3.11 or later and the standard library suffice, from the repository root:

```sh
python3 hadwiger_nelson_odd21_affine/verify.py
python3 hadwiger_nelson_odd21_affine/source_check.py
python3 hadwiger_nelson_odd21_affine/controls.py
python3 hadwiger_nelson_odd21_affine/full_audit.py
```

The local check takes roughly half a minute; the optional global audit takes
a few minutes on one CPU. Expected reports are committed as compact JSON.
All coordinates, metrics and colourings are regenerated. There is no SAT
dependency, floating-point predicate, unprovided certificate, or large input
file. Controls check field signs against exact rational enclosures, compare
all 210 source-pair rows, recover prescribed forms, and compare degeneracy
with its induced-subset definition on all simple graphs through order five.

The trust boundary is the explicit source table, exact field arithmetic,
finite enumeration completeness, the elementary degeneracy argument, and
Python execution integrity. The target benchmark remains Parts' 509 vertices,
as reported in [Parts' original paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.12665) and
[Haugland's August 2026 introduction](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04542v4#S1),
checked on 2026-09-08. No result in this package improves that benchmark.

This closes and retires affine homogenization of this fixed 21-point source.
No extra source, auxiliary-vertex ladder, or nonlinear deformation experiment
is included or started here.
