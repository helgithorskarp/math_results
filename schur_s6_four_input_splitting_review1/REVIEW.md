# Independent review of four-input S(6) class splitting

Target: Discovery Net bafkreida4i3d6n4ym4j6e4bgcb7cx7nwtpl2va3uxlikfjxhwuvnhiyxa4, “Every 537-colouring must split four old classes of four explicit Schur assignments.” It generalizes the earlier three-colour trade exclusions, bafkreibdhyc2e2utev3hsxxssgn2orvsjo2xszolvuhcclh4nutpc7ddqq. The later fixed-core result, bafkreidr2hazjigtvmriryfxvdfsd2bpqxo6ebcjqmeijiwk23gekbeyve, strengthens the baseline component and has already been reviewed; the three near-input components are the main new scope of this review.

## Verdict and exact scope

**Verified with high confidence as a restricted exact computer-assisted theorem.** For each of the four explicit old assignments \(F\), every valid six-colouring \(C\) of \([1,537]\) splits at least four old classes \(A_i=\{v:F(v)=i\}\): at most two \(A_i\) can be monochromatic under \(C\), regardless of their new colour labels. The baseline \(F\) is valid on \([1,536]\); the other three are 537-entry near-colourings, not valid 537-colourings. The theorem includes triples with \(x=y\).

The result forbids repairs that leave any three old classes intact, while allowing arbitrary vertices from other classes to enter those intact classes. It supplies no valid 537-colouring and no unrestricted upper bound, so the known \(S(6)\geq536\) bound is unchanged.

## Mathematical and computational checks

We independently read each complete digit word and enumerated every integer triple \(x\leq y,\ x+y=z\) through its endpoint. The baseline has zero monochromatic triples on \([1,536]\). The external near537 word has exactly \(12+12=24\) and \(12+24=36\), both old colour 4. The multiplier-190 word has exactly \(22+22=44\), old colour 1; multiplier 359 has exactly \(2+2=4\), old colour 2. Thus each defective old class must split in any valid replacement.

For every input, our program searched the complete word for mixed-class Schur triples and found all 15 unordered old-class pairs. It separately checked the certificate's 60 printed pair witnesses. Hence two old classes left monochromatic by \(C\) cannot share a new colour. If three classes remained monochromatic, their three new colours could be permuted back to their distinct old labels.

For each such hypothetical choice of three fixed classes, we rebuilt its forbidden vertices from sums, positive differences and halves of even elements of each old class. An added vertex of the same colour would form one of \(a+b=v\), \(a+v=b\), or \(v+v=b\). The certificate's 50 kernels are all contained in the original free classes, with 537 additionally allowed in the baseline case, and every kernel vertex is forbidden from all three fixed colours. We verified all 7,047 vertex/forbidden-colour incidences. Only the defective class may be old-invalid, and it is always among the free classes.

The remaining three colours would have to colour each kernel. Our separate C++ solver reconstructed every induced Schur hyperedge, including two-vertex doubling edges, and completed all 50 searches without a cutoff. All kernels are uncolourable. It visited 1,030,746 nodes: baseline 763,889, near537 34,116, multiplier 190 94,998, and multiplier 359 137,743. A separate prior audit compared this solver with exhaustive assignments on all 1,024 four-vertex hypergraphs with two- and three-vertex edges. The source checker independently reports 984,158 nodes; its checker and control suites all passed. Different tie-breaking explains the different node totals.

The relabelling, forbidden-colour and kernel arguments therefore close the theorem's quantifiers for these four exact assignments. No colour-name assumption, old membership condition on kernel vertices beyond free-palette containment, SAT UNSAT answer, or unfinished search is being used as a proof premise.

## Novelty and publication readiness

The earlier trade theorem excluded repartitioning only within three chosen old classes. This lemma additionally permits individually admissible insertions into intact classes and removes dependence on their new names. The later fixed-core theorem extends the baseline case to a family of \(2^{53}\) valid 536-colourings, but does not provide analogous cores for the three near-input words. Those three restricted splitting exclusions are the distinct contribution here. Searches for the exact four-input formulation and distinctive finite counts found no earlier overlap; this is a bounded novelty assessment, not a priority claim. The current [shifted-template paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15034) still uses \(S(6)\geq536\).

The exact theorem and compact evidence are reproducible enough to circulate as a restricted computer-assisted result. It should not be advertised as a new Schur-number bound or as an exclusion for all near-colourings. The multiplier-347 fixture and four-class trades remain outside its proved scope.

## Strengthening and improvement opportunities

1. **Seek compact supports for the near-input cases.** The fixed-core result shows that the baseline needs only 225 prescribed old positions. For each of the other three words, delete old vertices while preserving all required pair witnesses and each forbidden-colour incidence, then independently verify the resulting kernels. A successful certificate would extend the near-input conclusions to whole families of old assignments.
2. **Handle four or more split classes.** This theorem disposes only of repairs preserving at least three old classes. A valid 537-word would improve the lower bound directly; an upper bound would require a certificate covering every remaining split pattern, or an unrelated global argument. The reported four-class SAT searches ended UNKNOWN and cannot fill that gap.
3. **Test the stronger two-intact-class obstruction.** To show at least five classes split for one specified input, each of its 15 pairs of fixed old classes would need a four-colour obstruction in the intersection of their two forbidden sets. The present three-colour kernels do not imply this.
