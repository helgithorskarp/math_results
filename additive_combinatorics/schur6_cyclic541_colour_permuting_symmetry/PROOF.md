# Colour-permuting multiplier groups at modulus 541

This is a restriction on symmetric constructions for the sixth classical
Schur number. It gives no new lower or upper bound for S(6), and does not
exclude an unrestricted colouring of [1,537] or [1,540].

## Theorem

Let c:[1,540] -> {1,...,6} avoid every monochromatic integer x+y=z,
including x=y, and suppose c(x)=c(541-x). Define

    G = {a in F_541^* : some permutation sigma of the six colours
                        satisfies c(ax)=sigma(c(x)) for every nonzero x}.

Products ax in this definition are taken modulo 541 in [1,540]. Then

    |G| belongs to {2,4,6,10,12}.

In particular |G| <= 12, even though its multipliers may permute colours.
The following are necessary possibilities for the action of a generator;
they are not existence claims.

| Group order | Cycle type on the six colours |
| --- | --- |
| 2 | identity |
| 4 | identity, (2)(1)(1)(1)(1), (2)(2)(1)(1), or (2)(2)(2) |
| 6 | (3)(3) |
| 10 | (5)(1) |
| 12 | (3)(3) or (6) |

The new finite computation proves that the otherwise possible case |G|=20,
with generator acting as (5)(1), cannot occur. This strengthens the earlier
[colour-preserving kernel result](../schur6_cyclic541_multiplier_obstruction/PROOF.md),
which did not exclude multipliers that move colour labels.

## Algebraic reduction

Reflection turns integer sum-freeness into modular sum-freeness: if a
congruence wraps, x+y=z+541 implies

    (541-x)+(541-y)=541-z.

Sums with modular result zero are outside the domain and are not forbidden.
All six colours must occur. One elementary justification, avoiding reliance
on the exact value of S(5), is the triangle-Ramsey recurrence. If q_r bounds
the order of a complete graph whose edges have r colours and no monochromatic
triangle, then q_1=2 and q_r <= r*q_(r-1)+1. At a vertex, each monochromatic
neighbourhood has no edge of that colour and therefore has at most q_(r-1)
vertices. The resulting bounds are 2,5,16,65,326 for r=1,...,5. Colouring an
edge {i,j} of K_541 by c(|i-j|) would give a triangle-free five-colouring if
c used at most five colours, a contradiction.

Because every colour occurs, the permutation associated with a multiplier
in G is unique. Thus G is a subgroup of the cyclic group F_541^*, with a
homomorphism to S_6. Its kernel K fixes each colour individually. The earlier
exact kernel result gives |K| in {2,4}: it excludes a factor 5 using the
maximum of 80 for order-10-invariant sum-free sets, and excludes a factor 3
using the monochromatic sixth-root triple 1+129=130. Reflection puts -1 in K.

The order of a permutation in S_6 belongs to {1,2,3,4,5,6}. Since G is cyclic,
|G|=|K|*|image(G)| and |G| divides 540. The possible orders are therefore

    2,4,6,10,12,20.

If |G|=20, its image must have order 5 and its kernel order 4. Any permutation
of order 5 in S_6 is conjugate to pi=(1 2 3 4 5)(6). The unique subgroup of
order 20 in F_541^* is generated by h=497. It remains to exclude

    c(hx)=pi(c(x)) for every nonzero x.                    (1)

The case |G|=6 has an image of order 3. No colour can be fixed by this image:
a nonempty fixed class would contain a scalar multiple of the sixth-root
triple. Thus its cycle type is (3)(3). For |G|=12, the image order is 3 or 6.
In the first case the same argument gives (3)(3). In the second case, squaring
the generator gives the order-6 subgroup, so the square of its permutation
must have no fixed colours. Of the order-6 cycle types, only (6) has this
property. Orders 2,4,10 give the remaining entries of the table directly.

## Exact finite exclusion of (1)

The subgroup H=<497> has order 20 and 27 multiplicative cosets. Order the
cosets by their least positive representatives r_i, starting at i=0. A colouring
satisfying (1) is determined by one state a_i in {0,...,5} for each coset,
where colours are now indexed from zero. At r_i*h^j its colour is

    (a_i+j) mod 5  if a_i<5,
    5              if a_i=5.

This is consistent after 20 steps. Since h^10=-1 and pi^10 is the identity,
it also guarantees reflection symmetry.

We normalize a_0=0 without losing a solution. Some nonfixed colour must occur:
otherwise the entire interval has the single fixed colour, contradicting
1+1=2. Scale an occurrence of a nonfixed colour to residue 1, then rotate
the five cycling labels so its colour is 0. These operations preserve (1)
and modular sum-freeness.

Boolean X_(i,a) means a_i=a. We impose exactly one state per coset. For each
nonzero modular triple x+y=z and each possible common colour, we forbid the
corresponding assignment to its coset states. If repeated coset indices demand
different states, that monochromatic assignment is already impossible; if
they demand the same state, duplicate literals are removed. In particular,
equal summands and binary constraints are included.

`encode.py` reduces the first summand to its coset representative. Multiplying
a triple by h^-j applies the same power of pi to all its colours, preserving
monochromaticity; hence this reduction is complete. The canonical CNF has
162 variables and 13,420 clauses, including the normalization unit.

`audit.py` independently generates the entire CNF by all 145,800 unordered
nonzero modular pairs, including repeated summands and wrapping sums. It
constructs the subgroup as the roots of X^20=1, propagates the actual colour
permutation around each coset, and imports no reduced-encoder routines. Its
clause set agrees with the reduced encoder entry by entry. Exhaustive state
truth tables at moduli 41 and 61 provide a satisfiable and an unsatisfiable
control, with 36 and 216 assignments respectively. Direct modular sum checking
agrees with the CNF on every control assignment.

## Certificate and independent verification

Glucose 3, through python-sat 1.9.dev15, produces a clausal refutation of the
normalized CNF. `rup.py`, which imports no solver library, verifies every
addition by reverse unit propagation (RUP). For a proposed clause C it assumes
all literals in C false, propagates units, and requires a contradiction.
Therefore each accepted C is a logical consequence of the original formula
and previously checked consequences. The final accepted clause is empty,
proving unsatisfiability. Deletion records are ignored: retaining verified
consequences does not change the formula's satisfying assignments.

The checked certificate has 24,594 records: 11,460 accepted additions and
13,134 ignored deletions. It occupies 863,049 bytes. It is regenerated in
memory and checked on every run, then discarded; the source repository does
not store a proof dump. `expected.json` records its digest, the canonical CNF
digest, and exact verification counts. Reproduction needs no unpublished
input or externally stored certificate.

The checker maintains two watched literals for each nonunit clause; each
RUP check starts with fresh assignments while retaining valid watch positions.
`checker_controls.py` compares this propagation with a full-clause scan on
seeded small formulas, and checks accepted implications against complete truth
tables. It also rejects an invalid lemma, a truncated proof, a spurious empty
clause, an out-of-domain literal, and a malformed record. All validation uses
explicit exceptions, with no dependence on Python assertions.

As a separate check, upstream DRAT-trim at commit
`2e3b2dc0ecf938addbd779d42877b6ed69d9a985` verified the same CNF and trace
and used zero RAT lemmas. Its report contains a harmless unmatched-deletion
notice; the independent RUP checker ignores all deletions by design.

This proves the exclusion of (1), removes order 20 from the algebraic list,
and proves the theorem and action table.

## Scope and trust boundary

The theorem combines the published order-10 kernel lemma, the displayed group
argument, a fully audited finite CNF encoding, and an independently checked
clausal certificate. Solver soundness is not assumed: the solver is only a
certificate producer. The source still depends on Python execution, exact
integer arithmetic, and the correctness of the small verifier. It has not been
formalized in a proof assistant or independently reviewed by another researcher.

Order 4,6,10,12 constructions remain open here. Exploratory bounded runs for
order 10 with a five-cycle, order 6 with two three-cycles, and both order-12
actions returned UNKNOWN, not impossibility certificates. No 537-colouring or
new global S(6) bound is asserted.

The finite order-20 exclusion and resulting action table were not found in
targeted current primary-source and committed-graph searches on 2026-09-28.
This is a search-relative novelty statement, not a priority claim. Multiplicative
equivalence of symmetric Schur partitions is already used by Fredricksen and
Sweet; the general group-action and SAT proof methods are standard.
