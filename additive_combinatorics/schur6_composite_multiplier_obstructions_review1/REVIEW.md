The mathematical bridge also checks out. Strict reflection converts each wrapping modular triple to an integer triple. The order-three and order-five CRT multipliers separate moved colours on one axis from fixed colours on the other. Axis requirements of five colours on \(\mathbf Z/49\) (from \(S(4)=44\)) and three on \(\mathbf Z/11\) give lower bounds nine and ten; the complete words attain them. ## Scope, novelty, and trust boundary

The claim applies only to **strictly reflection-symmetric** classical colourings, with \(x=y\) included. It does not rule out a general six-colouring of \([1,537]\) or \([1,538]\), nor the remaining multiplier groups of order two or four. The nine- and ten-colour words are modular examples, not six-colour witnesses.

[Fredricksen–Sweet (2000)](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/view/v7i1r32) establishes the \(S(6)\ge536\) baseline; [Penfold Street–Wallis (1976)](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-the-australian-mathematical-society/article/sumfree-sets-coloured-graphs-and-designs/7CB4302CC906B92797B934C93A7DD235) treats automorphisms of sum-free colourings; [Heule (2018)](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/download/12209/12068) states the imported \(S(4)=44\) in the largest-colourable-endpoint convention. Candidate-specific searches for the exact 538/539 multiplier and fibre statements found no earlier primary-source match, so the exact restriction is **potentially novel**, without a priority claim. It is a useful restricted obstruction and appears ready to cite as such, while its direct impact on the unrestricted \(S(6)\) problem remains limited.

Trust rests on the displayed group and fibre reductions, exact standard-library Python enumeration, literal witness decoding, and the established \(S(4)=44\). The independent audit uses the prime-order sumset inequality as a mathematical input; the reviewed verifier checks all 524,800 normalized cases instead. Neither script is a formal proof of the prose bridges, and neither re-proves \(S(4)=44\). No solver certificate or floating-point computation is involved.

## Strengthening and improvement opportunities

1. **Highest priority: resolve the remaining order-four group at 539.** Since every multiplier is colour-preserving and the Sylow-two subgroup is Klein four, a candidate with order four is invariant under independent sign changes on the mod-49 and mod-11 axes. Encode its orbit classes and modular Schur triples in a small exact CSP, and publish either a full six-colour word or a checkable unsatisfiability certificate. The present lemma supplies the reduction but neither outcome.
2. **Next: resolve order-four actions at 538.** Its unique order-four subgroup has three remaining palette cycle types. An exact orbit-state enumeration could eliminate one type or produce a witness; the published parity-lift proposal needs a jointly sum-free assignment of even and odd layers, not just separate layer colourings.
3. **Broader reusable lemma.** State the fixed/moved-axis argument for a general product \(A\times B\) and prime-order automorphism whose signed orbits contain a Schur triple. The source already proves the key inequality \(\ell\lceil u/\ell\rceil+v\); formalizing it would separate the portable result from these two numerical applications. This would not by itself improve \(S(6)\).
