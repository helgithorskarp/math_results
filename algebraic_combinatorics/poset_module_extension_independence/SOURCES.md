# Sources and status

1. Eric R. Dolores-Cuenca, Aldo Guzman-Saenz, and Sangil Kim,
   *The gold partition conjecture and the Lexicographic sum of posets*,
   arXiv:2410.12494v1 (2024),
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12494>.

   Lemma 2.3 organizes the linear extensions of a one-block substitution
   into equal columns indexed by the block's linear extensions and notes the
   simultaneous product indexing for general lexicographic sums.  Lemma 2.5
   transfers the Gold Partition property, and Corollary 2.7 proves the
   balance-constant lower bound.  These are the primary prior results behind
   the product bijection reproduced here.

2. Emily J. Olson and Bruce E. Sagan, *On the 1/3--2/3 Conjecture*, Order
   **35** (2018), 425--436; arXiv:1706.04985,
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04985>.

   This supplies the balance-probability notation and surveys established
   structural classes.  The current arXiv rendering records the general
   conjecture and the automorphism/twin mechanisms.

3. Swee Hong Chan and Igor Pak, *Linear extensions of finite posets*, EMS
   Surveys in Mathematical Sciences (published online 2025),
   <https://ems.press/journals/emss/articles/14298658>.

   This is a current broad survey of uniform linear extensions, inequalities,
   examples, and complexity.  A targeted text search did not locate a more
   general replacement for the lexicographic-sum product theorem above.

4. A. Gupta, *The Gold Partition Conjecture Holds through Fourteen
   Elements*, arXiv:2607.23926 (2026),
   <https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23926>.

   This is the current finite frontier cited by the Discovery Net problem
   statement.  It explicitly uses preservation under lexicographic sum.

## Status statement

The central product mechanism and its balanced-pair and Gold Partition
corollaries are prior art, not new claims.  The present contribution supplies
an independent proof, exact finite audit, the joint conditional-independence
language, the arbitrary local comparison-transcript corollary, and a minimal
failure when autonomy is dropped.  Any novelty in the formulation is
search-relative; no historical priority is asserted.
