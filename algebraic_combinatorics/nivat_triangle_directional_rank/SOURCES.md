# Primary sources and claim boundary

Checked 2026-09-21.

1. Jarkko Kari and Etienne Moutot, *Nivat's conjecture and pattern complexity
   in algebraic subshifts*, Theoretical Computer Science 777 (2019), 379--386,
   [published article](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304397519300088),
   [arXiv:1806.07107](https://arxiv.org/abs/1806.07107).  Their Theorem 10
   proves the generalized Nivat property for the binary four-dot system.  Its
   proof uses a two-factor integer tensorization.  Their conclusion asks
   whether GNP can fail without a proper-sublattice mechanism.

2. Jarkko Kari and Michal Szabados, *An algebraic geometric approach to
   Nivat's conjecture*, Information and Computation 271 (2020), 104481,
   [published article](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ic.2019.104481),
   [arXiv:1510.00177](https://arxiv.org/abs/1510.00177).  This establishes the
   general annihilator-ideal and periodic-decomposition framework.  Formula
   (7) is a characteristic-two calculation for the specific Farey kernel,
   not a restatement of its general decomposition theorem.

3. Pyry Herva and Jarkko Kari, *On the periodic decompositions of
   multidimensional configurations*,
   [arXiv:2409.14948](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14948), version revised
   2026-05-27.  This supplies current context on annihilators and periodic
   decompositions.  The gauge, period, and annihilator-gcd results here are
   proved directly and do not invoke its main theorems.

4. Michal Szabados, *Nivat's conjecture holds for sums of two periodic
   configurations*, [arXiv:1710.05360](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.05360).
   This concerns rectangular complexity for sums of two periodic
   configurations, not the arbitrary-window three-direction invariant here.

5. The prerequisite class reduction and the decomposition (1) are in the
   [preceding source package](../nivat_binomial_farey_reduction/).

Targeted searches of these papers and of current arXiv records found no
statement matching the gauge-invariant aperiodic-component count or formula
(7) for the Farey-triangle kernel.  The contribution is therefore presented
as an elementary structural lemma apparently absent from the searched
sources, not as a claim of exclusive historical priority.

The result does not settle the generalized Nivat property of the triangle
kernel, does not assert a pattern-complexity lower bound, and does not address
arbitrary products of line polynomials outside the binary Farey reduction.
