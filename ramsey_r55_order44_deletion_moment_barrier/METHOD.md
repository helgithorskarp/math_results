# The averaged deletion-moment test and its exact boundary

The physical question is whether a graph on 44 vertices can avoid both a
clique and an independent set of order five. Its 44 vertex-deleted cards
would all be good43. This package tests a convex necessary consequence of
that simultaneous requirement. It does not represent the full cards.

## A common density vector for the entire deletion deck

For an allowed seven-vertex graph H, group its isomorphism class with that
of its complement. Write x_H for the fraction of seven-subsets of a graph
G belonging to that pair. There are 464 such pairs, covering all 928 good
unlabeled seven-vertex graphs. The enumeration covers every one of the
2^21 labeled graphs using the group generated by adjacent vertex swaps.
It assumes no automorphism of G. Every x_H is nonnegative and their sum
is one. Each coefficient column below is the sum of the coefficient for
H and its complement; this averages the two orientations of G without
requiring that they be isomorphic.

Let x^(v) be the density vector of G-v. Every seven-set survives in
exactly 37 of the 44 cards. Since 44 C(43,7)=37 C(44,7),

    (1/44) sum_v x^(v) = x.                         (1)

Consequently every linear inequality and every linear positive
semidefinite matrix valid on good43 is valid at x after averaging over
all cards. The same x also satisfies the corresponding order-44
constraints. The test uses one common vector for both orders, rather
than unrelated feasible vectors for individual cards. Equation (1) is
an exact whole-deck join at the level of densities. It loses the actual
labels, adjacency stars, and correlations between full cards.

## Necessary degree and codegree domains

The imported bound R(4,5)<=25 gives n-25<=d(v)<=24 in either colour.
We use degree domains 18..24 for the cards and 19..24 for the parent.
The same-colour common neighbourhood of an edge is triangle-free and
has no independent five-set. The imported R(3,5)<=14 gives codegree at
most 13, in both colours. This cap is unrestricted for the physical
target; no maximum-codegree subfamily is selected.

The inherited good43 edge window 392..511 is not a premise of the
certificate. Indeed the good44 degree range implies 418<=e(G)<=528,
so each card has 394<=e(G)-d(v)<=509. Thus that inherited window cannot
add a physical restriction at order 44. Its source was unreviewed and
uncommitted in Discovery Net at this boundary.

## Exact finite sampling, including overlaps

Fix an ordered root of size t. Let f_i count exterior a-subsets of
rooted flag type i. The matrix f f^T is positive semidefinite. Average
it over all embeddings of the root and both colour orientations.

To recover this matrix from seven-vertex densities, a pair of exterior
sets with union size s receives weight

    w_s = L C(n-t,s)/C(7-t,s).                      (2)

Here L clears the denominators. A specified union survives sampling a
(7-t)-set with probability C(7-t,s)/C(n-t,s), so (2) cancels that
probability, including intersections between the two exterior sets.
The root-embedding factor (7)_t/(n)_t is common and positive.

The unweighted matrix families are an ordered edge with two exterior
vertices, a vertex with two or three exterior vertices, the two
three-root types with one or three edges and two exterior vertices,
and all 32 allowed five-root types with one exterior vertex. Colour
reversal covers the other three-root types. Dimensions and all
coefficient arrays are regenerated, including removal only of
coordinates identically zero on every allowed column.

Weighted squares use the nonnegative quantities 24-d, d-(n-25),
13-c, and (z-j)(z-j-1), the last being nonnegative at every integer z.
For a chosen root and union U, write z=r+z_out, where r counts eligible
vertices in U. The linear weight b-z has coefficient

    (b-r)w_s - z_out,H w_(s+1).

The consecutive-integer quadratic has coefficient

    (r-j)(r-j-1)w_s + 2(r-j)z_out,H w_(s+1)
      + z_out,H(z_out,H-1)w_(s+2).                 (3)

The last term counts ordered distinct exterior vertices. All required
unions have at most seven vertices. We use j=n-25..23 for degree
localizers and j=0..12 for edge-codegree localizers.

Conditioned scalar rows keep every six-vertex flag rooted at a vertex
or edge. For example, deleting w from an induced seven-set H gives
the upper-degree contribution

    24-d_H(v)-(n-7) 1[vw is red].

Summing its possible seventh vertices above a fixed rooted six-set F
gives (n-6)(24-d_G(v)). Lower-degree and codegree rows follow by the
same count. These are 1,584 rows at each ambient order.

## Integer support and the additional exact equality

For each possible degree d, the signed Lagrange numerator with all
other domain elements as roots is nonnegative on the degree domain.
For codegree we retain every polynomial with five distinct roots in
0..13 whose sign is nonnegative throughout that integer interval.
There are 110 such codegree rows. Polynomial coefficients are expanded
in the binomial basis by forward differences and lifted using (2).

At order 44 the degree domain has six elements. Therefore the
degree-six polynomial

    P(z) = product_(j=19..24) (z-j)

vanishes at every vertex. Its sum is expressible on seven-vertex
supports and supplies the equality E x=0. The generator divides E by
the gcd of its integer coefficients. A preliminary point for the
order-44 inequalities alone violated three order-43 support rows;
neither that point nor the preliminary solver result certifies the
joint test. The final certificate must satisfy both complete displayed
systems and this equality exactly.

These are finite counting identities, not asymptotic approximations.
For another direct whole-deck interpretation, if T_F counts induced
four-vertex graphs of type F and t_F(v) counts those containing v,
then the card count is T_F-t_F(v). Its covariance across deleted
vertices is

    (1/n) sum_v t(v)t(v)^T - (4T/n)(4T/n)^T.

The products sharing v use at most seven vertices. Thus overlap
moments retain information beyond card means, but still do not
encode the literal cross-card adjacency join.

## What an exact feasible certificate establishes

The specified system consists of the simplex, E x=0, both sets of
scalar inequalities, and both sets of PSD matrices. If a rational x
satisfies them, evaluating any nonnegative combination of scalar
rows, PSD matrix pairings, and arbitrary multiples of the displayed
equalities prevents a negative constant contradiction. This proves a
limitation of this exact cone. It says nothing about additional
inequalities, stronger cones, or existence of a physical good43 or
good44. In particular, local distributions supported on good seven-
vertex graphs are not distributions on full good43 cards.

The checker uses integer scalar products, an exact zero residual for
E, and fraction-free Bareiss elimination to verify positive leading
principal minors. Sylvester's criterion then proves positive
definiteness. Discovery tolerances and solver status play no role in
acceptance.

Generation uses signed 64-bit integers. A conservative bound on any
matrix partial sum is 20160*918*60*C(43,6)<10^16<2^63; the factors
bound the number of root/set-pair terms, the absolute quadratic
expansion, and the largest sampling weight. Scalar polynomial bounds
are checked from their exact forward differences. Certificate
evaluation and all Bareiss operations use arbitrary-precision Python
integers. The remaining trust boundaries are the written counting
bridge, generator, checker, Python/NumPy semantics, and hardware.
This is same-author evidence, not external review or formal proof.
