# Sources and limits of novelty

Primary-source audit: 21 September 2026. The proposed result is the uniform
exclusion of residue-pencil witnessing systems from cardinality minimizers
over finite commutative local rings of length two with odd residue field.
It is a structural consequence of established finite-field incidence
geometry. No claim of historical priority, best general ring bound, or
Euclidean Kakeya progress is made.

1. Aart Blokhuis and Francesco Mazzocca, *The Finite Field Kakeya Problem*,
   [arXiv:0911.4370v1, primary PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0911.4370).
   Proposition 7 gives the exact minimum `(q^2+2q-1)/2` in the affine
   plane of odd order `q`. The definition in Section 1 and Proposition 7
   were inspected directly. This is the nontrivial imported theorem in
   our proof; our finite checks do not replace its universal proof.
   We do not use or reprove their classification of equality cases.

2. Evan P. Dummit and Márton Hablicsek, *Kakeya sets over non-archimedean
   local rings*, Mathematika **59** (2013), 257--266,
   [primary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/1112.3381).
   Definitions, Lemma 2.1, Theorem 2.2 and Proposition 3.1 were inspected.
   They establish the local-ring framework, measure-zero constructions,
   and planar finite-quotient lower bounds. Their formal-power-series
   construction has concurrent first reductions. Our length-two
   cardinality comparison does not obstruct such infinite measure-zero
   constructions. Scale-sensitive cardinality minimization is distinct
   from a limiting measure statement.

3. Manik Dhar, *The Kakeya Set conjecture over Z/NZ for general N*,
   Advances in Combinatorics **2024**:2,
   [primary manuscript, v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2110.14889v3).
   Definition 1.1 supplies the primitive projective-direction convention;
   Definitions 1.3 and Conjecture 1.4 distinguish exact minimization from
   the asymptotic ring conjecture, which this paper resolves. The
   introduction and Section 6's construction statement/parameterization
   were inspected. We do not describe the asymptotic ring conjecture as
   open or claim a new general lower bound. The polynomial-image method
   for constructing line unions is established prior methodology.

4. Larry Guth, Hong Wang and Joshua Zahl, *A streamlined proof of the
   Kakeya set conjecture in R^3*,
   [primary manuscript](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.14411).
   The introduction and Theorem 1.1 confirm the graph root's current
   three-dimensional status. Our finite-ring result neither improves
   that theorem nor treats the Euclidean conjecture in higher dimensions.

The discriminant identity for the union of quadratic-intercept lines is
an elementary instance of the familiar parabola construction; it is not
claimed as a new method. Here its explicit length-two completion supplies
a comparison certificate with cardinality `(q^4+q^2)/2`. We do not claim
that this construction is the smallest previously known one.

Bounded searches used Kakeya/Besicovitch, local or length-two rings,
prime-square rings, Hjelmslev planes, residue pencils, and concurrency.
The inspected primary texts did not supply the displayed pencil bound or
its strict comparison with a uniform competitor. This is a bounded
literature distinction, not an exhaustive absence claim. The new content
is the residue-fiber assembly and the resulting excluded extremal class.
No assertion covers even residue order, higher length, arbitrary finite
rings, inclusion-minimal sets, or all extremizers.
