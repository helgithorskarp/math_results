# Literature and scope

The graph-first source of this question is Toeplitz's square-peg problem.
The quantitative target was selected after reading the relevant primary
papers, rather than inferring novelty from an empty graph neighborhood.

1. Terence Tao, *An integration approach to the Toeplitz square peg problem*,
   [arXiv:1611.07441](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.07441), 2016/2017.
   Tao proves square existence for two branches of Lipschitz constant less
   than one. Square existence in our restricted family is not a new claim.
2. Ludovic Rifford, *A quantitative version of Tao's result on the Toeplitz
   Square Peg Problem*, [arXiv:2106.01914](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.01914), 2021;
   [author's complete manuscript](https://math.univ-cotedazur.fr/~rifford/Papiers_en_ligne/Peg_Rifford.pdf).
   Theorem 1.1 gives a universal bound C times the maximum vertical branch
   gap for two 1-Lipschitz branches. The displayed proof takes C=0.018;
   the discussion immediately after the theorem suggests the optimum 1/2.
   We do not improve the unrestricted theorem. We settle the affine-branch
   subclass with sharp constant 2/3, so that subclass cannot attain 1/2.
3. Joshua Evan Greene and Andrew Lobb, *Square pegs between two graphs*,
   [arXiv:2407.07798](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07798), 2024.
   Theorem A and Corollaries B/C prove square existence below Lipschitz
   constant 1+sqrt(2), and all rectangle similarity classes at constant one.
   Their spectral-invariant method is not used in the elementary quantitative
   argument here.

The full Rifford manuscript and Greene–Lobb introduction and relevant scope
were inspected on 2026-09-20. Targeted searches for affine/constant/straight
branches, quantitative square size, and the factor 2/3 did not locate this
sharp affine-branch bound or the uniquely inscribed rational extremizer.
These checks do not establish historical priority. The continuity-based
horizontal-square construction is elementary and is not presented as a new
existence theorem. The contribution is the sharp metric statement, its
all-orientation extremizer, and the exclusion of affine branches from the
proposed 1/2 extremal regime.

We make no assertion about an optimal quantitative constant for two arbitrary
non-affine branches, nor a solution of the square-peg problem for arbitrary
Jordan curves. The proof is self-contained, apart from elementary real
analysis and Euclidean geometry; none of the cited deeper existence theorems
is a proof dependency.
