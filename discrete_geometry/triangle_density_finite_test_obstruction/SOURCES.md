## Contribution and search scope

The new assertion is that **every finite fixed suite**, even one containing
inadmissible triangles, admits excluded near-identity homotheties with the
same density-status answers. The countable sufficiency follows by assembling
the previously known map-dependent witnesses. Together they make the
fixed-suite cardinality exact and clarify the need for coefficient-dependent
tests in the earlier theorem.

Norm transitivity and the norm/similarity-factor identity are classical.
The inclusion of compositum norms in an intersection of norm groups is
not claimed new, nor is the polynomial-part idea. We do not equate that
inclusion with equality, invoke a multinorm principle, or claim a new theorem
on general norm-group intersections. The arithmetic construction is given
self-contained for the geometric application.

Bounded primary-source and graph checks on 2026-09-21 covered triangle-density
preservers, finite admissibility tests, common quadratic-form similarity
factors, and norm methods. They did not locate this exact finite-testing
statement. Historical priority is not established. General literature on
similarity factors treats much wider field-extension questions; it is not
used as evidence that this simple arithmetic mechanism is new.

The old affine-group correction remains complete. No further published
error is alleged. No claim is made about a dense set of pairwise rational
distances, nonlinear maps, four-anchor density, or deciding rationality from
arbitrary real-number descriptions.
