# Sources and scope of the literature check

Checked 2026-09-20.

1. G. Kiss, R. D. Malikiosis, G. Somlai, M. Vizer,
   **Fuglede's conjecture holds for cyclic groups of order pqrs**,
   *Journal of Fourier Analysis and Applications* (2022).
   [Publisher full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00041-022-09972-0),
   [arXiv:2011.09578](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.09578).
   Theorem 1.4 supplies spectral-to-tiling in $\mathbb Z_{210}$.
   Since 11 does not divide 210, there is no spectral 11-set there.
   This is the only non-elementary theorem imported for the
   specialization. The general theorem states its base assumption
   explicitly.

2. G. Somlai, **Fuglede's Conjecture on Cyclic Groups of Square-Free
   Order: The Case of Rapidly Growing Prime Factors**,
   [arXiv:2607.26534, full primary HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.26534).
   Proposition 3.2 obtains equal level sizes and a common spectrum
   when $p>n$. Here $p=11<n=210$, so that proposition does not
   settle the targeted case. We instead impose a contained full
   fiber and use an exact deletion identity.
   The cyclotomic coefficient-equality mechanism is standard and
   also appears in the level arguments of this literature.

The committed research graph already contains cardinality-local
prime-boundary lifting and Gram/inertia descent theorems around
cardinality $2p$. The latter explicitly leave the divisible cardinality
$2p$ outside their conclusions because projections can collide.
Our claim treats the full-fiber family within that regime. It does
not increase their numerical cutoff.

The proposed new content is the graph-spectrum dichotomy as used
after deleting a full fiber, the necessary-and-sufficient common
binary-valuation normal form, and its explicit universal complement.
No match for this combined statement was found in bounded searches
for spectral sets of size $2p$, contained cosets/full prime fibers,
and cyclic tiling, or in the inspected primary statements.
This supports novelty only relative to the checked sources, not
an exclusive historical-priority claim.
