# Primary source and correction boundary

Checked 2026-09-20:

Pietro Corvaja, Amos Turchet, Umberto Zannier, *Rational distances from given
rational points in the plane*, Geometriae Dedicata 219, article 59 (2025),
DOI 10.1007/s10711-025-01019-0.

- [Published article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10711-025-01019-0)
- [Published PDF](https://d-nb.info/1372527907/34)
- [arXiv record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.02030)
- [arXiv v2 full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.02030v2)

Theorem 1 gives the rational-distance density criterion in terms of a
rational Gram form representing a nonzero rational square.
Theorem 2 gives the squared-distance criterion in terms of rationality of
the Gram matrix. These are Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 in arXiv v2.
They are dependencies, not new results of this note.

The introductory paragraph immediately following the addendum to
Theorem 1, on published pages 2–3, asserts preservation by rational invertible
ambient matrices and by scales whose squares are sums of two rational
squares, and identifies a maximal affine group. The same statement appears
after Theorem 1.1 in arXiv v2.
The displayed counterexamples in [PROOF.md](PROOF.md) refute those
preservation assertions under the stated ambient action. The preserver
theorem gives an exact replacement.

Rational right changes of the triangle's edge basis do preserve
admissibility. They correspond to ambient maps that depend on the input
triangle. This distinguishes that valid fact from one fixed ambient map
acting on all triangles.

The version-of-record text and arXiv v2 were checked. The arXiv record
listed v2, dated 2024-12-10, as its latest version; targeted searches did not
locate an erratum or this replacement classification. This is a bounded
search statement, not a claim that no correction exists anywhere.

The two density theorems and the remaining results of the paper are not
disproved or independently reviewed here. The correction concerns only
the introductory affine-group assertion. No statement about the full
Erdos–Ulam problem follows, and no exclusive historical priority is claimed.
