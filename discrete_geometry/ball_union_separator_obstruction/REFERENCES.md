# Sources and scope

1. Igors Gorbovickis, *The central set and its application to the
   Kneser--Poulsen conjecture*, Discrete & Computational Geometry 59
   (2018), 784--801.
   [Author manuscript, arXiv:1511.08134](https://arxiv.org/html/1511.08134).
   Section 2.3, especially Proposition 2.7, supplies a divide-and-conquer
   principle when the separator volume is invariant. Section 2.5
   identifies compensation for a changing separator as the missing step
   in that method. Those observations and the underlying
   inclusion-exclusion identity are prior work, not discoveries here.
   The inspected statement uses central sets and genuine contractions;
   our example instead tests the weaker local-volume-only inference.

2. Károly Bezdek and Robert Connelly, *Pushing disks apart -- the
   Kneser--Poulsen conjecture in the plane*, Journal für die reine und
   angewandte Mathematik 553 (2002), 221--236.
   [Author manuscript, arXiv:math/0108098](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0108098).
   Establishes the planar theorem for pairwise contractions/expansions.
   Our center pairs do not satisfy its contraction hypothesis.

3. Igors Gorbovickis, *Kneser--Poulsen conjecture for a small number of
   intersections*.
   [Author manuscript, arXiv:1006.0529](https://arxiv.org/html/1006.0529).
   Theorem 2.2 and Corollary 2.3 impose a sparse-intersection condition at
   the initial endpoint of an expansion. This is different from a rule
   comparing only the volumes of overlapping bags.

4. Károly Bezdek, *Selected topics from the theory of intersections of
   balls*.
   [Author manuscript, arXiv:2411.10302](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.10302).
   Contemporary context for Kneser--Poulsen and related ball problems.

The explicit four-unit-disk witness was not found in the primary sources
inspected on 2026-09-21. This is not a historical-priority claim. Its value
is as a compact, strictly nondegenerate counterexample and regression
fixture for an overly weak overlap-gluing rule. The qualitative warning
about separator deficits is already published. No open conjecture is
claimed solved or disproved.
