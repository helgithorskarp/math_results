# Primary sources and claim boundary

Checked 2026-09-20.

1. Jarkko Kari and Etienne Moutot, *Nivat's conjecture and pattern
   complexity in algebraic subshifts*, Theoretical Computer Science 777
   (2019), 379–386,
   [published article](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0304397519300088),
   DOI [10.1016/j.tcs.2018.12.029](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcs.2018.12.029),
   [author manuscript](https://emoutot.perso.math.cnrs.fr/static/publi/karimoutot19.1.pdf).
   The ordinary binary four-dot positive theorem is journal Theorem 11
   (manuscript Theorem 10). Their negative example, journal Theorem 12
   (manuscript Theorem 11), separates arithmetic lines in different cosets
   of \(2\mathbb Z^2\). The conclusion raises the role of a polynomial's
   support lattice.

2. Cleber F. Colle and Eduardo Garibaldi, *A Modular Structure Theorem
   for Minimal Periodic Decompositions and Periodicity of Configurations
   with \(P_\eta(4,n)\leq4n\)*, 2026 preprint,
   [arXiv:2606.10193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10193),
   [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.10193).
   Theorem 1.3 claims the width-at-most-four case of the rectangular
   conjecture. This was checked for current problem context, is not
   independently reviewed here, and is not a proof dependency.

The contribution here is an elementary extension of the first source's
mechanism: an all-direction determinant criterion, an exact
incidence-window formula with restricted sharp thresholds, and a
factor-level obstruction. The full-support square-free example still
contains a divisor supported in a proper sublattice; it does not establish
a different cause of failure. Bounded searches did not locate these exact
combined statements, but no claim of exclusive priority follows.

Original Nivat concerns complete axis-aligned rectangular windows.
The six-site windows here can omit lattice points in their convex hull.
Neither the rectangular nor the lattice-convex version is refuted.
The positive result is specifically over \(\mathbb F_2\). No assertion
about other fields or all products of binomials is intended.
