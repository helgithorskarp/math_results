# Prior work and literature boundary

Primary-source checks: 2026-09-22. This is a bounded literature audit, not
an exhaustive priority search. No source inspected states the exact
all-dimensional local Firey-volume inverse theorem and sharp symmetric
facet-dependent jet order proved here. The result is new to the searched
sources; historical priority is not established. None of these external
papers is represented as posing this particular inverse problem.

1. **Prior graph theorem and its independent review.**
   [Local planar Firey-area reconstruction](https://github.com/helgithorskarp/math_results/blob/main/discrete_geometry/firey_area_reconstruction/PROOF.md)
   proves the germ and sharp 2r-jet statements in dimension two.
   Its [review](https://github.com/helgithorskarp/math_results/blob/main/discrete_geometry/firey_area_reconstruction_review1/REVIEW.md)
   accepts that scope and explicitly excludes higher dimensions.
   The current theorem generalizes its germ and finite-jet assertions;
   its planar singular-Gram facet detector does **not** extend verbatim.

2. **Prior planar transform.**
   [Sharp planar Firey stability, Section 2](https://github.com/helgithorskarp/math_results/blob/main/discrete_geometry/firey_planar_sharp_stability/PROOF.md)
   supplies the earlier two-dimensional support-measure transform. At d=2,
   our kernel is Phi_2(t)=1+t arctan(t), giving the same normalization.
   The new proof uses a spherical cofactor argument in general dimension.

3. **Classical support calculus and Cheng-Yau's cofactor identity.**
   Colesanti, Livshyts and Marsiglietti,
   [On the stability of Brunn-Minkowski type inequalities](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.06586),
   Section 2 and Lemmas 6.1-6.2. In particular, Lemma 6.2 states the
   divergence-free property of the cofactor of the *spherical* curvature
   matrix. We use this established identity and give its Codazzi explanation;
   it is not an invention of this contribution. Standard Hausdorff continuity
   of volume and surface area measures underlies the nonsmooth passage.

4. **Finite moments and polytopes.** Astrid Kousholt,
   [Reconstruction of n-dimensional convex bodies from surface tensors](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.08240),
   Lemma 3.1 and Theorem 3.2. The theorem obtains the sharp order m-d+2 for
   ordinary surface tensors of m-facet polytopes. Positive-polynomial support
   reduction and regular-polygon cancellation are established methods.
   Here the data are homogeneous moments of the polar cone measure; the
   antipodal projective argument gives order 2(r-d+2) for 2r facets.
   A separate radial-ratio argument recovers facet distances. We do not claim
   novelty for general finite moment inversion.

5. **L_p surface measures and the critical exponent.** Lutwak, Yang and Zhang,
   [On the L_p-Minkowski problem](https://math.nyu.edu/~yangd/papers/tams.pdf),
   equations (1.3)-(1.5). The L_p mixed-volume inequality and its dilation
   equality case prove our one-term corollary after finite-support recovery.
   The two-term constructive theorem does not require L_p uniqueness.
   At p=d the unnormalized surface measure is dilation invariant, precisely
   matching the exceptional scale ambiguity in our statement.

6. **Even-degree projection caveat.** Ryabogin and Zvavitch,
   [The Fourier transform and Firey projections of convex bodies](https://www.math.kent.edu/~zvavitch/Fourier-Firey.pdf),
   Theorem 3 and the following remark. General even-p projection data are
   noninjective. We first force every competing measure onto the finite
   normal-line set and recover its entire L_p measure; thus no general
   even-p injectivity is assumed.

Search terms included Firey segment-volume formulas, translation-volume
germs, inverse problems for Firey sums, symmetric facet reconstruction,
surface tensors, projective positive moment recovery, and spherical
cofactor identities. The broader graph's planar Rogers-Shephard equality
problem was already resolved and is not claimed as a new solution here.
