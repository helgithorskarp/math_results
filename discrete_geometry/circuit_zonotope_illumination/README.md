# Exact illumination of circuit and cactus zonotopes

A zonotope generated by a linear circuit of k vectors, of rank k-1, has
illumination number

\[
 I(Z)=I_f(Z)=\binom{k}{\lfloor k/2\rfloor},
\]

for arbitrary real generator directions and positive lengths. Here I_f
denotes fractional illumination. A family of its subset-labelled vertices
can be illuminated by a single direction precisely when its labels form an
inclusion chain. Symmetric chains construct an optimal cover, while a middle Boolean
layer provides a matching pairwise antipodal obstruction.

For any rank-d zonotope containing circuits of sizes k_1,...,k_s whose spans
form an internal direct sum, put r=sum_j(k_j-1). Then

\[
 I(Z)\le 2^{d-r}\prod_j\binom{k_j}{\lfloor k_j/2\rfloor}.
\]

This bound is sharp for direct products of the indicated circuit factors
and segments. In particular, a positively weighted connected cactus
graphical zonotope has

\[
 I(Z_G)=I_f(Z_G)=2^b\prod_j\binom{k_j}{\lfloor k_j/2\rfloor},
\]

where b counts bridges and k_j are the cycle lengths. The graph must have
at least one edge. Edge-disjoint cycles in a general graph need not have
independent spans, so edge disjointness alone does not justify the bound.

Read [PROOF.md](PROOF.md) for the self-contained universal argument, exact
hypotheses, prior work, and limitations. The known circuit values 3, 6, 10
in dimensions 2, 3, 4 are recovered. No historical priority claim is made,
and the general illumination conjecture is not resolved here.

## Reproduce

From the repository root, using CPython 3.11 or newer:

```sh
python3 discrete_geometry/circuit_zonotope_illumination/verify.py
```

Only the Python standard library is used. There is no network input, random
seed, solver, floating-point calculation, or external data file. The program
prints the deterministic [EXPECTED.json](EXPECTED.json), with status
`VERIFIED` and evidence payload SHA-256

```text
83b5ab5407493f0fb92ee0ddb9d0a3aa560748b34a5e73b398729da3f2eb27c2
```

The digest is of `json.dumps(evidence, sort_keys=True, separators=(',', ':'))`
encoded as UTF-8. A reference CPython 3.11.2 run took 2.38 seconds with a
reported child maximum resident set of 17,060 KiB. Timings are informational.

## What the verifier checks

- Symmetric-chain partitions for k=2,...,12: disjointness, completeness,
  symmetry, saturation, and the exact number of chains.
- Ten rational circuit instances: k=2,...,7, plus k=3,...,6 after invertible
  rational coordinate changes, unequal positive segment scalings, and
  generator reorientations.
- For each geometric instance, an independent facet computation finds all
  supporting normals orthogonal to rank-(d-1) generator sublists. Exact
  support values and active-normal ranks identify the vertices from all
  distinct images of box corners.
- The constructed directions strictly decrease every active facet at each
  covered vertex. In circuit instances their illuminated vertices agree
  exactly with the predicted permutation prefixes.
- Middle-layer witnesses are actual distinct vertices, and every pair
  has opposing active supporting normals. This certifies a lower bound
  against **all** directions, including fractional illumination.
- All 117 coefficient vectors in {-1,0,1}^k for k=2,3,4, including ties,
  obey the strict chain criterion; zero directions illuminate no vertex.
- Five cactus products, including two cycles and bridges, have matching
  constructed covers and pairwise antipodal lower certificates.
- The Hamiltonian circuit bounds illuminate the full graphical zonotopes
  of K_4 and K_5. These are upper-bound checks, not exactness claims.
- Four malformed or non-circuit inputs are rejected. Two edge-disjoint
  4-cycles in K_{2,4} have individual ranks 3,3 and combined rank 5,
  exposing an invalid weakening of the direct-sum hypothesis.

Finite exact checks corroborate the written proof; they do not establish
the universal theorem by extrapolation. The trust boundary is the proof,
the readable checker and Python's integer/Fraction arithmetic. There is
no proof-assistant formalization or hidden large certificate. The source
and compact expected output are covered by [SHA256SUMS](SHA256SUMS).
