# Odd-valuation projection theorem

Let R=Q(sqrt(33)) and E=R(i sqrt(3))=Q(i sqrt(3),i sqrt(11)), with their
specified physical embeddings in the complex plane. Write N(x)=x conjugate(x).
Choose either embedding sigma:R -> Q_2. Normalize its valuation by v(2)=1.

**Theorem.** Let D in R be positive in the physical embedding, and suppose
v(sigma(D)) is odd. Every unit-distance graph whose complex point coordinates
belong to E(sqrt(D)) is four-colourable. More explicitly, write each point
uniquely as x+y sqrt(D), x,y in E. The colour is the existing E residue-digit
colour of x, using the place sigma. There is no size, denominator, location,
symmetry, or construction-history restriction.

## 1. The base-field local norm

Put omega=(-1+i sqrt(3))/2. The embedding of E into Q_2(omega) extending sigma
sends complex conjugation to omega -> omega^2 and fixes Q_2. Every element
is A+B omega with A,B in Q_2, and its norm is A^2-AB+B^2.

For integral A,B not both even, that norm is odd: its reduction over F_2 is
one at each of the three nonzero pairs. Factoring out 2 to the minimum
coefficient valuation proves, for a nonzero element,

    v(N(A+B omega)) = 2 min(v(A),v(B)).

In particular every nonzero E norm has even valuation. Norm valuation zero
is equivalent to integral local coordinates A,B, not both even.

Define C_sigma(x) as the pair of zeroth binary digits of these two local
coordinates. For arbitrary Q_2 coordinates, if their difference is integral,
the negative binary digits coincide. The difference of their zeroth digits
is then the residue of that difference modulo 2. Therefore

    v(N(x-x'))=0  implies  C_sigma(x) != C_sigma(x').

This is stronger than requiring physical norm one. It is the residue-digit
argument in the pinned existing field package, with no assumption that the
points themselves are integral. The colour map on the whole field is not
asserted to be additive.

## 2. Projection proof

Odd valuation implies D is not a square in R. Since D is physically positive,
it cannot be minus three times a square in R either, so it is not a square
in E=R(i sqrt(3)). Thus 1,t with t=sqrt(D) are independent over E and the
representation z=x+yt is unique. Complex conjugation fixes t.

If N(z)=1, comparison of the constant coefficient in

    N(z)=N(x)+D N(y)+t[x conjugate(y)+conjugate(x)y]

gives N(x)+D N(y)=1. If x,y are both nonzero, the two summands have respectively
even and odd 2-adic valuation. They have unequal valuations, so the valuation
of their sum is their minimum. Since their sum is one, the minimum is zero.
Only the even-valued term can have valuation zero. Therefore v(N(x))=0.
The case y=0 gives N(x)=1 directly; x=0 is impossible because D N(y) would
have odd valuation.

Apply this observation to the difference of two arbitrary points at unit
distance. Their E projections have norm valuation zero, so section 1 assigns
them different colours. This proves the theorem.

The projection generally does **not** preserve physical unit distances.
It maps unit differences to E elements with *local norm valuation zero*,
which is exactly the condition used by the colour rule.

For the second embedding of R, apply the E automorphism
(a,b,c,d) -> (a,-b,c,-d) in the basis
1,sqrt(33),i sqrt(3),i sqrt(11), then use the pinned first-place colour routine.
The implementation tests odd valuation exactly and rejects a failed hypothesis.

## 3. The final named A159 construction phase

The proposed 16-contact quadratic over E has trace
T=(-3,3,-1,3)/16 and constant coefficient V=(1,0,1,0)/2. A physical root is

    u = (-3,3,-1,3)/32
        + sqrt(330-42 sqrt(33)) * (-3,-1,3,3)/192.

The exact checker verifies u^2-Tu+V=0 and N(u)=1. Its field can equally be
written E(sqrt(d)) for d=(55-7 sqrt(33))/54, since D=324d. The physical D is
positive. At the embedding sqrt(33) -> r, r congruent to 1 modulo 8, the exact
valuations are v(d)=5 and v(D)=7. The theorem therefore gives a proper
four-colouring of the **whole field**, not just one orbit or one phase.

For a concrete physical decision, take the pinned A159 source. B=A union uA
has 317 distinct points, 1308 complete unit edges and 16 nonzero cross edges.

(A[18],u A[113]):

    g(z)=A[18]+(u A[113]-A[18]) conjugate(A[28]-A[13]) (z-A[13]).

## 5. Checks, scope, and prior work

The normal and optimized Python runs reproduce the physical 474-point graph
and colour word. The metric checker has one specialized four-coefficient
norm formula and a separate generic reduction in R[t] using
r^2=33,t^2=330-42r, applied to Cartesian x^2+y^2. Both audit all 112101 pairs.
An earlier independent representation using Fraction-valued complex field
multiplication gives the identical complete points, edges and word.

The colour controls construct 450 exact stereographic unit vectors in six
odd-valuation presentations, including 360 with a nonzero new-radical
coefficient, and verify 1350 translated unit edges. Translation samples have
even denominators and need not be locally integral. Both real places,
negative odd valuations, and precision beyond 256 bits are exercised.

The even-valuation hypothesis cannot simply be dropped from this projection
rule. For D=5, z=(7+i sqrt(15))/8 is a physical unit vector, while its
E projection 7/8 has the same base colour as zero. The controls verify this
counterexample exactly and reject D=5 as an input to the certified rule.
It is a failure of that projection rule, not a proof of five-chromaticity.

The underlying local/residue approach is prior work: see David Speyer's
25--26 April 2018 comments in
[Polymath16, second thread](https://dustingmixon.wordpress.com/2018/04/22/polymath16-second-thread-what-does-it-take-to-be-5-chromatic/),
and the wider number-field framework in David A. Madore,
[The Hadwiger--Nelson problem over certain fields](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.07023).
The explicit odd-valuation projection argument and its application here are
provided in full, without a claim of novelty for the general method.

No five-chromatic sub-509 plane witness is produced. The selected second
