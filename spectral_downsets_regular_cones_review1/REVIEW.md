## Exact hypotheses, matrices and scope

Let \(G\) be any finite simple \(d\)-regular graph on \(h\) leaves, with
\(2\le d\le h-2\). An actual graph has \(h\ge4\) and even \(hd\).
There is no connectedness, transitivity or bipartiteness hypothesis. The
downset generated by \(K_1\vee G\) contains the empty set, the center
singleton \(a\), leaf singletons \(C\), spokes \(X\), and leaf edges
\(E_G\), with

\[
\ell=hd/2,\quad N=2+2h+\ell,\quad m=N-1,\quad s=h+1,
\quad\rho=\frac{s}{N-s}<1.
\]

The center star \(S\) has size \(s\); each leaf star has size
\(d+2<s\). An H certificate is a real symmetric matrix \(M\), zero when
the indexing sets intersect, with \(M\mathbf1=\mathbf1\) and
\(L=(N-s)M+sI_N\succeq0\). The empty vertex retains its allowed loop.
A cap means \(M\preceq I_N\). Off-diagonal weights may be signed.

Write \(A\) for leaf adjacency and \(B\) for unoriented vertex-edge
incidence. Then \(BB^T=dI+A\), \(B\mathbf1=d\mathbf1\),
\(B^T\mathbf1=2\mathbf1\). Put

\[
\alpha=\frac{h-d}{d},\quad q=\frac2d,\quad
w=\frac{2(d-1)}{d(h-2)},\quad
z=\frac{2(2d-h)}{d(h-4)+2}.
\]

In order \((a,X,C,E_G)\), the centered nonempty core is

\[
C_0=\begin{pmatrix}
h&-\mathbf1^T&-\mathbf1^T&q\mathbf1^T\\
-\mathbf1&(h+1)I-J&-I+(2/d)A&wJ-(1+w)B\\
-\mathbf1&-I+(2/d)A&hI-\alpha A&-B\\
q\mathbf1&wJ-(1+w)B^T&-B^T&(h+2+z)I-(1+z)B^TB+zJ
\end{pmatrix}.
\tag{1}
\]

Diagonal entries are \(s-1\); distinct intersecting entries are \(-1\).
Direct incidence counts give \(C_0\mathbf1=C_0\mathbf1_S=0\).
All denominators are positive in the stated domain. The scope excludes
degree zero, degree one, and complete leaf graphs, which have separate
constructions and kernel/equality cases.

## Complete incidence decomposition and determinant proof

The forms \(\sum_{ij\in E_G}(y_i-y_j)^2\) and
\(\sum_{ij\in E_G}(y_i+y_j)^2\) give \(-d\le\lambda\le d\).
On \(\mathbf1_h^\perp\), the complement graph also gives
\(\lambda\le h-d-2\). These statements cover disconnected graphs.

Let \(b\) count all bipartite components. The equation
\((dI+A)y=0\) forces alternating values along edges: one dimension per
bipartite component, none per nonbipartite component. A regular bipartite
component has equal parts, so these vectors have total zero. Consequently
\(\operatorname{rank}B=h-b\).

There are four orthogonal types of modes:

* \(\ker B\) on the edge level, dimension \(\ell-h+b\), has scalar
  eigenvalue \(h+2+z>0\). Its vectors have total zero.
* For each adjacency eigenvector \(y\perp\mathbf1_h\) with
  \(g=d+\lambda>0\), its spoke, singleton and normalized
  \(B^Ty/\sqrt g\) edge copies carry
  \[
  T(\lambda)=\begin{pmatrix}
  h+1&-1+2\lambda/d&-(1+w)\sqrt g\\
  -1+2\lambda/d&h-\alpha\lambda&-\sqrt g\\
  -(1+w)\sqrt g&-\sqrt g&h+2+z-(1+z)g
  \end{pmatrix}.
  \tag{2}
  \]
  Orthogonality follows from
  \(\langle B^Ty,B^Tv\rangle=\langle y,(dI+A)v\rangle\).
  Nonconstant \(\lambda=d\) modes from component differences are included.
* Each of the \(b\) modes with \(\lambda=-d\) has zero edge image and
  carries the two-by-two block with diagonal \(h+1,2h-d\) and off-diagonal
  \(-3\). No division by a zero edge norm is made.
* In the unnormalized constant basis \((a,C_{\rm sum},X_{\rm sum},E_{\rm sum})\),
  the Gram matrix is \(FKF^T\), where
  \[
  F=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\0&1\\-1&0\\0&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
  K=\begin{pmatrix}h&-h\\-h&hd\end{pmatrix}.
  \]
  Norms are \(1,h,h,\ell\). Here \(K\succ0\), with determinant
  \(h^2(d-1)\). The two kernel directions are the star and its complement.

The dimensions sum to
\((\ell-h+b)+3(h-1-b)+2b+4=m\). This is an exhaustive decomposition,
including arbitrarily many bipartite components. The first two leading
minors of (2) are positive, because
\(h-\alpha\lambda\ge d\), \(|-1+2\lambda/d|\le3\), and
\((h+1)d-9\ge1\).

For the remaining determinant, set
\(H=h-2\), \(E=d(h-4)+2\), \(b_0=-d+2\lambda\),
\(c_0=hd-(h-d)\lambda\), \(k=dh-2\), and
\(e_0=E(h+2-g)+2(2d-h)(1-g)\). Expansion gives

\[
P=dH^2\big((h+1)c_0d-b_0^2\big)e_0
-gE\big((h+1)d^3H^2+c_0k^2-2b_0kdH\big)
=d^3H^2E\det T(\lambda).
\tag{3}
\]

[audit_polynomials.py](audit_polynomials.py) instead derives \(P\) from
the Gram matrix on spoke \(y\), singleton \(y\), and unnormalized
\(B^Ty\): multiply that Gram matrix by \(dHE\), take its exact determinant,
then perform exact polynomial division by \(gHE^2\). This works in
\(\mathbb Z[h,d,\lambda]\), including the polynomial identity at \(g=0\).
No author polynomial code or coefficient input is imported.

## Upper cap and a stronger centered buffer

Set \(U_0=NI_m-J_m-C_0\). The author's signed cap argument is valid;
an entrywise Laplacian argument alone would fail in the sparse regime.
The elementary bounds \(0<w<2/(h-2)\), \(-1<z<1\), and
\(\sqrt g\le\sqrt{2d}\le d\le h-2\) give diagonal-minus-absolute-row-sum
bounds for \(NI-T(\lambda)\) of

\[
\ell-2,\qquad\ell-1,\qquad\ell-h+2-z.
\]

All three are at least **two**. For the last, if \(d=2\), then
\(z=-(h-4)/(h-3)\le0\); if \(d\ge3\), then \(\ell-h\ge h/2\)
and \(z<1\). The inequality \(2|x_ix_j|\le x_i^2+x_j^2\) converts
these bounds into quadratic-form bounds. The missing-edge two-level block
is a principal block, and the edge-kernel slack \(h+\ell-z\) is also above two.

On constants, \(U_0\mathbf1=\mathbf1\). On its perpendicular, the two
positive core eigenvalues have sum \(h+d+3\), computed with the norms
\(1,h,h,\ell\), rather than the unnormalized Gram trace. Therefore
\(U_0\succeq(N-h-d-3)I\succeq(\ell+1)I\) there. This proves the refinement

\[
U_0\succeq 2I_m-\frac1mJ_m
=I_m+P_{\mathbf1^\perp}.
\tag{4}
\]

It also verifies the original statement \(U_0\succeq I_m\), with
\(\ker(U_0-I_m)=\mathbb R\mathbf1\).

## Doubled partition repair, with a positive endpoint margin

Label the center \(h\) and leaves \(0,\ldots,h-1\). Color singleton
\(i\) by \(2i\pmod s\) and edge \(\{i,j\}\) by \(i+j\pmod s\).
Intersecting distinct sets have different colors; the center star contains
exactly one member in every color. If \(q_c\) are class sizes, let
\(W_{AB}=\mathbf1_{\text{color}(A)=\text{color}(B)}\) and
\(C_P=sW-J_m\). This is a PSD simplex Gram, with prescribed support and
\(C_P\mathbf1_S=0\).

Every class is nonempty. Their sizes are unequal, since
\(2m=s(d+4)-(d+2)\) and \(0<d+2<s\) rule out \(s\mid m\).
Also \(2N=s(d+4)-d\), so \(N\not\equiv0,1\pmod s\).
Put \(q_{\max}=\max_c q_c\) and
\(\beta=sq_{\max}-N\); the preceding congruences give \(\beta\ge1\).
The partition total quadratic form is
\(s\sum_cq_c^2-m^2>0\), so it does not kill \(\mathbf1\).

For \(C_\epsilon=(1-\epsilon)C_0+\epsilon C_P\), every
\(0<\epsilon<1\) has kernel exactly \(\mathbb R\mathbf1_S\).
The author's choice is \(\epsilon=1/[2(\beta+1)]\).
In fact **every \(0<\epsilon\le1/(\beta+1)\) is strictly capped**.
Here is a quantitative proof including the endpoint.

Let \(\mathcal K\) be the span of the indicators of maximum-size color
classes, \(Q\) its orthogonal projector, and
\(m_{\max}=\sum_{q_c=q_{\max}}q_c<m\). Since
\(U_P+\beta I=s(q_{\max}I-W)\succeq s(I-Q)\), (4) gives

\[
U_{1/(\beta+1)}
=\frac{\beta(U_0-I)+(U_P+\beta I)}{\beta+1}
\succeq\frac{\beta P_{\mathbf1^\perp}+s(I-Q)}{\beta+1}.
\]

For \(e=\mathbf1/\sqrt m\), put \(a_0=\|Qe\|^2=m_{\max}/m\in(0,1)\).
On the plane spanned by \(Qe,(I-Q)e\), the numerator has trace
\(\beta+s\) and determinant \(\beta s(1-a_0)\). Its least eigenvalue
is at least determinant divided by trace. The other invariant directions
have eigenvalues \(\beta\) or \(\beta+s\), at least the same bound.
Therefore, with the explicit positive rational number

\[
\mu=\frac{\beta s(m-m_{\max})}{m(\beta+1)(\beta+s)}>0,
\]

\[
U_{1/(\beta+1)}\succeq\mu I_m,\qquad
U_\epsilon\succeq\big[1-\epsilon(\beta+1)(1-\mu)\big]I_m.
\tag{5}
\]

The second inequality interpolates \(U_0\succeq I\) and the endpoint
bound. For the published half-weight this improves its buffer \(1/2\)
to \((1+\mu)/2\). These are sufficient bounds, not asserted optimal.

The lift \(R=[-\mathbf1_m^T;I_m]\),
\(L=J_N+RC_\epsilon R^T\), \(M=(L-sI)/(N-s)\), satisfies support,
row normalization and \(0\preceq L\preceq NI\), with rank \(N-1\).
Both spectral endpoints are simple. Indeed the summands in the lower
lift have orthogonal ranges, and \(NI-L=RU_\epsilon R^T\) has kernel
exactly \(\mathbb R\mathbf1_N\). The unit endpoint gap is at least
\([1-\epsilon(\beta+1)(1-\mu)]/(N-s)\), because
\(RR^T\succeq I_N-J_N/N\).

Empty-to-nonempty entries of \(L\) equal
\(1-\epsilon(sq_c-m)\); their minimum is
\(1-\epsilon(\beta+1)\). They stay nonnegative in the enlarged interval
and vanish on maximum-size classes at its endpoint. No stricter empty
entry positivity is asserted there.

## Exact sign boundary for this construction

If \(h\le2d\), (1) has nonempty off-diagonal entries at least \(-1\);
the same is true of \(C_P\). Together with the preceding empty-row bound,
all off-diagonal entries of \(M_\epsilon\) are nonnegative.

If \(h>2d\), some adjacent leaf singletons have distinct colors. A singleton
can have the same color as at most one other singleton, whereas its leaf
degree is at least two. For that leaf edge \(ij\),

\[
(M_\epsilon)_{\{i\},\{j\}}
=\frac{(1-\epsilon)(2d-h)}{d(N-s)}<0.
\tag{6}
\]

Thus in the proved repair interval, **off-diagonal nonnegativity holds if
and only if \(h\le2d\)**. Every sparse graph forces a negative entry in
this specified repaired family. Varying the mixing weight within it cannot
remove that obstruction. This does not rule out a different nonnegative
H certificate, a different core, or a different averaged partition repair.

## Maximality, equality, products and the fractional handoff

For an intersecting family of nonempty members with indicator \(x\) and
size \(t\), any H matrix satisfies

\[
\big(x-(t/N)\mathbf1\big)^TL\big(x-(t/N)\mathbf1\big)=t(s-t).
\tag{7}
\]

Hence \(t\le s\); equality forces a centered kernel vector. The actual
center star forces nullity at least one for every real H matrix, even an
uncapped competitor. The repaired rank \(N-1\) is therefore maximal.
Its one-dimensional kernel forces the indicator to be the center star
(subtract the empty equation and evaluate a center singleton). Its unique
maximum family is also elementary classical rank-two structure: an
intersecting edge family is a star or a triangle, and all other stars and
triangles are smaller here. These combinatorial facts are not new.

For any nonempty finite list of factors on disjoint supports, let
\(N_P=\prod N_j\), \(p=\max_j s_j/N_j\), \(s_P=pN_P\), and \(c\)
count the eligible factors. Each base has simple unit and lower endpoints
and all other eigenvalues of absolute value less than one. The tensor has
lower endpoint \(-p/(1-p)\) only by choosing one eligible lower factor
and all other factors unit. Its lower multiplicity is \(c\), giving rank
\(N_P-c\). Eligible centered star cylinders are independent (orthogonal
across factors) and force the same upper rank bound for every H matrix.
Equality indicators lie in their span; the empty coordinate makes their
coefficients sum to one, and center-singleton coordinates make them zero
or one. Exactly one is one, giving precisely the \(c\) eligible stars.
This proof applies throughout the enlarged repair interval. No large tensor
expansion or sampled graph eigenbasis is the all-products proof.

## Strengthening and improvement opportunities

**Proved here:** the centered buffer (4), the full doubled repair interval
including its strictly capped endpoint, the rational endpoint margin \(\mu\),
the improved buffer for every originally published matrix, and the exact
off-diagonal sign boundary (6). Neither the interval nor margins are asserted
sharp. The public code validates both repairs for 170 complete small labelled
graphs and all sixteen attributed larger examples.

**Most consequential next extension:** nonregular leaf graphs have
\(BB^T=D+A\), with a varying degree diagonal. The existing scalar adjacency
mode reduction no longer diagonalizes all coupled blocks. A new joint
operator inequality or direct Gram decomposition preserving the center-star
kernel and both caps is needed. Small successful examples or a numerical
failure would prove no universal statement.

**Concrete optimization:** the exact cap endpoint of the affine pencil
\(U_\epsilon\) could exceed the sufficient doubled interval. Deciding it
requires the generalized eigenvalue/Schur condition for the actual pair
\(U_0,U_P\); the lower norm bound alone supplies no sharp upper endpoint.
The color-class quotient can give stronger sufficient rational bounds.

**Sign frontier:** seeking nonnegative sparse certificates requires changing
the specific core or repair, since (6) is unavoidable throughout this family.
An exact alternate primal matrix or a complete dual obstruction is needed;
this sign calculation is not a nonexistence theorem for alternatives.
Formalizing the mode decomposition and determining-grid degree bounds would
separately reduce the remaining ordinary proof trust boundary.
