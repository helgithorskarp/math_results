# A 271-point feasibility barrier for a Moser exterior of the triangle kernel

## Result

Let `D={0,1,omega}` be a unit equilateral triangle, let `X` be the union of
`D` and the three complete unit circles centred at its vertices, and let `P`
be the standard 12-point patch in `X`.  Let `W` be a congruent copy of the
seven-point Moser spindle such that:

1. every point of `W` is outside `X`;
2. there are no unit edges from `W` to `P`; and
3. every generic component `Q(u)=K3 square C6` of `X-D` has at most three
   unit-edge incidences with `W`.

Then the complete strict unit-distance graph on `X union W` is
four-colourable.

This closes one inverse-design feasibility architecture under the exact
triangle-exterior cap bridge.  It is a restricted-family exclusion, not a
five-chromatic graph, a global theorem about arbitrary seven exterior points,
or progress below the published 509-point record.  Components with four or
more exterior incidences and Moser placements having patch contacts are
outside the statement.

The finite kernel has at most 271 vertices.  Each of the seven exterior points
has at most six neighbours in `X`, so there are at most 42 exterior-to-shell
incidences and at most 14 important components.  Hence

```text
12 + 7 + 14*18 = 271.
```

## Why every three-contact component is controlled

Pin the centre colours to `0,1,2`.  A generic component has 11 possible
column states and 44 compatible ordered transitions.  Exhausting all 64,824
unordered multisets of three forbidden-colour incidences gives exactly 18
nonextendible cases: for one shell vertex, the three restrictions forbid all
three colours other than its owner-centre colour.  A separate constructive
check uses the two standard three-colour rows and at most one recolouring to
colour every other case.

Thus a three-contact component can block a Moser colouring only when all
three contacts share one shell vertex and its three Moser neighbours use the
three non-owner colours.

Three Moser vertices can share such a unit neighbour only when their
circumradius is one.  Exact arithmetic in
`Q(sqrt(3),sqrt(11))` finds precisely ten triples:

```text
013  023  046  056  124  125  126  145  245  345.
```

For each owner colour and each triple, introduce one of 30 blocker variables.
A repeated blocker type is irrelevant because it excludes exactly the same
Moser colourings.  If a physical collection of components blocked every
Moser four-colouring, the selected variables would satisfy:

- one coverage clause for each of the 384 proper Moser four-colourings; and
- at-most-two capacity clauses for each Moser vertex and owner circle.

The capacity is geometric: the unit circle around a fixed exterior point and
the unit circle around one centre meet in at most two points.  Distinct
three-contact components correspond to distinct shell vertices.

The resulting canonical CNF has 30 variables and 522 clauses: 384 coverage
clauses and 138 capacity clauses.  It is unsatisfiable.  Therefore some proper
Moser four-colouring survives every physically possible collection of
three-contact blockers.  The standard patch three-colouring and the component
extension procedure then colour the complete infinite support.

## Campaign boundary

The result explains two failed state searches.  A three-colourable exterior
cycle never needs colour 3, so every component with three incidences extends.
A Moser exterior does require all four colours, but the only geometrically
possible three-contact blockers cannot collectively cover its 384 colourings
without violating owner-circle capacity.

The active R3 triangle-kernel feasibility lane is therefore retired at this
boundary.  The general cap bridge is not disproved.  A future use needs an
external positive signal involving patch contacts, a different four-chromatic
exterior, or a component with at least four incidences; those are not licensed
as adjacent sweeps by this negative theorem.
