# Deletion-capacity localization at the lexicographic `S_13` boundary

This note gives a short structural reduction around the unresolved
`FC(4,9)` boundary studied by Pulaj and Wood in
[Local Configurations in Union-Closed Families](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01331).
It complements the earlier
[one-block Poonen-dual obstruction](https://github.com/njallskarp/math_source_code_open/tree/9d74deb1f90e9f6cbbc7a31d2bbd7896a63018e0/frankl_lex_segment_transition_obstruction):
instead of trying to transport that dual, it uses the exact lower-order
thresholds to constrain every extension which could still be Non-FC.

## Definitions

A family `C` is **Frankl-complete (FC)** if every union-closed family
containing `C` satisfies Frankl's conjecture.  FC is upward-monotone: if
`C` is FC and `C` is contained in `D`, then `D` is FC.

Let `L_13` be the first 13 four-subsets of `[9]` in lexicographic order.
Writing `ij` for the pair `{i,j}`, it has the particularly simple form

```text
L_13 = { {1,2} union e :
         e in {34,35,36,37,38,39,45,46,47,48,49,56,57} }.
```

Pulaj and Wood proved that `L_13` is Non-FC.  We ask only how a family of
additional, distinct four-sets can extend this particular boundary family.

## The theorem

Let `G` be a family of four-subsets of `[9]` disjoint from `L_13`, and suppose
that `L_13 union G` is Non-FC.  Then:

1. every member of `G` contains both `8` and `9`;
2. `|G| <= 3`;
3. if `|G| = 3`, then, after removing `{8,9}` from each added block, the
   resulting three-edge graph on `[7]` is one of

   ```text
   {16,57,67}, {17,56,67},
   {26,57,67}, {27,56,67},
   {36,57,67}, {37,56,67},
   {46,57,67}, {47,56,67},
   {56,57,67}.
   ```

   Under the evident subgroup generated by `(1 2)`, `(3 4)`, `(6 7)`,
   and `(8 9)`, these nine candidates have just three orbits, represented
   by `{16,57,67}`, `{36,57,67}`, and `{56,57,67}`, of sizes `4,4,1`.

Consequently **every four-uniform superfamily of `L_13` on `[9]` having at
least 17 blocks is FC**.  At distances one, two, and three, the deletion bound
leaves respectively `21`, `45`, and `9` candidates (or `9`, `16`, and `3`
orbits under the same subgroup).

These are necessary conditions for Non-FC, not assertions that the surviving
candidates are Non-FC.

## Proof

The known exact values include

```text
FC(4,6) = 7,   FC(4,7) = 10,   FC(4,8) = 12
```

and `FC(4,8)=12` implies the following deletion-capacity lemma.  If a
four-uniform family on `[9]` is Non-FC, then at most 11 of its blocks can avoid
any fixed point `v`.  Indeed, 12 such blocks are distinct four-subsets of the
eight-set `[9] - {v}`, hence are FC by that threshold; upward monotonicity then
makes the whole family FC.

The point degrees of `L_13` are

```text
(13,13,6,6,4,3,3,2,2),
```

so the numbers of its blocks avoiding points `1,...,9` are

```text
(0,0,7,7,9,10,10,11,11).
```

Thus the numbers of additional blocks allowed to avoid the respective points
are at most

```text
c = (11,11,4,4,2,1,1,0,0).                 (1)
```

The last two zeroes prove that every added block contains `{8,9}`.  Write the
added blocks as `{8,9} union e`, where the distinct pairs `e` form a simple
graph `H` on `[7]`, put `m=|G|`, and write `d_i` for degrees in `H`.  Equation
(1) becomes

```text
m - d_i <= c_i   for i in [7].              (2)
```

In particular,

```text
d_5 >= m-2,   d_6 >= m-1,   d_7 >= m-1.     (3)
```

If `m >= 5`, then (3) gives
`d_5+d_6+d_7 >= 3m-4 > 2m`, impossible because `H` has total degree `2m`.
If `m=4`, equality with `2m` would force every edge endpoint to lie in
`{5,6,7}`, but a simple graph on those three vertices has only three edges.
Hence `m <= 3`.

For `m=3`, (3) gives `d_6,d_7 >= 2` and `d_5 >= 1`.  The edge `67` must be
present: otherwise each of the three edges contributes at most one to
`d_6+d_7`, contradicting `d_6+d_7 >= 4`.  The other edges therefore have the
form `6a` and `7b`, with `a,b in [5]`, and at least one of `a,b` equals `5`.
This gives exactly the nine displayed candidates.  The orbit statement is a
direct action calculation.

For `m=2`, (2) says precisely that both vertices `6` and `7` are covered.
There are 20 pairs containing edge `67` and 25 pairs of the form
`{6a,7b}` with `a,b in [5]`, giving 45.  For `m=1`, all 21 edges on `[7]`
survive the bound.  Conversely, the checker verifies directly that these are
exactly the families satisfying all nine deletion capacities.

## Reproduce

Python 3.10 or later is sufficient; there are no third-party dependencies.

```bash
./run_checks.sh
```

`verify.py` reconstructs `L_13`, its capacity vector, every surviving edge
family, and the subgroup orbits.  `independent_check.py` uses bit masks and a
direct scan of all remaining four-sets.  `test_verify.py` exercises boundary
and mutation cases.  Expected output is recorded in `EXPECTED_OUTPUT.txt`.

## Scope and trust boundary

This result does **not** decide whether any of the 21 one-block, 45 two-block,
or nine three-block candidates are FC.  It does not prove `FC(4,9)=14`, the
lexicographic initial-segment conjecture, or Frankl's conjecture.  Its only
external mathematical input is the published equality `FC(4,8)=12`.  The
retained computation checks finite enumeration and symmetry
only; the localization and radius-three conclusion have the proof above.
