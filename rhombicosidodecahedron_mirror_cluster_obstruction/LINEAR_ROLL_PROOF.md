# A global linear roll bound enlarges the RID receiver caps

**Author: six-rupert-3. Role: researcher. 2026-09-30.**

This is an analytic theorem with exactly checked finite hypotheses. It is
not a formalization or an independent review. The global Rupert property of
the standard rhombicosidodecahedron (RID) remains unresolved.

Let `phi=(1+sqrt(5))/2`, and let `K` be the convex hull of all even
permutations, with independent signs, of

```
(+/-1,+/-1,+/-phi^3),
(+/-phi^2,+/-phi,+/-2phi),
(+/-(2+phi),0,+/-phi^2).
```

The vertex set `V` has sixty elements, `K=-K`, and every vertex has squared
norm `R^2=7+8phi`. Let `G` be its sixty-element proper rotation group, put
`d=(0,1,-phi^2)`, and let `T={g d/||d||:g in G}`. It has twenty directed
normals, or ten unoriented axes.

**Theorem.** Let `B1,B2:R^3 -> R^2` have orthonormal rows. If the unit normal
of `B2` has chord distance at most **1/7000** from `T`, there are no
`t in R^2` and `lambda>=1` with

\[
                 \lambda B_1K+t\subset\operatorname{int}(B_2K).       \tag{1}
\]

The source normal and planar roll are unrestricted, and the receiver cap
boundary is included. This enlarges the prior radius `1/2000000` by the
factor `2000/7`. The new geometric ingredient is a **global** roll bound for
the exact threefold shadow, replacing the previous square-root estimate.

## 1. Complete shadow and edge derivatives

Set `n0=d/||d||`, `P=I-dd^t/(d dot d)`, `S=conv(PV)`, and

\[
             \rho^2=20/3+8\phi,\qquad Jx=n_0\times x.                 \tag{2}
\]

[linear_roll_certificate.py](linear_roll_certificate.py) constructs the
exact convex hull in the oriented orthogonal chart with axes `e_x` and
`d cross e_x`. It is a cyclic dodecagon: all twelve hull vertices, and only
those projected vertices, have squared radius `rho^2`. All sixty projected
vertices are distinct. The checker validates every oriented hull edge
against **all sixty original vertices**, including hidden and collinear ones.

For an oriented edge `(p,q)`, its outward normal is
`m=(q-p) cross d`. The certificate checks

\[
    m\cdot(p-v)\ge0\quad(v\in V),\qquad m\cdot p>0.                    \tag{3}
\]

Since `m perpendicular d`, this is exactly the projected support inequality.
Put `ell=m/||m||`. There are two alternating edge types:

| type | `h=ell dot p` squared | `(ell dot Jp)^2` | original supporting vertices |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| short | `17/3+8phi` | `1` | 2 |
| long | `5+8phi` | `5/3` | 4 |

At the starting endpoint `ell dot Jp>0`; at the ending endpoint it is
negative, with the same magnitude. Each circle vertex has one incident
edge of each type and both derivative signs. Long-edge endpoint contacts
exist for **each** sign. Their unit support and derivative magnitude are

\[
                  h=\phi^3=1+2\phi,\qquad
                  \kappa=\sqrt{5/3}.                                \tag{4}
\]

The four-vertex long-edge ties are retained in (3); their middle projected
points are collinear boundary points. All 720 edge/vertex inequalities are
checked. The earlier certificate establishes that the whole shadow has
rotation group `C6`, generated by sixty degrees, realized by the body's

## 2. Roll estimate on the entire planar rotation circle

Let `D` be the planar unit disk. Suppose a planar rotation `U` satisfies

\[
                          US\subset S+\eta D.                       \tag{5}
\]

Choose `U0 in C6` so that `W=U U0^{-1}` has angle `alpha` in
`[-pi/6,pi/6]`. Since `U0S=S`, `WS=US`. Put

\[
              r=\|W-I\|_{op}=2\sin(|\alpha|/2).                     \tag{6}
\]

For `alpha>0`, choose a positive-derivative long-edge endpoint from (4);
for `alpha<0`, choose a negative one. Its outward support displacement is

\[
 \ell\cdot(Wp-p)=\kappa\sin|\alpha|-h(1-\cos\alpha)
       =r\{\kappa\cos(|\alpha|/2)-h r/2\}.                           \tag{7}
\]

It is at most `eta` by (5). Over this **whole** reduced interval, rigorous
rational comparisons give

\[
 \kappa>12909/10000,\quad h<42361/10000,\quad
 \cos(|\alpha|/2)>9659/10000,\quad r<5177/10000.                     \tag{8}
\]

Indeed `(433/250)^2<3`,
`r^2<=2-sqrt(3)<2-433/250<(5177/10000)^2`, and
`cos(|alpha|/2)^2>=(2+sqrt(3))/4>(9659/10000)^2`.
The other bounds follow by squaring `kappa` and comparing `1+2phi` exactly.
The brace in (7) is therefore greater than

\[
 \frac{12909\,9659}{10^8}-\frac{42361\,5177}{2\,10^8}
        =\frac{6014633}{40000000}>\frac3{20}.                         \tag{9}
\]

For `r>0` we conclude `r<(20/3)eta`; for `r=0` the weak bound is immediate:

\[
                   \min_{U_0\in C_6}\|U-U_0\|_{op}
                                      \le(20/3)\eta.                 \tag{10}
\]

In particular `eta=0` forces an actual shadow symmetry. There is no initial
small-roll assumption, circle-point assignment or sampled-angle cover.
This is a reusable polygon criterion when a cyclic roll group, signed
contacts as in (4), and the endpoint inequality (9) are available.

## 3. Diameter controls the arbitrary source normal

Write `delta=1/7000`. Central symmetry removes translation: reflecting (1)
gives the opposite translated inclusion, and their convex midpoint is a
centered strict inclusion. Since `0 in K` and `lambda>=1`, it also implies
unit-scale containment. We may use `B1K subset B2K`.

For thirty antipodal vertex representatives put
`f(n)=min_i |a_i dot n|` and `c0=1/sqrt(3)`. The inherited complete
calculation gives

\[
 \operatorname{diam}(BK)^2=4(R^2-f(n)^2),\quad \max f^2=1/3,\quad
 \beta=(19-8\phi)/29<1/3-1/1000.                                    \tag{11}
\]

All sign regions except the ten optimal ones have `f^2<=beta`. The ten
optimal region axes are precisely `T`. Choose `n0 in T` within `delta` of the
receiver normal `n2`. The minimum of absolute linear forms is itself
`R`-Lipschitz. Since `R<9/2`, diameter containment forces

\[
            f(n_1)\ge f(n_2)\ge c_0-R\delta>1/2.                    \tag{12}
\]

Using `c0<3/5`,

\[
 f(n_1)^2\ge(c_0-R\delta)^2
      >1/3-(27/5)\delta>1/3-1/1000.                                \tag{13}
\]

Thus the arbitrary source lies in an optimal sign region. Let `n10` be its
directed optimizer. The inherited balanced active triangle has inradius
greater than two and yields

\[
 f(n_1)\le c_0(n_1\cdot n_{10})-2\|P_{n_{10}}n_1\|.               \tag{14}
\]

Positivity implies `n1 dot n10>0`. With `s=||P_n10 n1||`, (12)-(14) give
`2s<=c0-f(n1)<=R delta<(9/2)delta`, and hence

\[
             \|n_1-n_{10}\|\le\sqrt2s<(13/4)\delta,                 \tag{15}
\]

using `2(9/4)^2<(13/4)^2`. The proper group is transitive on all twenty
directed optimizers, including antipodes. An independent source body
symmetry aligns `n10` with the receiver's `n0` without changing the source
shadow. From now on (15) measures distance from `n0`.

## 4. Full relative rotation, including roll

Let `Ai` be the minimal proper rotation from `n0` to `ni`, and set
`B0=B2 A2`. There is a planar `U in SO(2)` such that

\[
                  B_2=B_0A_2^t,\qquad B_1=UB_0A_1^t.               \tag{16}
\]

The operator distance `||Ai-I||` equals the normal chord distance.
Vertex norms are below `9/2`, so containment implies

\[
 U(B_0K)\subset B_0K+\eta D,\qquad
        \eta<(9/2)(13/4+1)\delta=(153/8)\delta.                     \tag{17}
\]

Since `B0K` is isometric to `S`, (10) chooses `U0 in C6` with

\[
                         \|U-U_0\|_{op}<(255/2)\delta.              \tag{18}
\]

Choose an axial body symmetry `h` and `sigma in {+1,-1}` satisfying
`sigma U0 B0 h=B0`. Replace `B1` by `B1'=sigma B1 h`, which has the same
source set because `hK=K=-K`. With `A1'=h^t A1 h`, `W=U U0^{-1}`,

\[
                             B_1'=W B_0(A_1')^t.                    \tag{19}
\]

The angle of `A1'` equals that of `A1`. Its normal stays within
`(13/4)delta` of `n0`. The row-frame half-turn is needed for the three odd
multiples of sixty degrees and leaves the cross-product normal unchanged.

Complete the row frames by their cross-product normals to proper matrices
`S0,S1',S2`. Then

\[
 Q=S_2^tS_1'=A_2 S_0^t\operatorname{diag}(W,1)S_0(A_1')^t,
                          \qquad B_1'=B_2Q.                         \tag{20}
\]

All three factor chords are less than `1/50`. A chord `x<=1/50` has angle
`2 arcsin(x/2)<(101/100)x` when `x>0`: its derivative is bounded by
`101/100`, since `(101/100)^2(1-1/10000)>1`.
Rotation angles obey the triangle inequality here. For a direct proof,
unit quaternions for angles `a,b`, `a+b<pi`, have product scalar part
at least `cos(a/2)cos(b/2)-sin(a/2)sin(b/2)=cos((a+b)/2)>0`.
Their product angle is at most `a+b`. Apply twice to (20); the sum is
less than one. The **complete** relative rotation angle therefore satisfies

\[
 \theta\le(101/100)(13/4+1+255/2)\delta
       =\frac{53227}{400}\delta<134\delta=67/3500.                  \tag{21}
\]

This controls roll as well as normal tilt. If all factors are identity,
`theta=0` and the shadow sets coincide.

## 5. Actual receiver contacts exclude this angle

Reduce the receiver plane into the three-wall chamber of the cell proof.
A proper body symmetry applied to both frames conjugates `Q` and preserves
its angle. If normal reversal is needed, a common planar reflection `O`
of both frames reverses their normals and preserves containment and `Q`;
their proper completions are multiplied on the left by the same
`diag(O,det O)`. Signed chamber reduction thus preserves (21).

The center there is `B=(0,phi^-2,1)`. Every other signed orbit center
violates some inward unit wall by more than `1/100`, as previously checked.
Because `delta<1/100`, the reduced cap must stay centered at `B/||B||`.
For the resulting unit receiver normal `n`, let `u=n/n_z`. The inherited
bounds `||B||<5/4`, `n_z>79/100` give

\[
                               \|u-B\|<3\delta.                    \tag{22}
\]

All other chamber cells have `y<=D_y`, while `B_y-D_y>1/10`.
Since `3delta<1/10`, the receiver is in the closed cell `ABD`, including
its boundary. Its four inherited support contacts have vertices `vj` and
edge vectors `ej`, with `||ej||=2`, and

\[
 m_j=e_j\times u,\quad m_j\cdot(v_j-v)\ge0\ (v\in V),\quad
                          T_j(u)=v_j\times m_j.                     \tag{23}
\]

No unique-support hypothesis is imposed. At `B` the torque tetrahedron
contains a ball of radius greater than `1/10`; all positive balanced
cofactors and four facet distances are rechecked by the inherited output.
Equation (22) and `R<9/2` imply

\[
                   \|T_j(u)-T_j(B)\|<27\delta.                      \tag{24}
\]

Taking support functions in every unit direction shows that the actual
torque hull contains a centered ball of radius greater than

\[
                        r=1/10-27\delta=673/7000.                   \tag{25}
\]

A uniform support-function lower bound by a positive number is equivalent
to containing that centered ball; facet persistence need not be assumed.
The new exact bound `||B||<11/10` also gives

\[
 \|u\|<11/10+3\delta<10/9,\quad
       M=R\max_j\|m_j\|<(9/2)\,2\,(10/9)=10.                      \tag{26}
\]

For `theta>0`, let `a` be the unit rotation axis of `Q`. Some actual torque
has `a dot T_j(u)>=r`. The elementary exponential remainder
`||Q-I-theta[a]_cross||<=theta^2/2` implies

\[
 m_j\cdot(Qv_j-v_j)\ge\theta\{r-M\theta/2\}>0,                     \tag{27}
\]

because (21) gives the exact margin

\[
 r-M\theta/2>1/10-27\delta-670\delta=3/7000>0.                      \tag{28}
\]

Yet even closed centered containment requires the inner point `B2 Q vj`
to stay on the inner side of this receiver support line. This is a
contradiction. At `theta=0` strict containment is impossible by equality.
Translation removal and scale reduction prove the theorem.
