# Two-cell growth of Kaplan's sixteen-cell Heesch-four polyhex

Researcher: **six-heesch-2**. This is a bounded computer-assisted result toward
finite Heesch number five for unmarked polyhexes. It is not a record improvement.

Let `S` be the sixteen-cell polyhex in `family.py`, the unique hole-free
Heesch-four sixteen-cell example in [Kaplan's author dataset](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~csk/heesch/hex/16hex_3up.txt).
Let `F` consist of all connected, hole-free eighteen-cell polyhexes obtained by
adding exactly two cells to `S`, identifying shapes under integer translations,
the six rotations, and reflections of the hexagonal grid.

**Claim.** `F` has exactly 324 members. Of these, 200 have `H_h = H_c = 0`,
72 have `H_h = 1` and `H_c <= 1`, and 52 tile the plane periodically. Thus
every member with a finite Heesch number has `H_h <= 1`. This specific growth
family cannot provide the requested finite Heesch number at least five.

The periodic witnesses use two tiles in a fundamental region for 32 shapes and
four tiles for 20 shapes. The 272 finite upper bounds are checked by
independent DRAT proof validation, rather than inferred from search timeouts.
No exact `H_c` classification is claimed for the 72 shapes with `H_h = 1`.

## Conventions and source context

Cells use axial coordinates with neighbor differences
`(1,0),(0,1),(-1,1),(-1,0),(0,-1),(1,-1)`.
Allowed orientations are generated by `(x,y) -> (-y,x+y)` and
`(x,y) -> (x+y,-y)`, followed by translation normalization.

We use Kaplan's corona conventions: each new tile touches the previous patch,
the previous boundary becomes interior, and all earlier patches are hole-free.
`H_c` also requires the final patch to be hole-free; `H_h` permits holes only in
the final patch. Plane tilers have infinite Heesch number and are excluded from
the finite-record target.

Primary context is [Kaplan, *Heesch Numbers of Unmarked Polyforms*, arXiv:2105.09438](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.09438),
published in *Contributions to Discrete Mathematics* 17(2), 150–171 (2022).
The seed's published `H_c = H_h = 4` is prior work, not a result claimed here.
The downloaded sixteen-cell text has SHA-256
`cee2e3ef029fbd8a603f489835265e2f2077a75908423c539384e2e3cd259aa0`.
The coordinates are included, so reproduction needs no external dataset.

## Why the finite reductions are complete

**Grid alignment.** Every boundary corner of a polyhex has angle 120 or 240
degrees, and every boundary edge is a unit edge. On a completely covered
boundary, a vertex on the interior of another edge would create angles 120
and 180 degrees, leaving an impossible 60-degree gap, or angles 240 and 180
degrees, which overlap. Therefore covered edges match vertex to vertex.
Each newly added polyhex shares a full unit edge with the old patch. The
regular hexagons on opposite sides of that edge determine the same honeycomb
grid. Inducting over coronas proves that unrestricted Euclidean coronas are
represented by our grid placements. This is the local form of the known
full-tiling angle argument in Paul Church, *Snakes in the Plane* (Waterloo
thesis, 2008), Proposition 2.2.1.1; no novelty is asserted for grid alignment.

**Family enumeration.** In a connected enlargement by two cells, one added
cell is adjacent to the seed, and the other is adjacent to the intermediate
shape. We generate every such pair of successive additions. Intermediate
holes are permitted and final holes are rejected. Canonicalization under all
twelve grid orientations gives one representative per free shape. The ordered
list is fixed by its SHA-256 in `summary.json`.

**Halo exact cover.** `contacts(S)` enumerates every disjoint congruent copy
covering at least one cell of `halo(S)`. Any contact has some cell `q` in the
halo and some oriented tile cell `p`, so its translation is `q-p`; every such
translation is tested. A first surround is exactly a disjoint exact cover of
the halo by these copies, with their complete footprints checked for overlap.
The direct backtracking algorithm chooses an uncovered halo cell and branches
over every legal covering tile. Its exhausted failures establish the 200
zero cases. For the 72 one cases, its explicit first surrounds prove `H_h>=1`.

**Monotone halo-closure encoding.** For a requested depth `K`, let `U_K` be all
placements reachable from the fixed central tile by at most `K` disjoint
contacts, excluding placements overlapping the center. This finite universe
contains every tile in an admissible `K`-corona. Let `d(T)` be its minimum
distance in this universe. For each `d(T) <= i <= K`, introduce `z(T,i)`.
Require the central `z(center,0)`, `z(T,i) -> z(T,i+1)`, at most one selected
`z(T,K)` covering each cell, and, for every halo cell `p` of every `T` with
`i<K`,

```
z(T,i) -> OR { z(U,i+1) : p is a cell of U, d(U)<=i+1 }.
```

Every genuine corona gives a satisfying assignment by setting `z(T,i)` when
the tile's corona level is at most `i`. Halo coverage follows because the
previous boundary is interior to the next patch. Thus UNSAT at `K` implies
`H_h < K`, even after all hole restrictions have been dropped. SAT alone is
only a relaxed packing observation and is not used to claim a Heesch lower
bound. Here the required upper instances have only `K=1` or `K=2`.
`corona.py` also offers safe first-surround contact pruning at larger depths;
no larger-depth instance is needed for this result.

The chosen CNFs use sequential at-most-one constraints from PySAT.
`bounds.csv` records each canonical index, UNSAT level, CNF hash, original
proof hash and proof size. Every original proof was accepted by DRAT-trim.
The raw CNFs and proofs remain local generated data and are not published.

**Plane-tiling certificates.** A witness has periods `(w,0),(s,h)` and a list
of oriented translated copies. The checker tests pairwise difference vectors:
`(dx,dy)` belongs to the period lattice exactly when `h` divides `dy` and
`w` divides `dx-s*(dy/h)`. The witness has exactly `w*h` occupied cells with
no repeated lattice class, so its lattice translates cover every grid cell
exactly once. This verifies both absence of overlap and infinite coverage.
The checker uses difference-vector membership, independently of the residue
bitmasks used to discover the witnesses.

## Reproduction

Environment used: Python 3.12.14, `python-sat==1.8.dev24` (Glucose 4.1), and
DRAT-trim commit `2e3b2dc0ecf938addbd779d42877b6ed69d9a985`, built with
GCC 12.2.0 using `make drat-trim -j1`. All solver and numerical-library threads
are one. Run from the repository root, keeping environments and proof files
in `scratch`:

```bash
python3 -m venv scratch/heesch-repro-env
scratch/heesch-repro-env/bin/pip install -r heesch_polyhex_two_cell_growth/requirements.txt
git clone https://github.com/marijnheule/drat-trim.git scratch/heesch-drat
git -C scratch/heesch-drat checkout 2e3b2dc0ecf938addbd779d42877b6ed69d9a985
make -C scratch/heesch-drat drat-trim -j1
OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 MKL_NUM_THREADS=1 \
  scratch/heesch-repro-env/bin/python heesch_polyhex_two_cell_growth/verify.py \
  --checker scratch/heesch-drat/drat-trim --work-dir scratch/heesch-repro-proofs
```

Expected final counts are `Hh0:200, Hh1:72, infinite:52`, with
`upper_bounds_checked:true`. Reproduction checks every CNF hash and every
newly generated UNSAT proof. Exact proof bytes may vary with solver builds;
their original hashes are provenance, not the acceptance condition.

`verify.py --quick` checks family identity, all first-surround decisions,
and all periodic witnesses, but **does not** check the 272 SAT upper bounds.
It is a geometry check, not a substitute for the complete command.

The trust boundary consists of the unformalized geometric and encoding
arguments above, the Python implementation, PySAT's cardinality encoding,
and DRAT-trim's implementation/compiler. Glucose UNSAT answers are checked by
the proof checker. No floating point, imported search corpus, timeout-based
exclusion, or presumed completeness at a fixed cutoff is used. Configured
placement/clause limits and checker timeouts cause failure, not a theorem.

The original seed remains a finite Heesch-four example. These results do not
exclude cell exchanges, other eighteen-cell shapes, other seeds, or growth by
more than two cells. A concrete next route is cell exchanges that preserve
the seed's corona structure while breaking the periodic tilings exposed here.
