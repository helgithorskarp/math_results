## 1. Quantified theorem and structural increment

Let G be **any finite simple d-regular graph** on h labelled vertices, where

```
h>=4,                 h/2 <= d <= h-2.
```

There is no vertex-transitivity, edge-transitivity, bipartiteness or
enumeration hypothesis. Necessarily hd is even. Form the rank-two downset
D(G) generated by the cone K_1 joined to G. Write a for the universal
center singleton, c_i for leaf singletons, x_i={a,i} for center spokes,
and e for leaf edges. Retain the empty set. Put

```
ell=hd/2,        N=2+2h+ell,       s=h+1,       rho=s/(N-s)<1.
```

The center star S={a,x_1,...,x_h} has size s. Every leaf star has size
d+2<s.

**Theorem.** There is an explicit rational symmetric H matrix M on D(G)
with row sums one, zero entries at intersecting pairs, and

```
-rho I <= M <= I,
M[A,B]>=0 for A!=B,
rank((N-s)M+sI)=N-1,
rank(I-M)=N-1.
```

The lower endpoint is simple and the upper endpoint is simple. The rank
N-1 is maximal among **all real H certificates**, including uncapped
competitors without a sign condition. S is the unique maximum intersecting
family. A centered predecessor has Hoffman PSD rank N-2 and a core upper
slack at least I; the explicit repair has core upper slack at least I/2.

For every nonempty finite list of such factors on disjoint ground supports,
let p_j=s_j/N_j, p=max_j p_j, N_P=product_j N_j, and
c=number of j with p_j=p. Their tensor has star size s_P=N_P p and
**unrestricted maximal Hoffman PSD rank N_P-c**. Its exactly c maximum
families are the full lifts of eligible center stars. Off-diagonal signs
are a base-matrix assertion; tensor entries may include signed empty loops.

In particular the theorem covers K_1 joined to K_(t,t) for every t>=2,
with N=t^2+4t+2,s=2t+1. More generally it includes nonbipartite and odd-h
leaf graphs, such as the triangular prism and complements of odd cycles.
The same formula covers arbitrary dense regular graphs of unbounded order.

## 2. Core and empty lift

Let A be the leaf adjacency matrix and B its unoriented vertex-edge
incidence matrix, with a one at each edge's two endpoints. Thus

```
A1=d1,       B1=d1,       B^T1=2*1,       BB^T=dI+A.
```

Set

```
alpha=(h-d)/d,
q=2/d,
w=2(d-1)/(d(h-2)),
z=2(2d-h)/(d(h-4)+2)=(2d-h)/(ell-2d+1).                  (1)
```

All denominators are positive under the hypotheses. In the nonempty order
(a,X,C,E), the symmetric core C_0 is

```
[ h       -1^T             -1^T                q*1^T                 ]
[ -1      (h+1)I-J         -I+(2/d)A           wJ-(1+w)B             ]
[ -1      -I+(2/d)A        hI-alpha*A          -B                     ]
[ q*1     wJ-(1+w)B^T     -B^T                (h+2+z)I-(1+z)B^TB+zJ ]. (2)
```

Each identity and all-one block has its indicated level dimensions. The
diagonal is h=s-1; every intersecting distinct entry is -1. For example
the leaf-edge diagonal is (h+2+z)-2(1+z)+z=h, its incident distinct entry
is -(1+z)+z=-1, and its disjoint entry is z. Singleton-singleton entries
are -alpha at leaf adjacency and zero elsewhere. Since 0<alpha<=1 and
q,w,z>=0, every off-diagonal entry of C_0 is at least -1.

Row counts prove **centering** C_0 1=0. The center row uses q ell=h;
the spoke row uses w(ell-d)=d-1; the singleton row uses
h-alpha*d=d; and a leaf-edge row uses
z(ell-2d+1)=2d-h. The center-star indicator v_S=(1,1_X,0,0) is also
killed: the only remaining leaf-edge equation is

```
q-2+(h-2)w=0.                                            (3)
```

For a core C on the m=N-1 nonempty members, define

```
R=[-1^T; I_m],       L=J_N+RCR^T,       M=(L-sI)/(N-s).    (4)
```

A PSD core with the prescribed entries gives H; this is the earlier empty
lift. Its two relevant identities are

```
rank L=rank C+1,
NI_N-L=R U R^T,          U=NI_m-J_m-C.                    (5)
```

The ranges of J_N and RCR^T are orthogonal, and R has full column rank.
Thus U>=0 is equivalent to the cap. For any centered core whose
off-diagonal entries are at least -1,

```
U=I_m+sum_(i<j)(1+C[i,j])(e_i-e_j)(e_i-e_j)^T >= I_m.     (6)
```

It remains to prove PSD and determine the whole kernel of (2).

## 3. Complete incidence decomposition for every G

G is connected: two components would each have at least d+1 vertices,
contradicting h<=2d. The eigenvalue d of A therefore has multiplicity
one. The identities

```
y^T(dI-A)y=sum_edges(y_i-y_j)^2,
y^T(dI+A)y=sum_edges(y_i+y_j)^2
```

give -d<=lambda<=d. On 1^perp the complement adjacency matrix has
eigenvalue -1-lambda and degree h-1-d. Its lower bound gives the sharper

```
-d <= lambda <= h-d-2.                                  (7)
```

No numerical spectrum is needed. Write b for the multiplicity of -d;
connectedness and the second quadratic identity imply b is zero or one.
In fact b=1 here can only occur at G=K_(d,d),h=2d, but this identification
is unnecessary for the decomposition.

**Edge-incidence kernel.** If Bv=0 then sum v=0. The edge-only subspace
ker B is invariant, and C_0 acts there by the positive scalar h+2+z.
Its dimension is ell-h+b, because BB^T=dI+A.

**Nonconstant leaf modes.** Let y be a unit adjacency eigenvector in
1^perp with eigenvalue lambda, and put g=d+lambda. If g>0, use the
orthonormal vectors y on the spoke level, y on the singleton level,
and B^T y/sqrt(g) on the edge level. The invariant block is

```
T(lambda)=
[ h+1            -1+2lambda/d         -(1+w)sqrt(g)       ]
[ -1+2lambda/d   h-alpha*lambda       -sqrt(g)           ]
[ -(1+w)sqrt(g)  -sqrt(g)             h+2+z-(1+z)g       ]. (8)
```

Distinct orthonormal leaf eigenvectors give orthogonal spaces, since
<B^Ty,B^Tv>=<y,(dI+A)v>. These images include every nonconstant vector
in range B^T. When lambda=-d, its edge image is zero and only the first
two levels remain, with block

```
[h+1, -3; -3, h+(h-d)].                                 (9)
```

This is positive definite. More generally the first two leading minors
of (8) are positive on (7): h-alpha*lambda>=d+2 and
|-1+2lambda/d|<=3, so their determinant is at least
(h+1)(d+2)-9>=11. The third leading minor is proved uniformly in Section 4.
Hence each present leaf mode is positive definite.

**Constants.** The remaining four vectors are a, C_sum, X_sum, E_sum.
Their ordinary Gram matrix is

```
F K F^T,
K=[h,-h; -h,hd],       F=[1,0; 0,1; -1,0; 0,-1].         (10)
```

The squared norms of the coordinate vectors are 1,h,h,ell; the displayed
matrix is a Gram matrix, not the normalized operator. K is positive
definite, since det K=h^2(d-1)>0. The constant block has rank two and its
kernel consists of the star relation a+X_sum and the relation C_sum+E_sum.

The complete dimensions are

```
(ell-h+b) + 3(h-1-b) + 2b + 4 = ell+2h+1 = N-1.          (11)
```

The edge kernel, all images of nonconstant leaf eigenvectors, and constants
are orthogonal and exhaust the core. Thus no transitivity assumption or
unaccounted incidence module is involved. Once Section 4 is applied,

```
C_0>=0,       ker C_0=span{v_S,1},
rank C_0=N-3,       rank L_0=N-2,       U_0>=I.            (12)
```

## 4. Exact uniform determinant certificate

This is a finite polynomial identity and coefficient-sign certificate,
**not** a finite graph census or a sampled-eigenvalue argument. Its full
small integer coefficient table is
[dense_cone_determinants.json](dense_cone_determinants.json); its generator
and checker [dense_cone_polynomials.py](dense_cone_polynomials.py) use only
addition and multiplication of polynomials over Z.

Write

```
a=h-d,       A=a-2>=0,       B=d-a=2d-h>=0,
X=a-2-lambda,       0<=X<=h-2=2+2A+B,
d=2+A+B,       h=4+2A+B,       g=h-2-X,
E=d(h-4)+2>0,
c=hd-a*lambda,       b=-d+2lambda,       k=dh-2,
e=E(4+X)+2B(1-g).                                       (13)
```

The letters b and E in this section are scalar numerators, distinct from
the multiplicity b and edge level E in Section 3. Define the polynomial

```
P=d(h-2)^2 * ((h+1)cd-b^2) * e
  -gE * ((h+1)d^3(h-2)^2 + c*k^2 - 2b*k*d*(h-2)).        (14)
```

Substitution in (8), or expansion of its rational unnormalized Gram
matrix, gives the exact identity

```
d^3(h-2)^2 E * det T(lambda) = P(A,B,X).                  (15)
```

The checker also verifies (15) independently by the six Leibniz terms:
multiply the unnormalized Gram matrix by d(h-2)E, expand its polynomial
determinant, and compare every coefficient with g(h-2)E^2 P. Both sides
are identical over Z[A,B,X]; there is no reconstruction or root-finding.

Write P=P0+P1*X+P2*X^2+P3*X^3. Expansion gives these certificates:

| Polynomial in A,B | Nonzero terms | Every nonzero coefficient | Constant coefficient |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: |
| P0 | 54 | positive integer | 2688 |
| P1 | 55 | positive integer | 2400 |
| P2+(2+2A+B)P3 | 44 | positive integer | 144 |
| -P3 | 21 | positive integer | 64 |

The table is checked entry by entry against the fixed polynomial (14),
not accepted from these aggregate counts. Since P3<=0 and
0<=X<=2+2A+B,

```
P >= P0+P1*X+[P2+(2+2A+B)P3]*X^2 > 0.                  (16)
```

The last inequality follows from the positive constant of P0. The positive
denominator in (15) proves det T>0 throughout (7), so Sylvester's criterion
closes every present three-level mode. At g=0 the three-level image is
absent; (9) handles that endpoint. At A=B=0, corresponding to h=4,d=2,
the same certificate still works; no limiting argument is used.

This symbolic certificate and the written incidence/dimension reduction
are the all-parameter proof. Extra matrix examples cannot replace either.

## 5. Explicit noncentered repair and signs

Label the h leaves by 0,...,h-1 and the center by h. With s=h+1,
color a singleton i by 2i mod s and a pair {i,j} by i+j mod s. This is a
proper partition of the nonempty intersection graph for **either parity
of s**: different members sharing i have different colors. Singletons at
different vertices are disjoint and may share a color. The center star
meets every color once, so all s classes are nonempty.

Let q_c be the class sizes, q_max their maximum, and m=N-1. They cannot
all be equal, because

```
2m=(h+1)(d+4)-(d+2),       0<d+2<h+1.                   (17)
```

In fact N mod s is never 0 or 1: those alternatives would require s to
divide d or d+2, respectively. Thus **every class in this theorem lies
outside the complete single-partition cap criterion** of PROOF.md.
This is a statement about that template, not a difficulty for the new core.

Define

```
C_P[i,j]=s*[color(i)=color(j)]-1,
beta=max(0,s*q_max-N),       epsilon=1/[2(beta+1)],
C_hat=(1-epsilon)C_0+epsilon*C_P.                        (18)
```

C_P is a PSD simplex Gram matrix with the prescribed entries, and kills
v_S. Its total quadratic form is

```
1^T C_P 1=s*sum_c q_c^2-m^2>0.                          (19)
```

Positive convex combinations of PSD matrices have intersected kernels.
By (12),(19), ker C_hat=span{v_S}: it removes exactly the total-core
direction. It follows that rank C_hat=N-2 and rank L_hat=N-1.

The partition's upper slack U_P=NI-s*[same color] has lower bound -beta I.
Using (6),

```
U_hat >= (1-epsilon)I-epsilon*beta I = I/2.              (20)
```

The nonempty off-diagonal entries of C_hat remain at least -1, so the
nonempty off-diagonal entries of M_hat from (4) are nonnegative. Its
empty-to-nonempty L entries are

```
L_hat[empty,j]=1-epsilon*(s*q_color(j)-m)>=1/2.            (21)
```

Indeed their subtracted quantity is at most epsilon*(beta+1)=1/2.
This proves **all** off-diagonal signs, including the empty row. The empty
loop is retained; there is no assertion that it must be nonnegative.
Equation (20), the full column rank of R, and (5) make the upper endpoint
simple. The lower endpoint is simple by the already determined kernel.

The core C_0 has total quadratic form zero and therefore also lies outside
every convex combination of s-class partition cores on this family: by
(17), every such integer class-size vector has strictly positive value
in (19). This is a separation of templates, not a general nonexistence
statement. The small mixture in (18) is allowed to use a different core.

## 6. Unrestricted rank and all product extremizers

For any H matrix on this downset and any intersecting family indicator x
of size a, put z=x-(a/N)1. Row sums and support give

```
z^T L z=a(s-a),       L=(N-s)M+sI.                       (22)
```

Thus a<=s and, at equality, Lz=0. The nonzero centered star vector
forces rank L<=N-1 for **every** H matrix. The constructed rank attains
this upper bound.

For the new matrix, Q=L-J=RC_hat R^T is PSD and has zero row sums.
At a=s, Qx=0. Since the family excludes empty, R^T x is its nonempty
indicator. Consequently C_hat R^T x=0 and (12),(18) force that indicator
to equal v_S. This proves uniqueness of the center star without importing
an extremizer classification.

For a product, the largest star has density max p_j. Each base matrix has
simple upper eigenvalue one, simple lower eigenvalue -rho_j, and every
other eigenvalue has absolute value strictly less than one. A negative
tensor eigenvalue reaches -max rho_j precisely when one eligible factor
is at its lower endpoint and every other factor is at one. Additional
nontrivial factors strictly reduce its magnitude. Hence the tensor's
lower multiplicity is c and its Hoffman PSD rank is N_P-c.

Centered eligible star lifts are mutually orthogonal between different
coordinates and lie in the kernel of every possible product H matrix.
They force rank <=N_P-c, proving unrestricted optimality. For the new
tensor, equality in (22) makes a maximum-family indicator a linear
combination of eligible coordinate-star indicators plus a constant.
Evaluation at the all-empty member makes the constant zero; evaluation
with just one center singleton makes every coefficient zero or one.
Two coefficients equal to one would give value two when both centers
are present. Exactly one is therefore one. These are precisely the c
eligible star lifts. No product-family enumeration is used in this proof.

## 7. Baseline, balanced case and exceptional-factor handoff

```

```

full arbitrary-graph proof. Its double-centered edge grid has scalar
2t+2; the adjacency-zero three-block has determinant

from (9). The core has exactly the two kernels in (12) for every t>=2.

epsilon=1/[2t(t-1)]. For this last count, the full rank-two family on
2t+1 vertices has cyclic classes of size t+1. Deleted pairs within the
two leaf parts use residues 1,...,2t-3 and 0,...,2t-4, respectively,
leaving three classes unaffected. No class is larger and one is unchanged.

would not be new. The **t=3 baseline** is useful because restriction of
