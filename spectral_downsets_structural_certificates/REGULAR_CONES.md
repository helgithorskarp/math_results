## 1. Quantified extension and established ingredients

Let G be **any finite simple d-regular graph** on h vertices, where

```
2<=d<=h-2.
```

No connectedness, transitivity, bipartiteness or enumeration assumption
is made. Form the rank-two downset D(G) generated by K_1 joined to G.
Keep the empty set and its allowed loop. Its nonempty levels are the
center singleton a, leaf singletons C, center spokes X, and leaf edges E.
Write

```
ell=hd/2,       N=2+2h+ell,       s=h+1,       rho=s/(N-s)<1.
```

The center star S={a,x_1,...,x_h} has size s; each leaf star has size
d+2<s. Necessarily h>=4 and hd is even for an actual graph.

**Theorem.** There is an explicit rational symmetric matrix M indexed by
D(G) with M1=1 and M[A,B]=0 when A intersects B, such that

```
-rho I <= M <= I,
rank((N-s)M+sI)=N-1,        rank(I-M)=N-1.
```

The rank N-1 is the greatest possible among **all real H certificates**,
including competitors without an upper cap. Both endpoints are simple.
Only S is a maximum intersecting family. Below d=h/2 the constructed
weights can be signed. For d>=h/2 this is exactly the earlier
[nonnegative dense-cone matrix](DENSE_REGULAR_CONES.md), not a new formula.

For any nonempty finite list of these factors on disjoint supports, put
p=max_j(s_j/N_j),N_P=product_j N_j and let c count factors attaining p.
Their tensor has largest star s_P=N_P*p and unrestricted maximal
Hoffman PSD rank N_P-c. Its maximum intersecting families are exactly
the c eligible center-coordinate stars.

The new scope is **every sparse regular degree**, including disconnected
graphs with several bipartite components, rather than h/2<=d<=h-2.
The centered formula is the same; new positivity regimes, a different
uniform cap proof and the complete disconnected-module treatment establish
its additional coverage. Ordinary H for all rank-two downsets already
follows from Vizing coloring, as in [PROOF.md](PROOF.md).
The largest-family star/triangle dichotomy is established elementary
rank-two mathematics. These classical conclusions are not claimed new.

## 2. Centered incidence core and all its modes

Let A be the adjacency matrix of G and B its unoriented vertex-edge
incidence matrix. Then

```
A1=d1,       B1=d1,       B^T1=2*1,       BB^T=dI+A.
```

Set

```
alpha=(h-d)/d,       q=2/d,
w=2(d-1)/(d(h-2)),   z=2(2d-h)/(d(h-4)+2).               (1)
```

In the nonempty order (a,X,C,E), the centered core is

```
[ h      -1^T             -1^T                q*1^T                 ]
[ -1     (h+1)I-J         -I+(2/d)A           wJ-(1+w)B             ]
[ -1     -I+(2/d)A        hI-alpha*A          -B                     ]
[ q*1    wJ-(1+w)B^T     -B^T                (h+2+z)I-(1+z)B^TB+zJ ]. (2)
```

The diagonal is h=s-1 and every distinct intersecting entry is -1.
The counts q*ell=h,w*(ell-d)=d-1,z*(ell-2d+1)=2d-h show C_0*1=0.
Also q-2+(h-2)w=0, so the center-star indicator v_S is killed.
The denominators in (1) are positive because h>=4,d>=2.

For every adjacency eigenvalue lambda,

```
-d<=lambda<=d.                                         (3)
```

Indeed dI-A and dI+A have the quadratic forms sum_edges(y_i-y_j)^2
and sum_edges(y_i+y_j)^2. On 1-perpendicular, the complement adjacency
has eigenvalue -1-lambda and degree h-1-d; its lower bound gives
lambda<=h-d-2. These statements do not require connectedness.

Let b be the **full multiplicity** of -d. Equivalently b counts bipartite
components: (dI+A)y=0 forces y_i=-y_j along every edge, giving one
alternating vector on a bipartite component and zero on a component
with an odd cycle. Regular bipartite components have equally sized
parts, so all these vectors lie in 1-perpendicular. Thus B has rank h-b.

**Edge kernel.** For v in ker B, its total is zero since B^T1=2*1.
The edge-only subspace is invariant with positive eigenvalue h+2+z.
Its dimension is ell-h+b.

**Nonconstant adjacency modes.** Take an orthonormal adjacency eigenbasis
on 1-perpendicular. For any vector y of eigenvalue lambda with
g=d+lambda>0, the spoke vector y, singleton vector y and edge vector
B^Ty/sqrt(g) give the symmetric block

```
T(lambda)=
[ h+1             -1+2lambda/d        -(1+w)sqrt(g)       ]
[ -1+2lambda/d    h-alpha*lambda      -sqrt(g)             ]
[ -(1+w)sqrt(g)   -sqrt(g)            h+2+z-(1+z)g         ]. (4)
```

Different orthonormal y give orthogonal images because
<B^Ty,B^Tv>=<y,(dI+A)v>. In a disconnected graph, nonconstant
lambda=d modes are present and included in (4); they are not absorbed
into the single all-one direction. At lambda=-d the edge image is zero,
and the first two levels have block

```
[h+1,-3; -3,2h-d].                                    (5)
```

The first two leading minors of (4) are positive on all of (3):
h-alpha*lambda>=d and |-1+2lambda/d|<=3 give determinant at least
(h+1)d-9>=1. Thus (5) is also positive definite. The final determinant
of (4) is positive by Section 3.

**Constants.** In the basis (a,C_sum,X_sum,E_sum), the ordinary Gram
matrix is F K F^T with

```
K=[h,-h; -h,hd],       F=[1,0; 0,1; -1,0; 0,-1].         (6)
```

The basis vectors have squared norms 1,h,h,ell. This is an unnormalized
Gram matrix, not the operator matrix. K is positive definite since
det K=h^2(d-1)>0. The two kernel relations are a+X_sum and C_sum+E_sum.

All the subspaces are orthogonal and their dimensions sum to

```
(ell-h+b)+3(h-1-b)+2b+4=ell+2h+1=N-1.                   (7)
```

The number b can exceed one. There is no missing connectedness bridge.
After applying the positivity certificate below, (2) is PSD and

```
ker C_0=span{v_S,1},       rank C_0=N-3.                (8)
```

## 3. Exact determinant certificates for every degree regime

Define scalar polynomial numerators, distinct from the edge-level notation:

```
H=h-2,           E=d(h-4)+2>0,        g=d+lambda,
b0=-d+2lambda,   c=hd-(h-d)lambda,    k=dh-2,
e=E(h+2-g)+2(2d-h)(1-g),
P=d*H^2*((h+1)*c*d-b0^2)*e
  -g*E*((h+1)*d^3*H^2+c*k^2-2*b0*k*d*H).               (9)
```

The exact identity is

```
d^3*H^2*E*det T(lambda)=P.                             (10)
```

[regular_cone_polynomials.py](regular_cone_polynomials.py) derives P and
independently expands the six Leibniz terms of the cleared rational Gram
matrix

```
[ (h+1)dHE   b0HE       -kgE     ]
[ b0HE       cHE        -gdHE    ]
[ -kgE       -gdHE      gedH     ].                     (11)
```

Its determinant agrees coefficient by coefficient with gHE^2*P over
the relevant integer polynomial ring. The third unnormalized coordinate
has squared norm g. Cancellation is an identity of polynomials, not a
division by an evaluated zero image. Direct rational substitutions provide
an additional check, including lambda=-d.

There are three exhaustive integer h,d regimes:

* **Dense, h<=2d.** The existing certificate in
  [DENSE_REGULAR_CONES.md](DENSE_REGULAR_CONES.md) uses
  A=h-d-2>=0,B0=2d-h>=0,X=h-d-2-lambda in[0,h-2].
  It proves positivity throughout the complement-bounded interval.
* **Middle, h=2d+1.** Set D=d-2>=0 and Z=d-1-lambda in[0,2d-1].
  The upper endpoint uses the complement bound lambda<=d-1.
  Substitute h=2d+1,d=2+D,lambda=d-1-Z in (9).
* **Sparse, h>=2d+2.** Set R=h-2d-2>=0,D=d-2>=0 and
  Y=d-lambda in[0,2d]. Substitute h=2d+2+R,d=2+D,lambda=d-Y.
  This includes lambda=d in a disconnected graph.

For the resulting polynomial in the spectral variable V=X,Z or Y,
write P=P0+P1*V+P2*V^2+P3*V^3 and let L be the interval upper endpoint.
Every nonzero coefficient of P0,P1,P2+L*P3,-P3 is a positive integer,
and their constants are positive. The complete small coefficient tables
are in [regular_cone_determinants.json](regular_cone_determinants.json).

| Regime | Term counts for P0,P1,P2+L*P3,-P3 | Constant coefficients |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Dense | 54,55,44,21 | 2688,2400,144,64 |
| Middle | 10,10,9,6 | 19680,14896,960,144 |
| Sparse | 50,49,37,17 | 36480,65632,2784,256 |

The checker reconstructs every coefficient from (9) and checks the
Leibniz identity (11); it does not accept these aggregate counts as proof.
Because P3<=0 and 0<=V<=L,

```
P>=P0+P1*V+(P2+L*P3)*V^2>0.                           (12)
```

Together with the positive denominator in (10), this proves det T>0
uniformly. Sylvester's criterion gives the positive definiteness used in
Section 2. The finite graph examples are not the infinite-coverage argument.
The middle regime is vacuous at odd d when hd is odd; no nonexistent
regular graph is imported into the theorem.

## 4. Uniform upper cap without an entry-sign hypothesis

Put U_0=NI_m-J_m-C_0,m=N-1. The dense weighted-Laplacian proof cannot
be used below d=h/2 because leaf-singleton off-diagonal entries can be
below -1. Instead decompose U_0 on the same orthogonal subspaces.

For all the current parameters,

```
0<w<2/(h-2),          -1<z<1,          1+z>0.             (13)
```

The lower bound follows from
1+z=[h(d-2)+2]/[d(h-4)+2]>0. If z>0 then d>2 and h>=d+2 gives
z<=2(d-2)/[d(d-2)+2]<2/d<1; otherwise z<=0. The w bound is immediate.
Also sqrt(g)<=sqrt(2d)<=d<=h-2.

On a nonconstant three-level mode, J acts as zero and U_0 has matrix
N I-T(lambda). Its diagonal entries are at least

```
h+ell+1,       ell+d+2,       h+ell-z.
```

Its off-diagonal absolute row sums are at most 3+h,3+d,2h-2.
For example (1+w)sqrt(g)<=h, and (2+w)sqrt(g)<=2h-2. Since ell>=h,
the respective diagonal-minus-row-sum margins are at least

```
ell-2>=2,       ell-1>=3,       ell-h+2-z>1.             (14)
```

For a symmetric matrix, the bound
2|v_i v_j|<=v_i^2+v_j^2 converts those margins into the same lower
quadratic-form bound. Thus every such block is strictly above I.
The missing-edge two-level mode is a principal block and obeys the same
bound. The edge-kernel slack scalar is h+ell-z>1.

For constants, the full total vector is a core kernel by centering and
has U_0 eigenvalue N-m=1. On its perpendicular, J vanishes. The two
positive core eigenvalues have sum

```
trace(K F^T diag(1,h,h,ell)^(-1) F)=h+d+3.              (15)
```

Consequently their individual values are at most that trace. On all
remaining constant directions U_0>=N-(h+d+3)>=ell+1>1. The trace uses
the normalized operator implied by (6), rather than the raw Gram trace.
This proves

```
U_0>=I,       ker(U_0-I)=span{1}.                       (16)
```

This cap mechanism applies to all regular degrees in the theorem,
including disconnected sparse graphs with signed core entries.

## 5. Explicit kernel repair, empty lift and product rank

Label leaves0,...,h-1 and center h. With s=h+1,color a singleton i by
2i mod s and a pair {i,j} by i+j mod s. Distinct members sharing i have
different colors; the center star exhausts all s colors. Let q_c be the
class sizes. They are not all equal because

```
2m=(h+1)(d+4)-(d+2),       0<d+2<h+1.                  (17)
```

In fact N modulo s is neither0 nor1: those congruences would force s to
divide d or d+2. Thus each class lies outside the single-partition cap
criterion, even though the new incidence matrix succeeds.

Set

```
C_P[i,j]=s*[same color]-1,
beta=max(0,s*max_c q_c-N),       epsilon=1/[2(beta+1)],
C_hat=(1-epsilon)C_0+epsilon*C_P.                       (18)
```

C_P is a PSD simplex Gram matrix with the prescribed support entries;
it kills v_S and has total quadratic form s*sum(q_c^2)-m^2>0.
The kernel of a strict convex combination of PSD matrices is the
intersection of their kernels. Equation (8) therefore gives

```
ker C_hat=span{v_S},       rank C_hat=N-2.
```

The partition slack U_P=NI-s*[same color] is at least -beta I. Hence
U_hat>=(1-epsilon)I-epsilon*beta I=I/2.

With R0=[-1^T;I_m],define

```
L=J_N+R0*C_hat*R0^T,       M=(L-sI)/(N-s).              (19)
```

The previously proved lift identities give M1=1,support,PSD,and

```
rank L=rank C_hat+1=N-1,
NI_N-L=R0*U_hat*R0^T.
```

R0 has full column rank and range1-perpendicular, so the upper endpoint
is simple. The lower endpoint is simple by rank L. The empty-to-nonempty
L entries are at least1/2 by the same beta bound. Nonempty off-diagonal
weights can remain negative below the dense degree range; no blanket
entrywise nonnegativity conclusion is made.

For any H matrix and an intersecting family of nonempty members, with
indicator x and size t,

```
(x-(t/N)1)^T L(x-(t/N)1)=t(s-t).                       (20)
```

At t=s,its centered indicator is in ker L. The center star forces rank
L<=N-1 for every real H matrix. For (19),ker C_hat is precisely its
star indicator span; evaluating the corresponding maximum-family vector
on nonempty indices forces x=v_S. This confirms unrestricted rank
maximality and the sole maximum family without an enumeration premise.

Every base has rho<1, a simple eigenvalue1,a simple eigenvalue-rho,
and all other eigenvalues of absolute value less than1. A tensor reaches
-max_j rho_j exactly when one eligible factor reaches its lower endpoint
and every other factor is at1. Therefore its lower multiplicity is c,
giving rank N_P-c. Centered eligible coordinate-star indicators are
independent and are forced kernels for every product H matrix, proving
unrestricted maximality. At equality, the centered family indicator is
a linear combination of the eligible centered star indicators. All
eligible stars have size s_P, so the empty coordinate forces the
coefficients to sum to one. Individual center singleton coordinates
make them zero or one. Exactly one coefficient is therefore one.
These are precisely the eligible star cylinders.

## 6. Fractional gap for every mixed exceptional/regular product

Use the **stronger capped** six-point D_* certificate from
[CAP_THEOREM.md](https://github.com/helgithorskarp/math_results/blob/main/spectral_downset_six_exact/CAP_THEOREM.md),
original cap source `145fedcf4a56269c398c1714c29567dce013ea23`.
It has N_*=32,s_*=11,six lower-endpoint directions and a simple upper
endpoint. Its known fractional dual is zero at empty/singletons,1/3 at
pairs and2/3 at triples,with total35/3. The regular-six source and
[independent review](https://github.com/helgithorskarp/math_results/blob/main/spectral_downset_regular_six_review5/REVIEW.md),
graph `bafkreiconcodpfzi5h4q72dqvnwxkzb7x65mtiujqlqynbbtudkcthcz3q`,
are credited inputs. No new D_* matrix, dual or review is claimed.

Take any finite product containing at least one D_* factor and any
number of the present regular-cone factors. Let N_P be its size and
p its largest star density. Because ell>=h,

```
(h+1)/(2+2h+ell)<=(h+1)/(3h+2)<=5/14<35/96.             (21)
```

The second inequality follows from h>=4. The exceptional density11/32
is also below5/14. Projecting its established fractional dual onto one
factor gives a feasible product dual of weight(35/96)N_P: nonempty
projections in a disjoint collection cannot repeat,and repeated empty
projections carry zero weight. Consequently

```
fractional independence >= (35/96)N_P >= (49/48)*s_P.     (22)
```

This **uniform fractional gap** holds even when a regular-cone factor
has larger density than D_*. It is a lower bound,not an exact optimum.
The capped tensor has maximal rank

```
N_P - (# eligible regular factors +6*# eligible D_* factors), (23)
```

Its maximum families are precisely the eligible coordinate stars; their
number is the subtracted quantity in (23).
This uses the same forced-star kernel and tensor mechanism as Section5.
If all regular factors have d>=3 or h>=10,then their densities are at
most11/32. Equality occurs only at h=10,d=2,so these factors can tie
D_*: the loss is6k plus the count of eligible ten-leaf degree-two
factors. Indeed 11N-32s=(11d/2-10)h-10, which is positive for d>=3
and nonnegative for d=2,h>=10, with equality exactly at h=10.
Here the dual/star ratio bound improves to35/33. This explicitly
handles a density tie rather than assuming exceptional dominance.
