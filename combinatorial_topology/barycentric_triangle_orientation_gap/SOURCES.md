# Sources and novelty boundary

Primary sources checked on 2026-09-20.

1. **Patrick Bennett, Ryan Cushman, Andrzej Dudek, Xavier Pérez-Giménez,
   “Almost-perfect packings and Tuza's conjecture in the random geometric
   graph” (2026)**,
   [arXiv:2606.09736](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09736).
   This supplies current context for the still-open general conjecture
   $\tau_\triangle(G)\le2\nu_\triangle(G)$. Its random-geometric setting
   is different from the barycentric graphs here. Our result proves an
   exact formula for the stated topological class, not the conjecture
   for arbitrary graphs.

2. **Jonathan L. Gross, Toufik Mansour, Thomas W. Tucker,
   “Partial duality for ribbon graphs, II: Partial-twuality polynomials
   and monodromy computations,” European Journal of Combinatorics 95
   (2021), 103329**,
   [publisher DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejc.2021.103329).
   Section 1 constructs the flag graph of a barycentric map; its
   orientability/bipartiteness equivalence is explicitly recorded.
   These are known ideas. We do not claim the zero-defect correspondence
   or the flag construction as new. The all-defect packing identity and
   the completion of a coherent subfamily are the proposed contribution.

3. **Vaidy Sivaraman, “Frustration in signed graphs” (2014)**,
   [arXiv:1403.7212](https://arxiv.org/abs/1403.7212),
   [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/1403.7212).
   Theorem 1 proves that vertex and edge frustration are equal for
   signed subcubic graphs, resolving a conjecture of Zaslavsky.
   This theorem applies to our signed facet-dual graph. Section 5 of
   PROOF.md includes an elementary proof for the simple-graph case
   and expressly credits Sivaraman. The matching bound of half the
   number of vertices is also recorded in this source.

4. **Sirui Chen, Jiaao Li, Zhouningxin Wang,
   “Frustration indices of signed subcubic graphs” (2025)**,
   [arXiv:2511.15226](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15226).
   This gives substantially stronger frustration bounds, with precise
   connectivity hypotheses and exceptional signed graphs. Its current
   abstract was checked to avoid presenting our elementary $6/5$ ratio
   consequence as a frontier bound. These stronger results are not
   needed for the exact identity or its proof, and no new numerical
   bound is claimed here.

Targeted searches included combinations of “triangle packing”,
“barycentric subdivision”, “flag graph”, “independence number”,
“orientability”, “nonorientable”, “hexagon”, and “frustration”.
No matching quantitative identity or coherent-subfamily completion
theorem was found in this bounded search. This supports an apparently
new connection relative to the sources inspected, not a global priority
claim. The proof is self-contained for the quantitative assertion;
the literature is needed for accurate attribution and context.
