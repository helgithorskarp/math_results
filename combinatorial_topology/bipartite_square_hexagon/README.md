# Hexagonal spheres in squares of bipartite graphs

Let `B` be a finite connected bipartite graph with no four-cycle as a
subgraph. Its square `B^2` joins vertices at distance at most two.

**Every six-cycle of `B` gives an induced octahedral sphere whose integral
homology class survives in `Cl(B^2)` and is primitive.** In particular,

\[
\operatorname{Cl}(B^2)\text{ is aspherical}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad B\text{ has no six-cycle}.
\]

In the no-six-cycle case the complex simplicially collapses to `B`; that
direction is already covered by Adamaszek's girth theorem. The surviving
spheres are detected by an explicit integral chain map to the lattice
generated by six-cycles in `H_1(B;Z)`.

Writing `K_X,K_Y` for the clique complexes of the two half-squares and
`L_6` for that cycle lattice, the same description gives

\[
0\longrightarrow H_2(K_X)\oplus H_2(K_Y)
\longrightarrow H_2(\operatorname{Cl}(B^2))
\longrightarrow L_6\longrightarrow0.
\]

The sequence splits as abelian groups, with a section represented by
spherical classes. Splitting here does **not** mean a wedge decomposition
of spaces or a splitting of fundamental-group modules. Six-cycle relations
can leave additional classes in the two half-squares.

[PROOF.md](PROOF.md) gives the proof, the fundamental-group description,
and the homology formulas in other degrees. [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md)
credits the prior graph-power, total-graph, Dowker, and bipartite-relation
machinery. The proposed addition is the ambient six-cycle survival and its
explicit cycle-lattice specialization. No historical-priority claim or
solution of Whitehead's conjecture is made.

## Reproduction

Python 3.11+ and the standard library suffice:

```bash
python3 verify.py --check
sha256sum -c SHA256SUMS
```

Expected line:

```text
PASS: collapses, integral hexagon detectors, and homology identities
```

The checker exhausts the 725 connected `C4`-free graphs on the prescribed
labelled bipartitions with `1<=|X|<=|Y|` and `|X|+|Y|<=7`. It verifies
5,907 actual collapse pairs, 78 integral hexagonal spheres, and homology
identities over `F_2,F_3`. Six larger/boundary fixtures are also checked over
`Q`, including the Heawood graph and cases with nonzero half-square `H_2`
or surviving `H_1`. A graph containing four-cycles shows why that exclusion
is necessary: a six-cycle sphere can then be killed by a cone.

Checks use explicit exceptions, not `assert`. No solver, floating-point
arithmetic, external data, or large certificate is required. The finite
audit corroborates the ordinary proof; it does not compute arbitrary
homotopy groups or certify integral torsion. Independent review is pending.
