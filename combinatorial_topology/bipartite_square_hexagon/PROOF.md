# Six-cycle survival and the exact asphericity boundary

## 1. Statement

Let `B=(X,Y;E)` be a finite connected simple bipartite graph with at least
one edge. Assume it has no cycle of length four, as a subgraph. Let

\[
K=\operatorname{Cl}(B^2),\qquad K_X=K[X],\qquad K_Y=K[Y],
\]

where `B^2` joins distinct vertices at graph distance at most two, and
`Cl` takes the clique complex. Orient the edges of `B` once and for all.
Because `B` is one dimensional, its integral cycle group

\[
Z_1(B;\mathbb Z)=H_1(B;\mathbb Z)
\]

is literally a subgroup of the free group of edge chains. Let `L_6` be
the subgroup generated by the oriented simple six-cycles of `B`.

**Theorem.**

1. Each six-cycle `C` induces an octahedral sphere `S_C` in `K`. There is
   an explicit homomorphism
   \[
   \Phi_*:H_2(K;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow L_6
   \]
   sending the appropriately oriented class of `S_C` to the edge cycle
   `C`. Thus every such sphere survives, its class is primitive, and
   `Phi_*` restricted to the spherical Hurewicz image is surjective.
2. There is a split exact sequence of abelian groups
   \[
   0\longrightarrow H_2(K_X;\mathbb Z)\oplus H_2(K_Y;\mathbb Z)
   \longrightarrow H_2(K;\mathbb Z)
   \overset{\Phi_*}{\longrightarrow}L_6\longrightarrow0.\tag{1}
   \]
   A section can be chosen with image consisting of spherical classes.
   For `j>=3`, the inclusions induce
   \[
   H_j(K_X;\mathbb Z)\oplus H_j(K_Y;\mathbb Z)
   \cong H_j(K;\mathbb Z).\tag{2}
   \]
   In degree one,
   \[
   H_1(K;\mathbb Z)\cong H_1(B;\mathbb Z)/L_6.\tag{3}
   \]
3. The map induced from `B` identifies
   \[
   \pi_1(K)\cong\pi_1(B)/\langle\!\langle
       \text{all six-cycle loops}\rangle\!\rangle.\tag{4}
   \]
   Loops are transported to a fixed basepoint by arbitrary paths; normal
   closure makes this independent of the choices.
4. `K` is aspherical if and only if `B` has no six-cycle. In that case
   `K` simplicially collapses to its subcomplex `B`.

Here primitive means that the sphere class generates a direct `Z` summand
of `H_2(K;Z)`. Splittings in (1) are not asserted canonical, equivariant,
or splittings of spaces. We do not assert that all hexagonal sphere classes
are independent or that they span all second homology.

The collapse direction, the isolated six-cycle octahedron, and the general
Dowker/bipartite-relation framework are prior art. The explicit survival
map and cycle-lattice description are the proposed specialized increment;
see [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md).

## 2. The star bridge

Vertices in different parts are adjacent in `B^2` exactly when they were
adjacent in `B`. A clique meeting both parts cannot have two vertices in
each, since those four vertices would form a four-cycle in `B`. Thus every
mixed clique lies in a centered star simplex

\[
\Sigma_v=\Delta(\{v\}\cup N_B(v)).
\]

Put `D=union_v Sigma_v`, `D_X=D[X]`, and `D_Y=D[Y]`. Then

\[
K=K_X\cup D\cup K_Y,\quad K_X\cap D=D_X,
\quad K_Y\cap D=D_Y.\tag{5}
\]

The pure faces of `D_X` or `D_Y` are precisely the sets with a common
neighbor. These are the standard Dowker complexes. `D` is the corresponding
mixed bridge in Kawamura's notation.

**Lemma.** `D` collapses onto the original graph `B`.

For each vertex `v` and each `sigma subset N_B(v)` of size at least two,
pair `sigma` with `sigma union {v}`. Such a `sigma` has unique common
neighbor `v`: two common neighbors of a pair of its vertices would give a
four-cycle. Every face outside `B` belongs to exactly one of these pairs.
For a fixed center, remove these pairs in decreasing order of `|sigma|`;
process the centers in any order. All proper cofaces of `sigma` other than
`sigma union {v}` are pairs with the same center already removed. There
are no cofaces from another center. Each is therefore an elementary free
face collapse, and exactly `B` remains.

We also need the classical bridge equivalences `D_X -> D <- D_Y`. For
completeness, order `X`. For every face `sigma=U union V` of `D` with
`V subset Y` nonempty, choose `x_sigma=min intersection_{y in V} N_B(y)`.
Pair faces not containing `x_sigma` with the faces obtained by adding it.
The common-neighbor set is nonempty by the definition of `D`; mixed faces
remain valid on adding `x_sigma`. Along a matching transition to a
different lower face, the `Y`-part must strictly decrease: if it stayed
fixed the chosen `x_sigma` would remain present. The matching is acyclic
and its critical faces are exactly `D_X`, so `D` collapses onto `D_X`.
Exchange the two colors for `D_Y`. This is the matching of Kawamura,
Theorem 2.5, specialized here, not a new Dowker theorem.

Every new edge `uv` of `B^2` has its endpoints in one part and has a
unique common neighbor. Replace its oriented edge chain by the unique
oriented two-edge path `p(u,v)` in `B`. Original edges map to themselves.
This defines an edge expansion map `e:C_1(D)->C_1(B)`. The expanded
boundary of every triangle in a star simplex is zero, so it induces
`H_1(D)->H_1(B)`. It is inverse to the isomorphism induced by `B -> D`. At the level of
paths, the triangle through the unique common neighbor homotopes each
new edge to its two-edge replacement, relative to endpoints. Thus the
same expansion identifies loop classes in `pi_1(D)` with those in
`pi_1(B)`, as used below.

## 3. Pure triangles and six-cycles

Consider a triangle on vertices `x_0,x_1,x_2` in `X`. Let `y_01,y_12,y_20`
be the unique common neighbors of its three pairs. If two of these
neighbors are equal, that neighbor is adjacent to all three vertices,
and uniqueness forces the third equal too. The triangle then belongs to
`D_X`, and its expanded boundary is zero.

Otherwise the three neighbors are distinct and

\[
x_0,y_{01},x_1,y_{12},x_2,y_{20},x_0\tag{6}
\]

is a simple six-cycle. Conversely each six-cycle supplies exactly one
such triangle in each part. There can be no chord of that cycle in `B`,
since a possible bipartite chord would create a four-cycle.

On the cycle's six vertices, `B^2` has exactly the twelve non-antipodal
edges. Indeed, antipodal vertices are in different parts and cannot have
distance two; an edge between them is the forbidden chord. Consequently
the induced subcomplex is exactly the boundary of an octahedron. Its
eight triangular faces comprise one all-`X` triangle, one all-`Y`
triangle, and six mixed star triangles.

## 4. The integral spherical detector

For an oriented triangle `[a,b,c]` of `K`, define

\[
\Phi[a,b,c]=
\begin{cases}
p(a,b)+p(b,c)+p(c,a),&a,b,c\in X,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.
\end{cases}\tag{7}
\]

Extend linearly to `C_2(K;Z)`. By Section 3 its values are in `L_6`:
they are zero on centered triangles and oriented six-cycles otherwise.

**Claim:** `Phi` annihilates the boundary of every tetrahedron.
For a tetrahedron entirely in `X`, all expanded edge paths cancel in the
alternating sum of its four triangular boundaries. For one entirely in
`Y`, all terms are zero. A mixed tetrahedron has a single vertex in one
part. The only potentially detected face is its three-`X` face; the fourth
vertex is then a common neighbor, so that face also has detector zero.
The two-plus-two color distribution is excluded by the four-cycle
hypothesis. This proves the claim and gives `Phi_*` on `H_2(K;Z)`.

Orient an octahedral sphere `S_C` so that its all-`X` face has coefficient
`+1` with the chosen cyclic ordering in (6). Its other seven faces have
detector zero. Hence

\[
\Phi_*[S_C]=[C]\in Z_1(B;\mathbb Z).\tag{8}
\]

The right side is nonzero: it has six distinct edges with coefficients
`+1` or `-1`. Evaluation on any one of these edges, with the appropriate
sign, composes with `Phi_*` to give a homomorphism `H_2(K;Z)->Z` taking
`[S_C]` to `1`. This proves survival and primitivity. Because `S_C` is an
actual sphere mapped into `K`, its class lies in the Hurewicz image.
Therefore `Phi_*` maps the spherical image onto all of `L_6`.

In particular, if any six-cycle exists then `pi_2(K)` is nonzero. This
uses a detected sphere map, not the invalid general implication that
nonzero `H_2` alone excludes asphericity.

## 5. Integral homology and fundamental group

Let `A=K_X union D` and `F=K_Y union D`, so `A intersection F=D`.
The bridge collapses onto `D_X` and `D_Y` from Section 2 extend over
`K_X` and `K_Y`, respectively. Thus `A` is homotopy equivalent to `K_X`
and `F` to `K_Y`, with the inclusions inducing the identifications.

The complex `D` contains the whole one-skeleton of `K`. Passing from `D`
to `A` adds only pure `X` faces of dimensions at least two. Its new
triangles have expanded boundary exactly the six-cycles, by Section 3.
Higher faces do not affect the fundamental group or first homology.
Consequently

\[
H_1(A)\cong H_1(B)/L_6,\qquad H_1(F)\cong H_1(B)/L_6,\tag{9}
\]

where both quotient maps come from the same `H_1(D)=H_1(B)`. The same
cell-attachment argument on `K` proves (4), and its abelianization proves
(3). General fundamental-group agreement of the two half-squares is
already the theorem of Larrión--Pizaña--Villarroel-Flores; (4) identifies
the relators in this restricted incidence class.

Since `D` is homotopy equivalent to a graph, Mayer--Vietoris for `A union F`
gives (2) and the exact segment

\[
0\to H_2(A)\oplus H_2(F)\to H_2(K)
\overset{\delta}{\longrightarrow} H_1(D)
\longrightarrow H_1(A)\oplus H_1(F).
\]

By (9), the last map, up to its standard sign, is the diagonal quotient
`z -> (z mod L_6,-z mod L_6)` and has kernel `L_6`. The connecting map
`delta` is `Phi_*` under the identification with graph cycles. To see this
directly, split a simplicial two-cycle into its pure `X` triangles and
triangles of `D`, assigned to `A`, and its remaining pure `Y` triangles,
assigned to `F`. The boundary of the `A` chain lies in `D`. Apply edge
expansion `e`: boundaries of its `D` triangles expand to zero, leaving
exactly (7). This proves (1), including its specified map.

As a subgroup of a free abelian edge group, `L_6` is free. Choose a basis
and express each basis vector as an integer combination of six-cycles.
The same combination of the sphere classes in (8) provides a section
of (1). Sums and differences of based sphere maps are again represented
by elements of `pi_2(K)`, so the section has spherical image. Equivalently
`Phi_*` composed with the Hurewicz map gives a split epimorphism of
abelian groups `pi_2(K) -> L_6`. No equivariant or module splitting follows
from this choice.

Relations among the graph six-cycles need not be relations among their
spheres: their differences can survive in the half-square summands in
(1). For example, subdividing every edge of `K_4` gives four six-cycles
whose cycle lattice has rank three, but the square complex has second
Betti number four. This is an inherited total-graph example, included to
make the distinction concrete.

## 6. Exact asphericity criterion and boundary

If `B` has no six-cycle, every pure triangle of `K` has a common neighbor.
Every larger monochromatic clique does too: fix two vertices; their
common neighbor is unique, and every third vertex of the clique must be
adjacent to that same neighbor. Mixed cliques are already in `D`. Hence
`K=D`, which collapses to `B` by Section 2. Graphs are aspherical.
Together with the explicit nonzero sphere in Section 4 this proves the
criterion. For bipartite graphs in the stated class, absence of six-cycles
is the same as girth at least eight, with forests given girth infinity.
The no-six-cycle direction is also Adamaszek's Proposition 2.2 at `r=2`.

The four-cycle exclusion is essential. Start with a six-cycle labelled
`0,1,...,5` and add vertex `6` adjacent to `0,2,4`. This is bipartite but
has four-cycles. Vertex `6` is universal in the square, so its clique
complex is a cone, and the old hexagonal sphere becomes nullhomotopic.

The theorem is about ambient clique complexes, which can have dimension
greater than two. It proves no inheritance statement for arbitrary
subcomplexes and does not resolve Whitehead's conjecture. It does not
classify graph squares without the two incidence hypotheses, nor assert
that `K` is a wedge of its half-squares and spheres. Finite homology audits
over fields do not settle integral torsion; the integral statements above
come from the chain map and exact sequence.
