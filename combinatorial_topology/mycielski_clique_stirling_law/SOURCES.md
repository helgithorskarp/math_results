# Primary sources and precise attribution

1. A. Hatcher, *Algebraic Topology*, Cambridge University Press, 2002,
   Chapter 0, Proposition 0.18 (homotopy-invariant attachments) and
   Exercise 25 (suspensions of disconnected graphs).
   https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/ATpage.html
   https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/AT.pdf
   The cone-gluing and suspension facts are standard topology. We apply them
   to explicitly described attachments; these general facts are not new.

2. P. Csorba, *Fold and Mycielskian on homomorphism complexes*,
   Contributions to Discrete Mathematics 3(2), 2008.
   https://cdm.ucalgary.ca/article/view/61954
   This studies Hom complexes and Mycielski constructions. Its suspension
   theorem concerns a different functor from the clique complex here.
   It is related context, not a premise or a result being corrected.

3. S. Goyal, S. Shukla and A. Singh, *Homotopy Type of Independence
   Complexes of Certain Families of Graphs*, Contributions to Discrete
   Mathematics 16(3), 2021; arXiv:1905.06926.
   https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.06926
   https://cdm.ucalgary.ca/article/view/71284
   This treats independence complexes of generalized Mycielskians of
   complete graphs. Independence and clique complexes of the same graph
   are different. Its formulas are not the wedge law proved here.

4. L. Santocanale, *Topological Properties of Event Structures*,
   Electronic Notes in Theoretical Computer Science 230(2), 2009, 149–160.
   Author manuscript:
   https://pageperso.lis-lab.fr/~luigi.santocanale/papers/GETCO06.pdf
   Proposition 1.8 discusses iterates M^n(K3) as excluded subgraphs for
   certain event structures; Section 3 treats antichain clique homology.
   The manuscript was checked at these passages. It does not state the
   arbitrary-base clique-complex wedge law, its Stirling coefficients, or
   the exact asphericity threshold claimed here. No event-structure result
   is inferred from this work.

The original graph construction is due to J. Mycielski, *Sur le coloriage
des graphes*, Colloquium Mathematicum 3 (1955), 161–162. We reproduce its
ordinary two-layer definition explicitly. No construction or chromatic
number theorem is claimed new.

## What is and is not new

The proposed contribution is the universal all-iterate homotopy formula for
finite connected K4-free graphs, expressed by local link cycle ranks,
repeated triangle incidences and triangle counts, together with its sharp
one/two/three-step asphericity and hereditary collapse classification.
The elementary cone argument, graph suspensions, incidence counts and
ordinary Stirling identities are classical tools. The proof is self-contained
apart from standard CW homotopy facts.

These are ordinary set-partition Stirling numbers, not graphical Stirling
numbers counting partitions into independent sets.

Graph-first selection used the committed Whitehead conjecture neighborhood,
which contains separate line-graph, total-graph and vertex-inflation results.
None is a premise for this different operator, and none is contradicted.
The general Whitehead conjecture is not resolved. There is no claim about
equivariance, naturality,
generalized-height Mycielskians, or arbitrary higher-dimensional bases.

Primary-source checks on 2026-09-21 did not locate this exact wedge law or
asphericity classification. This is a bounded search, not an exhaustive
priority determination. The new result has not yet received independent
review or proof-assistant formalization.
