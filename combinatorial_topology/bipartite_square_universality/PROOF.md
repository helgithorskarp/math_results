# Complete sphere relations and a construction with a cone half-square

All graphs are finite and simple. The square of a graph joins distinct
vertices at distance at most two; `Cl` denotes its clique complex. Homology
has integer coefficients unless specified otherwise. Realizations are
implicit in homotopy equivalences. A `C4` is forbidden as a subgraph, not
only as an induced cycle. Wedges and quotient spaces below are ordinary,
nonequivariant spaces unless explicitly stated otherwise.

## 1. Construction and complete statement

Let G be connected, with vertex set V, edge set E, n>=2, and m=|E|. Define
a bipartite graph B(G) with parts

    X = {z} disjoint-union {x_e : e in E},
    Y = V disjoint-union {p_e : e in E}.

For every e={u,v}, its four incidences are

    x_e--u,  x_e--v,  x_e--p_e,  z--p_e.                 (1)

Write C=Cl(G), regard G as the one-skeleton subcomplex of C, and put
K=Cl(B(G)^2). Then:

**Theorem A (homotopy realization).** B(G) is connected and C4-free, with
2m+n+1 vertices and 4m edges. Its X half-square complex is a cone, and

    K ~= (C/G) wedge (2m-n copies of S^2).                (2)

In particular K is simply connected. If C is simply connected, then

    K ~= C wedge (3m-2n+1 copies of S^2).                 (3)

The counts are nonnegative: m>=n-1 and n>=2. In the single-edge case both
counts in (2),(3) are zero and K is contractible.

**Theorem B (all integral hexagonal-sphere relations).** Each simple
six-cycle in B(G) supports an induced octahedral sphere in K. There are
exactly two kinds of such spheres:

1. `t_[u,v,w]` for every oriented triangle [u,v,w] of G;
2. `q_v(e,f)=-q_v(f,e)` for every vertex v and two distinct incident edges.

Choose the sphere orientations so their pure-Y triangular faces have
coefficient +1 in the orientations [u,v,w] and [v,p_e,p_f], respectively.
These spheres generate H2(K). A complete set of integer relations is

    q_v(f,g) - q_v(e,g) + q_v(e,f) = 0                 (4)

for every three distinct edges incident with v, and

    t_[b,c,d] - t_[a,c,d] + t_[a,b,d] - t_[a,b,c] = 0  (5)

for every oriented four-clique [a,b,c,d] of G. There are no further
relations except integer combinations of (4),(5). In particular there
are no relations mixing the two types that are not already consequences
of the displayed separate relations. Equivalently,

    H2(K) ~= C2(C)/d C3(C)  direct-sum
             direct-sum_(v in V) I(E(v)),               (6)

where I(E(v)) is the kernel of the coordinate-sum map Z[E(v)]->Z.
The isomorphism sends q_v(e,f) to [f]-[e]. Formula (6), with the indicated
generators and signs, is natural for graph isomorphisms and is an integral
Aut(G)-module isomorphism. No equivariant version of (2) or (3) is claimed.

Each individual hexagonal sphere class generates a direct infinite-cyclic
summand of H2(K). Nevertheless, integer combinations of these classes can
be nonzero torsion.

**Corollary C (universality and torsion).** Every connected simply
connected finite simplicial complex L is, up to homotopy, a wedge summand
of Cl(B^2) for a finite C4-free bipartite B having one cone half-square.
The complementary summands are explicitly many S2s. Every finite abelian
group occurs as the torsion subgroup of H2 of such a square complex with
one cone half-square;
H2 is generated by its individually primitive hexagonal sphere classes.
Section 7 gives a fully specified example with

    |V(B)|=338, |E(B)|=608, H2(Cl(B^2)) = Z^391 + Z/2.

Universality and arbitrary torsion for general C4-free bipartite squares
already follow from Adamaszek's total-graph theorem. The construction here
adds the cone half-square and the complete generation-and-relations theorem
on actual hexagonal spheres. In particular it puts arbitrary torsion inside
the subgroup generated by those spheres; the total-graph construction does
not do this, as explained in Section 6.

This is not a classification of all C4-free bipartite squares, a new
asphericity criterion, or a solution of Whitehead's conjecture. The new
construction is linear in the size of G; this is not a bound in a succinct
facet encoding of an arbitrary input complex.

## 2. The existing bridge and the homotopy mechanism

We recall the precise prior machinery used. For a C4-free bipartite graph
B=(X,Y), set K_X=Cl(B^2[X]), K_Y=Cl(B^2[Y]), and

    D = union_(v in V(B)) Delta({v} union N_B(v)).

A mixed clique cannot contain two vertices of each color: such four
vertices would give a C4. Thus

    Cl(B^2) = K_X union D union K_Y.                     (7)

The pure-color parts D_X,D_Y of D are the Dowker complexes. Kawamura's
Observation 2.1 and Theorem 2.5 give this bridge decomposition and its
collapses D->D_X,D_Y. They extend over the corresponding half-square:

    A=K_X union D ~= K_X,    F=K_Y union D ~= K_Y,
    A intersection F = D.                               (8)

These decompositions and collapses are prior art, not new theorems here.
For C4-free B, D also collapses onto B: pair every neighbor subset sigma
of a vertex v of size>=2 with sigma union {v}, in decreasing size. A pair
of neighbors has only one center. Therefore all other cofaces have been
removed before a pair is deleted, and every face of D outside B belongs
to exactly one such pair. This is also established in the predecessor
hexagon package. In particular D has the homotopy type of a graph.

All spaces in (7),(8) are finite subcomplexes, so their inclusions are
cofibrations and their unions are homotopy pushouts. If K_X is
contractible and K_Y is simply connected, (8) implies

    Cl(B^2) ~= K_Y wedge Sigma B.                        (9)

Indeed the union is the homotopy cofiber of D->F. Since D is homotopy
equivalent to a connected graph and F is simply connected, that map is
nullhomotopic: contract a spanning tree and then its finitely many loops.
The cofiber of a null map is F wedge Sigma D. The graph's suspension is
a wedge of beta1(B) two-spheres. Nullity, rather than just a zero map on
homology, is essential to this conclusion. Cofiber and wedge splitting
facts are classical and are not claimed as new machinery.

## 3. The two half-squares of B(G)

Two x_e vertices have a common neighbor precisely when their edges of G
share an endpoint; their private p_e are distinct. The vertex z shares
p_e with every x_e. Hence K_X is a cone over Cl(L(G)).

On Y, the original vertices induce G, the p_e vertices induce a complete
graph, and v is adjacent to p_e exactly when v is incident with e. A
mixed clique with two original vertices u,v contains at most the single
p_{uv}; with one original vertex v it is in the simplex on
{v} union P(v), where P(v)={p_e:e incident with v}. Consequently

    K_Y = C union W,
    W = Delta(P) union union_v Delta({v} union P(v))
                   union_(uv in E) Delta({u,v,p_{uv}}),
    C intersection W = G.                              (10)

Here W is contractible. Within W, each original edge uv has just the
coface {u,v,p_{uv}}; delete these free pairs for all edges. What remains
is Delta(P) with the cones v*Delta(P(v)) attached along their bases.
Each P(v) is nonempty. Each cone collapses to its base, independently
of the other cones, leaving Delta(P).

This is a collapse inside W, not a claim that these free pairs remain
free in K_Y; original triangles can prevent such an extension. The
homotopy-pushout argument, not an invalid extension of the collapse,
now gives

    K_Y ~= C/G.                                         (11)

Collapsing the entire connected one-skeleton kills all fundamental
group generators, so C/G is simply connected. Thus (9) applies.

For completeness B(G) is C4-free because two distinct vertices of X
have at most one common neighbor: z and x_e share only p_e, while
x_e,x_f share at most the common endpoint of two distinct simple edges.
It is connected because G is connected (the displayed paths also
connect z to every original edge). Its cycle rank is

    beta1(B(G)) = 4m-(2m+n+1)+1 = 2m-n.

Equations (9),(11) prove (2). If C is simply connected, the inclusion
of the connected graph G in C is nullhomotopic by the same spanning-tree
argument, so its cofiber is C wedge Sigma G. Since beta1(G)=m-n+1,
equation (3) follows.

## 4. Relative chains determine all sphere relations

This section also supplies a reusable presentation for a general connected
C4-free bipartite B. A noncentered pure-color triangle has three distinct
pair centers, giving a unique six-cycle; conversely every six-cycle gives
one such triangle of each color. Choose an orientation on each cycle and
orient its two pure triangles so that expanding each same-color edge to
its unique two-edge path gives that cycle. Let T be the free abelian group
on these oriented six-cycles, and let delta:T->Z1(B) be cycle expansion.

For each pure-X tetrahedron, take its simplicial boundary, discard its
centered triangular faces, and express the remaining faces in the chosen
T basis. Let R_X be the subgroup generated by these vectors. Define R_Y
in the same way. There is no division or saturation: these are integer
images of the displayed boundary matrices.

The relative complex (A,D) has no cells in degrees0 or1. Its degree2
group is T, and its degree3 boundaries generate exactly R_X. Similarly
for (F,D). Mixed faces are already in D. Hence

    H2(A,D)=T/R_X,     H2(F,D)=T/R_Y,
    H2(K,D)=(T/R_X) direct-sum (T/R_Y).                 (12)

Higher-dimensional cells do not change degree2 homology. Since H2(D)=0,
the long exact sequence identifies H2(K) with

    {([a],[b]) in T/R_X + T/R_Y : delta(a)+delta(b)=0}.
                                                               (13)

The sphere over a hexagon has opposite coefficients on its two pure
faces. Up to the harmless choice of its overall orientation, it is the
pair (t,-t) in (13). Thus the kernel of the sphere map T->H2(K) is exactly

    R_X intersection R_Y.                              (14)

The quotient of H2(K) by the sphere subgroup is

    ker(delta)/(R_X+R_Y).                               (15)

To check (15), map ([a],[b]) to a+b modulo R_X+R_Y. Its kernel consists
precisely of pairs equivalent to (t,-t); every element of ker(delta)
is the image of ([a],0). These are canonical integer statements with
consistent orientation choices, not rational-rank formulas.

When K_X is contractible, A is contractible. The long exact sequence
of (A,D) then says delta:T/R_X->H1(D) is an isomorphism. In particular

    R_X=ker(delta),    R_Y subset R_X.

Equations (13)--(15) therefore give the stronger conclusion

    T/R_Y --isomorphism--> H2(K)                        (16)

via the actual hexagonal sphere classes (orient them with pure-Y
coefficient +1). This proves both generation and completeness of the
relations on the Y side. No assertion about generation follows merely
from the earlier cycle-lattice detector; (12)--(16) supply the missing
argument.

## 5. Specializing the presentation to B(G)

All noncentered pure-Y triangles in (10) are either original triangles
[u,v,w] of G or [v,p_e,p_f] for distinct e,f incident with v. Every
pure p triangle is centered at z; every triangle [u,v,p_{uv}] is
centered at x_{uv}. This proves the stated list and count

    number of hexagons = number of triangles(G)
                           + sum_v binom(deg(v),2).       (17)

Likewise, a pure-Y tetrahedron is one of:

- an original four-clique of G;
- [v,p_e,p_f,p_g], with e,f,g incident with v;
- a tetrahedron entirely on P.

There are no tetrahedra with two or three original vertices and the
remaining p vertices: a p_e is incident with just two endpoints, and two
distinct edges of a simple graph cannot share both endpoints. An all-P
tetrahedron is centered at z and gives the zero relation. The other
two types give exactly (5) and (4), with an irrelevant common minus
sign for the latter. Equation (16) proves that this list is complete.

The triangle generators modulo (5) are C2(C)/dC3(C). For a fixed v,
the generators q_v(e,f) modulo (4) form the oriented-edge group of a
simplex modulo its triangle boundaries. Its boundary map identifies
this quotient with I(E(v)). One can check integrality directly: fix e0,
use (4) to express every q_v(e,f) as q_v(e0,f)-q_v(e0,e), and these
deg(v)-1 generators map to a basis [f]-[e0]. This proves (6).

The construction, orientation signs, sphere map and both relation
families commute with graph isomorphisms. The decomposition into the
two generator types is preserved. Therefore the resulting isomorphism
in (6) is Aut(G)-equivariant over Z, independently of any selected e0.

## 6. Primitivity, universality, and a local-relations obstruction

For C4-free B, the predecessor's integral detector can be described by
sending a pure-Y oriented triangle to its expanded graph boundary and
all other triangles to zero. It vanishes on all tetrahedron boundaries:
all-Y expanded edges cancel; all-X is zero; a mixed tetrahedron has
only a centered pure face; a2+2 color distribution is impossible.
It therefore gives H2(K)->Z1(B), and sends each oriented hexagonal
sphere to its nonzero simple cycle, up to sign. Evaluation on any edge
of that cycle is a homomorphism H2(K)->Z taking its sphere class to1.
This proves that each such class is primitive and has infinite order.
This detector and primitivity theorem are prior graph results, reused
here rather than claimed as newly discovered.

For comparison, take the ordinary subdivision graph S(G), whose square is
the total graph T(G). On the edge side, any four pairwise intersecting
edges of a simple graph have a common endpoint. Thus every tetrahedron on
that side is centered, and its relation subgroup R_edge is zero. By (14),
the actual hexagonal sphere classes in Cl(T(G)) are independent over Z.
Adamaszek's theorem Cl(T(G)) ~= Cl(G) wedge t(G)S2 already supplies universal
homotopy types up to S2 wedges and arbitrary torsion for C4-free bipartite
squares. But that torsion cannot lie in their free hexagon subgroup. Our
construction with one cone half-square supplies the additional generation property,
with all its torsion expressed by combinations of these primitive spheres.

For any finite connected simply connected simplicial complex L, its
barycentric subdivision is flag: pairwise comparable nonempty faces
form a chain. Let G be its one-skeleton. Then Cl(G)=sd(L) is homotopy
equivalent to L, so (3) proves the realization claim. A point can be
handled separately by the single-edge input graph. More generally any
connected finite CW homotopy type has a finite simplicial model; this
standard fact is only needed for the Moore-space formulation below.

For any finite abelian group A, take a finite simply connected Moore
space with reduced homology A in degree2 (a wedge of mapping cones of
degree maps S2->S2 suffices), and a finite simplicial model. The same
construction gives H2(K)=A plus the free summands in (3). In particular
the sphere subgroup can have arbitrary finite torsion despite the
primitivity of each displayed generator. Hurewicz identifies H2(K)
with pi2(K), since K is simply connected.

Three-term fan relations alone do not suffice. Take G=K4. Its construction
has17 vertices,24 edges, and16 hexagons: four original triangles and
twelve incident-edge pairs. There are four independent fan relations,
each using only q generators, and the additional relation

    t_123 - t_023 + t_013 - t_012 = 0.

It cannot be generated by the fan relations because their supports use
no t generator. The complete presentation gives H2(K)=Z^11. This is a
structural counterexample to the proposed fan-only presentation, not a
claim that17 vertices is minimal.

## 7. Compact exact 2-torsion witness

Start with the six-vertex complex P with triangular facets

    012 013 024 035 045 125 134 145 234 235.

Its one-skeleton is K6. In the cycle basis given by the ten edges outside
the star at vertex0, the ten triangle boundary columns have determinant
of absolute value2. Hence H1(P)=Z/2 and H2(P)=0; the checker verifies this
integer determinant directly. No recognition of P as a projective plane
is needed for the argument.

Let L=sd(P)*S0 be its simplicial suspension. It is flag and simply
connected, with H2(L)=Z/2 by the suspension homology isomorphism. Its
one-skeleton G has33 vertices,152 edges,240 triangles and120 tetrahedra.
Its degrees are12 six times,6 fifteen times,8 ten times, and31 twice.
Thus B(G) has338 vertices and608 edges; (3) gives

    K ~= L wedge (391 copies of S2),
    H2(K)=Z^391 + Z/2.

Formula (17) counts2071 hexagons. The presentation has only local star
relations and120 original four-clique relations; it need not enumerate
the exponentially many faces of the large simplex on the152 p vertices.

The generator produces, and the verifier directly checks, a direct
order-two certificate:
an integral2-cycle z in L, an integral3-chain w with dw=2z, and a mod2
2-cocycle eta with eta(z)=1. Under (6), the corresponding combination
of t hexagon classes therefore has order exactly2. Construction uses
the oriented sum of the ten triangles of P, the barycentric subdivision
chain map, and suspension. This certificate proves torsion integrally;
the assertion is not inferred from a mismatch of finite-field Betti
numbers. Every individual hexagon class nevertheless remains primitive.

## Evidence and limits

The Python checker uses exact integers and finite fields. It separately
constructs square graphs and their clique3-skeleta for small inputs,
checks all sphere chains, tests the actual relative boundary matrices,
and verifies the collapses used in (10). It also verifies the compact
torsion chain/cocycle certificate and the construction's incidence counts.
The large torsion example uses the proved reduction; a full enumeration
of its square's large simplices is neither required nor claimed.

The universal homotopy equivalences and integral completeness are written
proofs, not consequences of finite tests. They depend on the credited
bridge theorem, the stated elementary collapses, homotopy-pushout and
cofiber facts, and the exact relative-chain argument. No formal proof
assistant or independent peer review is claimed. Primary attribution and
the search-relative novelty boundary appear in SOURCES.md.
