# Sources and attribution

Checked 21 September 2026. The recurrence and sharp asphericity corollary
are proved in this directory. We claim no global priority: a bounded
literature search cannot establish absence from all earlier work.

1. Michał Adamaszek, *Clique complexes and graph powers*, Israel Journal
   of Mathematics **196** (2013), 295--319,
   [author manuscript, arXiv:1104.0433v3](https://arxiv.org/html/1104.0433),
   [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11856-012-0166-1).
   Section 5 was read. Theorem 5.2 supplies the one-step equivalence
   `Cl(LG) ~= Cl(G)^(2)`, used here as prior work. Theorem 5.1 gives a
   different sphere-wedge formula for the **total graph** `T(G)`;
   the present operator is `L^2(G)`. The star/triangle decomposition
   and null-attachment method are also classical ingredients visible
   in that section. No novelty is assigned to those ingredients.

2. Shuchita Goyal, Samir Shukla and Anurag Singh, *Topology of Clique
   Complexes of Line Graphs*, Art of Discrete and Applied Mathematics
   **5**(2) (2022), P2.06,
   [author manuscript, arXiv:2009.12130v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2009.12130),
   [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.26493/2590-9770.1434.bf4).
   The accessible manuscript was read. Its Lemma 3.2 gives the same
   one-step equivalence; its results include the subcubic non-`K4`
   wedge-of-circles case. The published numbering may differ; the
   cited lemma number is explicitly that of arXiv v2. Direct journal
   access was unavailable in this audit. Neither result is claimed new.

3. Yair Caro, Josef Lauri and Christina Zarb, *Index of parameters of
   iterated line graphs*, Discrete Mathematics **345**(8) (2022), 112920,
   [author manuscript, arXiv:2105.02496v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2105.02496),
   [journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.disc.2022.112920).
   Theorem C and its proof in Section 4.3 were read. They give the
   maximum-degree index three for prolific graphs (graphs other than
   paths, cycles and the claw), with finer exceptional-family
   classifications. Thus the degree-growth bound here is already
   known; the new-to-this-audit content is its use with the explicit
   homotopy splitting to obtain a sharp asphericity threshold.

4. Erich Prisner, *Homology of the line graph and of related graph-valued
   functions*, Archiv der Mathematik **56**(4) (1991), 400--404,
   [publisher record](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF01198228),
   [author bibliography](https://www.eprisner.de/CV/Papers.html).
   This relevant earlier work was identified and its bibliographic
   record verified. **Its full text was not accessible**: the publisher
   showed subscription-preview content, and the PDF request returned
   HTML rather than a PDF. Accordingly we do not claim novelty for
   homology recurrences or assert that this paper lacks the present
   result. The primary object here is an explicit homotopy splitting,
   proved directly, with a sharply stated corollary.

5. John Nicholson, *Stably free modules and the unstable classification
   of 2-complexes*, Mathematische Annalen **394** (2026), article 66,
   [primary article](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00208-026-03403-x).
   The introduction supplies current context for Whitehead asphericity
   and the Eilenberg--Ganea alternative. The graph-root problem remains
   unresolved by the present work; no subcomplex theorem is inferred
   from our operator obstruction.

Bounded concept searches included iterated line graphs with homotopy,
clique complexes with asphericity, sphere splitting, and the older
line-graph homology literature. No identical iterated homotopy formula
or fifth-iterate asphericity statement was found in the inspected full
texts. This is a modest structural corollary built on established work,
not a claim to solve a major conjecture. The inaccessible older paper
and journal-version access gaps remain explicit priority limitations.
