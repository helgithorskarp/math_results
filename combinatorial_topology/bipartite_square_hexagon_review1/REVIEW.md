## Proof reconstruction

Let `D` be the union of the centered star simplices of the bipartite graph
`B`.  A mixed clique of `B^2` cannot have two vertices in each color, since
that would give a four-cycle.  Thus

```text
Cl(B^2) = K_X union D union K_Y,
```

where `K_X` and `K_Y` are the two half-square clique complexes.  If a set of
at least two same-color vertices has a common neighbor, that neighbor is
unique.  Pairing such a set with the face obtained by adjoining its center,
in decreasing face size, is therefore an elementary collapse of `D` to `B`.
The usual Dowker matching instead collapses `D` to either pure-color part.
Because its matched faces meet the opposite color, the same matching extends
over `K_X union D` and `K_Y union D` without touching the corresponding
half-square.

A pure triangle has three pair-centers.  If two centers agree, all three do
and the triangle lies in `D`.  If they are distinct, the alternating six
vertices form a simple six-cycle; a bipartite chord would create a
four-cycle.  Conversely every six-cycle gives one pure triangle in each
color, and its six vertices induce the boundary of an octahedron in `B^2`.

Expand every same-color square edge to its unique two-edge path in `B`.
Define `Phi` on an oriented two-simplex to be the expanded boundary when the
simplex is pure in `X`, and zero otherwise.  Its value is zero for a centered
triangle and the oriented edge chain of a six-cycle otherwise.  The boundary
of every tetrahedron is killed: expanded edges cancel for an all-`X`
tetrahedron; an all-`Y` tetrahedron contributes zero; a `3+1` mixed
tetrahedron has a centered pure face; and `2+2` is excluded by the
four-cycle hypothesis.  Since simplicial three-boundaries are generated by
tetrahedra, `Phi` descends integrally to `H_2`.

On the octahedral sphere of a six-cycle, exactly its pure `X` face is
detected, so its image is the nonzero edge cycle itself.  An edge-coordinate
functional takes that sphere class to `1`; hence the class survives and is
primitive.  It is represented by an actual sphere map, not inferred merely
from nonzero homology.

For `A=K_X union D` and `F=K_Y union D`, the extended Dowker collapses give
`A` homotopy equivalent to `K_X`, `F` homotopy equivalent to `K_Y`, and
`A intersect F=D` homotopy equivalent to the graph `B`.  Passing from `D` to
either side attaches no new edges; the new two-cells are precisely the pure
triangles whose expanded boundaries are six-cycles.  Therefore each map on
first homology is the quotient by the lattice `L_6`, and van Kampen gives the
analogous normal-closure quotient on `pi_1`.  In the integral
Mayer--Vietoris sequence, the kernel of the diagonal quotient
`H_1(D) -> H_1(A) direct-sum H_1(F)` is exactly `L_6`.  Splitting a
two-cycle between the two sides shows directly that the connecting map is
the same `Phi`, not merely a map with the same rank.

Finally, `L_6` is free as a subgroup of the free edge-chain group.  Lifting a
basis by integral combinations of the detected sphere maps splits the short
exact sequence with spherical image.  If no six-cycle exists, every pure
triangle, and hence every larger pure clique, has a common center, so the
whole complex equals `D` and collapses to `B`.  If a six-cycle exists, its
detected sphere gives nonzero `pi_2`.  These two directions prove the exact
asphericity criterion.

## Human premises and completeness reductions

The high-confidence verdict depends on the following human-audited links;
the finite computation is not a substitute for them.

1. **Mixed-clique reduction.** A mixed clique with at least two vertices of
   each color contains a `K_{2,2}`, hence a four-cycle.  This makes
   `K_X union D union K_Y` exhaustive.
2. **Unique-center reduction.** Two distinct common neighbors of two
   same-color vertices form a four-cycle.  This supplies both uniqueness of
   edge expansion and uniqueness of every bridge-collapse pair.
3. **Bridge-collapse completeness.** Every face of `D` outside the original
   graph occurs in exactly one pair.  Decreasing size removes every other
   coface before a proposed lower face, including pure superfaces.
4. **Dowker-matching completeness.** Every face meeting the opposite color
   has a chosen common neighbor.  In a nontrivial matching transition its
   opposite-color part strictly decreases, excluding cycles.  The matching
   extends over a half-square because matched faces themselves meet the
   other color.
5. **Triangle dichotomy.** Equality of any two pair-centers forces equality
   of all three; otherwise the six named vertices are distinct and form a
   chordless six-cycle.  Thus no third kind of pure triangle is omitted.
6. **Chain-map reduction.** To descend a homomorphism defined on two-chains,
   it suffices to kill boundaries of all three-simplices.  The four possible
   color distributions are exhausted, with `2+2` impossible.
7. **Spherical primitivity.** The oriented octahedron is an integral
   two-cycle, its detected edge cycle has a coefficient `+1` or `-1`, and
   edge evaluation supplies a retraction to `Z`.  This proves both survival
   and primitivity without a torsion assumption.
8. **Cell-attachment reduction.** `D` already contains every edge of
   `Cl(B^2)`.  New triangles, and only new triangles, affect `pi_1` and
   `H_1`; their expanded attaching loops are exactly all simple six-cycles.
9. **Mayer--Vietoris identification.** `D` is graph-like, so the displayed
   exact segment has no hidden `H_2(D)` term.  Both outgoing maps from
   `H_1(D)` are the same quotient up to sign, and direct chain splitting
   identifies the connecting map with `Phi` integrally.
10. **Splitting reduction.** A subgroup of a free abelian edge group is
    free.  Expressing a basis of `L_6` as integer combinations of its cycle
    generators and taking the same combinations of based sphere maps gives
    a genuine group section with spherical Hurewicz image.
11. **Asphericity completeness.** The triangle dichotomy forces all larger
    monochromatic cliques to share the unique center when no six-cycle is
    present.  Conversely the detector proves a specific sphere has nonzero
    Hurewicz image, hence nonzero `pi_2`.

These reductions also explain why checking only Betti-number identities
would be inadequate: it would not establish the integral connecting map,
the normal closure in `pi_1`, sphericality of a section, or completeness of
the collapse.

## Adversarial smallest examples

The independent checker uses a separate representation and algorithmic
route from the producer's program.  It found the following.

- `P4` and `C8` contain no six-cycle; their square clique complexes coincide
  with the star bridge and the proposed collapse endpoints are exact.
- `C6` produces exactly one octahedral sphere and a rank-one detected cycle
  lattice.
- Subdividing every edge of `K4` produces four six-cycles but a cycle lattice
  of rank three.  All four spheres are detected, exercising the warning that
  cycle generators need not be independent and that a relation in `L_6`
  need not be a relation between the sphere classes.
- Adding a new vertex adjacent to the three vertices of one color of `C6`
  preserves the old induced octahedral boundary but creates four-cycles.
  The new vertex is universal in the square, so the ambient clique complex
  is a cone.  This directly tests that the four-cycle exclusion is essential.

For a broader finite frontier, the checker exhausts all connected `C4`-free
labelled graphs in the `3 x 5` and `4 x 4` incidence rectangles: 2,565 and
5,704 graphs respectively.  Up to exchanging colors, these are all
eight-vertex bipartitions capable of containing a six-cycle.  It independently
validates 2,268 octahedral sphere chains and detector images, every proposed
bridge collapse at its deletion time, both complete Dowker transition
digraphs, every tetrahedron boundary, the no-hexagon equality `K=D`, and the
rational ranks predicted by the exact sequence.  All checks passed.

The producer's exact checker also passed its manifest and its exhaustive
frontier through seven vertices, together with `C6`, `C8`, overlapping-cycle,
Heawood, subdivision, and field-characteristic fixtures.  Agreement between
the programs is only supplementary; items 1--11 are the completeness
argument connecting examples to the universal result.

## Source and literature integrity

The linked source at commit
`cad50af4369e20b48a079e1fdf91c840f35d8f34` matches the graph statement and
states its computational and novelty boundaries accurately.

Adamaszek's primary manuscript, [*Clique complexes and graph
powers*](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1104.0433), identifies the square of `C6` as
an octahedral sphere and already supplies the no-six-cycle collapse direction
at `r=2`.  Kawamura's [journal
paper](https://cdm.ucalgary.ca/article/download/62059/46707/177343) gives the
general bridge decomposition and its collapses to the Dowker complexes.
Larrión--Pizaña--Villarroel-Flores' [author
manuscript](https://xamanek.izt.uam.mx/map/papers/Fundame06w.pdf) gives the
general fundamental-group agreement among the square and half-squares.  The
target credits these facts and scopes its increment to the explicit integral
six-cycle detector and its consequences.

A bounded exact-phrase and structural search found no primary source stating
this full ambient survival map and exact sequence for arbitrary finite
`C4`-free bipartite incidence graphs.  That supports only the target's
search-relative novelty language; neither that search nor this review proves
historical priority.

## Strengthening and improvement opportunities

- Name the inclusions in the split exact sequence explicitly; this would make
  the natural part of the statement easier to distinguish from the
  noncanonical spherical section.
- Record one formula for the Mayer--Vietoris sign convention.  The proof is
  correct up to the harmless standard sign, but an explicit convention would
  make reuse of `Phi` more mechanical.
- A proof-assistant formalization of the collapse order and chain-level
  connecting-map identification would reduce the remaining trust boundary
  more effectively than extending the finite enumeration.
- Determining when relations among six-cycles lift to relations among their
  sphere classes is a natural next problem.  The subdivided-`K4` example
  shows that the answer is not encoded by `L_6` alone.
