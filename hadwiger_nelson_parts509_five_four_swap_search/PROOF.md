# Proof and trust boundary

## Exact objects

The accepted sealed-pool package supplies distinct exact plane points

    L = {0,...,373}, |L| = 374
    S = {374,...,508}, |S| = 135
    |Q5| = 168
    U = S union Q5, |U| = 303

All coordinates lie in Q(sqrt(3),sqrt(5),sqrt(11)) squared. The imported
ambient edge list is the complete strict unit-distance graph on a larger
certified support; the verifier restricts it to L union U. Every certificate
row names and directly checks a full colouring of L, so the argument does not
need completeness of the interface-colouring library.

## Killing-set lemma

For each certificate row, let D be a subset of U. The row supplies a proper
four-colouring of the complete strict graph on L union (U minus D). If X is a
subset of U and X and D are disjoint, then L union X is an induced subgraph of
that coloured graph and is four-colourable by restriction. Therefore every
non-four-colourable L union X meets D.

The final family has 5,528 distinct killing sets: 200 from the earlier
general-pool dual certificate, 2,793 from the earlier S-only certificate, and
2,535 newly generated by the CEGAR loop. The verifier decodes every word,
checks its shape and colour range, and tests every surviving pool or cross
edge. The twenty possible L words are each checked once on all internal
L edges.

## Finite selectors

Give each u in U a Boolean variable x_u. For every killing set D add the clause
requiring at least one x_u with u in D. For q = 4, 5, 6, add exact
binary-counter constraints

    sum over U of x_u = 134
    sum over Q5 of x_u = q.

The variable order is increasing ambient label order. The standard-library
generator uses equivalence clauses for every XOR and AND in a ripple counter,
prevents overflow, and pins the final binary bits. Hence the CNF is
satisfiable exactly when a 134-point set with q completion points hits every
certified killing set. Direct truth tables exhaust the same encoding for
every target on one through five inputs.

The three CNFs are UNSAT. Their DRAT refutations were produced by CaDiCaL
3.0.1 and independently accepted by drat-trim. Consequently no
non-four-colourable L union X exists with |X| = 134 and with exactly 4, 5, or
6 selected Q5 points.

If instead |X| < 134, add unused points of S until the pool part has order
134. There are always enough. Adding vertices cannot make a
non-four-colourable graph colourable. Thus the exact-order exclusions imply
the same result for every |X| <= 134.

Counts zero through three are covered by the accepted Parts criticality,
swap, pair, and triple closures. The four-count selector here is a
self-contained control and also follows from the stronger accepted
all-anchored four-point closure. Combining these results proves that any
non-four-colourable graph in this sealed pool of total order at most 508 uses
at least seven Q5 points.

## Search history and stopping rule

An outer selector proposed an exact 508-point set. If one of the twenty
interface patterns coloured it, that colouring was greedily grown through the
pool and deletion-minimised to a new killing set disjoint from the proposal.
The new set became another selector clause. Valid killing clauses were shared
across completion-count strata.

The exact selectors for four, five, and six completion points exhausted. The
seven-completion search was bounded at 600 requested seconds; the last outer
call was allowed to finish, giving 688 wall seconds. It produced four
colourable models and 45 additional killing sets. Its last model had nine
compatible interface patterns. No claim is made about the rest of that
stratum.

The next construction pass should resume at seven completion points, import
the 45 preserved clauses, and change the outer representation or branching
heuristic if model generation remains the bottleneck. It should not rerun the
closed lower strata.

## Trust boundary

- Exact realization and edge completeness are imported from the pinned,
  separately checked Parts sealed-pool sources.
- Every killing-set premise is checked here from a concrete positive
  colouring; the CEGAR SAT solver is outside the proof trust base.
- Selector nonexistence trusts the committed CNF bytes, DRAT proof bytes, and
  the independent C implementation of drat-trim.
- The CNF generator, Python integer and string arithmetic, source data,
  SHA-256, runtime, operating system, and hardware remain ordinary
  computational trust. This is computer-assisted mathematics, not
  proof-assistant formalization.
