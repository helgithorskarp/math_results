# The one-anchor family of the delete-5-add-4 closure of the Parts 509-graph: exact enumeration and declared-set computation

**Claim status:** exact computer-assisted theorem (closure of the one-anchor family).  Solver-free certificate: every undeclared pair (A, u) is covered by an explicit proper 4-colouring of G − u that extends to A, replayed by the verifier; declared pairs are conservative; the maximal |Û(A)| is 1 < 5, so no 5-subset D can satisfy D ⊆ Û(A).  Completeness of the enumeration rests on the float-screen bounds stated below (interval certificate for the non-K coordinates, forward-error bound for the intersection points).

## Setting and lemma

G = (V, E) is Parts's 509-vertex 5-chromatic unit-distance graph, V ⊂ K² with
K = Q(√3, √5, √11).  For D ⊆ V and a set A of points outside V, G − D + A is the strict
unit-distance graph on (V \ D) ∪ A.  The committed closures give: G − D + A is 4-colourable
for |A| ≤ 3, |D| ≥ |A| + 1.  For |A| = 4, |D| = 5 the committed lemma (one-anchor lemma)
shows: if G − D + A is 5-chromatic then in a vertex-critical 5-chromatic subgraph every added
point x has n(x) + m(x) ≥ 4 (n = vertex neighbours, m = neighbours inside A), and a point
with n(x) ≤ 1 (a *one-anchor point*) has n(x) = 1, m(x) = 3 and A is of

* **type I**: A = {x, y₁, y₂, y₃} with y₁, y₂, y₃ ∈ Q at unit distance from x, or
* **type II**: A = {x, y, b, d} with b, d ∈ Q, |b − d| = √3, {x, y} the two common unit
  neighbours of b and d,

where Q is the set of points outside V with ≥ 2 vertex neighbours: 1,158 points Q3 (≥ 3
neighbours, K-rational), 2,705 points Q2K (exactly 2, K-rational), 135,468 non-K
intersection points (all from the committed delete-4-add-3 closure; 139,331 points).

## Enumeration (`one_anchor2.py`)

For every anchor v ∈ V and every q ∈ Q with |v − q| ≤ 2 the two intersection points of the
unit circles around v and q are generated (tangent pairs give the midpoint); coincident
points (radius 1e-6, scipy connected components) are grouped, and a group with generator
set G_x ⊆ Q of size ≥ 2 is a candidate x with Q-neighbourhood G_x (complete up to the
float tolerance: every q ∈ Q at unit distance from x generates x).  A candidate is
discarded only when exact arithmetic in K shows that it is not an added one-anchor point:

* x = w ∈ V: w is at unit distance (exact) from v and from a generator; the two exact
  intersection points are ≥ 6·10⁻⁵ apart, x is within 10⁻⁶ of w, hence x = w;
* x = p ∈ Q3 ∪ Q2K (then n(x) ≥ 2): same argument (exact unit distance from v and from a
  generator, K-rational or non-K, both decided in K);
* x = p, a non-K point of Q: v must be one of p's two generating vertices, and p at unit
  distance from a non-K generator of x is decided by the exact non-K unit-pair list of the
  delete-4-add-3 closure.

Everything else is kept (over-inclusive).  Type I configurations are the 3-subsets of G_x
satisfying the necessary degree condition (a point with exactly two vertex neighbours needs
a second neighbour inside A); type II configurations come from pairs of G_x at distance √3
whose mirror point y has a vertex at unit distance.  The vertex-neighbour lists and internal
edges of the new points x, y use the over-inclusive tolerance 10⁻⁵ (if two distinct
intersection points within 10⁻⁶ were merged, every true unit distance is still within
10⁻⁵ of the merged coordinate); Q-points keep their exact neighbour lists.  Extra (false)
edges and extra (spurious) configurations only make the tests below more conservative.

**Post-filter (`filter_configs.py`).**  A type II configuration whose mirror point y is
exactly a vertex w ∈ V (exact test: w at unit distance from b and from d in K, hence
w ∈ {x, y}, and w = y since |x − y| = 1 and y is within 10⁻⁶ of w) re-adds a vertex: for
D ∌ w the point y is a twin of w, for D ∋ w the graph G − D + A contains
G − (D \ {w}) + (A \ {y}), a delete-4-add-3 instance, 4-colourable by the committed closure.
Such configurations cannot give a 5-chromatic graph on 508 vertices and are dropped
(they are exactly the configurations that made the SAT phase slow: for u = w the instance
G − w + A contains a copy of G).

**Counts (`stats.json`, `summary.log`; 509 anchors, 3,836 CPU-seconds).**  4,180,637 candidate
groups with ≥ 2 generators; discarded exactly: 4,884 (x is a vertex), 9,680 (x ∈ Q3 ∪ Q2K),
126,485 (x is a non-K point of Q); kept 4,039,588 candidate points (4,038,020 distinct; 16,047
with ≥ 2 K-rational generators, 659,601 with one, 3,363,940 generated by non-K points only;
669 near-coincidences with a vertex or a Q-point that the exact tests could not confirm were
kept).  Q-degree of the kept points: 2 for 3,574,353 of them (only type II can use them), 3
for 312,684, 4 for 91,803, ≥ 5 for 59,180, maximum 46.  Configurations: 133,415 type I and
22,213 type II before deduplication; 153,892 distinct point sets (133,403 type I, 20,489
type II).  The post-filter dropped 11,195 type II configurations with y ∈ V (3 near-vertex
cases kept), leaving **142,697 configurations** (133,403 type I, 9,294 type II).  Internal
edge patterns among the four points (0 = x): star + one edge among the y's 112,599; type II
(x~y, both adjacent to b, d) 23,743 before / 9,294 after the filter; pure star 8,251; other
patterns 8,304.

## Declared sets (`declared_sets.py`)

For each configuration A and each vertex u, (A, u) is *covered* if a stored proper
4-colouring of G − u (the committed base, swap, pair and triple witness libraries plus the
fresh rows found here) extends to A (list colouring with the internal edges); uncovered pairs
are decided by CaDiCaL (one incremental solver per u, configuration clauses under a selector
literal, conflict budget 20,000).  Û(A) = declared vertices ('unsat' or 'budget') ⊇ U(A) =
{u : G − u + A is 5-chromatic}.  A 5-chromatic G − D + A with |D| = 5 needs D ⊆ U(A) ⊆ Û(A),
so |Û(A)| ≥ 5 is necessary; every such configuration is tested directly on all 5-subsets.

**Result.**  Over the 142,697 configurations and 509 vertices: 2,457 declared pairs
(budget: 74, unsat: 2,383), 1 fresh witness colourings, 2,458 SAT calls,
3714 s with 2 workers.  Histogram: |Û(A)| = 0: 140,240, |Û(A)| = 1: 2,457.  Candidates with |Û(A)| ≥ 5: 0.
Hence no configuration of the one-anchor family gives a 5-chromatic G − D + A with |D| = 5.

The verifier (`verify_one_anchor.py certificate.json.gz --workers 2`, output in `expected_verify.txt`)
reports `all_checks=true` in 1898 s: all fresh rows proper, every uncovered pair declared,
max |Û(A)| = 1.

## Trust boundary

* Solver-free and exact: the discards of candidate points (K arithmetic of the sibling
  `kfield.py`, the exact non-K unit-pair list of the delete-4-add-3 closure), the post-filter,
  the replay of every fresh witness colouring against the edge list of G − u, and the
  coverage recomputation of the verifier (`verify_one_anchor.py`): every undeclared pair
  (A, u) is covered by a stored proper 4-colouring of G − u that extends to A.
* Float screens (completeness of the enumeration): the coordinates of V, Q3, Q2K are
  converted with `kfield.to_float` (relative error < 10⁻¹⁵); the non-K coordinates carry the
  exact interval certificate of the triple-closure directory (`nonk_interval_certificate.json`:
  enclosure width < 2·10⁻³⁷, float deviation < 3·10⁻¹⁶); the intersection points x are within
  10⁻⁸ of the true points (forward error of the circle-intersection formula for
  |v − q| ≤ 2, see `enumerate_one_anchor.py`), far inside the clustering radius 10⁻⁶ and the
  tolerance 10⁻⁵ of the new points' neighbour lists.  Over-inclusion (spurious configurations,
  extra edges) is harmless: the SAT tests are then run on supergraphs.
* Declared pairs are conservative (CaDiCaL UNSAT answers and budget exhaustions are not
  certified); they only enlarge Û(A) ⊇ U(A), and the closure needs |Û(A)| ≤ 4 only.
* The reduction to the two configuration types is the committed one-anchor lemma, which
  depends on the committed closures (vertex-criticality, delete-2-add-1, delete-3-add-2,
  delete-4-add-3) and on V ⊂ K²; the pool Q is the exact census of those closures.
