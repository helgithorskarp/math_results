# Sources and novelty boundary

## Discovery Net source

This result strengthens
`bafkreigxl23ibbfoco47z7gx2i2rcsk6rvz422pfhqdvcxnwnli7edvhim`,
*Global weighted ray-chamber formula for arbitrary normals*.  That result
gives a finite candidate arrangement and an exact formula but permits walls

minimal under an explicit generic condition, counts its walls, and gives the
first nonzero derivative jump at each one.

## Primary literature checked

- Z. Xu, *Multivariate Splines and Polytopes*, Journal of Approximation
  Theory 163 (2011), 377--387; arXiv:0806.1127,
  https://arxiv.org/abs/0806.1127.  This gives an explicit multivariate
  truncated-power framework for polytope volumes and cube slices.
- W. Dahmen, *On multivariate B-splines*, SIAM Journal on Numerical Analysis
  17 (1980), 179--191, https://doi.org/10.1137/0717017.  This constructs
  multivariate B-splines from truncated powers and relates smoothness to knot
  configuration.
- C. de Boor and K. Hoellig, *B-splines from parallelepipeds*, Journal
  d'Analyse Mathematique 42 (1982/83), 99--115,
  https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02786872.  This is a foundational source for box
  splines as projected polyhedral volumes.

These sources supply the general spline setting, but the bounded search found
no statement of the specialized \(q\,2^{q-1}\) wall count, the product jump
formula, or the dependence of exact smoothness on the number of active normal
coordinates for cube--crosspolytope Minkowski sections.  This is a
search-relative novelty assessment, not a historical-priority claim.

## Trust boundary

The proof uses the previously established global subset formula, simple-pole
residues, and exact beta-integral tail coefficients.  The checker uses only
CPython and `fractions.Fraction`.  Its 27-halfspace polygon engine is an
independent finite-dimensional corroboration, not a premise of the proof.
No floating-point computation, solver, external dataset, or hidden
certificate is used.
