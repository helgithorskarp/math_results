# Primary sources and claim boundary

Checked 2026-09-22. The problem was selected from Discovery Net's
illumination neighborhood, before the literature search. Existing
ordinary/fractional illumination results suggested asking which
polytopes multiply illumination numbers with every partner.

1. E. D. Baladze and V. G. Boltyanski, *Illumination of direct sums of two
   convex figures*, Beiträge zur Algebra und Geometrie **47** (2006),
   345--350. [Primary full paper, official EMIS mirror](https://emis.dsd.sztaki.hu/journals/BAG/vol.47/no.2/b47h2bal.pdf).
   The full paper was read. Theorem 3 gives multiplicativity when the
   factors, except possibly the last, have as many pairwise antipodal
   boundary points as their illumination numbers. This supplies a
   classical sufficient condition recovered by our criterion for
   polytopes. Lemma 3 already gives the regular-pentagon square value
   eight using a five-cycle incidence cover. Our rational example
   reproduces that mechanism. No priority is claimed for that value,
   for product failure, or for the associated fiber-count argument.

2. M. Naszódi, *Fractional illumination of convex bodies*, Contributions
   to Discrete Mathematics **4**(2) (2009), 83--88.
   [Publisher page](https://cdm.ucalgary.ca/article/view/62022).
   This introduces fractional illumination and its covering
   interpretation. The publisher record and mathematical abstract were
   checked; the direct PDF endpoint was intermittently unavailable in
   this pass. We use only the standard definition and prove the finite
   incidence reduction and all product identities in the present note.

3. L. Lovász, *On the ratio of optimal integral and fractional covers*,
   Discrete Mathematics **13**(4) (1975), 383--390.
   DOI 10.1016/0012-365X(75)90058-8. The primary publisher record and
   abstract were read through live web search; direct programmatic
   access returned HTTP 403, so no programmatically unverified URL is
   included here.
   This is classical provenance for fractional-to-integer greedy cover
   bounds. Our proof supplies the elementary decay estimate directly.
   Neither the rounding method nor the abstract finite-family power
   argument is presented as new. Standard finite LP duality is the
   other imported classical fact; no infinite LP duality is required.

4. L. Rotem, A. Schejter and B. A. Slomka, *The complex Illumination
   problem*, Combinatorica **46** (2026), article 3.
   [Publisher full text](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00493-025-00195-7).
   Sections 1.2 and 2 discuss fractional illumination and products of
   discs. These are current primary context for the graph's root.
   Their exact polydisc theorem and correction to an earlier cyclic
   construction are not new claims of this note. The classical
   Hadwiger conjecture remains outside the present result.

5. E. R. Scheinerman and D. H. Ullman, *Fractional Graph Theory: A
   Rational Approach to the Theory of Graphs*, Wiley, 1997;
   authors' freely distributed 2008 text, Section 1.6, printed pp. 7--11
   (PDF pages 18--22, counting the cover as page 1).
   [Full text](https://scispace.com/pdf/fractional-graph-theory-a-rational-approach-to-the-theory-of-157bznenje.pdf).
   Definitions and proofs were checked during the 2026-09-22 attribution
   update. The ground set is our vertex set and hyperedges are illuminated
   subsets: their `k(H), k_f(H)` are our integer and fractional cover
   numbers, with no hypergraph dualization. Their rectangle product is
   exactly (8). Theorem 1.6.1 matches (4); Theorem 1.6.2 matches the
   abstract power limit in (1). Lemma 1.6.3 is the finite-hypergraph
   antecedent of (3). The abstract tensor and power-limit mechanism is
   therefore prior art, as is the mixed finite-cover argument. Our
   arbitrary-convex-body fiber proof is its geometric application.

Additional reading during selection included V. Boltyanski and
H. Martini, *Illumination of direct vector sums of convex bodies*,
Studia Scientiarum Mathematicarum Hungarica **44**(3) (2007), 367--376,
DOI 10.1556/SScMath.44.2007.3.6. Its direct publisher endpoint returned
an access error, so no claim of a full-text audit is made. The 2006
primary paper above states the relevant predecessor results and cites
the then-submitted manuscript. We also read the product section of
Wu--Fan--He (2020), *Covering functionals of Minkowski sums and direct
sums of convex bodies*, DOI 10.7153/mia-2020-23-88. No quantitative
homothety-ratio statement from it is a premise or a target here.

The bounded search covered the exact product-paper titles, fractional
illumination with product/multiplicativity/Cartesian-power terms, the
primary papers above, and the relevant committed graph neighborhood.
It did not locate the explicit all-partner iff criterion for polytopes.
This is a search-relative observation, not an exhaustive priority claim.
The unavailable 2007 full text is a material bibliographic limitation.
Its published abstract mentions an equality condition. It cannot establish
whether the precise all-partner characterization here was already proved;
that priority question remains unresolved until the full paper is compared.

The scoped result is: a polytope multiplies ordinary illumination with
every convex-body partner exactly when its finite fractional covering
LP has no gap, with a mixed illumination bound for arbitrary convex-body
partners and a finite strict-power bound otherwise. These geometric
statements and the finite certificate are the only potentially new content;
no abstract finite-hypergraph tensor or power-limit theorem is claimed. No
classification of all such polytopes, nonpolytopal power-limit theorem,
new general Hadwiger bound, or new pentagon value is asserted.
