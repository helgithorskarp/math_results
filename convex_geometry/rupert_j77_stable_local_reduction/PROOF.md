# J77: small-angle passage limits lie on 25 axes

Author: **six-rupert-2**, role **researcher**, 2026-09-30.

Let `K` be the standard paragyrate diminished rhombicosidodecahedron,
Johnson solid **J77**, in the exact vertex model of the
[preceding construction](../rupert_j77_fixed_outer_local/model.py).
Let `P_n` denote orthogonal projection perpendicular to a unit normal `n`.
Put `s=sqrt(5)`, `phi=(1+s)/2`, `A=(0,phi,1)`, and let `G` be the
verified ten-element `C5v` vertex symmetry group generated by the 72-degree
rotation about `A` and the reflection `(x,y,z) -> (-x,y,z)`.

Define a set of **unoriented** axes

```
E = G.[(0,1,0)]
  union G.[(5-3s, 5-s, -2s)]
  union G.[(9-7s, -5+13s, 22-8s)]
  union G.[(-16+6s, 25-7s, 23-11s)].
```

The four projective orbits are disjoint and have respectively
**5, 5, 5, 10 axes**, so `|E|=25`. Let `Ehat` contain both unit-vector
representatives of each axis.

**Theorem.** For every unit normal `n0` outside `Ehat`, there exist
`delta(n0)>0` and `theta0(n0)>0` such that, for every unit `n` with
`||n-n0||<delta(n0)`, every proper rotation `Q` with
`0<angle(Q)<=theta0(n0)`, every planar translation `t`, and every `lambda>=1`,

```
lambda P_n(QK)+t is not contained in P_n(K).
```

Both projection directions can vary: the inner direction in the body's
coordinates is `Q^{-1}n`. On every compact subset of the sphere avoiding
`Ehat`, a single positive angle bound suffices. Consequently every
accumulation axis of receiver or inner normals in a sequence of strict
passages whose full relative rotation angles tend to zero belongs to `E`.

This is a reduction to possible limits. Membership in `E` does not assert
the existence of passages. No global uniform angle, reverse-local theorem
at the exceptional axes, or global non-Rupert theorem is proved. The
[previous theorem](../rupert_j77_fixed_outer_local/PROOF.md) excludes small
rotations at every **fixed** outer direction, including these axes.

## 1. Translation and scale

The independently regenerated J77 model has 55 vertices, 50 in antipodal
pairs and five unpaired cap vertices. Three independent antipodal pairs
put `0` in `int(K)`. Central symmetry of the whole body is unavailable.

Let `R=max_{v in K}||v||`; here `R^2=(11+4s)/4`. If
`P_n(QK)+t subseteq P_n(K)` and `theta=angle(Q)`, then for every unit
`m perpendicular to n`, the support functions satisfy

```
m.t <= h_K(m)-h_{QK}(m) <= R ||Q-I|| <= R theta.
```

Taking `m=t/||t||` when `t!=0` gives **`||t||<=R theta`**. This bound
uses only convex containment and a rotation; it does not use symmetry.

Scale reduces to one: if `lambda>=1` and
`lambda P_n(QK)+t subseteq P_n(K)`, then
`P_n(QK)+t/lambda subseteq (1/lambda)P_n(K) subseteq P_n(K)`.
Thus it suffices to exclude unit-scale containment for nonzero rotations.

## 2. Persistent contacts give stable local exclusions

The proof works separately in each closed projective parent triangle.
Its normal chart has `u_y>0`. A stored contact `(a,b,j)` defines

```
m(u)=(V_b-V_a) cross u,    g(u)=V_j cross m(u).
```

The checker verifies at all three corners that `m(u)` supports `K` at
`V_a,V_b,V_j`, with positive offset whenever the normal is nonzero.
All support inequalities are affine linear in `u`, so they hold throughout
the triangle and its positive cone. In particular these contacts persist
for receiver normals approaching a boundary from the parent interior.
At a direction where the relevant strict criterion holds, its probes
are nonzero and bounded in a sufficiently small neighborhood.

Write `Q=exp(theta [a]_cross)`, `||a||=1`. The rotation remainder obeys

```
||Q-I-theta[a]_cross|| <= theta^2/2.
```

Three exact criteria are used.

**Paired contacts.** If `V_j,-V_j` are both vertices and the opposite
normals `m,-m` support the entire body with equal offsets, summing their
two containment inequalities gives
`m.(QV_j-V_j)<=0`, cancelling `t`. A positive tetrahedron of four
torques contains a centered ball of some radius `r>0`. Therefore some
contact has linear term at least `theta r`. Probe norms remain bounded,
and the quadratic rotation error cannot cancel it for sufficiently small
positive `theta`. Strict positive cofactors persist by continuity near
the direction, within the incident parent.

**Full contact simplices.** Put
`t_eff=(t_x-u_x t_y/u_y, t_z-u_z t_y/u_y)`. Since `m.u=0`,
`m.t=m_x t_eff,x+m_z t_eff,z`. Six columns

```
f(u)=(g_x,g_y,g_z,m_x,m_z)
```

with strictly positive signed cofactors form a simplex containing a
centered five-dimensional ball of radius `r>0`. For any rotation axis
and any translation, some column pairs with `(a,t_eff/theta)` by at
least `r`, since `||(a,t_eff/theta)||>=1`. Its containment inequality
again has a positive linear term and bounded quadratic error. Continuity
gives a receiver neighborhood within the parent.

**Normal-balanced point certificates.** At a boundary point `u0`, for
each signed coordinate vector `s e_k`, the checker reconstructs positive
weights satisfying the full six-coordinate identity

```
sum_i w_i (g_i(u0),m_i(u0)) = (s e_k,0,0,0).
```

Here probes are first normalized to support offset one. For nearby unit
normals within the parent, continue each probe as a fixed positive
multiple of `(V_b-V_a) cross n`; normalize this continuation to agree
with the point probe at `n0`. The corresponding weighted torque differs
from `s e_k` by `O(||n-n0||)`, while the weighted normal is
`O(||n-n0||)`. These constants depend on the finite point certificate.

For a unit rotation axis choose `k,s` with `s a_k>=1/sqrt(3)`.
Multiply and sum the corresponding containment inequalities. Section 1
bounds the translation error, giving

```
0 >= theta/sqrt(3) - C ||n-n0|| theta - D theta^2
```

for finite constants `C,D`. Choose a sufficiently small receiver
neighborhood, then a sufficiently small positive angle. The right side
is positive, a contradiction. This proves stable local exclusion for
these certificates despite J77's unpaired cap. Normal balance is exact
at `n0`; it is not silently assumed to persist at nearby normals.

## 3. Exact coverage and the four remaining rays

The verifier uses the hash-pinned, public
[previous exact kernel and certificate](../rupert_j77_fixed_outer_local/)
from source commit `11645dc3c95a20c41d08e5a8d8c28215b6838d88`.
It regenerates the cupola model, symmetries, and chamber partition and
replays all selected two-dimensional simplices. No solver, floating
hull, sampled direction, or private file enters verification.

The projective chamber is `pos(A,r0,r1)`, where
`r0=(0,1,-phi)` and `r1` is its 36-degree rotation about `A`.
A positive linear slice makes it a closed triangle with `u_y>0`.
The exact partition has 31 polygons, 44 parent triangles and 191
midpoint-refined leaf triangles. The previous simplex coefficients are
nonnegative and their cofactors are strictly positive on every leaf
interior. Corners and open edges are checked separately: positivity on
one incident leaf is not presumed to control approach through another.

The exact audit finds **41 bad corner-parent incidences on 21 rays** and
**four bad open-edge-parent incidences**. Here "bad" means loss of strict
cofactors in every selected simplex of that leaf; it does not mean that
a passage exists. The replacement fixture provides:

* Four segment simplices, three paired and one full. Their binary
  cubic/quintic cofactors have nonnegative coefficients and are strictly
  positive throughout each open segment. Every selected contact is
  additionally checked on the **entire incident parent triangle**.
* **21 point-parent certificates**, with **126 signed rotational
  normal equilibria**, covering all bad corner-parent incidences except
  the four stated rays. Their contacts also support throughout the
  respective parent triangles.

The checker demands exact equality of required and supplied edge and
point incidence sets. It verifies that the remaining four rays have
exactly the primitive projective representatives printed above. Thus,
at every other chamber point, every incident closed leaf has a strict
criterion that remains valid near that point within its parent.

For a fixed nonexceptional unit normal, consider the finitely many
symmetry images of leaf triangles containing it. Section 2 gives a
positive local angle and a relative receiver neighborhood in each.
The other closed triangles have positive distance from this normal.
Take the minimum of the finitely many neighborhoods and angle bounds.
This gives a full receiver cap, using the relevant certificate separately
on each incident region. It proves the theorem without assuming one
probe family stays supporting across a boundary.

The group symmetries preserve projections and translations; conjugation
preserves a rotation's angle and properness, even for reflections.
Changing `n` to `-n` leaves its projection unchanged. The checker
enumerates each projective orbit to closure and computes their disjoint
union of 25 axes. Hence precisely the stated candidate axis union
remains from this cover.

## 4. Compactness and passage limits

The receiver caps above cover any compact set avoiding `Ehat`. A finite
subcover and the minimum of its positive angle bounds give the compact-set
conclusion. If receiver normals in a passage sequence accumulate outside
`Ehat`, the cap there excludes the sequence once the angles tend to zero.
Thus receiver accumulation axes are in `E`. Finally
`||Q^{-1}n-n||<=2 sin(theta/2)` tends to zero, so the inner accumulation
axes belong to the same set.
