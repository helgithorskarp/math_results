# J77: near-identity passage limits lie on five axes

Author: **six-rupert-2**, role **researcher**, 2026-09-30.

Let `K` be the standard paragyrate diminished rhombicosidodecahedron,
Johnson solid J77, in the verified 55-vertex model. Put `s=sqrt(5)`,
`phi=(1+s)/2`, `A=(0,phi,1)`. Let `G` be its verified `C5v` symmetry group,
generated by rotation through 72 degrees about `A` and reflection in `x=0`.
An axis denotes a projective, unoriented direction. Define

```
E = G.[(5-3s, 5-s, -2s)].
```

This orbit has **five axes**. Write `Ehat` for its ten unit representatives.

**Theorem.** For every unit receiver normal `n0` outside `Ehat`, there are
`delta(n0)>0` and `theta0(n0)>0` such that, for all unit normals `n` with
`||n-n0||<delta`, proper rotations `Q` with `0<angle(Q)<=theta0`, planar
translations `t`, and scales `lambda>=1`,

```
lambda P_n(QK)+t is not strictly inside P_n(K).
```

Here `P_n` is orthogonal projection perpendicular to `n`. Both projection
directions vary: the inner direction in body coordinates is `Q^{-1}n`.
A positive angle bound works uniformly on any compact set avoiding `Ehat`.
Thus every accumulation axis of receiver or inner normals in a strict
passage sequence whose full relative angles tend to zero lies in `E`.

The five axes are a candidate set, not asserted passage-admitting or sharp.
No uniform angle on the entire sphere or global non-Rupert theorem follows.
The [fixed-outer theorem](../rupert_j77_fixed_outer_local/PROOF.md) still
excludes small rotations when any one of these receivers is held fixed.

An explicit additional result is that receiver normals within chord distance
`1/200` of the five-axis orbit of `(0,1,0)` admit no strict passage with
relative angle at most `1/200` radians, for any translation and `lambda>=1`.
The two other newly excluded orbits have existential receiver/angle caps;
this proof does not print quantitative radii for them.

## 2. A critical-direction exclusion criterion

The following argument applies to convex vertex sets without central
symmetry. Consider a closed projective triangle with corner `u*` and two
outgoing directions `d0,d1`. Write nearby receiver rays in this triangle as

```
u=u*+x d0+y d1,   x,y>=0.
```

The chart has `u_y>0`. Persistent contacts select vertices `v_i` and probes
`m_i(u)`, linear in `u`, perpendicular to `u`, and supporting the full body
at `v_i` throughout the triangle. Define `g_i(u)=v_i cross m_i(u)`.
Our probes are divided by their **fixed critical offsets**. Their support
offsets are one at `u*`; they need not be one at nearby directions.

Assume a nonzero vector `a` with `a.u*=0` satisfies all contact inequalities
`a.g_i(u*)<=0`. Let `I` index the contacts where equality holds. Suppose:

1. Positive weights `w_i`, `i in I`, have sum one and satisfy
   `sum w_i (g_i(u*),m_i(u*))=0` in all six coordinates.
2. The rows `(g_i,m_i,x,m_i,z)`, `i in I`, have rank four in five dimensions.
   Their kernel is therefore the line spanned by `(a,0,0)`.
3. At least one other contact has `a.g_i(u*)<0`.
4. Both displacement stresses are positive:
   `kappa_k=sum w_i a.(v_i cross m_i(dk))>0`, for `k=0,1`.

Finally suppose limiting hidden-vertex and antipodal radial-pair inequalities
give an inconsistent linear system for the two scaled receiver coordinates.
Then no sequence of nonidentity closed containments with receiver rays
tending to `u*` and relative rotations tending to identity can remain in
this triangle. The proof below establishes the required rates rather than
assuming a first-order perturbation ansatz.

### 2.1 The first-order cone locks the limiting motion

Reduce scale to one using `0 in int(K)`: a containment at `lambda>=1`
implies unit-scale containment after dividing its translation by `lambda`.
For unit-scale containment, support functions give

```
||t||<=R ||Q-I||<=R angle(Q),   R=max_v ||v||.
```

Indeed, apply the support inequality to the unit vector in the direction
of `t`. This bound is valid for an asymmetric body.

Suppose a sequence of such containments approaches the corner and identity.
Write its Cayley vector as `w=epsilon alpha`, where
`epsilon=||w||/||a||>0` and `||alpha||=||a||`. Then

```
Qv-v = [2 epsilon alpha cross v
        +2 epsilon^2 alpha cross (alpha cross v)]
        /[1+epsilon^2 ||a||^2].
```

The physical translation may be represented for every probe by

```
T=(t_x-u_x t_y/u_y, 0, t_z-u_z t_y/u_y),
b=T/(2 epsilon).
```

The chart stays bounded near the corner, and the support-function bound
makes `b` bounded. Each persistent contact gives

```
g_i(u).alpha +(1+epsilon^2 ||a||^2)m_i(u).b
  +epsilon[(alpha.v_i)(alpha.m_i(u))-||a||^2 m_i(u).v_i] <= 0.   (1)
```

Along any convergent subsequence of `(alpha,b_x,b_z)`, its limit satisfies
the critical five-dimensional inequalities. The positive active equilibrium
forces every active inequality to be equality. Rank four puts the limit on
the line `(c a,0,0)`. Its fixed rotation norm forces `|c|=1`, and the negative
contact forces `c=1`. All accumulation points agree, hence

```
alpha -> a,   b -> 0.                                      (2)
```

No quantitative rate for `alpha-a` or for `b` is required here.

### 2.2 The displacement stress proves the missing rate

Weight (1) by the positive active weights and sum. Both weighted vectors
vanish at `u*`; linearity makes their nearby values linear in `x,y`.
Using (2), the sum is

```
kappa_0 x+kappa_1 y +o(x+y)+O(epsilon) <= 0.
```

The last bound is uniform, since there are finitely many probes and the
rotation norm is fixed. Both kappas are positive. For sufficiently late
terms the little-o error is bounded by half their minimum times `x+y`.
It follows that **`x+y=O(epsilon)`**. Thus, along a subsequence,

```
u=u*+epsilon r,  r=r0 d0+r1 d1,
(r0,r1) has a finite limit, with r0,r1>=0.                   (3)
```

This is why the subsequent inequalities apply to arbitrary approaching
passage sequences, including ones whose displacement rate was initially
unknown.

### 2.3 Hidden vertices and radial pairs constrain that limit

A probe `m(u)` may support at `v` throughout the triangle while another
vertex `h` ties only at `u*`. Such a vertex still must fit into the receiver.
Normalize its critical offset to one. Divide
`m(u).(Qh-v+T)<=0` by `epsilon`. Equations (2)--(3) yield

```
m(r).(h-v) <= -2 a.(h cross m(u*)).                         (4)
```

For a pair `v,-v` in `K` with `u*.v=0`, suppose each is uniquely radially
exposed at the critical projection. The opposite probes

```
m_v(u)=v-u(u.v)/||u||^2,   -m_v(u)
```

continue to support the receiver at these two vertices in a neighborhood.
Summing their support inequalities cancels translation exactly. Insert the
Cayley formula and divide by `epsilon^2`. Since `alpha->a`, the limit is

```
-2(v.r)[u*.(a cross v)]/||u*||^2
   <= 2[||a||^2 ||v||^2-(a.v)^2].                          (5)
```

The correction to `alpha` does not need an `O(epsilon)` estimate: the
critical radial torque is zero, so only its limit appears in (5).

## 4. Antipodal projected classes remove translation locally

This extends the class mechanism of **six-rupert-3**'s
[paired/singleton proof](../../rhombicosidodecahedron_mirror_cluster_obstruction/PROOF.md),
source `cc52961fa42c1edd69390493b5cb3819770d36ac`, graph
`bafkreig3erec7rq3afffflefrepyeb2blvwiguqw2fxj6ntte6wc7g6bfq`.
Whole-body central symmetry is replaced by an antipodal condition on the
selected classes. This distinction is necessary for J77.

Work in the properly permuted, scaled coordinates
`W=2*(V_z,V_x,V_y)`, with `P(x,y,z)=(x,y)`. All vertices have squared norm
`R^2=11+4s<20`. For a projected point `p`, let `C_p` be its full preimage
class. Assume `C_-p=-C_p` and

```
p.(p-Pq)>=m>0 for every q outside C_p.                     (6)
```

For orthonormal-row frames `B1,B2` within operator distance `eta` of `P`,
the support comparison for `v in C_p`, `w in C_p`, `q outside C_p` differs
from (6) by at most `4R^2 eta+2R^2 eta^2`. If this is below `m`, the outer
support in direction `s=B1v` occurs in `B2 C_p`; the opposite direction's
support occurs in `B2 C_-p`. Both `v` and `-v` are inner vertices. Strict
translated containment gives

```
||s||^2+s.t < max_(w in C_p) s.B2w,
||s||^2-s.t < max_(w in C_p) s.B2w.
```

Adding cancels `t`. Cauchy--Schwarz then gives, for every selected class,

```
max_(v in C_p)||B1v|| < max_(v in C_p)||B2v||.              (7)
```

This uses the exact antipodal classes and their support gaps, with no
assumption that the other body vertices have antipodes.

### 4.1 The J77 classes and the scalar contradiction

The selected vertices in `W` coordinates, with signs independent, are

```
(+/-a,+/-1,+/-1), (+/-1,+/-a,+/-1), (+/-b,+/-c,0),
a=2+s, b=(3+s)/2, c=(5+s)/2.
```

They form eight doubleton classes and four singleton classes, all fully
antipodal. The exact checker verifies 640 outside-class inequalities (6);
their minimum is **one**, including all unpaired cap vertices. Their common
radius satisfies `a^2+2=b^2+c^2=R^2`.

Take `eta=1/100`. The support error is below `201/250<1`. Let the positively
oriented unit normals of `Bi` be `(u_i,z_i)`, where `u_i=(x_i,y_i)` and
`||u_i||<=eta`, `z_i=sqrt(1-||u_i||^2)>1/2`.
For a doubleton class, its smallest axial absolute value is
`z_i-|p.u_i|>0`. Inequality (7) therefore says

```
|p.u_1|-|p.u_2| < r=z_1-z_2
```

for `p=(a,+/-1),(1,+/-a)`. The two singleton pairs give
`|(b,+/-c).u_1|>|(b,+/-c).u_2|`. Put
`X=x_1^2-x_2^2`, `Y=y_1^2-y_2^2`. The singleton inequalities imply

```
D=b^2 X+c^2 Y>0.                                         (8)
```

If `r<=0`, summing the squared doubleton inequalities gives
`a^2 X+Y<0` and `X+a^2 Y<0`. Their positive combination

```
D=[(a^2b^2-c^2)/(a^4-1)](a^2X+Y)
 +[(a^2c^2-b^2)/(a^4-1)](X+a^2Y)
```

is negative, contradicting (8).

If `r>0`, let `H=2 sqrt(a^2+1) eta`. Squaring the `(1,+/-a)` inequalities
and summing gives `X+a^2Y<rH`. The exact normal identity is
`X+Y=-r(z_1+z_2)`. Consequently

```
D < r/(a^2-1) * [-(a^2b^2-c^2)(z_1+z_2)+(c^2-b^2)H] < 0.
```

For the last sign the checker proves
`a^2b^2-c^2>48`, `c^2-b^2<16`, `sqrt(a^2+1)<5`, and
`sqrt(1-eta^2)>1/2`. The negative term exceeds 48 in magnitude and the
positive term is below `8/5`. This again contradicts (8).

Thus no strict translated containment exists for the two frame
neighborhoods. Closed equality is not excluded by this class argument;
the main theorem concerns strict Rupert passages.

### 4.2 Explicit receiver and relative-angle caps

For a unit receiver normal within chord `1/200` of `e_z` in `W` coordinates,
choose the shortest proper rotation taking `e_z` to it. Its operator distance
from identity equals that chord. The corresponding projection frame `B`
therefore has `||B-P||<=1/200`. If `angle(Q)<=1/200`, then
`||BQ-P||<=1/200+||Q-I||<=1/100`. Section 4 applies to `B,BQ`.
Transforming back to `V` gives the stated cap at the original y-axis;
body symmetries transport it to five axes. Scale reduces to one as above.
