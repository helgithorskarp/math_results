# J77 has a uniform positive gap in the full relative rotation angle

Author: **six-rupert-2**, role **researcher**, 2026-09-30.

Let K be the standard paragyrate diminished rhombicosidodecahedron,
Johnson solid J77, in the independently reconstructed 55-vertex model.
For a nonzero vector u, let P_u denote orthogonal projection onto
u's perpendicular plane. For a proper rotation Q, angle(Q) is its
full angle in [0,pi], rather than only an angle between projection axes.

**Theorem.** There exists theta_*>0 such that, for every unit receiver
normal n, every proper rotation Q with angle(Q)<=theta_*, every
planar translation t, and every scale lambda>=1,

    lambda P_n(QK)+t is not strictly contained in P_n(K).

Both projections may vary: the inner projection normal in body coordinates
is Q^{-1}n. The theorem supplies an **existential uniform positive angle**;
it does not print a numerical angle and does not resolve J77's global
Rupert property. Exact closed equal-shadow families occur arbitrarily close
to identity. Consequently replacing strict containment by closed containment
would make the theorem false.

This is an unformalized analytic proof with exact finite hypotheses replayed
by standard-library Python over Q(sqrt(5)). The finite verification and the
analytic argument below are separate parts of the evidence. No independent
review or proof-assistant verification is claimed.

## 1. The remaining directions and their body reflection

    E0=(5-3s,5-s,-2s),  s=sqrt(5).

Here C5v is the verified body symmetry group generated by a rotation
through 72 degrees about (0,(1+s)/2,1) and a reflection in x=0.
The present checker replays that entire source, including its pinned
25-axis reduction and fixed-receiver kernel. It remains to exclude strict
passage sequences tending jointly to identity and one of these five axes.

Work at the affine representative

    e=E0/20=((5-3s)/20,(5-s)/20,-s/10),  N=e.e,
    H=(-s/2,(7+3s)/4,-(1+3s)/4),  H.e=1.

In the symmetry chamber's normalized triangle, e has barycentric
coordinates (0,1/2,1/2); it is in the relative interior of its equatorial
edge. The plane reflection

    M_e=I-2 e e^T/N

permutes all 55 vertices and fixes exactly vertices **9,14,54** in the
published indexing. These three lie in the mirror plane. Each is uniquely
exposed by its own radial probe at this projection; all **162** strict
vertex comparisons are checked.

The exact partition has 44 parent triangles. The checker solves the
barycentric system for every parent, including boundary incidences. Exactly
parents **21,23,28,30,31,32,33** contain e, and e is a corner of all seven.
No incident region is omitted. A nearby receiver can be folded to this half
chamber: the projective action -M_e fixes e, changes the sign of the
component along the fivefold axis, and preserves the equatorial edge.
At the normalized chamber generators, -M_e negates the axial generator
and swaps the other two; these identities follow directly from its formula.
Their two coordinates are positive near e. Renormalizing the reflected ray
to H.u=1 preserves these signs. Conjugating by a
body reflection preserves properness and the full angle of a rotation;
changing the receiver representative's sign does not change its projection.
Thus these seven incidences cover a full neighborhood of this axis.

For comparison, the proper rotation

    Q0=M_u M_e

always satisfies P_u(Q0 K)=P_u(K), since M_e K=K and P_u M_u=P_u.
Near e its Cayley vector is (e cross u)/(e.u). This generic reflection
identity is an equality family, not a strict passage construction.

## 2. Exact contact facts for each of the seven regions

Let a parent have corner e and outgoing vectors d0,d1. Write its nearby
receiver rays in the chart H.u=1 as

    u=e+x d0+y d1,  x,y>=0.

Each fixture selects **32 noncollapsed persistent contacts** (Va,Vb,v_i).
Set

    h_i=((Vb-Va) cross e).v_i > 0,
    m_i(u)=((Vb-Va) cross u)/h_i,
    g_i(u)=v_i cross m_i(u).

The denominator is the fixed critical support offset. Therefore m_i is
linear in u, m_i(e).v_i=1, and the other offsets need not equal one.
The checker verifies supporting-edge incidence and every vertex inequality
at every parent corner. Linearity extends support to the entire closed
parent, without assuming persistence across different parents.

Use the five-dimensional translation chart with translation's y
coordinate zero, and define rows

    G_i=(g_i,x,g_i,y,g_i,z,m_i,x,m_i,z) at u=e.

There are exactly six common rows for which g_i(e) is parallel to e.
For each parent the finite certificate proves:

1. Six strictly positive weights w_i, of sum one, satisfy
   sum w_i (g_i(e),m_i(e))=0 in **all six coordinates**.
2. The six common five-dimensional rows have rank three. Their kernel is
   the two-dimensional space L={(a,0,0): a.e=0}. A nonzero minor and
   two independent kernel vectors are checked, so both the lower and
   upper rank bounds are justified.
3. Two independent tangent vectors a0,a1 satisfy every one of the 32
   necessary inequalities. Two oriented facet rows force their coefficients
   to be nonnegative. Hence the complete necessary first-order cone is
   pos{(a0,0,0),(a1,0,0)}.
4. All four drift corners are strictly positive:

       kappa_jk=sum_i w_i a_j.(v_i cross m_i(dk)) > 0,
           j,k in {0,1}, with the sum over the six common rows.    (1)

There are **42** positive weights and **28** strictly positive drift
corner checks in total. The weights and these inequalities concern the
actual asymmetric J77 body; no central-symmetry premise is used.

## 3. Receiver and motion rates for arbitrary closed containment sequences

The origin lies in the interior of K, as checked using three independent
antipodal core pairs. A strict containment at scale lambda>=1 implies a
strict containment at scale one, with translation divided by lambda:
shrink the entire containment about the origin. Thus it suffices to study
unit-scale containments. Necessary estimates below use closed containment.

For such a containment, support functions in the direction of its planar
translation give

    ||t|| <= R ||Q-I|| <= R angle(Q),  R=max_v ||v||.              (2)

Indeed m.t<=h_K(m)-h_K(Q^T m)<=R||Q-I|| for the unit planar vector m
pointing along t. This estimate requires no central symmetry.

Suppose nonidentity closed containments approach u=e and Q=I while
remaining in one parent. Write the Cayley vector

    w=eta alpha,  eta=||w||>0,  ||alpha||=1.

The exact Cayley formula is

    Qv-v = [2 eta alpha cross v
           +2 eta^2 alpha cross (alpha cross v)]/(1+eta^2).       (3)

For every perpendicular probe, replace the physical planar translation by

    T=(t_x-u_x t_y/u_y,0,t_z-u_z t_y/u_y),  b=T/(2 eta).          (4)

All parent corners have u_y>0; this chart is bounded near e. Moreover
t=P_u T, since t is planar. Equation (2) makes b bounded. Each
persistent contact gives the exact necessary inequality

    g_i(u).alpha +(1+eta^2)m_i(u).b
      +eta[(alpha.v_i)(alpha.m_i(u))-m_i(u).v_i] <= 0.            (5)

Any subsequential limit of (alpha,b_x,b_z) lies in the critical cone.
The positive common equilibrium first makes all six common inequalities
equalities; the rank and facet checks then give

    alpha_* in pos{a0,a1},  ||alpha_*||=1,  b_* = 0.             (6)

Weight (5) by the common weights. Both weighted vectors vanish at e.
Linearity, boundedness and (6) give, along that subsequence,

    kappa_0(alpha_*) x+kappa_1(alpha_*) y
                +o(x+y)+O(eta) <= 0.

Each kappa_k is linear in alpha_*. By (1) it is strictly positive on
the compact set of unit vectors in the two-ray cone. It follows that
x+y=O(eta). In particular there are a receiver cap, an angle cap and a
constant C such that every **nonidentity closed** containment in those
caps has unit receiver distance at most C angle(Q) from the critical
axis. To see uniformity, negate this assertion and select a sequence with
receiver and angle tending to the corner and zero, but distance/angle
unbounded. A subsequence stays in one of the seven parents and has the
limits (6); the displayed stress estimate contradicts its unbounded ratio.
The affine and unit/projective distances are comparable in this chart.
Identity closed containments are excluded from this rate assertion.

Once u-e=O(eta), (5) bounds each common critical row G_i.(alpha,b)
above by O(eta). Their positive equilibrium bounds each below by
-O(eta) as well. Rank three then bounds distance to their common kernel:

    alpha.e=O(eta),  b=O(eta),
    w.e=O(eta^2),   T=O(eta^2).                                 (7)

These conclusions prove the needed rates for arbitrary approaching
sequences; no differentiability or assumed perturbation ansatz is used.

## 4. A necessary polynomial system for the limiting tangent direction

Represent the actual tangent component of w in the basis a0,a1.
The sum of its two coefficients is positive and comparable to eta by
(6). Call that sum epsilon. For a bounded parameter tau write exactly

    a(tau)=tau a0+(1-tau)a1,
    w=epsilon a(tau)+epsilon^2 z e,
    u=e+epsilon r,  r=r0 d0+r1 d1,
    T=2epsilon^2(c_x,0,c_z).

By (7), z,r0,r1,c_x,c_z are bounded and r0,r1>=0. The actual tau may
be slightly outside [0,1]; all of its accumulation points belong to that
closed interval by (6). Extract simultaneous limits and use tau for the
limiting parameter. The following inequalities are necessary for those
limits. They remain valid at both endpoints.

For each persistent edge, include **every** vertex h tying its critical
support, with outer edge endpoint v. Its containment inequality, divided
by epsilon and passed to the limit, gives

    m(r).(h-v) <= -2 a.(h cross m(e)).                           (8)

Translation vanishes at this order. The code normalizes the probe exactly
as in section 2.

For each of the six common persistent contacts, divide by 2epsilon^2.
The term g_i(e).a(tau) is identically zero for the actual tangent motion,
not merely at its limit. Formula (3) gives

    a.(v_i cross m_i(r))+z g_i(e).e+m_i(e).c
             <= ||a||^2-(a.v_i)(a.m_i(e)).                      (9)

For each uniquely exposed fixed mirror vertex v in {9,14,54}, its
receiver support probe is

    m_v(u)=v-u(u.v)/||u||^2.

It continues supporting at v in a neighborhood by the strict radial
comparisons. Its first-order rotation term vanishes. Dividing the
containment inequality by 2epsilon^2 gives

    -(v.r)[e.(a cross v)]/N+v.c
             <= ||a||^2 ||v||^2-(a.v)^2.                       (10)

Finally include r0,r1>=0. These yield **102** necessary inequalities per
parent, linear in

    X=(r0,r1,z,c_x,c_z),   A(tau) X <= B(tau).                  (11)

Every entry of A has degree at most one and every entry of B has degree
at most two over Q(sqrt(5)). Only the first two columns of A depend on
tau. The public polynomial kernel regenerates all rows directly from
vertices and contacts; the certificate does not supply trusted matrices.

## 5. Exact coordinate bounds on the entire closed parameter interval

For tangent a, let r_m be the unique vector with

    e cross r_m/N=a,   H.r_m=0.

Equivalently r_m=a cross e+q e, with q chosen to make H.r_m=0.
Its two coordinates in d0,d1 are affine polynomials in tau. Define
X_m=(r_m0,r_m1,0,0,0). The kernel finds exactly **41** rows satisfying

    A_i(tau) X_m(tau)=B_i(tau)

as full polynomial identities. No assertion that X_m satisfies every
other row is needed.

For each of the seven parents, four exact nonnegative coordinate duals
cover the closed interval [0,1]: coordinates k=0,1 with signs +1,-1.
Each of these **28** certificates uses one interval piece and at most five
of the universally tight rows. Cramer polynomials produce weights
mu_i=num_i/den with identities

    sum mu_i A_i = sign unit_k,
    sum mu_i B_i = sign X_m,k.                                  (12)

The checker proves den>0 and num_i>=0 on the full interval by exact
Bernstein coefficients. It independently reconstructs each Bernstein
polynomial in the power basis, and audits every coefficient's algebraic
sign by rational arithmetic. The complete five-coordinate identity is
checked, including coordinates outside the determinant minor. Thus the
rank minor is a method to construct the weights, rather than a restricted
system substituted for (11). Exact Gaussian determinant evaluations at
0,1/2,1 are supplementary independent checks; interval validity comes
from polynomial identities and **152** Bernstein sign coefficients, not
sampling. The kernel supports common-factor cancellation, but none is
needed by these 28 chosen bases.

Apply nonnegative weights to (11). The two signs in (12) give both upper
and lower bounds for each receiver coordinate. Consequently every feasible
limiting system satisfies

    r=r_m,  e cross r/N=a.                                     (13)

Only the receiver coordinates are needed here; uniqueness of z or c
is not claimed. If r_m lies outside the parent receiver cone, that
parameter has no feasible limiting system.

## 6. Reflection bootstrap excludes strict passages at the mirror axis

Assume a sequence of strict passages tends to receiver axis e and
identity rotation. Reduce scale to one and fold to one of the seven
parents. Apply sections 3--5 to a subsequence. Its nonzero limiting tangent
vector a satisfies (13). For each actual term define another proper
rotation

    Qtilde=M_u Q M_e.

This presents **exactly the same projected inner body**:

    P_u(Qtilde K)=P_u(M_u Q M_e K)=P_u(QK).                      (14)

The translation is unchanged, so it is the same strict containment.
Because a is tangent to e, expansion of the two plane reflections and
(3) gives

    Qtilde=I+2epsilon [e cross r/N-a]_cross+o(epsilon),

where [v]_cross denotes the linear map x -> v cross x. Equation (13)
therefore implies angle(Qtilde)=o(epsilon).

For a strict containment Qtilde cannot be identity: a translated equal
shadow, even at scale one, cannot be strictly contained in itself. For
example, strict containment of compact planar convex bodies with positive
area implies strictly smaller area, whereas a translated identical shadow
has equal area. This also disposes of Q=I in the theorem.

Now apply the **already proved closed-containment rate bound** of section 3
to Qtilde and the same receiver. It gives receiver distance o(epsilon)
from the critical axis. In contrast, (13) says

    P_e r = a cross e != 0,

so the actual unit receiver distance from that axis is a nonzero constant
times epsilon, plus o(epsilon). This is a contradiction.

The sequential contradiction supplies a receiver cap and a positive angle
cap excluding strict containments at e. Transport by the body symmetry
gives caps at all five projective axes. Combine them with the stable caps
at all other receivers from the preceding five-axis theorem. Compactness
of the unit sphere gives a finite subcover. The minimum of its positive
angle caps is the required **uniform** theta_*. No numerical lower bound
for that minimum is claimed.

**Local closed-containment classification at the mirror axes.** There also
exist receiver and angle caps at each of these five axes such that every
closed containment at scale lambda>=1 has lambda=1, t=0, and either
Q=I or Q=M_u M_e (with the corresponding transported body mirror).
Indeed, if a sequence of nonidentity closed containments tending to this
axis and identity had Q different from M_u M_e at every term, its
alternative Qtilde would be nonidentity. The rate bound, limiting
coordinate relation and reflection contradiction above would apply
without a strictness assumption. Thus a sufficiently small neighborhood
contains no such sequence. For Q=I or Q=M_u M_e the two shadows coincide;
area forces lambda=1, and support functions in the direction of a
nonzero translation rule out t!=0. This classification is local to the
five mirror-axis caps; a global classification of closed equality is
not asserted.
