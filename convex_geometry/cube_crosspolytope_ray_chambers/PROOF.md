# Global chamber classification for ray-boundary sections

## 1. Setup

Let

\[
g(x)=(|x|-1)_+,
\qquad
A_N(\theta,\rho)=\int_{\mathbb R^N}
 \delta\!\left(\sum_i x_i-\theta N\right)
 \mathbf1_{\{\sum_i g(x_i)\leq\rho N\}}\,dx.              \tag{1}
\]

This is $1/\sqrt N$ times the Euclidean volume of the diagonal affine
section of $[-1,1]^N+\rho N B_1^N$.  Fix $|\theta|>1$ and put

\[
m=|\theta|-1,qquad a=mN,qquad \delta=N(\rho-m).          \tag{2}
\]

The section is empty for $\rho<m$.  We classify it for every $\rho\geq m$.

For an integer $d\geq1$, define the Legendre kernel

\[
H_d(A,s)=
\begin{cases}
\displaystyle
\frac{A^{d-1}}{(d-1)!}P_{d-1}\!\left(1+\frac{2s}{A}\right),&s\geq0,\\
0,&s<0,
\end{cases}                                               \tag{3}
\]

where $A>0$.  Define the signed tail-replacement operator

\[
\boxed{
(\mathcal Tf)(A,s)=\mathbf1_{\{s\geq2\}}
 \int_0^{s-2}\left[
 \frac12 f(A+2+u/2,s-2-u)-f(A+2+u,s-2-u)
 \right]du.}                                             \tag{4}
\]

## 2. Exact global theorem

**Theorem 1 (all ray chambers).**  For every $N\geq2$, $|\theta|>1$, and
$\rho\geq m$,

\[
\boxed{
A_N(\theta,\rho)=
\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}\binom Nr\mathcal T^rH_{N-r}(a,\delta).} \tag{5}
\]

Only terms with $2r\leq\delta$ are nonzero.  Hence on the chamber

\[
2R\leq\delta\leq2(R+1),                                  \tag{6}
\]

formula (5) contains only $0\leq r\leq\min(R,N-1)$.  It is therefore an
explicit finite classification, rather than an asymptotic or recursive
geometric definition.

The $r=0$ term is exactly the first-segment Legendre law

\[
H_N(a,\delta)=\frac{a^{N-1}}{(N-1)!}
P_{N-1}\!\left(1+\frac{2\delta}{a}\right).               \tag{7}
\]

For $0\leq\delta\leq2$, all other terms vanish and (7) recovers the earlier
boundary theorem.  Formula (5) extends it through every successive entrance
of opposite-tail coordinates.

## 3. Explicit truncated-power coefficients

The operator formula can be flattened into rational coefficients.  Write

\[
H_d(A,s)=\sum_{q=0}^{d-1}h^{(d)}_{d-1-q,q}A^{d-1-q}s^q
\quad(s\geq0),                                           \tag{8}
\]

where

\[
h^{(d)}_{d-1-q,q}
=\frac{(d-1)_q\,d^{\overline q}}
{(d-1)!(q!)^2}.                                          \tag{9}
\]

Here $(d-1)_q$ is falling and $d^{\overline q}$ is rising.  On a monomial,
one application of $\mathcal T$ has the coefficient action

\[
\boxed{
A^p s^q\longmapsto
\sum_{i=0}^p\binom pi
\left(2^{-i-1}-1\right)
\frac{i!q!}{(i+q+1)!}
(A+2)^{p-i}(s-2)_+^{q+i+1}.}                             \tag{10}
\]

Let $\mathscr R$ denote the rational coefficient map in (10), without the
displayed shifts.  Then (5) is equivalently

\[
\boxed{
A_N(\theta,\rho)=
\sum_{r=0}^{N-1}\binom Nr\mathbf1_{\{\delta\geq2r\}}
\sum_{p,q}
(\mathscr R^r h^{(N-r)})_{p,q}
(a+2r)^p(\delta-2r)^q.}                                  \tag{11}
\]

All coefficients in (11) are rational and generated by (9)--(10).  Every
monomial satisfies

\[
p+q=N-1,qquad q\geq r.                                  \tag{12}
\]

Thus every chamber is a polynomial of total degree $N-1$ in its shifted
variables.  The source checker implements (9)--(11) directly with rational
arithmetic.

## 4. Exact knot smoothness

**Theorem 2 (genuine chamber knots).**  As a function of $\delta$, the section
is polynomial between the knots

\[
2,4,\ldots,2(N-1).                                       \tag{13}
\]

At the knot $\delta=2r$, $1\leq r\leq N-1$, it is exactly
$C^{r-1}$ but not $C^r$.  More precisely, the right-minus-left jump of the
$r$th derivative is

\[
\boxed{
\partial_\delta^rA_N(2r+)-\partial_\delta^rA_N(2r-)
=\binom Nr\frac{(-1)^r}{2^r(N-r-1)!}
(a+2r)^{N-r-1}.}                                        \tag{14}
\]

There are no further knots after $2(N-1)$.  In terms of $\rho$, the knots
are $m+2r/N$, and the derivative jump acquires the chain-rule factor $N^r$.

## 5. Coordinate replacement proof

By reflection it is enough to take $\theta=1+m$.  Relative to the point
$(1,\ldots,1)$, a coordinate is of one of the following forms:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{state}&\text{extra radial slack}&
 \text{amount added to the required plus excess}\\ \hline
1+e&0&0\\
1-y,\ 0\leq y\leq2&y&y\\
-1-e,\ e\geq0&2+2e&2+e.
\end{array}                                               \tag{15}
\]

After the non-plus coordinates have been fixed, the plus excesses have a
prescribed sum.  If there are $p\geq1$ plus coordinates and that sum is $P$,
delta integration contributes

\[
\frac{P^{p-1}}{(p-1)!}.                                  \tag{16}
\]

First replace the bounded inactive state in (15) by a fictitious unbounded
one $y\geq0$.  With $k$ fictitious inactive coordinates and $d-k$ plus
coordinates, (16) gives

\[
\sum_{k=0}^{d-1}\binom dk\frac1{(d-k-1)!}
 \int_{\substack{y_i\geq0\\\sum y_i\leq s}}
 (A+\textstyle\sum y_i)^{d-k-1}dy.                       \tag{17}
\]

Expanding the integrand and applying Vandermonde's identity turns (17) into
$H_d(A,s)$ in (3).  This is the Legendre baseline.

For one labeled coordinate, the difference between the physical non-plus
states and the fictitious unbounded inactive state is supported beyond slack
two.  Put $u=2e$ for the physical negative tail.  It has slack cost $2+u$,
plus-excess shift $2+u/2$, and Jacobian $de=du/2$.  The fictitious inactive
tail $y=2+u$ has the same slack cost, shift $2+u$, and unit Jacobian.  Their
signed difference is exactly the bracket in (4).

Expand the product of the $N$ coordinate state measures around the
fictitious baseline.  Choosing the $r$ coordinates on which the signed
replacement is used gives the factor $\binom Nr$; the other $N-r$ coordinates
give $H_{N-r}$.  Since all coordinates non-plus cannot meet
$\sum x_i=(1+m)N$, the $r=N$ term is zero.  Fubini's theorem now gives (5).
Every replacement costs at least two units of slack, proving the chamber
truncation in (6).

## 6. Coefficient action and smoothness proof

For a monomial in the remaining kernel, expand the shifted first argument in
(4).  The beta integral

\[
\int_0^S u^i(S-u)^qdu
=\frac{i!q!}{(i+q+1)!}S^{i+q+1}                           \tag{18}
\]

and the signed moment $2^{-i-1}-1$ give (10).  One application preserves
$p+q$ after increasing it by one and raises the slack exponent by at least
one.  Starting from $H_{N-r}$ and applying $\mathcal T$ exactly $r$ times
proves (11)--(12).

The smallest new slack exponent at order $r$ is obtained from the
$A^{N-r-1}/(N-r-1)!$ term of $H_{N-r}$ and by taking $i=0$ at every
replacement.  Its coefficient, including the label choice, is

\[
\binom Nr\frac{(-1)^r}{2^r r!(N-r-1)!}.                  \tag{19}
\]

All other new terms vanish to higher order at $\delta=2r$.  Multiplication by
$r!$ proves the nonzero derivative jump (14), and therefore the exact
smoothness classification.

## 7. Low-dimensional examples

With $x_+=\max(x,0)$, the complete $N=2$ formula is

\[
A_2=a+2\delta-(\delta-2)_+.                              \tag{20}
\]

For $N=3$ it is

\[
\begin{aligned}
A_3={}&\frac{a^2}{2}+3a\delta+3\delta^2\\
&-\frac32(a+2)(\delta-2)_+
-\frac{21}{8}(\delta-2)_+^2
+\frac38(\delta-4)_+^2.                                 \tag{21}
\end{aligned}
\]

These show concretely how the first Legendre polynomial piece is corrected
when one and then two opposite-tail events become possible.

## 8. Verification boundary

The verifier constructs (11) from the rational recurrence (9)--(10), then
compares it with the adjacent general affine-stratum engine, which directly
enumerates plus, minus, and inactive labels by a different decomposition.  It
tests every chamber through and beyond the last knot in dimensions two
through eight, several offset magnitudes, reflection, all knot endpoints, the
Legendre specialization, homogeneity, vanishing orders, and every derivative
jump through dimension twenty.  Computation corroborates the formulas; the
coordinate replacement proof establishes all dimensions and radii.
