## 1. Statement

Let $0<a<1$, $b=1-a^2$, and
$U=ru$, $V=sv$ with $|u|=|v|=1$. Define

$$
C_a(U,V)=\int_0^1(a+btU)^4(a+btV)^4\,dt,\qquad
O_a(U,V)=9\int_0^1(1-atU)^4(1-atV)^4\,dt.
\tag{1}
$$

Put

$$
m=\frac{r+s}{2},\quad h=\frac{r-s}{2},\quad
\xi=\frac{\operatorname{Re}(U+V)}2,\quad
\sigma=\frac{r^2+s^2}{2}=m^2+h^2.
$$

**Theorem.** If

$$
r,s\ge(1+a)^{-1},\qquad r+s\le2,\qquad |C_a(U,V)|\ge1,
\tag{2}
$$

then, with $\kappa=256/32955$,

$$
\boxed{\xi>a+\kappa\frac{b}{a}.}
\tag{3}
$$

There is no small-imbalance, individual reciprocal-disk, original-root,
centroid or origin-channel hypothesis. A sharper variance-sensitive version is

$$
\xi-a\ge
\frac{b}{6aK^3}\bigl(1-2h^2M^3\bigr)>
\kappa\frac{b}{a},
\quad
M=1+2a^2(1-a),\quad K=(a+b)^2+b^2h^2.
\tag{4}
$$

Consequently,

$$
(1-m)+\frac14\{r|u-1|^2+s|v-1|^2\}
< (1-a)-\kappa\frac{b}{a}.
\tag{5}
$$

The right side must be positive. This controls the weighted
critical-reciprocal phases and radial-budget slack, without identifying
them with angles of original polynomial roots.

## 2. The established polar envelope

The squared-modulus arithmetic–geometric mean inequality gives

$$
|C_a(U,V)|
\le H_a(\xi,\sigma):=
\int_0^1[a^2+2ab\xi t+b^2\sigma t^2]^4\,dt.
\tag{6}
$$

Indeed the bracket is the average of $|a+btU|^2$ and $|a+btV|^2$,
so it is nonnegative; the product of those squared moduli is at most
the square of their average. The triangle inequality and fourth powers
give (6). This is the degree-nine two-value specialization of the
established raw second-moment polar envelope, not a new communication identity.

The radial hypotheses give

$$
(1+a)^{-1}\le m\le1,\quad |h|\le m-(1+a)^{-1}
\le\frac{a}{1+a},\quad |\xi|\le m\le1,\quad \sigma\le1+h^2.
\tag{7}
$$

We first bound $H_a(a,\sigma)$ uniformly over this entire imbalance range.
Write

$$
\Phi(t)=a^2+2a^2bt+b^2t^2,\qquad
F_a(a)=\int_0^1\Phi(t)^4\,dt.
$$

The previously published degree-nine polar defect is

$$
F_a(a)\le1-\frac23b^2.
\tag{8}
$$

For completeness the present checker reconstructs its entire short proof:
with $A=a^2$, $D=1-A$,

$$
1-\int_0^1[A+2ADt+D^2t^2]^4\,dt=D^2Q(A),
$$

$$
Q(A)=\frac89+\frac{23}{21}A-\frac1{105}A^2
-\frac{428}{315}A^3-\frac{121}{105}A^4
+\frac{169}{105}A^5-\frac{128}{315}A^6.
$$

Its degree-six Bernstein coefficients are

$$
\left(\frac89,\frac{15}{14},\frac{94}{75},\frac{41}{30},
       \frac{19}{15},1,\frac23\right).
\tag{9}
$$

Nonnegative Bernstein basis functions sum to one, so $Q\ge2/3$.
Both convolution and a separate multinomial integration give the full
factor identity; inverse finite differences recover the complete power
polynomial from (9). This reproduction retains credit to the earlier source.

## 3. Absorbing unrestricted radial variance

$$
M=1+a^2b+\frac{a^2b^2}{(1+a)^2}=1+2a^2(1-a).

Here we used (7) and the increasing coefficients in $\Phi$.

Equations (7) and (11) imply

$$

$$

For $0\le x\le y\le M$, factorization gives
$y^4-x^4\le4M^3(y-x)$. Applying this pointwise and integrating $t^2$
yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
H_a(a,\sigma)
&\le \int_0^1[\Phi(t)+b^2h^2t^2]^4\,dt\\
&\le F_a(a)+\frac43b^2h^2M^3\\
&\le1-\frac23(1-2h^2M^3)b^2\\

\end{aligned}
\tag{13}
$$

If $\xi\le a$, the true bracket in (6) is nonnegative and bounded
above by its value at $\xi=a$. Equations (6) and (13) contradict
$|C_a|\ge1$. Hence $\xi>a$. This step extends the earlier balanced
mean argument to the full admissible radial-imbalance interval.

## 4. Quantitative forcing and angular loss

For $\eta\in[a,\xi]$, the bracket defining $H_a(\eta,\sigma)$
is nonnegative and at most $K=(a+b)^2+b^2h^2$.
Differentiation of the polynomial integral therefore gives

$$
0\le \frac{\partial H_a}{\partial\eta}
=8ab\int_0^1t[a^2+2ab\eta t+b^2\sigma t^2]^3\,dt
\le4abK^3.
\tag{14}
$$

Using $H_a(\xi,\sigma)\ge1$ and (13), the fundamental theorem of
calculus gives the first inequality in (4). To make it uniform, observe

$$
K\le (5/4)^2+[a(1-a)]^2\le\frac{13}{8}.
\tag{15}
$$

The two scalar bounds are elementary:
$5/4-(a+b)=(a-1/2)^2$ and
$1/4-a(1-a)=(a-1/2)^2$.
Since $1-2h^2M^3>1/5$ by (12),

$$
\frac{b}{6aK^3}(1-2h^2M^3)
>\frac{b}{30a(13/8)^3}
=\frac{256}{32955}\frac{b}{a}.
$$

This proves (3)–(4). Finally,

$$
r|u-1|^2+s|v-1|^2=4(m-\xi),
$$

so (5) is the exact identity $1-\xi$ followed by (3).

## 6. Remaining first-power proof boundary

For an actual monic degree-nine disk-root polynomial with a simple marked
zero rotated to $a\in(0,1)$, write critical reciprocals
$U_j=(a-w_j)^{-1}$. If four are $U$ and four are $V$, a hypothetical
first-power failure gives $r+s\le2$.
Gauss–Lucas gives the radius lower bound in (2), and the standard polar
communication identity gives $|C_a(U,V)|\ge1$. Thus (3)–(5) are
necessary in any such failure.

The standard origin identity additionally gives
$|O_a(U,V)|\le r^4s^4$. To finish the unrestricted two-value case,
one still needs an origin lower bound on the actual-mean domain, or a
different communication obstruction. The conjectured estimate

$$
\frac{|O_a(U,V)|^2}{r^8s^8}\ge3-2a
\quad\text{when (7) holds and }\xi\ge a
$$

is **not proved here**. Bounded floating-point probes are private heuristic
guidance, absent from the certificate. The false comparison (16) cannot
provide that estimate. Individual disk constraints, centroid information
or the second origin identity may still be useful; the present theorem
does not show that they suffice.
