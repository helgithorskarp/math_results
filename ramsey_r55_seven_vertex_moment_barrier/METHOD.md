# Finite counting identities and certificate boundary

Let `n=43`, `k=7`. Encode graph edges in lexicographic order of unordered
vertex pairs. Canonicalization uses the group generated by adjacent vertex
swaps, acting on every labeled adjacency mask. It does not use a presumed
automorphism of a physical candidate.

For each allowed seven-vertex graph `H`, pair its isomorphism class with its
complement. Every pair has two classes, since seven vertices have an odd
number of possible edges. Let `x_H` be the fraction of seven-subsets of a
putative physical graph whose induced graph belongs to this pair. Then
`x_H >= 0` and `sum x_H=1`. Every coefficient column is the sum of the
coefficient for `H` and its complement. Consequently the averaged matrix
or scalar value represents the sum of the corresponding counts in `G`
and its complement. We never require `G` to be self-complementary.

## Conditioned linear rows

Fix a six-vertex induced flag `F` rooted at a vertex `v`. Delete `w` from
an induced seven-set `H`. The upper-degree contribution is

`24 - d_H(v) - (n-k) 1[vw is red]`.

The lower-degree contribution is its analogous
`d_H(v)+(n-k)1[vw is red]-18`. Summing over all choices of the additional
vertex outside a fixed `F` yields `(n-6)(24-d_G(v))`, or
`(n-6)(d_G(v)-18)`. Grouping by the isomorphism class of the rooted `F`
therefore gives nonnegative rows. The five unrooted flag vertices may be
permuted; the root is fixed.

For a red edge `uv` in `F`, the same argument gives

`10 - c_H(uv) - (n-k) 1[w is a common red neighbor of u,v]`.

The root edge is unordered in these scalar rows. Grouping over the
permutations preserving this pair gives the conditioned codegree rows.
Adding complementary colors is legitimate because the degree and book
bounds hold in both. There are exactly 1,584 rows in the generated system.

## Exact finite square lifting

Fix an ordered root of size `t` and a family of flags made by adding an
unordered set of `a` exterior vertices. Write `f_i` for the number of
exterior sets of flag type `i`. The matrix `f f^T` is positive
semidefinite. Average it over all embeddings of the root into `G` and its
complement.

In an induced `k`-vertex graph, consider two exterior sets `A,B` with union
`U` of size `s`. Its exact lifting coefficient is

`w_s = L * binom(n-t,s) / binom(k-t,s)`.

Here `L` clears all denominators in the relevant family. A fixed union of
size `s` occurs in a uniformly sampled exterior `(k-t)`-set with probability
`binom(k-t,s)/binom(n-t,s)`. Thus the coefficient above cancels this
probability exactly, including all overlaps. No asymptotic approximation
or disjointness assumption is made. The average over the root embeddings
contributes the positive common factor `(k)_t/(n)_t`.

The unweighted square families are:

- ordered red edge, two exterior vertices: dimension 20, multiplier 10;
- vertex, two exterior vertices: dimension 6, multiplier 60;
- vertex, three exterior vertices: dimension 20, multiplier 60;
- ordered three-root type with one edge or three edges, two exterior
  vertices: dimensions 72 and 71, multiplier 12;
- all 32 allowed unlabeled five-root types, one exterior vertex:
  dimensions 29, 30, or 32 after removing identically zero coordinates,
  multiplier 2.

The two three-root types represent all four types after color reversal.
All five-root types are kept; complementary redundancy is harmless.
Roots are explicitly ordered at matrix evaluation. Only exterior
permutations are factored when defining flag coordinates. Summing over
all root embeddings accounts for their automorphisms without any quotient
weights. The zero-coordinate deletion is determined over every allowed
seven-vertex column.

## Weighted squares

If a nonnegative integer count is `d`, the weights `24-d`, `d-18`,
`10-c`, and `(d-j)(d-j-1)` are used only in domains where they are
nonnegative. In particular `(z-j)(z-j-1)>=0` for every integer `z`.
For the selected root and union `U`, write the relevant degree or
codegree as `r+z`, where `r` counts eligible vertices inside `U` and `z`
counts those outside `U`.

The exact lifted entry for `(b-d) f f^T` is

`(b-r) w_s - z_H w_(s+1)`.

The exact lifted entry for `(d-j)(d-j-1) f f^T` is

`(r-j)(r-j-1) w_s + 2(r-j) z_H w_(s+1)`
`+ z_H(z_H-1) w_(s+2)`.

The final term counts ordered distinct exterior vertices. Sampling cancels
their probability with the corresponding union size, as before. The
linear localizers are the degree upper and lower bounds on vertex
two-exterior flags and the book bound on edge two-exterior flags. Integer
localizers use `j=18..23` with the vertex flags and `j=0..9` with the four
edge one-exterior flag types. Every required union fits in seven vertices.

## Scalar integer-support polynomials

For each `d in {18,...,24}`, use the polynomial

`sign(product_(e != d)(d-e)) * product_(e != d)(z-e)`.

It is nonnegative on the allowed degree set and has degree six. There are
also 56 degree-five polynomials obtained by choosing five distinct zeros
from `{0,...,10}` and retaining either sign precisely when the polynomial
is nonnegative on that entire integer set. No completeness assertion
about all possible polynomial certificates is required.

Expand each polynomial in the binomial basis by exact forward differences:
`P(z)=sum_s Delta^s P(0) binom(z,s)`. The degree row uses
`60 binom(42,s)/binom(6,s)`, and the codegree row uses
`10 binom(41,s)/binom(5,s)`. These are the same finite sampling identity.
The codegree scalar sum uses unordered red edges; matrix roots remain
ordered. The distinction is retained in the direct-count checks.

## Exact strict feasibility and its implication

The supplied vector is `x_i=p_i/D`, with integer positive numerators and
`D=sum p_i=1000000173109`. Verification first checks graph identities and
the exact sum, then evaluates all scalar numerators and matrix numerators.
All scalar numerators are positive. Each symmetric matrix is positive
definite by Sylvester's criterion: fraction-free Bareiss elimination
computes all its leading principal minors with exact divisions, and all
are positive. The checker hashes the complete list of minors for every
matrix. Numerical eigenvalues are not used for acceptance.

Suppose a proposed contradiction uses nonnegative scalar multipliers and
positive semidefinite matrix multipliers from precisely this system,
together with the simplex constraints. Evaluating it at this exact
feasible vector prevents a negative constant from being obtained. Thus
this cone cannot exclude the target. This conclusion is about the
specified relaxation only: actual graph densities also satisfy further
identities and integer conditions. No physical existence or nonexistence
is inferred from this control.

## Validation and arithmetic bounds

`validate.py` builds the finite formulas with ambient order eight and
compares them with literal global counts in the cycle `C8` and cube `Q3`.
It sums all eight induced seven-subsets, then compares every entry to
direct products of whole-graph flag counts, multiplied by the exact
sampling factor. The factors for ordered roots of sizes one, two, three,
and five are respectively `420,60,60,6` for the families above. Conditioned
six-flag scalar rows have factor two. The two fixtures and their
complements avoid the forbidden five-sets; their degrees need not lie in
`18..24`, because this check tests polynomial identities, not positivity
under the target assumptions.

Generation uses signed 64-bit integers. A uniform conservative bound for
each generated coefficient and partial accumulated sum is `10^15`:
there are at most 20,160 root/set-pair terms in a column; lifting weights
are at most `60*binom(42,6)`; the largest square-localizer coefficient
expansion is bounded by the displayed quadratic expression with
`r,z<=6`, `j<=23`; applying the actual union sizes gives a bound below
`10^15`. The seven scalar degree polynomials have total absolute binomial
expansion bounded by `14 sum_s |Delta^s P(0)| w_s binom(6,s)`, also below
`10^15`. The other scalar polynomials are smaller. Certificate evaluation
uses Python arbitrary-precision integers whenever the explicit product
bound could exceed signed 64-bit range. Bareiss minors always use Python
integers. The trust boundary is the mathematical finite-count derivation,
the generator, ordinary Python/NumPy execution, and the integer checker;
there is no proof-assistant formalization or independent external review.
