The declared construction test averages the published geometric fractional
dual inequality uniformly over a finite set of distinct plane isometries.
It then bounds every congruence discrepancy by one common Følner boundary
ratio. This proof concerns that sufficient test, not all proofs or colourings
of the resulting physical union.

Let H be the published Snail support and let T be the full finite set of
isometries taking some subset of H of size at least two onto another such
subset, including self-congruences. This is exactly the generator convention
in Theorem 1 of Matolcsi--Ruzsa--Varga--Zsámboki. It includes the identity and
is inverse-closed. Let R be a finite nonempty set of distinct isometries and
put M=|R|, b=|RT minus R|, and V=the union of r(H) over r in R. Coincident
physical points are merged. The graph on V includes every unit pair.

For a hypothetical proper four-colouring of V, use its four colour classes,
including empty classes if necessary, as a regular fractional colouring of
weight four. Pull its restrictions back along all r in R and average. Call
the aggregate on a source subset A by m(A). For nonempty A, 0<=m(A)<=1,
and m({v})=1 for every source vertex. The averaged total weight is four.

If t(A)=B, cancellation of the terms indexed by R intersect Rt leaves at
most b terms in each tail. Each term is in [0,1], so

    |m(A)-m(B)| <= b/M.

The bound is b/M, rather than twice this value: both tails have the same
cardinality and their sums each belong to [0,b]. Terms in a tail are evaluated
only on source images already lying in V. No extension of the colouring to
the whole plane is assumed.

The external dual has value

    z = 4000716307/1000000018,
    g = z-4 = 716235/1000000018.

Its convention is y^T C - z e + 1 >= 0 on every independent set. Summing
against the averaged colouring gives

    g <= sum_j y_j (m(A_j)-m(B_j)).

Validity of that dual inequality and the geometric congruences are imported
from the source paper and supplement; this package does not reproduce their
498,168 independent-set inequalities or symbolic coordinate verification.

Singleton rows contribute zero. At each remaining distinct subset A, combine
its signed coefficients into w_A. Within each connected component of the
listed congruence graph, sum_A w_A=0. Therefore, if the aggregates in that
component have range at most b/M, its contribution is at most

    (b/M) sum_{w_A>0} w_A.

Summing over components gives the sufficient contradiction test

    L b/M < g,
    L = (1/2) sum_A |w_A| = 2380948335743/500000009.

This coefficient is sharp for the relaxation that treats each component's
aggregates as arbitrary real numbers in an interval of width b/M: put
positive-coefficient entries at one endpoint and negative entries at the
other. Such assignments need not arise from colourings. No optimality over
valid colourings, alternative duals, or sharper geometric estimates is claimed.

The uncombined non-singleton coefficient sum is
2991183105354/500000009. Thus regrouping improves the sufficient test, but
does not make it usable at the physical cap.

First, b>=1. Choose three distinct source points a,b,c. The half-turns about
the midpoints of {a,b} and {a,c} belong to T; their product is a nonzero
translation. Hence the group generated by T is infinite. If RT=R for a finite
nonempty R, closure would force R to contain an infinite translation orbit,
a contradiction. Consequently this test requires

    M > L/g = 4761896671486/716235,
    M >= 6648512.

Second, H contains a unit edge. For example, the first and seventh columns
of the G27 table differ by omega_1*omega_3, whose norm is one. For a fixed
ordered source unit edge, an isometry is determined by its ordered target
edge and its choice of orientation. If the complete unit graph on V has m
edges, then M<=4m, including possible source automorphisms and different
isometries with the same image set.

For n distinct plane points, two different vertices have at most two common
unit neighbours, by intersection of their unit circles. Therefore

    sum_v binom(deg(v),2) <= 2 binom(n,2).

Cauchy--Schwarz and sum_v deg(v)=2m give

    2m^2 - nm <= n^2(n-1),
    m <= floor((n + sqrt(n^2(8n-7)))/4).

At n=508, this yields m<=8216 and M<=32864. The declared error budget is
at least

    L/32864 = 2380948335743/16432000295776 > g.

Thus no union with at most 508 distinct physical points can pass this
particular sufficient contradiction test. Combining the same necessary
inequalities gives n>=17648 for a support that could pass it; this number
is a floor for this certificate test only, not a lower bound for any class
of five-chromatic plane graphs.

The conclusion does not say that the true congruence discrepancies reach
the error estimate, or that the union is four-colourable. Nonuniform weights,
smaller generator sets, other duals, individual congruence defects, genuine
colouring-dependent cancellations and direct SAT refutations are outside
the declared test. No new physical support or candidate was constructed.
