# Graph, certificate and activation semantics

## Exact graph and theorem

Use the committed global labels L={0,...,373}, S={374,...,508} and the
specified 168-point completion set Q5. Coordinates are those pinned in
manifest.json, represented at common denominator 288 in the bit-mask
basis of Q(sqrt(3),sqrt(5),sqrt(11)). Let X be `pool_labels` in
certificate.json and put H=L union X, including every exact unit edge.

The graph has 574 distinct vertices and 2707 edges. X has 200 points,
of which 110 lie in S and 90 in Q5. Its omitted S labels are

```text
374 375 376 377 378 379 380 381 382 383 385 387 390 391 394
395 396 411 413 415 422 427 435 454 494
```

The exact global-label edge stream consists of `a,b` followed by a
newline for each sorted edge. SHA-256:

```text
37d330b472e101c001e04aca6a1dc52ddf4f048d025adce0794f4e521682f575
```

**Theorem.** H is five-chromatic, and H-v is four-chromatic for every
v in X. Consequently L union Y is four-colourable for every proper
subset Y of X.

No assertion about deleting an L vertex is included. In particular
this is not a full vertex-criticality theorem or a minimum-order result
over the entire 303-point pool. The order is larger than the record.

## Positive certificates

Vertices are ordered as all L labels followed by sorted X. The string
`five_colouring` supplies a digit in {0,1,2,3,4} for each vertex. Every
strict edge has differently coloured endpoints, proving chi(H)<=5.

There is one deletion row per v in X. Its pool string is indexed by
sorted X and has a dot exactly at v. Its pattern index selects a full
374-character L colouring from the pinned interface_L.json witness
table. The checker concatenates that L row with the pool row and checks
every retained strict edge of H-v. This proves chi(H-v)<=4. Only the
explicit proper colourings are used: completeness of that table plays
no role in positive verification.

Once chi(H)>=5 is established below, chi(H-v)>=4 follows because adding
one vertex raises chromatic number by at most one. For any proper
Y subset X, choose v in X minus Y and restrict the verified colouring
of H-v to L union Y. This proves the proper-subset corollary without
enumerating all subsets.

## Negative certificate on the full graph

For every vertex v of H introduce four Boolean variables C(v,c).
The CNF contains an at-least-one clause for each vertex and

    not C(v,c) OR not C(w,c)

for each strict edge vw and each colour c. At-most-one clauses are
unnecessary: a satisfying assignment gives at least one available
colour at each vertex, and choosing any such colour produces a proper
colouring because all same-colour edge pairs are forbidden. Conversely,
a proper four-colouring satisfies the clauses by setting its one chosen
colour true at each vertex.

The three unit clauses pin vertices (0,149,152) to colours (0,1,2).
The checker verifies that these vertices form a triangle. In any proper
four-colouring they have distinct colours, so a global permutation
achieves the pins. The pinned and unpinned graph formulas are therefore
equisatisfiable.

The resulting full-graph CNF has 2296 variables and 11405 clauses.
Kissat produced a DRAT refutation and drat-trim accepted it. The
ordinary graph-CNF equivalence thus gives chi(H)>=5, completing the
theorem with the positive five-colouring. This proof uses no interface
classification or assumption-core verdict. It concerns the exact full
graph on the displayed vertices, not a dequantified family formula.

The producing proof has 7336948 bytes, SHA-256

```text

```

It can be regenerated by the README commands. The source reconstructs
the exact CNF and checks a supplied proof using drat-trim; without the
proof argument, it explicitly reports only the positive checks.

## Discovery encoding and bounded minimalization

The discovery oracle retains all of L and uses its previously proved
twenty-class interface to represent possible L boundary colourings.
This completeness theorem is a discovery assumption, eliminated from
the final full-graph certificate above.

For each pool vertex v introduce an activation a_v and four colour
variables. Include only

    not a_v OR C(v,0) OR C(v,1) OR C(v,2) OR C(v,3)

as the activation-dependent vertex clause. All pool edge clauses and
pattern-conditioned cross-edge clauses remain unguarded, and at least
one pattern is required. Only positive activation assumptions for a
selected set X are passed to the solver. Inactive colour variables may
all be false, so they need no colour and cannot obstruct a colouring.

For a proper colouring of L union X, select its normalized boundary
pattern, set the corresponding selected colours and activations true,
and set all absent-vertex colours and activations false. This satisfies
the formula. Conversely, under the positive X assumptions every vertex
of X has some true colour. Choose a true pattern and one true colour at
each selected vertex; the edge and cross-edge clauses give a proper
colouring compatible with that pattern's L witness. Extra active or
coloured vertices outside X do not invalidate restriction to X. This
proves the fixed-selection equivalence.

If the solver returns an UNSAT core of positive assumptions, those
selected vertices alone still refute the activation formula. Such
returns guide discovery, but are not independent proofs. The final
selected graph receives the separate DRAT certificate above.

Discovery starts from U minus vertex 374 and makes one deletion pass,
trying original S vertices before Q5 points in increasing label order.
An UNSAT answer permits replacement by its returned assumption core;
a SAT answer saves a directly checked colouring after that deletion.
Subsequent cores only shrink the selected set. Therefore a saved
colouring for an earlier trial without v restricts to the final H-v.
Every final selected vertex has such a positive witness. An UNKNOWN
answer would permit neither a deletion nor a minimality conclusion;
none occurred in this run.

The activation formula has 1535 variables and 7040 clauses. All 1131
selections in 58 finite abstract fixtures were compared with a direct
backtracking graph-colouring definition. The 221 returned small
non-colourable cores were also checked directly. The known 509-vertex
and deletion-of-397 fixed controls gave the expected opposite answers.
These are implementation calibrations, not a universal proof by testing.

## Trust and limits

Coordinates and every strict unit edge are reconstructed with the
reviewed integer geometry reader; its source and coordinate tables
are pinned. Positive colourings are checked directly on the resulting
graph. Negative verification trusts the elementary CNF bridge and
the DRAT checker. The final certificate does not trust discovery's
UNSAT verdicts, the twenty-class completeness theorem, or the deletion
order. The checks are author verification and ordinary mathematical
arguments, not external peer review or proof-assistant formalization.

This one bounded extraction and certification is complete. It gives
a non-four-colourable seed involving many completion points and a
proper-subset closure inside that seed. It gives no <=508 graph, no
new full-pool lower bound, and no assertion that every critical graph
in this coordinate family resembles this one. A prospective audit of
L deletions is a separate phase and has not started.
