**Verdict: accept with high confidence as a conditional local theorem.** If a classical six-colouring \(C\) of \([1,537]\) exists, forbidding monochromatic \(x+y=z\) including \(x=y\), its first 536 entries differ from the specified Fredricksen–Sweet baseline at at least 54 positions under every global relabelling. ## Proof reduction checked

The previously audited certificate gives 51 disjoint mandatory edit groups when 537 has colour 2, 4, 5 or 6. A colouring with at most 53 edits has one regular edit in each group and at most two additional, or *slack*, edits. The prior 53-edit review excludes zero or one slack. Any two-slack assignment can designate distinct positions \(j,k\) as its extra edits, whether they lie inside or outside groups. Each group then has exactly one edit among its vertices other than \(j,k\), while \(j,k\) are edited; all positions outside these groups and slacks retain the baseline colour. This is the exact finite model checked in the final step. Colours 1 and 3 already require at least 64 and 55 edits from complementary pairs alone.

The first no-slack refutation core shows that at least one slack lies among 16, 16, 36 or 16 sensitive positions, respectively. For each such first slack \(j\), a verified one-slack refutation core shows that the second slack \(k\) must affect one of its tagged premises. One-hot clauses persist. A group pairwise at-most-one-edit clause can disappear only when \(k\) is one of its vertices; a Schur clause with substituted fixed vertices can weaken only when \(k\) is one of those vertices. A one-slack group clause requiring a regular edit outside \(j\) remains valid even if \(k\) is in that group, because the two-slack model still requires a regular edit outside both. These facts make the dependency pruning sound. The surviving unordered pairs number 151, 178, 959 and 225, for 1,513 exact cases total.

## Novelty, scope, and trust boundary

The [Fredricksen–Sweet paper](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/view/v7i1r32) established the 536-colouring. A [July 2026 primary preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15034) still cites \(S(6)\ge536\). Targeted primary-source searches found no earlier external 54-edit theorem for this specified baseline; this is **potential novelty**, not a historical priority claim. In the committed graph it is a strict refinement of the reviewed 53-edit finding. Its exact restricted statement is reproducible and suitable for citation, while a numerical advance on unrestricted \(S(6)\) still needs either a valid 537-colouring or a global exclusion of all such colourings.

The trust boundary is the displayed disjoint-group and two-slack reductions, the public baseline and complete witness data, exact standard-library Python execution, sound core-dependency tags, and exhaustive verification of the surviving cases. No SAT solver result, floating point, hidden witness, or cutoff is used. The independent audit shares the baseline and original witness data with the source and reuses its own previous independent validators; it is not a proof-assistant formalization.

## Strengthening and improvement opportunities

1. **Seek a 55-edit obstruction with a third slack.** A third layer of verified core sensitivity could prune triples of slack positions, but every surviving exact instance would still need a checked refutation. The present 54-edit proof alone gives no such bound.
2. **Publish compact core fixtures.** Recording clause indices, origin triples, and dependency sets for the small no-slack and one-slack cores would make the 1,513-case reduction easier to inspect without regenerating every large formula. Each fixture should be checked against a fresh definition-level encoder.
3. **Use local exclusions in a global search with their scope intact.** Combine the 54-edit baseline constraint with the independently reviewed 50-edit constraints for the modulus-537 unit orbit as redundant search cuts. A classical 537-colouring need not be modular sum-free, so the 54-edit baseline theorem cannot be transported to every unit transform without new proof.
