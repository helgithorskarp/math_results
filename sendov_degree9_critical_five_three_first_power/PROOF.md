## 1. Polynomial statement and a useful origin minimum

Let \(p\) have degree nine, all zeros in the closed unit disk, and critical
multiset \(\{\zeta_U^5,\zeta_V^3\}\). Coincidence of the two points is allowed.
At every marked zero \(a\), counting critical multiplicities,

\[
 S_1(a):=5|a-\zeta_U|^{-1}+3|a-\zeta_V|^{-1}\ge8.       \tag{1}
\]

A collision contributes \(+\infty\). The inequality is strict for
\(|a|<1\). Equality is precisely
\(p(z)=C(z^9-a^9)\), \(C\ne0,\ |a|=1\). Endpoint identities and the
binomial equality example are credited antecedents, not a new assertion
about ordinary Sendov. The unrestricted degree-nine first-power endpoint
and other critical multiplicities are not settled here.

The new analytic input is the following asymmetric **weighted-mean origin
minimum**. For
\[
 -\tfrac16\le\eta\le\tfrac1{10},\quad
 U=(1+3\eta)u,\quad V=(1-5\eta)v,\quad |u|=|v|=1,
\]
put
\[
 \mu=\frac{5\Re U+3\Re V}{8},\qquad
 O=9\int_0^1(1-bsU)^5(1-bsV)^3\,ds,
\]
\[
 R=(1+3\eta)^{10}(1-5\eta)^6>0.
\]
Whenever \(0\le b\le\mu\),
\[
 \boxed{\ |O|^2/R\ge1\ }.                             \tag{2}
\]
Equality holds exactly at \(b=1,\eta=0,u=v=1\).
In particular (2) is strict for \(b<1\). It uses the **weighted actual
complex mean**, with both signs of the radius imbalance and every common
phase. No assumption about the sign of a bisector phase or of a signed
norm coefficient is imposed.

## 2. Centering at the weighted complex mean

Write
\[
 K=\frac{1+15\eta}{4}\in[-3/8,5/8],\qquad
 A=1+3\eta=\frac45(1+K),\quad B=1-5\eta=\frac43(1-K).
\]
Thus \(5A+3B=8\). The two radii have fixed labels: exchanging them
exchanges multiplicities as well. Weighted AM-GM gives \(A^5B^3\le1\).
If \(b=0\), then \(O=9\) and \(|O|^2/R\ge81>1\), so assume \(b>0\).

Define
\[
 M=\frac{5U+3V}{8}
   =\frac{(1+K)u+(1-K)v}{2}=\rho w,
 \quad |w|=1,\quad w=x+iy.
\]
The triangle inequality and its reverse give
\(|K|\le\rho\le1\). Because \(\mu=\rho x\ge b>0\), \(\rho,x>0\).
Set \(q=\rho^2\) and
\[
 c=\frac{q-K^2}{1-K^2}\in[0,1],\quad
 \delta^2=c(1-c),\quad
 q=K^2+(1-K^2)c,
\]
where the sign of \(\delta\) records the relative orientation. The exact
two-focus geometry gives
\[
 U=\frac{4w}{5\rho}
       \{q+K+i(1-K^2)\delta\},\qquad
 V=\frac{4w}{3\rho}
       \{q-K-i(1-K^2)\delta\}.                         \tag{3}
\]
For clarity, put \(S=(1+K)\bar w u,T=(1-K)\bar w v\).
Then \(S+T=2\rho\), \(|S|=1+K,|T|=1-K\), so
\[
 \Re S=(q+K)/\rho,\qquad
 (\Im S)^2=(1-q)(q-K^2)/q.
\]
Since \((1-q)(q-K^2)=(1-K^2)^2c(1-c)\), these equations prove (3),
including the parallel and opposite-direction limits. No square root
sign has been discarded.

Now \(t=b/(\rho x)\in(0,1]\). Define
\[
 \alpha=\frac45\{q+K+i(1-K^2)\delta\},\qquad
 \beta=\frac43\{q-K-i(1-K^2)\delta\}.
\]
Equation (3) rewrites the original integral as
\[
 I=9\int_0^1(1-txs\,w\alpha)^5
                   (1-txs\,w\beta)^3\,ds.              \tag{4}
\]
This is polynomial in \(t,c,x,K\) and \(i\delta\), after using
\(\delta^2=c(1-c)\). Here \(y^2=1-x^2\). The proof below establishes
the stronger cube statement \(|I|^2\ge R\) for **every**
\(t,c,x\in[0,1]\), \(K\in[-3/8,5/8]\), and either sign of both
\(\delta,y\). Thus it also covers zero-mean limiting coordinates where
the inverse change of variables is undefined.

## 5. The necessary polar mean in the 5+3 budget

Let \(0<a<1\), \(D_a=1-a^2\), \(U=ru,V=sv\), and assume
\[
 r,s\ge\ell:=(1+a)^{-1},\qquad 5r+3s\le8,\qquad
 \left|C:=\int_0^1(a+D_a tU)^5(a+D_a tV)^3\,dt\right|\ge1.
\]
Put \(\xi=(5\Re U+3\Re V)/8\) and
\(\sigma=(5r^2+3s^2)/8\). Convexity on the triangular radius budget gives
\[
 \sigma\le1+\frac53(1-\ell)^2
       =1+\frac53\frac{a^2}{(1+a)^2}.                   \tag{11}
\]
Indeed its three vertices are \((\ell,\ell)\),
\(((8-3\ell)/5,\ell)\), and \((\ell,(8-5\ell)/3)\).
The corresponding moments are \(\ell^2\),
\(1+(3/5)(1-\ell)^2\), and \(1+(5/3)(1-\ell)^2\).
Since \(\ell\le1\), the last is largest. Convexity reduces every budget
point to these vertices; the code checks both nontrivial exact identities.

The classical weighted squared-modulus AM-GM polar estimate yields
\[
 |C|\le\int_0^1
  [a^2+2aD_a\xi t+D_a^2\sigma t^2]^4\,dt.              \tag{12}
\]
Its bracket is the weighted average of two squared moduli and is
nonnegative. If \(\xi\le a\), increase it using (11) and replacing
\(\xi\) by \(a\), to obtain \(|C|\le H_{53}(a)\), where
\[
 H_{53}(a)=\int_0^1
 [a^2+2a^2D_a t+D_a^2(1+5a^2/[3(1+a)^2])t^2]^4\,dt.
\]
The degree-22 polynomial
\[
 W_{53}(a)=\frac{(1+a)^8[1-H_{53}(a)]}{(1-a)^2}
\]
has all **23** Bernstein coefficients at least \(8/9\).
The exact checker reconstructs it by quadratic convolution with a common
denominator, and independently by a direct multinomial integral using
\(D_a^2/(1+a)^2=(1-a)^2\). Exact division has zero remainder, and the
complete inverse basis identity is checked. Thus
\[
 H_{53}(a)\le1-\frac{8(1-a)^2}{9(1+a)^8}<1,
 \quad\text{and therefore}\quad\xi>a.                  \tag{13}
\]

## 6. Deduction for every disk-root polynomial in the stated class

Rotate a nonzero marked zero to real \(a=|a_{\rm old}|\).
Handle a critical marked zero by the infinite convention. Otherwise let
\(U=(a-\zeta_U)^{-1}\), \(V=(a-\zeta_V)^{-1}\). Gauss--Lucas gives
\(r=|U|,s=|V|\ge(1+a)^{-1}\).

For \(0<a<1\), suppose \(S_1\le8\), and put
\[
 m=\frac{5r+3s}{8}\le1,\quad
 \eta=\frac{r-s}{5r+3s},\quad b=am<1.
\]
Then \(r=m(1+3\eta),s=m(1-5\eta)\). With fixed labels the correct
necessary signed bounds are
\[
 -\frac{b}{3(1+b)}\le\eta\le\frac{b}{5(1+b)},            \tag{14}
\]
and hence \(-1/6\le\eta\le1/10\). For \(\eta\ge0\), the smaller
radius is \(s\) and \((m+b)(1-5\eta)=(1+a)s\ge1\).
Replacing \(m\le1\) by one proves the upper bound. For \(\eta\le0\)
use \(r\) in exactly the stated lower-radius inequality to obtain the
lower bound. There is no unjustified radius-swap symmetry.

The classical communication identities, with \(z_1,\ldots,z_8\) the
other zeros, give
\[
 \frac{9\int_0^1(1-atU)^5(1-atV)^3dt}{U^5V^3}
       =\prod_{j=1}^8(-z_j),
\]
\[
 C=\int_0^1(a+(1-a^2)tU)^5(a+(1-a^2)tV)^3dt
       =\prod_{j=1}^8\frac{1-az_j}{a-z_j}.              \tag{15}
\]
These follow by integrating \(p'\) from \(a\) to \(0\) and to \(1/a\).
Define \(N_{\rm actual}\) as the squared modulus of the first numerator
divided by \(r^{10}s^6\). They imply \(N_{\rm actual}\le1\) and
\(|C|\ge1\), because
\(|1-az_j|^2-|a-z_j|^2=(1-a^2)(1-|z_j|^2)\ge0\).
Equation (13) forces \(\xi>a\). For the normalized reciprocals
\(U/m,V/m\), the weighted real mean is
\[
 \mu=\xi/m>a/m\ge am=b.
\]
The new minimum (2) therefore applies and is strict, since \(b<1\):
\[
 N_{\rm actual}
   =m^{-16}\frac{|9\int_0^1(1-b\tau U/m)^5(1-b\tau V/m)^3d\tau|^2}
                    {(1+3\eta)^{10}(1-5\eta)^6}
   >m^{-16}\ge1.
\]
This contradicts (15) and proves strict (1) for every interior nonzero root.

At \(a=0\), the classical derivative product gives
\(\prod|q_j|=9/\prod|z_j|\ge9\). AM-GM yields
\(S_1\ge8\,9^{1/8}>8\). This applies to every critical multiset.

At a simple boundary root \(a=1\), the classical logarithmic derivative
identity gives
\[
 5U+3V=2\sum_{j=1}^8(1-z_j)^{-1},\qquad
 \Re(1-z_j)^{-1}\ge1/2.
\]
Thus \(S_1\ge\Re(5U+3V)\ge8\), without a polar estimate.
If equality holds, \(U,V\) are positive real and \(5r+3s=8\).
Now \(m=1,\mu=b=1\), the radius bounds give (14) with \(b=1\), and
the origin identity together with (2) forces \(\eta=0,U=V=1\).
Every critical point is zero, so \(p(z)=C(z^9-1)\). Conversely this
polynomial attains equality. Undo rotation to obtain the stated case.
